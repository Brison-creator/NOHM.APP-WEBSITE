Notices before court.

Notice to quit for nonpayment (3 full days, JD-HM-7)

Connecticut’s notice to quit for unpaid rent, in the statute’s words, served by a state marshal.

When
Rent is still unpaid 9 days after it was due (from the 10th day; 5 days later if your online payment system blocked it).
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(a)(1)(D); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b)–(c); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-15a(a); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-75(f)(2)
Good to know
  • Wait out the grace period first: rent unpaid 9 days after it was due (4 days for a week-to-week tenancy). For rent due on the 1st, the notice can be served on the 10th at the earliest (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-15a(a)).
  • If your online rent payment system kept the tenant from paying, the grace period is 5 days longer for that month, and you can’t serve a nonpayment notice to quit until it ends (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 47a-15a(a), 47a-23(a)(1)(D)).
  • For a month-to-month or week-to-week tenancy, the notice ends the rental agreement for the month or week in which it is delivered, even if it is delivered in the month after the unpaid one (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(d)).
  • The tenant can’t cure by paying after it’s served. After a judgment for nonpayment, a tenant who deposits the full arrearage with the clerk within 5 days may ask for a stay of up to 3 months (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 47a-37, 47a-39).
  • The last statement on the notice is the one Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(e) allows: payments you take after the quit date are for use and occupancy only, so taking them doesn’t undo the notice.
  • This is Connecticut’s notice to quit in the words Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b) sets. The Judicial Branch’s own form, JD-HM-7 (Notice to Quit (End) Possession), says the same; the court accepts either.
  • Since October 1, 2025 only a proper officer can serve it: give it to a state marshal, who serves an attested copy on every adult tenant and occupant and completes the return of service. Mail and hand delivery by you are not allowed.
  • Name every lessee and occupant. An occupant whose name you don’t know can be named “John Doe” or “Jane Doe” (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b)).
  • Attach the Judicial Branch’s Right to Counsel (CT-RTC) notice (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-75(f)(2)): jud.ct.gov, linked from form JD-HM-7.
  • Three full days: neither the day it is served nor the quit date counts, and the tenant has until midnight of the quit date. Weekends count, and it may be served on any day of the week (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(c)).
  • Write the town where you sign it beside the date: the statute’s form asks for the date and place of signing.
  • Connecticut has no separate demand for possession: this notice to quit is the notice the court needs before a summary process case.
  • Tenants who are 62 or older or have a disability, living in a building or complex of 5 or more units, can be evicted only for a reason Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23c lists.

15-day pretermination notice (lease violation)

Says what the tenant did wrong and that the lease ends in at least 15 days unless a fixable breach is fixed.

When
The tenant broke the lease or a rule (not rent, and not a serious nuisance).
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-15; Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-11
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do, and the lease term, rule or § 47a-11 duty it breaks: the law requires the notice to specify the acts or omissions (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-15).
  • The 15 days run from when the tenant receives it. The statute sets no way to deliver it; a state marshal gives you proof of the day. NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.
  • Only a breach that can be fixed by repairs or by paying damages can be cured. If the tenant fixes it in time, the lease goes on.
  • If it isn’t fixed, the rental agreement ends on the date in this notice. You must then have a state marshal serve a notice to quit (JD-HM-7), at least 3 full days, before you file (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(a)(1)(C)).
  • If substantially the same act or omission happens again within 6 months, you can go straight to a notice to quit (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-15).
  • Skip this notice and serve a notice to quit directly for nonpayment, for a serious nuisance (bodily harm or a threat of it, substantial and wilful destruction, an immediate and serious danger to safety, or prostitution or illegal drug sales on the premises), or for letting back in a household member evicted for drug sales (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 47a-15, 47a-11(h)).
  • Tenants who are 62 or older or have a disability, in a building or complex of 5 or more units, can be evicted only for a reason Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23c lists; material noncompliance is one.

Notice to quit for lapse of time (3 full days, JD-HM-7)

Ends a month-to-month (or a lease that ran out) with Connecticut’s notice to quit, served by a state marshal.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, or a lease has ended; the quit date ends a rental period.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(a)(1)(A); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b)–(c); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-3b; Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-75(f)(2)
Good to know
  • Connecticut has no 30-day notice for a month-to-month: the notice to quit for lapse of time gives at least 3 full days, and the quit date may not come before the end of the tenant’s rental period. NOHM picks the last day of a rental period at least 3 full days out; you may give more.
  • A tenancy without a fixed term is month to month, or week to week if rent is paid weekly (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-3b).
  • If a written lease expressly waives the notice to quit when it ends by lapse of time, the notice isn’t needed (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-25).
  • A tenant protected by Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23c (62 or older, or with a disability, in a building or complex of 5 or more units) can’t be evicted for lapse of time at all.
  • A federally backed property (CARES Act) needs a 30-day notice to vacate; the court asks for the CARES Act Affidavit of Compliance (JD-HM-41) before judgment.
  • This is Connecticut’s notice to quit in the words Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b) sets. The Judicial Branch’s own form, JD-HM-7 (Notice to Quit (End) Possession), says the same; the court accepts either.
  • Since October 1, 2025 only a proper officer can serve it: give it to a state marshal, who serves an attested copy on every adult tenant and occupant and completes the return of service. Mail and hand delivery by you are not allowed.
  • Name every lessee and occupant. An occupant whose name you don’t know can be named “John Doe” or “Jane Doe” (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b)).
  • Attach the Judicial Branch’s Right to Counsel (CT-RTC) notice (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-75(f)(2)): jud.ct.gov, linked from form JD-HM-7.
  • Three full days: neither the day it is served nor the quit date counts, and the tenant has until midnight of the quit date. Weekends count, and it may be served on any day of the week (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(c)).
  • Write the town where you sign it beside the date: the statute’s form asks for the date and place of signing.
  • Connecticut has no separate demand for possession: this notice to quit is the notice the court needs before a summary process case.
  • Tenants who are 62 or older or have a disability, living in a building or complex of 5 or more units, can be evicted only for a reason Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23c lists.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (JD-HM-32, JD-HM-8, JD-HM-20) Court

What to put on the Judicial Branch’s summary process forms, where to get them, and the deadlines after you file.

When
The quit date in the notice to quit has passed and the tenant is still there.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23a; Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 47a-26 to 47a-26d; Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 47a-35 to 47a-42
Good to know
  • Connecticut’s eviction is “summary process” in the Superior Court: the Housing Session where there is one, otherwise the judicial district court. You choose where the tenant lives or where the premises are (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23a(b)).
  • Use the Judicial Branch’s forms: the summons (JD-HM-32) and the complaint for nonpayment (JD-HM-8) or lapse of time (JD-HM-20). This worksheet tells you what goes on them.
  • There is no court complaint form for a lease violation or a nuisance: that complaint is drafted by hand (Practice Book § 10-29). Ask a lawyer.
  • A state marshal serves the summons and complaint. If you represent yourself, the clerk signs your summons.
  • A company (an LLC or a corporation) should appear through a lawyer: Connecticut courts generally require it (NOHM couldn’t confirm the rule’s text). If there is more than one plaintiff, all may have to sign and appear.
  • The Judicial Branch’s guide, A Landlord’s Guide to Eviction (JDP-HM-14), walks through each form.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-7c
Good to know
  • For a lease entered into on or after January 1, 2024, you must offer the tenant a walk-through before they move in. If they ask for one, use the Department of Housing’s Pre-Occupancy Walk-Through Checklist, and both sign duplicate copies; a condition noted on it can’t be charged against the security deposit (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-7c). The checklist is on the Department of Housing’s mandatory forms page.
  • This checklist is a record of its own; it doesn’t replace the Department’s checklist when the tenant asks for the walk-through.
  • From April 1, 2026, every written lease must have the Department of Housing’s Rental Terms Summary Form, completed, as its first page; otherwise a civil penalty of one month’s rent (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-7d(c)).
  • Before or at the start of the tenancy, give the tenant in writing the name and address of the manager and of the person who receives notices and service (§ 47a-6(a)).
  • In a building or complex of 5 or more units, give the Department of Housing’s notice to protected tenants (Form AM-011) at the start of every tenancy and every renewal (§ 47a-23c(e)).
  • Within 30 days of receiving a security deposit, tell the tenant in writing the amount and the escrow bank’s name and address (§ 47a-21(h)(4)(A)): use the escrow notice.
  • Other papers: whether the unit or a unit next to it you own has bed bugs (§ 47a-7a(c)); the fire sprinkler statement in 12-point bold where one is required (§ 47a-3f); a receipt for every cash payment, and the ledger on request (§ 47a-3a); the federal lead-paint disclosure for a home built before 1978.
  • A security deposit can’t be more than two months’ rent, or one month’s for a tenant 62 or older (§ 47a-21(b)).

Security deposit escrow notice

Tells the tenant how much deposit you hold and the bank holding it in escrow.

When
Within 30 days after you receive a security deposit.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-21(h)(4)(A)
Good to know
  • Put the whole deposit in an escrow account at a financial institution in Connecticut right away (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-21(h)(1)), and send this within 30 days of receiving it.
  • The deposit earns interest at no less than the deposit index the Banking Commissioner sets each year, paid on each anniversary of the tenancy or credited toward rent (§ 47a-21(i)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-16
Good to know
  • Connecticut asks for “reasonable” notice, written or oral, and entry at reasonable times; it sets no hours. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • Without the tenant’s consent you may enter only in an emergency, during a long absence (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-16a), with a court order, or after the tenant abandoned or surrendered the unit (§ 47a-16(d)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 31 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-4e
Good to know
  • A rent increase takes effect only after written notice at least 45 days ahead; for a tenancy of a month or less, notice as long as one full term (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-4e). For a month-to-month NOHM asks for 31 days, the longest month. For a lease longer than a month, give 45.
  • A tenant who doesn’t answer the notice hasn’t agreed to the increase. You can’t raise the rent during a lease’s term unless it allows it.
  • No statewide cap. A tenant protected by § 47a-23c (62 or older, or with a disability, in 5 or more units) may be charged only a fair and equitable increase, which they can challenge before the town’s fair rent commission (§ 47a-23c(c)).
  • No increase within 6 months after a tenant’s good-faith code or fair rent complaint, repair request, or union activity (§ 47a-20).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 21 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-21(d)(2)
Good to know
  • Send the deposit and accrued interest, less damages, with this statement, within 21 days after the tenancy ends or 15 days after the tenant gives you a forwarding address in writing, whichever is later (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-21(d)(2)).
  • You may deduct unpaid rent or utility charges owed to you, damage beyond the tenant’s duties under § 47a-11, and the reasonable cost of a lock change the tenant didn’t pay; not a condition noted on the move-in walk-through checklist (§§ 47a-21(a), 47a-7c).
  • Include interest at no less than the deposit index for each year, except for any month the tenant was more than 10 days late with rent, unless you charged a late fee for it (§ 47a-21(i)).
  • Missing the deadline costs twice the deposit; missing only the interest, $10 or twice the interest, whichever is more (§ 47a-21(d)(2)).

Notice of belief of abandonment

Tells an occupant who seems to have left for good that you will reenter, and how long their belongings will be kept.

When
The occupant left without notice: most belongings gone, and rent unpaid over two months or they said they won’t return.
The law
Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-11b
Good to know
  • Abandonment means the occupant left without notice and doesn’t intend to return: they removed substantially all their belongings, and either haven’t paid rent for more than two months or said they won’t be back (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-11b).
  • Send it to each occupant’s last-known address both by regular mail and by certified mail, return receipt requested. It must give your phone number and mailing address.
  • If they don’t contact you within 10 days of receiving it, you may reenter without a notice to quit or a court case. Inventory what’s left, remove it, and keep it at least 30 days after the notice; then you may dispose of it as you see fit (§ 47a-11b(c)–(d)).
  • Not for after an eviction: then the state marshal moves belongings to the town’s storage (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-42). If the sole occupant died, § 47a-11d’s different notice and Probate Court steps apply; ask a lawyer.
