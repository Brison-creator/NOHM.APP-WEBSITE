Connecticut notice to quit for lapse of time (3 full days, JD-HM-7)

Ends a month-to-month (or a lease that ran out) with Connecticut’s notice to quit, served by a state marshal.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenYou want a month-to-month tenant out, or a lease has ended; the quit date ends a rental period.
  • The lawConn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(a)(1)(A); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b)–(c); Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-3b; Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-75(f)(2)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Connecticut has no 30-day notice for a month-to-month: the notice to quit for lapse of time gives at least 3 full days, and the quit date may not come before the end of the tenant’s rental period. NOHM picks the last day of a rental period at least 3 full days out; you may give more.
  • A tenancy without a fixed term is month to month, or week to week if rent is paid weekly (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-3b).
  • If a written lease expressly waives the notice to quit when it ends by lapse of time, the notice isn’t needed (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-25).
  • A tenant protected by Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23c (62 or older, or with a disability, in a building or complex of 5 or more units) can’t be evicted for lapse of time at all.
  • A federally backed property (CARES Act) needs a 30-day notice to vacate; the court asks for the CARES Act Affidavit of Compliance (JD-HM-41) before judgment.
  • This is Connecticut’s notice to quit in the words Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b) sets. The Judicial Branch’s own form, JD-HM-7 (Notice to Quit (End) Possession), says the same; the court accepts either.
  • Since October 1, 2025 only a proper officer can serve it: give it to a state marshal, who serves an attested copy on every adult tenant and occupant and completes the return of service. Mail and hand delivery by you are not allowed.
  • Name every lessee and occupant. An occupant whose name you don’t know can be named “John Doe” or “Jane Doe” (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(b)).
  • Attach the Judicial Branch’s Right to Counsel (CT-RTC) notice (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-75(f)(2)): jud.ct.gov, linked from form JD-HM-7.
  • Three full days: neither the day it is served nor the quit date counts, and the tenant has until midnight of the quit date. Weekends count, and it may be served on any day of the week (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23(c)).
  • Write the town where you sign it beside the date: the statute’s form asks for the date and place of signing.
  • Connecticut has no separate demand for possession: this notice to quit is the notice the court needs before a summary process case.
  • Tenants who are 62 or older or have a disability, living in a building or complex of 5 or more units, can be evicted only for a reason Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-23c lists.
