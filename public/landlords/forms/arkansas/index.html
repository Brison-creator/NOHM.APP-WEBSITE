Notices before court.

3-day notice to quit (rent not paid)

Ends the tenancy for unpaid rent and demands the home back in 3 days.

When
Rent is more than 5 days late.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-60-304(3); Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-701(b); Ark. R. Civ. P. 6(a)
Good to know
  • Rent must be more than 5 days late before you serve this (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-701(b)): serve it no sooner than the 6th day after the due date.
  • Arkansas’s notice to quit is a demand to leave, not to pay. You may still accept full payment, but taking rent after the notice can waive it; ask a lawyer before you do.
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it certified or registered (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-303(b)); NOHM adds 3 business days if you mail it.
  • Don’t use the criminal “failure to vacate” law (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-16-101) without a lawyer.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com, codes.findlaw.com

14-day notice of noncompliance

Tells the tenant what they did wrong: fix it in 14 days, or the lease ends.

When
The tenant broke a lease term (other than rent).
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-701(a)
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the law requires the notice to specify it.
  • If the tenant fixes it in time, the lease goes on. If not, it ends on the date in the notice, and you can demand possession and file for unlawful detainer.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a date at least 30 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-704(b)
Good to know
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ written notice instead (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-704(a)).
  • Ending on the day before rent is due is the cleanest choice, though Arkansas doesn’t require it.
  • If the tenant stays after the date, you may bring an action for possession (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-704(c)).

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing for unlawful detainer.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-60-304(1)–(2); Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-704(c)
Good to know
  • Arkansas sets no waiting period for this demand. NOHM fills in 3 days; give more if you like.
  • Hand it to the tenant or mail it certified, and keep proof: the court will ask how you demanded possession.
  • If the tenant stays after the date, file the complaint for unlawful detainer.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com, codes.findlaw.com

Court papers.

Complaint for unlawful detainer Court

The eviction filing: the complaint, your sworn affidavit, and the Notice of Intention to Issue Writ of Possession for the clerk.

When
The notice or demand ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-60-304; Ark. Code Ann. § 18-60-307; Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-701(c)
Good to know
  • File the complaint and the affidavit with the clerk of the district or circuit court in the county where the rental is. The clerk issues the summons and the Notice of Intention (the last page); the sheriff or a process server serves them on each tenant.
  • The tenant has 5 days after service, not counting Sundays and legal holidays, to file a written objection. If they don’t, the court orders the writ of possession. If they do, get a hearing date and send the Notice of Hearing.
  • No bond is needed for an eviction under Arkansas’s landlord-tenant act (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-701(c)(1)).
  • An individual landlord can file for themselves. A company (an LLC or a corporation) generally must be represented by a lawyer in court.
  • Sign the affidavit in front of a notary.
  • Bring the lease, the notice and your proof of serving it, and the rent ledger.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com, codes.findlaw.com, codes.findlaw.com

Notice of hearing Court

Tells the tenant when and where the court will hear your claim for possession.

When
The tenant filed a written objection and the court gave you a hearing date.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-60-307(c)–(d)
Good to know
  • Get the date, time and place from the court first.
  • Send it by certified mail, postage prepaid, to the tenant or to their lawyer of record, and keep the receipt (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-60-307(c)).
  • At the hearing you show the court why you are entitled to possession; the tenant can answer it.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com

Move-in.

Move-in defect form (Healthy Homes Act)

The Healthy Homes Act’s form for the tenant to list defects in water, power, plumbing, roof and heat or air.

When
Give it when the tenant gets possession; they have 2 business days to return it.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-502
Good to know
  • Hand it to the tenant when possession is available to them. If they sign it without noting a defect and take possession, or don’t return it within 2 business days, that defect generally isn’t held against you (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-502(c)).
  • It applies to leases entered into or renewed after November 1, 2021.
  • Fix what they note. A tenant whose rent is current can end the lease if a defect they gave you written notice of isn’t fixed within 30 days (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-502(d)).
  • Keep the signed form with the lease.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-602
Good to know
  • Arkansas law sets no advance notice for entry; your lease may. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • A tenant may not unreasonably refuse you entry for these reasons (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-17-602(a)). Come at reasonable times.
  • In an emergency you may need to go in right away; tell the tenant as soon as you can.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Arkansas statute sets the notice for a rent increase. The Attorney General advises at least one rental period; NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date.
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Arkansas has no rent cap, and cities and counties can’t control rents.
  • You can’t raise rent to retaliate against a victim of domestic abuse (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-16-112).

Read the law: arkansasag.gov, codes.findlaw.com

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 60 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-16-305
Good to know
  • Mail this and any refund first class to the tenant’s last known address within 60 days after the tenancy ends and they give back possession; that counts as returning it (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-16-305(b)).
  • Deduct only unpaid rent and damage from the tenant’s breaking the lease, each on its own line.
  • If the letter comes back and you can’t find the tenant after a reasonable effort, the money becomes yours 180 days after you mailed it.
  • Keeping a deposit wrongfully can cost up to twice the amount kept, plus costs and attorney’s fees.
  • The deposit law doesn’t apply if you, your family and your companies own 5 or fewer units and manage them yourselves; it does if you pay someone to manage them (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-16-303). Itemizing is still the safe practice.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com, codes.findlaw.com, arkansasag.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the lease ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Ark. Code Ann. § 18-16-108
Good to know
  • Arkansas doesn’t require this notice; property left when a lease ends is considered abandoned (Ark. Code Ann. § 18-16-108(a)). Giving one heads off disputes.
  • Use it only after the lease has ended, by its term, a notice, or the court’s writ, and the tenant has left: never while they still have possession.
  • Photograph the property before you move or dispose of it.

Read the law: codes.findlaw.com
