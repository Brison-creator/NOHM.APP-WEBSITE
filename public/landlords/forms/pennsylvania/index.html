Notices before court.

10-day notice to quit (rent unpaid)

Demands the rent due and tells the tenant to move out within 10 days of service.

When
Rent is due and unpaid (Pennsylvania has no grace period).
The law
68 P.S. § 250.501(a)(3), (b); 68 P.S. § 250.501(f); 1 Pa.C.S. § 1908
Good to know
  • This is a notice to quit, not to pay: the law gives no right to stay by paying within the 10 days. But in a case only for unpaid rent, the tenant can stop the eviction any time before the order for possession is carried out by paying the rent in arrears and the costs (68 P.S. § 250.503(c); Pa.R.Civ.P.M.D.J. 518).
  • Ten days from service, not counting the day of service. If the tenth day is a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, it runs to the next day that isn’t (1 Pa.C.S. § 1908).
  • Serve it personally, leave it at the principal building on the premises, or post it conspicuously on the leased premises (68 P.S. § 250.501(f)). Pennsylvania’s statute has no mail method.
  • Check your lease: it may shorten this notice or waive it, which the law allows (68 P.S. § 250.501(e)). The court’s complaint asks whether notice was given or wasn’t required by the lease.
  • NOHM demands rent only. Whether lease fees called “additional rent” can be demanded isn’t settled; claim them in the complaint instead.
  • You can’t evict for rent being paid into escrow under the City Rent Withholding Act (Pa.R.Civ.P.M.D.J. 582).
  • Philadelphia evictions go to Municipal Court and have their own good-cause and diversion rules; NOHM hasn’t confirmed them.
  • This notice to quit is the only notice before filing: Pennsylvania needs no separate demand for possession, so NOHM has none.

Notice to quit for a lease breach (15 or 30 days)

Ends the lease for breach of its conditions: 15 days to move out, or 30 for a lease of more than a year.

When
The tenant broke a condition of the lease.
The law
68 P.S. § 250.501(a)(2), (b); 68 P.S. § 250.501(f); 1 Pa.C.S. § 1908
Good to know
  • The statute gives no right to cure; whether the tenant can is up to your lease. Describe the breach exactly.
  • Drug-related grounds under 68 P.S. § 250.505-A (a conviction for selling, making or distributing drugs on the premises, a second drug violation there, or a police seizure of illegal drugs there) need only 10 days (§ 250.501(d)). Ask a lawyer.
  • You can’t end or refuse to renew a lease because a tenant joined a tenants’ association (68 P.S. § 250.205).
  • Serve it personally, leave it at the principal building on the premises, or post it conspicuously on the leased premises (68 P.S. § 250.501(f)). Pennsylvania’s statute has no mail method.
  • Check your lease: it may shorten this notice or waive it, which the law allows (68 P.S. § 250.501(e)). The court’s complaint asks whether notice was given or wasn’t required by the lease.
  • Philadelphia evictions go to Municipal Court and have their own good-cause and diversion rules; NOHM hasn’t confirmed them.
  • This notice to quit is the only notice before filing: Pennsylvania needs no separate demand for possession, so NOHM has none.

Notice to quit at the end of the term (15 or 30 days)

Ends a month-to-month, or a lease at its end: 15 days, or 30 for a lease of more than a year.

When
You want the tenant out at the end of the term; no reason is needed.
The law
68 P.S. § 250.501(a)(1), (b); 68 P.S. § 250.501(f); 1 Pa.C.S. § 1908
Good to know
  • Month to month, or a lease of a year or less: 15 days. A lease of more than a year: 30 days (68 P.S. § 250.501(b)).
  • The statute doesn’t require the date to end a rental period. Ending it the day before rent is due is the cleanest choice.
  • Pennsylvania has no statewide just-cause rule, but you can’t end or refuse to renew a lease because a tenant joined a tenants’ association (68 P.S. § 250.205).
  • The notice-to-quit process doesn’t apply to someone who never was a tenant (68 P.S. § 250.501(g)).
  • Serve it personally, leave it at the principal building on the premises, or post it conspicuously on the leased premises (68 P.S. § 250.501(f)). Pennsylvania’s statute has no mail method.
  • Check your lease: it may shorten this notice or waive it, which the law allows (68 P.S. § 250.501(e)). The court’s complaint asks whether notice was given or wasn’t required by the lease.
  • Philadelphia evictions go to Municipal Court and have their own good-cause and diversion rules; NOHM hasn’t confirmed them.
  • This notice to quit is the only notice before filing: Pennsylvania needs no separate demand for possession, so NOHM has none.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (AOPC 310A, SCRA affidavit, AOPC 311A)

What to copy onto Pennsylvania’s Landlord/Tenant Complaint and its companion forms, where to get them, and each step and deadline after you file.

When
The notice to quit ran out (or your lease waives it) and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Pa.R.Civ.P.M.D.J. 502–519; 68 P.S. §§ 250.501–250.503
Good to know
  • The complaint must be on the state’s form (Pa.R.Civ.P.M.D.J. 503A), and courts refuse altered ones, so NOHM doesn’t write it: copy these values onto AOPC 310A.
  • For each tenant who is a person, attach the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act affidavit (Rule 503D).
  • An LLC or corporation may appear through an officer, or an employee or agent with personal knowledge and an officer’s written authorization filed for this case (Rule 207).
  • Philadelphia evictions go to Municipal Court, with their own rules; this worksheet is for the magisterial district courts.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Pennsylvania doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: walk through with the tenant, both sign it, and keep a copy for the list of damages at move-out.
  • When you put a deposit over $100 in escrow, tell the tenant in writing the bank’s name and address and the amount: use NOHM’s Notice of deposit escrow account (68 P.S. § 250.511b(a)).
  • From about December 21, 2026 (Act 64 of 2026), your lease must say whether parking is provided and, if so, whether there is a fee and how much; you can’t add or change a parking fee during the lease unless parking availability or a third party’s pass-through fee changes.
  • From the same date (Act 67 of 2026), any application fee must be reasonably related to your actual administrative costs.
  • Built before 1978: give the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet. Philadelphia and other cities add their own disclosures; NOHM hasn’t confirmed them.

Notice of deposit escrow account

Tells the tenant the name and address of the bank holding their deposit, and the amount.

When
When you deposit a security deposit over $100 in escrow.
The law
68 P.S. § 250.511b(a)
Good to know
  • Deposits over $100 go in an escrow account at a bank regulated by the Federal Reserve, the Comptroller of the Currency or the Pennsylvania Department of Banking, and you must then tell each tenant in writing the bank’s name and address and the amount (68 P.S. § 250.511b(a)).
  • From the second anniversary of the deposit it must earn interest; you may keep 1% a year as an administrative fee and pay the rest of the interest to the tenant each year on the lease’s anniversary (§ 250.511b(b)–(c)). A bond can take the escrow’s place (§ 250.511c).
  • Cap: two months’ rent in the first year of the lease, one month’s from the second year on, and after five years a rent increase can’t raise the deposit (§ 250.511a).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • No Pennsylvania statute sets notice for entry; your lease governs. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours, except in an emergency.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Pennsylvania statute sets notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days, which covers the 15 or 30 days a notice to quit would need (68 P.S. § 250.501(b)), and starts the new rent on a due date.
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Pennsylvania has no statewide rent cap. After a lease’s fifth year, a rent increase can’t raise the deposit (68 P.S. § 250.511a(d)).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
68 P.S. § 250.512(a)
Good to know
  • Give the written list of damages, with the balance (including any unpaid interest), within 30 days after the lease ends or you accept the tenant’s surrender, whichever is first (68 P.S. § 250.512(a)).
  • No list within 30 days: you lose the right to withhold any of the deposit and to sue for damage to the premises. Not paying the difference: liability for double the amount wrongly kept (§ 250.512(b)–(c)). You bear the burden of proof.
  • If the tenant didn’t give you a new address in writing, you aren’t liable under this section (§ 250.512(e)); send it to the last known address anyway.
  • Interest: if you held the deposit more than two years, add the interest owed, less your 1% a year (§ 250.511b).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
68 P.S. § 250.505a(d)–(e)
Good to know
  • Property is abandoned only if: the tenant left after a written lease ended; an eviction order or order for possession was entered and the tenant left and removed substantially everything; such an order was executed; the tenant gave a forwarding address in writing and left with substantially everything; or the tenant left without saying they’d return, rent is more than 15 days late, and you posted notice of their rights (68 P.S. § 250.505a(b)).
  • Before you remove or dispose of anything, mail this notice first class to the premises and every forwarding address, emergency ones included (§ 250.505a(d)–(e)). The tenant has 10 days from the postmark to retrieve it or ask you to store it, for up to 30 days from the notice, at their cost.
  • Never on premises someone still lives in without permission, and hold 30 days if you know of a protection-from-abuse order (§ 250.505a(f), (h)). A deceased tenant’s property goes by the decedents’ estates law instead (§ 250.505a(c)).
  • Breaking these rules: treble damages, attorney’s fees and costs (§ 250.505a(i)).
