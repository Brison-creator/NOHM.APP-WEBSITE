Kansas 10-day notice of nonpayment and notice to quit

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid and that the lease ends if it isn’t paid in 10 days; it is also the notice to leave the premises.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenRent is past due (Kansas has no grace period).
  • The lawK.S.A. 58-2564(b); K.S.A. 58-2507; K.S.A. 61-3803; K.S.A. 60-206(a)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Kansas has two rules for unpaid rent. The Residential Landlord and Tenant Act gives the tenant 3 days to pay (K.S.A. 58-2564(b)); an older law that was never repealed gives a tenant for three months or longer 10 days’ notice to quit (K.S.A. 58-2507). No Kansas court has said which one controls, so NOHM gives 10 days, the longer and safer period. Ten days also meets the 3-day rule.
  • Rent is due without demand on the day the lease sets; Kansas has no grace period (K.S.A. 58-2545(c)). You can serve this the day after rent is unpaid.
  • If you mail it, the law allows two more days from the date of mailing (K.S.A. 58-2564(b); 61-3803); NOHM adds them. The 10th day moves to the next business day if it falls on a weekend or legal holiday (K.S.A. 60-206(a)).
  • Deliver it in hand, or leave it with someone over 12 who lives there; post it on the home only if no such person is there; or mail it to the tenant at the home (K.S.A. 61-3803). Keep proof of how and when.
  • Taking late rent without reservation waives your right to end the lease for that late payment (K.S.A. 58-2566). If you take a partial payment, say in writing that you accept it with reservation of your rights.
  • This notice is also the notice to leave the premises every Kansas eviction lawsuit needs (K.S.A. 61-3803): if the rent stays unpaid past the date, you can file without another notice.
