Notices before court.

10-day notice of nonpayment and notice to quit

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid and that the lease ends if it isn’t paid in 10 days; it is also the notice to leave the premises.

When
Rent is past due (Kansas has no grace period).
The law
K.S.A. 58-2564(b); K.S.A. 58-2507; K.S.A. 61-3803; K.S.A. 60-206(a)
Good to know
  • Kansas has two rules for unpaid rent. The Residential Landlord and Tenant Act gives the tenant 3 days to pay (K.S.A. 58-2564(b)); an older law that was never repealed gives a tenant for three months or longer 10 days’ notice to quit (K.S.A. 58-2507). No Kansas court has said which one controls, so NOHM gives 10 days, the longer and safer period. Ten days also meets the 3-day rule.
  • Rent is due without demand on the day the lease sets; Kansas has no grace period (K.S.A. 58-2545(c)). You can serve this the day after rent is unpaid.
  • If you mail it, the law allows two more days from the date of mailing (K.S.A. 58-2564(b); 61-3803); NOHM adds them. The 10th day moves to the next business day if it falls on a weekend or legal holiday (K.S.A. 60-206(a)).
  • Deliver it in hand, or leave it with someone over 12 who lives there; post it on the home only if no such person is there; or mail it to the tenant at the home (K.S.A. 61-3803). Keep proof of how and when.
  • Taking late rent without reservation waives your right to end the lease for that late payment (K.S.A. 58-2566). If you take a partial payment, say in writing that you accept it with reservation of your rights.
  • This notice is also the notice to leave the premises every Kansas eviction lawsuit needs (K.S.A. 61-3803): if the rent stays unpaid past the date, you can file without another notice.

14-day notice to remedy a breach (30 days to terminate)

Says what the tenant did wrong: fix it in 14 days, or the lease ends at least 30 days after they get the notice.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease (other than rent).
The law
K.S.A. 58-2564(a); K.S.A. 61-3803; K.S.A. 60-206(a)
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the statute requires the notice to specify the acts and omissions that are the breach. It must be a material breach of the rental agreement, or of the tenant’s duties that materially affects health and safety (K.S.A. 58-2564(a); 58-2555).
  • Tell the tenant they can fix it: leaving out the right to remedy can cost you the rent after they leave (Fenn v. Windsor at Kingsborough, 226 Kan. 653).
  • If the same or a similar breach happens again after the 14 days, the law lets you end the agreement on 30 days’ notice after receipt with no chance to remedy it (K.S.A. 58-2564(a)). NOHM’s notice always offers the 14 days; giving more than the law requires is allowed.
  • A tenant who refuses you lawful access can be given this notice too (K.S.A. 58-2571(a)).
  • You can’t evict a tenant on the basis of, or as a direct result of, their being a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, human trafficking or stalking (K.S.A. 58-25,137).
  • The statute doesn’t say how to deliver this notice; NOHM uses the ways K.S.A. 61-3803 allows for the notice to leave, which this notice also is. Deliver it in hand, or leave it with someone over 12 who lives there; post it on the home only if no such person is there; or mail it to the tenant at the home (K.S.A. 61-3803). Keep proof of how and when. The 14 and 30 days run from receipt: NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rent-paying date at least 30 days after the tenant gets it.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
K.S.A. 58-2570(b); K.S.A. 61-3803
Good to know
  • The tenancy must end on a rent-paying date at least 30 days after the tenant receives the notice (K.S.A. 58-2570(b)); NOHM ends it with the rental period that closes the day before. NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ written notice instead (K.S.A. 58-2570(a)). A tenant in the military with orders needs to give you only 15 days.
  • A lease for a definite term ends on its own date with no notice. If the tenant stays, give the notice to leave the premises (NOHM’s demand for possession) at least 3 days before you file (K.S.A. 61-3803). This notice is that notice for a month-to-month: no second one is needed.
  • A tenant who holds over willfully and not in good faith can owe up to 1½ months’ rent or 1½ times your actual damages, whichever is greater (K.S.A. 58-2570(c)).
  • You can’t end a tenancy to retaliate against a tenant’s good-faith complaint to a code agency or to you, or for joining a tenants’ union (K.S.A. 58-2572).
  • If you ask the tenant to sign a move-out paper with terms not in the lease, it must carry the warning K.S.A. 58-2570(e) sets, in at least 10-point bold type.
  • Deliver it in hand, or leave it with someone over 12 who lives there; post it on the home only if no such person is there; or mail it to the tenant at the home (K.S.A. 61-3803). Keep proof of how and when.

Notice to leave the premises (3 days)

The written notice to leave every Kansas eviction lawsuit needs, delivered at least 3 days before you file.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
K.S.A. 61-3803; K.S.A. 58-2570(c)
Good to know
  • Every Kansas eviction lawsuit needs a written notice to leave the premises delivered at least 3 days before you file (K.S.A. 61-3803). The 3 days are three 24-hour periods from the hour it is delivered or posted, weekends and holidays included; if you mail it, allow 2 more days from the date of mailing.
  • NOHM’s rent, lease-violation and month-to-month notices already include this notice. Use this one when a lease for a fixed term ran out, or when the notice that ended the tenancy didn’t tell the tenant to leave.
  • Hand it to the tenant, or leave it with someone over 12 who lives there; post it only if no such person is there; or mail it to the tenant at the home. Keep proof of how and when.
  • If the tenant is still there after the date, file the petition for eviction: see NOHM’s filing worksheet.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Kansas Judicial Council forms) Court

Everything the Kansas Judicial Council’s eviction forms ask for, filled in from the lease, with where to get each form and the steps and deadlines after filing.

When
The lease has ended or been terminated, the notice to leave has run, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
K.S.A. 61-3803; K.S.A. 61-3804; K.S.A. 61-3805; K.S.A. 61-3807; K.S.A. 61-3808
Good to know
  • Kansas’s courts use the Kansas Judicial Council’s statewide eviction forms. Fill those in from this worksheet; this worksheet isn’t filed.
  • The Judicial Council’s site couldn’t be opened while NOHM researched it, so the form titles and dates come from its listing, and the values here follow what the statute requires a petition to say (K.S.A. 61-3804). Check each form for anything else it asks.
  • An individual landlord may file for themselves. A business (an LLC or a corporation) generally must file through a licensed attorney: the Shawnee County District Court says so in writing, and Kansas follows the common-law rule outside small claims. NOHM couldn’t confirm a statewide rule; ask the clerk.
  • Bring the lease, the notice and your proof of how it was delivered, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Joint move-in inventory

The written record of the home’s condition and the furnishings and appliances, made together with the tenant and signed by both.

When
Within 5 days after the tenant moves in: walk through it together, both sign two copies, and give the tenant one.
The law
K.S.A. 58-2548
Good to know
  • Kansas requires it: within 5 days after the tenant moves in or gets possession, you (or your representative) and the tenant inventory the home together, write down its condition and any furnishings or appliances, both sign two copies, and the tenant keeps one (K.S.A. 58-2548).
  • Required in writing at or before the start of the tenancy: the name and address of the manager and of the owner or the owner’s agent for legal papers and notices (K.S.A. 58-2551). Use NOHM’s owner and manager disclosure.
  • A deposit can’t be more than one month’s rent for an unfurnished home, 1½ months’ for a furnished one, plus up to ½ month’s for a pet (K.S.A. 58-2550(a)).
  • A lease can’t make the tenant waive their rights, confess judgment, pay your attorney’s fees, or excuse you from liability (K.S.A. 58-2547).
  • Kansas requires no mold, flood, bed bug or smoke detector disclosure. A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

Disclosure of manager and owner

Tells the tenant in writing who manages the home and who receives legal papers and notices for the owner.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, and again whenever it changes.
The law
K.S.A. 58-2551
Good to know
  • Required in writing at or before the start of the tenancy (K.S.A. 58-2551(a)), and kept current (§ 58-2551(b)).
  • Anyone who fails to give it becomes your agent for service of process and for receiving notices and demands (K.S.A. 58-2551(c)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
K.S.A. 58-2557
Good to know
  • Kansas requires reasonable notice and entry at reasonable hours, but sets no number of hours (K.S.A. 58-2557(a)). NOHM suggests at least 24 hours; your lease may say more.
  • In an extreme hazard involving the potential loss of life or severe property damage you may enter without the tenant’s consent (K.S.A. 58-2557(b)). If the tenant is away more than 30 days, you may enter at times reasonably necessary (§ 58-2565(b)).
  • Don’t abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant (K.S.A. 58-2557(c)); the tenant may end the lease or recover damages (§ 58-2571).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Kansas statute sets the notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date: a month-to-month can’t be ended on less (K.S.A. 58-2570(b)), so the tenant has the same time to decide.
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • No rent cap, and cities and counties can’t control rents (K.S.A. 12-16,120).
  • You can’t raise rent to retaliate against a tenant’s good-faith complaint to a code agency or to you, or for joining a tenants’ union; an increase from a good-faith rise in your costs is allowed (K.S.A. 58-2572).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 14 days after you work out the deductions, and no later than 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
K.S.A. 58-2550(b)
Good to know
  • Deduct only accrued rent and the damage from the tenant’s failure to follow the lease or their duties under K.S.A. 58-2555, each itemized in writing (K.S.A. 58-2550(b)).
  • Return the balance within 14 days after you work out the deductions, and never more than 30 days after the tenancy ends, you get the home back and the tenant asks for it. If the tenant doesn’t ask within 30 days after the tenancy ends, mail what’s due to their last known address (K.S.A. 58-2550(b)).
  • Keeping a deposit wrongfully lets the tenant recover what was due plus damages of 1½ times the amount wrongfully withheld (K.S.A. 58-2550(c)).
  • No interest or separate account is required. The deposit cap is one month’s rent unfurnished, 1½ months’ furnished, plus ½ month’s for a pet (K.S.A. 58-2550(a)). The tenant can’t use it as the last month’s rent unless the lease allows it (§ 58-2550(d)).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
K.S.A. 58-2565(d)
Good to know
  • Use it once the tenant has abandoned or surrendered the home, or after the writ of restitution is executed (K.S.A. 58-2565(d)). You may presume abandonment when the tenant is 10 days behind on rent and has removed a substantial part of their belongings, unless they told you otherwise (§ 58-2565(b)).
  • Store the property at the tenant’s expense. Publish this notice once in a newspaper of general circulation in the county at least 15 days before the sale, and mail a copy to the tenant’s last known address within 7 days after publishing. Sell or dispose of it no sooner than 30 days after you took possession of it (K.S.A. 58-2565(d)). NOHM fills in 30 days from this notice, which meets both.
  • The notice must state the tenant’s name, a brief description of the property and the approximate date of the sale (K.S.A. 58-2565(d)).
  • Apply the proceeds first to your costs of taking, storing, noticing and selling the property, then to what the tenant owes; you may keep the balance, except as to a secured creditor who gave you notice (K.S.A. 58-2565(e)).
  • Make reasonable efforts to rent the home again (K.S.A. 58-2565(c)).
