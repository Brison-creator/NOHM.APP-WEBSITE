Notices before court.

Five-day demand notice for nonpayment of rent

The statute’s own demand, mailed: pay all rent in arrears within 5 days of mailing, or an eviction may be filed.

When
Rent is 15 days in arrears.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-35(a); R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-56(a)
Good to know
  • Send it only once rent is 15 days in arrears (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-35(a)): rent due on the 1st, mail it on the 16th or later.
  • Mail it first class and fill in the certificate of mailing; the five days run from the date of mailing. File no earlier than the 6th day after mailing (§ 34-18-35(b)).
  • Rhode Island’s statute counts from the date of mailing and doesn’t say whether a weekend or holiday extends the period; NOHM moves a last day that falls on one to the next business day.
  • The tenant can cure by paying all the rent in arrears before you file. If they had no demand notice in the 6 months before you file, they can also cure at the hearing by paying the arrears and court costs (§ 34-18-35(e)).
  • To file a nonpayment eviction you must be registered in the statewide rental registry, and show the court proof when you file (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-58(g)).
  • This is the statute’s own form (§ 34-18-56(a)); the Judiciary’s DC-55 has the same words.
  • If the rent isn’t paid, file the complaint: Rhode Island needs no separate demand for possession, so NOHM has none.

Notice of noncompliance (20 days to remedy)

The statute’s own notice, mailed: remedy the breach within 20 days, or the rental agreement ends at least 21 days after mailing.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease or a tenant’s duty under § 34-18-24.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-36(a); R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-56(b)
Good to know
  • Describe the breach in the notice’s details: they fill the line after “because you:”. Then, by hand on the printed notice, write what the tenant must do to remedy it (the acts, repairs or payment of damages) and the termination date NOHM shows, at least 21 days after mailing (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-36(a)).
  • Mail it first class and fill in the certificate of mailing; the twenty days run from the date of mailing.
  • Rhode Island’s statute counts from the date of mailing and doesn’t say whether a weekend or holiday extends the period; NOHM moves a last day that falls on one to the next business day.
  • If the tenant remedies it before the date, the agreement goes on. If not, file no earlier than the day after the termination date (§ 34-18-36(b)).
  • The same breach again within 6 months of a good-faith notice: you may end the agreement on at least 20 days’ written notice, with no time to remedy (§ 34-18-36(e)).
  • No notice is needed for drug or violent-crime breaches of § 34-18-24(8)–(10), or for some seasonal tenants: you may file at once (§ 34-18-36(f)). Ask a lawyer.
  • This is the statute’s own form (§ 34-18-56(b)); the Judiciary’s DC-62 has the same words.

30-day notice of termination of tenancy

The statute’s own notice ending a month-to-month tenancy at least 30 days out, the day after a rental period ends.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out; no reason is needed.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-37(b); R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-56(c)
Good to know
  • Deliver it at least 30 days before the date in it (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-37(b)). The statute’s form names “the first day after the end of your current rental period”: set the date to a rent due date at least 30 days out (rent due on the 1st, the 1st of a month).
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 10 days; a year-to-year tenancy, 3 months before the end of the occupation year (§ 34-18-37(a), (c)).
  • Mail it first class and fill in the certificate of mailing, or hand it to the tenant (§ 34-18-14(b)).
  • If the tenant stays, you may file the day after the tenancy ends: no separate demand for possession is needed, so NOHM has none (§ 34-18-38(a)). A willful holdover can owe up to three months’ rent or three times actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney’s fees (§ 34-18-38(c)).
  • When a tenant dies, an adult resident or live-in caregiver may continue the agreement for up to 3 months or the rest of the term (§ 34-18-38(d)).
  • This is the statute’s own form (§ 34-18-56(c)); the Judiciary’s DC-63 has the same words.

Read the law: webserver.rilegislature.gov, webserver.rilegislature.gov

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (DC-54, DC-38)

What to copy onto the Judiciary’s eviction complaint, where to get it, and each step and deadline after you file.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws §§ 34-18-9, 34-18-10; R.I. Gen. Laws §§ 34-18-35, 34-18-36, 34-18-38; R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-56(d)–(j)
Good to know
  • Rhode Island’s statute sets the eviction complaints’ form (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-56(d)–(e)) and the Judiciary’s DC-54 and DC-38 follow it, so NOHM doesn’t write the complaint: copy these values onto the Judiciary’s form.
  • For a nonpayment case, bring proof you are registered in the statewide rental registry (and lead-compliant where required): without it the case can’t proceed (§ 34-18-58(g)).
  • Attach the notice you mailed, with its certificate of mailing.
  • NOHM hasn’t confirmed whether an LLC or corporation may file without a lawyer in District Court; ask the clerk.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Rhode Island doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: walk through with the tenant, both sign it, and keep a copy for the deposit.
  • In writing at or before move-in: the name, address and number of the person managing the premises, and of an owner or the owner’s agent for service of process and notices (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-20(a)). Keep it current.
  • In the lease: every fee beyond rent, next to the rent, with a statement that additional fees may apply; which utilities the rent includes and which the tenant pays; any renter’s-insurance requirement (§ 34-18-15(a), since January 1, 2025).
  • Before signing: any outstanding minimum housing code violations (§ 34-18-22.1).
  • Register the unit in the statewide rental registry within 30 days of renting it, and re-register by October 1 each year; pre-1978 units need a lead certificate of conformance or exemption (§ 34-18-58).
  • No application fee beyond the actual cost of a background or credit check (§ 34-18-59). A nonresident landlord designates an agent (§ 34-18-22.3).
  • A 2026 law adds a shoreline-access disclosure for shoreline property (S 2734); NOHM hasn’t confirmed its enactment. Built before 1978: the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-26(c)
Good to know
  • Give at least 2 days’ notice and enter only at reasonable times, except in an emergency or where notice is impracticable (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-26(c)).
  • Without consent you may enter in an emergency, or during an absence of more than 7 days if reasonably necessary to protect the property (§ 34-18-26(b)). Don’t use entry to harass the tenant.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 60 days before the new rent starts.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-16.1(a)
Good to know
  • Give written notice at least 60 days before a rent increase takes effect (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-16.1(a)).
  • A month-to-month tenant over 62 needs at least 120 days: set the date that far out (§ 34-18-16.1(b)).
  • Rhode Island has no rent cap. An increase within 6 months of a tenant’s complaint can be presumed retaliatory (§ 34-18-46). A fee change in an unwritten tenancy needs 30 days’ written notice (§ 34-18-15(a)).

Read the law: webserver.rilegislature.gov, webserver.rilegislature.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 20 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-19(b)
Good to know
  • Deliver this itemized notice and the balance within 20 days after the latest of: the tenancy ending, the tenant giving back possession, or the tenant giving you a forwarding address for the deposit (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-19(b)). First-class mail counts as delivery (§ 34-18-14(b)).
  • Deduct only unpaid rent, reasonable cleaning and trash removal, and physical damage beyond ordinary wear and tear.
  • Failing to comply: the amount due, plus twice the amount wrongly withheld, plus attorney’s fees (§ 34-18-19(c)). The lease can’t waive it.
  • The deposit can’t exceed one month’s rent (a separate furniture deposit of up to a month’s rent is allowed for furniture worth $5,000 or more). No interest or separate account is required.

Read the law: webserver.rilegislature.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • Rhode Island’s landlord-tenant act sets no procedure for property a tenant leaves behind. NOHM fills in 14 days to hold it, its own choice, not the law’s; giving notice heads off disputes.
  • After an eviction, the sheriff or constable removes the property and the tenant pays moving and storage before it is released (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-50).
  • If the tenant may have abandoned the unit, send NOHM’s 7-day notice of abandonment first (§ 34-18-40).
  • Photograph the property before you move or dispose of it. Never remove a tenant’s things while they still have possession (§ 34-18-44).

Read the law: webserver.rilegislature.gov, webserver.rilegislature.gov

Notice of abandonment (7 days to reply)

The certified letter the law requires when a tenant seems to have abandoned the unit: reply within 7 days, or you will re-rent it.

When
The tenant left without notice, rent is more than 15 days unpaid, and their things are gone.
The law
R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-40; R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-11(1)
Good to know
  • A unit is abandoned when the tenant has vacated without notice and doesn’t intend to return, as shown by rent unpaid for more than 15 days and substantially all their possessions removed (R.I. Gen. Laws § 34-18-11(1)).
  • Send it by certified mail, return receipt requested, to the tenant’s last known address (§ 34-18-40). If it comes back undeliverable, or the tenant doesn’t contact you within 7 days, make reasonable efforts to re-rent the unit at a fair rental.
  • NOHM fills in a reply date 10 days out, to allow for the certified mail; the statute’s period is 7 days.

Read the law: webserver.rilegislature.gov, webserver.rilegislature.gov
