Notices before court.

10-day demand to pay rent or move

Demands the rent due within 10 days, or the home back, with the mediation and repayment-plan statements the law requires.

When
Rent is past due (no grace period for the demand; late fees wait 7 days).
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-104(1)(d); Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-106; Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-108(1); Colo. Rev. Stat. § 2-4-108
Good to know
  • NOHM gives 10 days, the residential notice. Only 5 days apply to an “exempt residential agreement” (a single-family home whose landlord owns five or fewer and whose lease says the 10-day notice doesn’t apply), and 3 days to employer-provided housing (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-104). A property with a federally backed mortgage or in a federal housing program needs 30 days under the CARES Act (court form JDF 99 A).
  • You must accept the tenant’s full payment of everything lawfully due under this demand, plus rent that has since come due, at any time until the judge enters judgment for possession (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-115(4)).
  • If the tenant gives you documentation that domestic violence, stalking or sexual misconduct caused the missed rent (a police report, a protection order, a signed self-attestation, or a letter from a qualified third party), offer a repayment plan within 3 business days: payments of at least $25 a month, no more than 9 months, no fees or interest (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-104(4)(e)).
  • The law requires the date and time to deliver up the premises (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-106(1)); NOHM states 11:59 p.m. on the last day.
  • Write it in English, Spanish, or the tenant’s primary language if you know or should know it’s another (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-106(3)). The court posts JDF 99 A in many languages.
  • If the tenant has told you they are a victim-survivor of domestic violence, stalking or sexual misconduct, make every reasonable effort to serve them in person; after three failed tries, post it AND send it by certified mail or a courier with proof of delivery (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-108(2)).
  • A late fee may be charged only after rent is 7 days late, up to $50 or 5% of the past-due rent, whichever is greater, and only if the lease sets it (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-105(1)).
  • NOHM’s calendar doesn’t know Frances Xavier Cabrini Day (the first Monday in October, a Colorado legal holiday): if the last day falls on it, move it a day later.

10-day demand to comply with the lease or move

Describes the violation and requires the tenant to comply within 10 days, or give the home back.

When
The tenant broke a material term of the lease.
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-104(1)(e); Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-106; Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-108(1); Colo. Rev. Stat. § 2-4-108
Good to know
  • Describe the violation specifically: the law requires it (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-106(1)). For conduct that disturbed others or a nuisance, describe the conduct and demand that it stop (§ 13-40-104(1)(j)).
  • NOHM gives 10 days, the residential notice. Only 5 days apply to an “exempt residential agreement” (a single-family home whose landlord owns five or fewer and whose lease says the 10-day notice doesn’t apply), and 3 days to employer-provided housing (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-104). A property with a federally backed mortgage or in a federal housing program needs 30 days under the CARES Act (court form JDF 99 A).
  • A second violation of the same condition after this demand allows a notice ending the tenancy in 10 days with no chance to cure (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-104(1)(e.5); court form JDF 99 B). A substantial violation (a violent or drug felony, endangering people or property) allows 3 days (§ 13-40-107.5). NOHM doesn’t draw those; the court’s forms are free.
  • A documented victim-survivor isn’t in unlawful detention if domestic violence, stalking or sexual misconduct caused or contributed to the violation (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-104(4)).
  • The law requires the date and time to deliver up the premises (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-106(1)); NOHM states 11:59 p.m. on the last day.
  • Write it in English, Spanish, or the tenant’s primary language if you know or should know it’s another (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-106(3)). The court posts JDF 99 A in many languages.
  • If the tenant has told you they are a victim-survivor of domestic violence, stalking or sexual misconduct, make every reasonable effort to serve them in person; after three failed tries, post it AND send it by certified mail or a courier with proof of delivery (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-108(2)).
  • NOHM’s calendar doesn’t know Frances Xavier Cabrini Day (the first Monday in October, a Colorado legal holiday): if the last day falls on it, move it a day later.

Notice to end a tenancy (21 to 91 days)

Ends a tenancy at the end of a period: 21 days for a month-to-month the just-cause law doesn’t cover; 90 days and a stated cause once it does.

When
You want a tenancy to end; after 12 months you need a cause the law allows.
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-107(1)–(3); Colo. Rev. Stat. §§ 38-12-1301 to -1307; Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-108(1)

The statute doesn’t set this notice’s exact words: check it before you serve one.

Good to know
  • For a tenancy the just-cause law doesn’t cover (the tenant there less than 12 months, an owner-occupied home of up to three units, and others in Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-1302): the notice must expire at the end of the period, served at least 21 days before for a month-to-month, 28 for a tenancy of six months to a year, 91 for a year or longer, and 3 for week-to-week (§ 13-40-107(2)). Court form JDF 99 B.
  • For a covered tenancy, end it only for a cause the statute allows. A no-fault cause (demolition or conversion, substantial repairs, the landlord or family moving in, a sale, refusal to sign a reasonable new lease, a history of late rent) needs at least 90 days’ notice ending with the lease term and a statement of the legal and factual basis (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-1303(3), (5)(b)). Court form JDF 99 C. NOHM gives 91 days once the tenant has been there 12 months.
  • A notice that doesn’t meet the just-cause law is a defense, and the case is dismissed (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-1306). A rent increase meant to get around it is barred (§ 38-12-1307).
  • Colorado’s court forms use no further demand after this notice: if the tenant stays, the eviction rests on it (court forms JDF 99 B and 99 C). Whether a court wants another demand is unsettled.
  • If the tenant has told you they are a victim-survivor of domestic violence, stalking or sexual misconduct, make every reasonable effort to serve them in person; after three failed tries, post it AND send it by certified mail or a courier with proof of delivery (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-108(2)).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (JDF 101, JDF 102)

What to write on Colorado’s standard eviction complaint and affidavit and the summons, filled in from the lease, with the steps and deadlines that follow.

When
The demand or notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. §§ 13-40-109 to -123; Colo. R. Civ. P. 4(c)(2)
Good to know
  • Colorado’s statute names the court’s standard complaint and affidavit (JDF 101) for a residential eviction; a different form must meet every requirement (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-110(1)(a), (f)). NOHM fills in what to write on JDF 101.
  • An individual landlord can file without a lawyer. A closely held company (three or fewer owners) may appear through an officer if $15,000 or less is at issue and the officer shows the court their authority; otherwise a lawyer is required (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-1-127(2)).
  • If the tenant consents, tell the court they are a victim-survivor (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-110(3.5)). Court records stay suppressed until the court orders possession (§ 13-40-110.5).
  • Bring the lease, the notice and your proof of serving it, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Colorado doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: a deposit can’t be kept for damage that existed before the tenancy (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-103(1)(a)), and a signed checklist shows what did.
  • Give the tenant a copy of the signed lease within 7 days, electronically unless they ask for paper; name yourself or your agent and an address in it (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-801(1)–(2)).
  • Unless you own five or fewer single-family homes and no more than five units, the lease must say that Colo. Rev. Stat. § 24-34-502(1) prohibits source-of-income discrimination (§ 38-12-801(2.5)).
  • Before the lease is signed, give the radon disclosure (NOHM’s form) and get it acknowledged (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-803). For a home built before 1978, the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Radon disclosure

The statute’s radon warning, with what you know of the home’s radon tests, for the tenant to acknowledge.

When
Before the tenant signs the lease.
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-803(2)
Good to know
  • Give it before the lease is signed, with the warning in bold, any radon test results, records and mitigation you know of, and the state health department’s radon brochure; get the tenant’s signed acknowledgment (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-803(2)).
  • If you don’t, the tenant may be able to void the lease; that right doesn’t apply to leases of one year or less entered on or after January 1, 2026 (§ 38-12-803(3)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • Colorado has no general statute on notice of entry; your lease decides. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • To fix a condition that makes the home unlivable, give written notice at least 24 hours ahead with the date and time and how long you expect to be there, unless the condition threatens life, health or safety or serious damage right away; the tenant may reasonably decline a time and propose another (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-503(6)).
  • To inspect or treat for bed bugs, give reasonable written or electronic notice at least 48 hours ahead, unless the lease sets another minimum or the tenant waives it (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-1004(1)(a)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 60 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-701(2)(a)
Good to know
  • Without a written lease, a rent increase needs at least 60 days’ written notice (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-701(2)(a)). With one, the lease decides; NOHM asks for 60 days either way.
  • Rent can go up only once in any 12 months of the tenant’s occupancy, whatever the lease (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-702).
  • Colorado has no rent cap, and cities and counties can’t control rents (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-301). An increase to retaliate, or to get around the just-cause law, is barred (§§ 38-12-509, 38-12-1307).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-103(1)
Good to know
  • Return it, with the written statement of the exact reasons for anything kept, within 30 days after the lease ends or the tenant surrenders the home, whichever is later, unless the lease sets a longer time of up to 60 days (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-103(1)(a), as HB 25-1249 rewrote it from January 1, 2026).
  • If the tenant asked in writing, send the photos, inspection reports, receipts, invoices or estimates for each amount kept within 14 days of the request (§ 38-12-103(8)).
  • Carpet replacement or repainting the whole unit only for substantial damage that can’t be repaired; carpet over 10 years old can’t count (§ 38-12-103(11)). A walk-through before move-out on either side’s request (§ 38-12-103(1.5)).
  • Keeping it wrongfully, after the tenant’s 7-day demand, can cost three times the amount plus attorney’s fees (§ 38-12-103(3)). The deposit can’t be more than two months’ rent (§ 38-12-102.5); a pet deposit no more than $300 (§ 38-12-106). No interest is required.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-20-116
Good to know
  • Colorado gives a landlord a lien on a tenant’s household goods left on the premises for unpaid rent, except pets, small kitchen appliances, utensils, beds, bedding, clothing, records and personal effects (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-20-102(3)).
  • Property is presumed abandoned once the owner hasn’t contacted you for at least 30 days. At least 15 days before you sell or dispose of it, send this notice by registered or certified mail, return receipt requested, signed by the addressee only; keep a copy for a year, and if it comes back unclaimed, publish the notice once in a newspaper in the county (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-20-116).
  • Property removed while a writ of restitution is executed: you have no duty to store it (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-122(3)).
  • When a tenant dies, you may take possession without court once the personal representative surrenders it, or 30 days after the death if rent is unpaid or the property is substantially removed (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-801(3.5), leases from September 1, 2025).
