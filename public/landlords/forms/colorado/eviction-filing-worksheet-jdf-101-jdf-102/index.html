What to write on Colorado’s standard eviction complaint and affidavit and the summons, filled in from the lease, with the steps and deadlines that follow.

The facts.

  • WhenThe demand or notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
  • The lawColo. Rev. Stat. §§ 13-40-109 to -123; Colo. R. Civ. P. 4(c)(2)
  • StageCourt papers

What to know.

  • Colorado’s statute names the court’s standard complaint and affidavit (JDF 101) for a residential eviction; a different form must meet every requirement (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-110(1)(a), (f)). NOHM fills in what to write on JDF 101.
  • An individual landlord can file without a lawyer. A closely held company (three or fewer owners) may appear through an officer if $15,000 or less is at issue and the officer shows the court their authority; otherwise a lawyer is required (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-1-127(2)).
  • If the tenant consents, tell the court they are a victim-survivor (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 13-40-110(3.5)). Court records stay suppressed until the court orders possession (§ 13-40-110.5).
  • Bring the lease, the notice and your proof of serving it, and the rent ledger.
