Notices before court.

3-day notice to leave (rent unpaid)

The notice to leave the premises Ohio requires before an eviction for unpaid rent, with the statute’s warning.

When
Rent is past due (Ohio’s statute has no grace period).
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.04(A); Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.02(A)(9); Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.02(B); Ohio Rev. Code § 1.14
Good to know
  • This is a notice to leave, not to pay. Taking rent after you serve it may waive it; some courts ask you to say in the complaint that you haven’t (Lakewood Municipal Court’s form does). NOHM couldn’t confirm that as a statewide rule.
  • Counting (Ohio Rev. Code § 1.14): the day you deliver it doesn’t count. File no earlier than the fourth day after delivery; if the third day is a Sunday or legal holiday, wait a day more. NOHM adds 3 days when you send it by certified mail.
  • Ohio has no grace period in the statute: serve it any time after a default.
  • A written lease: unpaid rent is a breach of the lease (§ 1923.02(A)(9)). An oral tenancy: unpaid rent forfeits the right of occupancy, and this notice ends the tenancy (§ 1923.02(B)).

30-day notice to remedy (health and safety)

Names the tenant’s breach of a duty that affects health and safety: fix it in 30 days, or the agreement ends.

When
The tenant’s breach of a § 5321.05 duty materially affects health and safety.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.11; Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.05(A); Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.02(A)(8)
Good to know
  • Only for a breach of the tenant’s duties under § 5321.05(A) that materially affects health and safety: safe and sanitary premises, garbage, plumbing, fixtures and appliances, no damage, not disturbing neighbors (not the drug duty, (A)(9)).
  • If it isn’t fixed and the agreement ends, you must still serve the 3-day notice to leave the premises before you file (§ 1923.04): NOHM has it.
  • Another breach of a written lease needs only the 3-day notice to leave (§ 1923.02(A)(9), § 1923.04).
  • Drug activity the police told you about has its own 3-day termination notice (§ 5321.17(C)); ask a lawyer.
  • The statute names no way to deliver this notice and runs it from receipt: NOHM uses § 1923.04’s ways and adds 3 days for certified mail.

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy at least 30 days before a rental date.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.17(B)
Good to know
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ notice before the date (§ 5321.17(A)).
  • If the tenant stays after the date, you must still serve the 3-day notice to leave the premises before you file (§ 1923.04): NOHM has it.
  • You can’t end a tenancy to retaliate for a complaint to a code agency, a complaint about your duties, or joining a tenants’ union (§ 5321.02).
  • The statute names no way to deliver it: NOHM uses § 1923.04’s ways and adds 3 days for certified mail.

3-day notice to leave (lease ended or broken)

The notice to leave the premises Ohio requires before any eviction other than for rent, with the statute’s warning.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.04(A); Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.02(A)(1); Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.02(A)(9)
Good to know
  • Ohio requires this notice before every eviction (§ 1923.04(A)): after the lease ran out, after your 30-day notice ended a month-to-month, after a 30-day notice to remedy wasn’t met, or for a breach of a written lease. For unpaid rent, use the 3-day notice to leave for rent.
  • Counting (Ohio Rev. Code § 1.14): the day you deliver it doesn’t count. File no earlier than the day after the date in it; NOHM moves a third day on a weekend or holiday to the next business day.
  • For a breach of a written lease, write what the tenant did under “Anything to add”, and choose “Another reason”.

Court papers.

Complaint in forcible entry and detainer Court

The eviction filing: the complaint for possession (and for rent and damages), with the summons in the words the statute requires, for the clerk.

When
The 3-day notice to leave ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.02; Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.04; Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.05; Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.06(B)
Good to know
  • Ohio has no statewide eviction court forms; courts publish their own. NOHM writes the complaint from the statute. Check your court’s local rules and forms: some ask more (Lakewood’s asks whether the tenant gets federal housing assistance, and that you haven’t taken rent since the notice).
  • File it with the clerk of the municipal or county court for the rental’s address, with copies for each defendant and the 3-day notice. The clerk issues the summons (the last page has the words the statute requires) and serves it by ordinary mail and as you ask: by bailiff, sheriff or certified mail (§ 1923.06).
  • The summons is served at least 7 days before the hearing, and the hearing on possession is no sooner than the 7th day after service is complete (§ 1923.06(A), (H)(1)). The answer day for rent and damages is 28 days after service (§ 1923.06(H)(2)).
  • Any defense may be raised at trial (§ 1923.061); either side may ask for a jury. A continuance is 8 days at most, unless you agree or the tenant posts a bond for the rent (§ 1923.08).
  • Only the owner or a licensed lawyer may sign and file. A property manager can’t, and a company owner needs a lawyer (Cleveland Bar Assn. v. Picklo, 2002, as courts’ notices to landlords say).
  • Taking rent after the notice to leave may waive it. NOHM couldn’t confirm that as statewide law, but some courts ask you to say you haven’t; ask a lawyer before you accept any.
  • Never lock the tenant out, shut off utilities or take their things to get them out (§ 5321.15).

Request for writ of execution Court

Asks the court for the writ that has the bailiff or sheriff restore the premises to you, with the writ in the statute’s form for the judge.

When
The court entered judgment for restitution of the premises and the tenant is still there.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.13(A); Ohio Rev. Code § 1923.14(A)
Good to know
  • After a judgment for restitution, the court issues the writ at your request, in the statute’s form (§ 1923.13(A)). The officer executes it within 10 days of receiving it (§ 1923.14(A)).
  • Local rules set when you may ask: in Lakewood Municipal Court, no sooner than 10 days after the judgment and no later than 30. Check your court’s rule.
  • An appeal with a stay and any bond the court requires stops the writ (§ 1923.14(A)).

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Ohio doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It’s good practice: a signed record of the home’s condition settles deposit disputes.
  • Every written rental agreement must name the owner and the owner’s agent, with their addresses (§ 5321.18(A)); for an oral one, give that notice in writing at the start: NOHM has it.
  • If you rent 3 or fewer units, say so in the written agreement (or in writing at move-in for an oral one) and the tenant’s rent-escrow remedy doesn’t apply (§ 5321.07(C)).
  • Ask the tenant to give you a forwarding address in writing when they leave (§ 5321.16(B)).
  • In counties over 200,000 people, owners file their information with the county auditor (§ 5323.02; NOHM hasn’t re-read the 2025 change). Homes built before 1978: the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Notice of owner and agent (oral lease)

Tells a tenant with no written lease the name and address of the owner and the owner’s agent.

When
At the start of an oral tenancy (a written lease must include it).
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.18
Good to know
  • A written rental agreement must contain the owner’s and the agent’s names and addresses (§ 5321.18(A)); with an oral one, give this written notice when the tenancy begins (§ 5321.18(B)).
  • For a company, give its principal place of business in the county (or else in Ohio) and the name of the person in charge.
  • Without it, the tenant is excused from the notices §§ 5321.07(A) and 5321.08(A) otherwise require (§ 5321.18(C)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.04(A)(8)
Good to know
  • Give reasonable notice and enter only at reasonable times; 24 hours is presumed reasonable (§ 5321.04(A)(8)). The statute names no way to give it.
  • In an emergency, or where notice is impracticable, you may enter without it. Don’t abuse the right of access (§ 5321.04(A)(7)); a tenant can recover damages for improper entry (§ 5321.04(B)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Ohio statute sets notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date, the notice a month-to-month needs to end (§ 5321.17(B)).
  • During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Ohio has no rent control; cities can’t impose it (§ 5321.19). You can’t raise rent to retaliate for a complaint (§ 5321.02); increases for improvements or higher costs are allowed.

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the agreement ends and you get possession back.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.16(B)
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the agreement ends and you get possession back, deliver a written notice itemizing every deduction, with the amount due, to the forwarding address the tenant gave you in writing (§ 5321.16(B)).
  • A tenant who didn’t give a forwarding address in writing can’t get damages or attorney’s fees for a late notice.
  • Interest: on any deposit over $50 or one month’s rent, whichever is greater, pay 5% a year on the excess, yearly, if the tenant stayed 6 months or more (§ 5321.16(A)).
  • Wrongful withholding costs the amount withheld again as damages, plus attorney’s fees (§ 5321.16(C)). Ohio has no deposit cap.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Ohio Rev. Code § 5321.15(B)
Good to know
  • No Ohio statute sets a notice, a storage period or a sale for a residential tenant’s belongings. NOHM fills in 30 days to hold them; ask a lawyer before disposing of anything of value.
  • Use it only after the tenant has moved out, or the writ was executed: never while they still have possession, and never to collect rent (§ 5321.15).
  • Manufactured homes in a park have their own rules (§§ 1923.12 to 1923.14).
