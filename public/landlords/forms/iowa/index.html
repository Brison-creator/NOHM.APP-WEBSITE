Notices before court.

3-day notice of nonpayment of rent

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid and the rental agreement ends unless they pay within 3 days.

When
Rent is unpaid when due (Iowa has no grace period).
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.27(2); Iowa Code § 562A.29A; Iowa Code § 562A.8A; Iowa Code § 4.1(34)
Good to know
  • Iowa has no grace period: rent is payable without demand when due, so you can serve this the day after (Iowa Code § 562A.9(3)).
  • Serve it one of the three ways Iowa Code § 562A.29A(1) allows. Simply handing it over without a signed, dated acknowledgment from a resident 18 or older is not one of them.
  • Posted and mailed: post it on the main entrance door, write the posting date on it, and mail it both regular and certified. Service is complete 4 days after mailing (§ 562A.29A(2)); NOHM adds those days.
  • If the rent isn’t paid in the 3 days, you can file for forcible entry and detainer without a separate notice to quit (Iowa Code § 648.3(1)).
  • Late fees are capped (Iowa Code § 562A.9(4)) and aren’t part of this notice.
  • Accepting rent knowing of the default can waive your right to terminate for it (Iowa Code § 562A.30(1)).
  • A tenant who gave you 7 days’ written notice before rent was due and fixed a repair you were required to make, costing up to a month’s rent, has a defense (Iowa Code § 562A.27(4)).
  • Iowa moves a last day that is a Sunday to Monday (Iowa Code § 4.1(34)); NOHM also moves a Saturday or a holiday, so the date is never early.

7-day notice of noncompliance

Specifies the breach: remedy it in 7 days, or the rental agreement terminates.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease, or a duty that affects health and safety.
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.27(1); Iowa Code § 562A.29A; Iowa Code § 4.1(34)
Good to know
  • For a material breach of the lease, or of a tenant duty in Iowa Code § 562A.17 that materially affects health and safety. Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do.
  • If substantially the same breach recurs within 6 months, you can end the agreement on at least 7 days’ written notice specifying the breach and the termination date, with no chance to remedy (Iowa Code § 562A.27(1)).
  • A clear and present danger (assault or threats, illegal firearm use, controlled substances) has its own 3-day notice with no cure (Iowa Code § 562A.27A): it’s in the forms list.
  • After the agreement terminates, serve a 3-day notice to quit before you file (Iowa Code § 648.3(1)): it’s in the forms list.
  • If the tenant fails to maintain the unit, you may instead give 7 days’ written notice to remedy, then fix it and bill the cost with the next rent (Iowa Code § 562A.28).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rental date at least 30 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.34(2); Iowa Code § 562A.29A
Good to know
  • A month-to-month tenancy ends on a periodic rental date, with at least 30 days’ written notice (Iowa Code § 562A.34(2)); NOHM ends it on the last day of a rental period.
  • Week-to-week: 10 days’ notice (§ 562A.34(1)). A term longer than month-to-month that renews: 30 days before the end of the term (§ 562A.34(3)).
  • If the tenant stays after the date, serve a 3-day notice to quit before you file (Iowa Code § 648.3(1)): it’s in the forms list.
  • You can’t end a tenancy to retaliate against a tenant (Iowa Code § 562A.36).

3-day notice: clear and present danger

Ends the tenancy, with no chance to cure, for activity that is a clear and present danger, and tells the tenant to quit in 3 days.

When
A tenant or someone there assaulted or threatened someone, used a firearm illegally, or had illegal drugs.
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.27A; Iowa Code § 562A.29A
Good to know
  • A clear and present danger includes physical assault or threats of it, illegal use or threatened use of a firearm, and possession of a controlled substance (Iowa Code § 562A.27A(2)); lawfully storing a firearm is not one.
  • State the specific activity. The notice must set out subsection 3 of the statute word for word; NOHM prints it.
  • Serve it one of the three ways Iowa Code § 562A.29A(1) allows; posted and mailed, service is complete 4 days after mailing, so give 4 more days.
  • After the 3 days you may file for forcible entry and detainer without another notice. If the tenant gives you written proof, before you file, that they took one of the measures in subsection 3 against someone else who caused the danger, this route isn’t open against them.
  • Ask a lawyer before you use it: it ends the tenancy without a chance to cure.

3-day notice to quit

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Iowa Code § 648.3; Iowa Code § 562A.34(4)
Good to know
  • Iowa requires this 3-day notice to quit before you file for forcible entry and detainer, on every ground except unpaid rent after the 3-day rent notice (Iowa Code § 648.3(1)).
  • Serve it one of the three ways Iowa Code § 648.3(2) allows: personal service, a resident 18 or older signing and dating an acknowledgment, or posting on the main entrance door (with the posting date) and mailing by both regular and certified mail. Mailed, service is complete 4 days after mailing (§ 648.3(3)): give 4 more days.
  • File within 30 days after the cause of action accrues: thirty days’ peaceable possession with your knowledge bars the case (Iowa Code § 648.18).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Small Claims Forms 3.6 and 3.1)

Iowa’s clerk takes only the Supreme Court’s forms: this lists them, fills in what to write on each from the lease, and sets out the steps and deadlines.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Iowa Code § 631.15; Iowa Code § 648.1; Iowa Code § 648.5; Iowa Code § 648.22
Good to know
  • Iowa’s standard small claims forms are the only forms a small claims case may use (Iowa Code § 631.15): print them from the Iowa courts’ site and copy the values from this worksheet.
  • An individual, partnership or company may bring the case; a company may be represented by an officer or employee, and an employee or a property manager may bring it for owners who are individuals (Iowa Code § 631.14). Appear in person at the hearing unless a lawyer appears for you.
  • Bring the lease, the notice and your proof of serving it, and the rent ledger to the hearing.
  • The filing fee is $115 for fees paid on and after July 1, 2026 (Iowa Code § 631.6(1)(a)).

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Iowa doesn’t require a move-in checklist, but deductions are measured against the unit’s condition at the start of the tenancy (Iowa Code § 562A.12(3)(a)(2)): a signed record is good practice.
  • Before the lease is signed, fully explain utility rates, charges and services the tenant doesn’t pay the utility directly for (Iowa Code § 562A.13(4)).
  • Give the owner and manager disclosure, and say whether the property is on the federal Superfund (CERCLIS) list, in writing before the tenancy starts (Iowa Code § 562A.13(1), (6)): it’s the next form.
  • Built before 1978: the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.
  • Late fees in the lease must stay within Iowa’s caps (Iowa Code § 562A.9(4)), and some lease terms are prohibited (Iowa Code § 562A.11).

Owner and manager disclosure

The written disclosure Iowa requires before the tenancy starts: who manages the home, who accepts notices and legal papers for the owner, and whether it’s a Superfund site.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, and again when any of it changes.
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.13(1); Iowa Code § 562A.13(6)
Good to know
  • Required at or before the start of the tenancy, and kept current (Iowa Code § 562A.13(1)–(2)). If you don’t, the person who signs the rental agreement for you becomes your agent for service and for performing the landlord’s obligations (§ 562A.13(3)).
  • Check whether the property is listed in the EPA’s Superfund information system (CERCLIS) before you answer.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.19(3)
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ notice and enter only at reasonable times (Iowa Code § 562A.19(3)). In an emergency you may enter without consent (§ 562A.19(2)).
  • Don’t use entry to harass the tenant. Other than this, you may enter only by court order, while the tenant is away more than 14 days as reasonably necessary, or after they abandon or surrender the unit (§§ 562A.19(4), 562A.29).
  • Give it any way Iowa Code § 562A.8 allows: in hand, by regular and certified mail, posted, or any way it actually reaches the tenant.

The notice of entry in full · Read the law: legis.iowa.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.13(5)
Good to know
  • Each tenant must be notified in writing at least 30 days before the increase takes effect, and not before the end of the lease term or any renewal (Iowa Code § 562A.13(5)).
  • Iowa has no rent cap, and cities may not limit rents (Iowa Code § 364.3(9)).
  • You can’t raise rent to retaliate against a tenant (Iowa Code § 562A.36).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Iowa Code § 562A.12(3)
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you receive the tenant’s mailing address or delivery instructions, return the deposit or give this statement (Iowa Code § 562A.12(3)). Miss it and you lose the right to keep any of it (§ 562A.12(4)).
  • Deduct only unpaid rent and other money due under the lease, restoring the unit to its move-in condition (not ordinary wear and tear), and costs of getting possession from a tenant who didn’t leave in good faith. Name the damage where you keep money for it. The burden of proof is yours.
  • Interest earned during the first five years of a tenancy is yours; after that it belongs to the tenant: enter it on the form.
  • If the tenant gives no address within one year after the tenancy ends, the deposit becomes yours (§ 562A.12(4)).
  • The deposit can’t exceed two months’ rent, and must be held in a federally insured account, not mixed with your own money (§ 562A.12(1)–(2)). Keeping it in bad faith can cost up to twice the monthly rent on top of actual damages (§ 562A.12(7)).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • Iowa Code chapter 562A has no procedure for property a tenant leaves behind, and landlords can’t hold it for rent (distraint is abolished, § 562A.31(2)). This letter isn’t a statutory notice.
  • NOHM fills in 14 days to hold it; no statute sets a period. Ask a lawyer before you dispose of anything of value.
  • Mobile homes and manufactured-home communities follow their own law (Iowa Code ch. 555B, § 562B.27).
