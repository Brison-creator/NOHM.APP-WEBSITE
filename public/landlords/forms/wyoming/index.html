Notices before court.

3-day notice to quit (rent not paid)

The court’s Notice to Quit Premises: leave within 3 days. Wyoming gives no chance to pay instead.

When
Rent is unpaid 3 days after it was due.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1003; Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1002(a)(i)
Good to know
  • Rent must be unpaid for 3 days after it is due before you can bring the action (W.S. 1-21-1002(a)(i)).
  • It’s a notice to leave, not to pay: Wyoming gives no right to cure.
  • Serve it by leaving a copy with the tenant, or, if they can’t be found, at their usual place of abode or business (W.S. 1-21-1003). Leaving it at the property isn’t enough if the tenant doesn’t live or work there. The server must be 18 or older.
  • Prove service with the court’s Affidavit for Service of Notice to Quit (FED Form 02), signed before a notary, and attach both to the complaint.
  • Wyoming has no statute on counting the 3 days. File on the 4th day after service or later.
  • The words are the court’s Notice to Quit Premises (FED Form 01); its instructions say not to change them. You may use the court’s form instead: wyocourts.gov.

3-day notice to quit (renter’s duties broken)

The court’s Notice to Quit Premises for a duty under W.S. 1-21-1204 or 1-21-1205: leave within 3 days.

When
The tenant broke a renter’s duty or did a prohibited act (damage, disturbing others, refusing entry).
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1003; Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1002(a)(vi); Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1204(a); Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1205(a)
Good to know
  • Write the violation as the court’s form does, naming the subsection and saying how: “W.S. 1-21-1204(a)(vii) by: keeping a dog the rental agreement prohibits”. The renter’s duties are in W.S. 1-21-1204(a); the prohibited acts (damage, interfering with others’ peaceful enjoyment, unreasonably refusing entry) in W.S. 1-21-1205(a).
  • Wyoming gives no right to cure.
  • Serve it by leaving a copy with the tenant, or, if they can’t be found, at their usual place of abode or business (W.S. 1-21-1003). Leaving it at the property isn’t enough if the tenant doesn’t live or work there. The server must be 18 or older.
  • Prove service with the court’s Affidavit for Service of Notice to Quit (FED Form 02), signed before a notary, and attach both to the complaint.
  • Wyoming has no statute on counting the 3 days. File on the 4th day after service or later.
  • The words are the court’s Notice to Quit Premises (FED Form 01); its instructions say not to change them. You may use the court’s form instead: wyocourts.gov.

Notice to end a month-to-month (the agreement’s notice)

Ends a month-to-month tenancy with the notice the written agreement asks for; no statute sets one.

When
You want to end a month-to-month tenancy.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 34-2-128; Wyo. Stat. § 34-2-129
Good to know
  • No Wyoming statute sets a notice for ending a month-to-month tenancy: the lease decides. A month-to-month tenancy exists only by an express written agreement (W.S. 34-2-129); give the notice it asks for, and date this notice to match.
  • A lease ends at the end of its term without notice, and isn’t renewed by the tenant staying or by your taking rent: that is only a tenancy by sufferance (W.S. 34-2-128).
  • If the tenant stays after the tenancy ends, serve the 3-day Notice to Quit Premises (the demand for possession) before you file (W.S. 1-21-1002(a)(i), 1-21-1003).

3-day notice to quit (tenant holding over)

The written notice to leave Wyoming requires before you file against a tenant who stayed after the lease ended.

When
The lease ended, or the agreement’s notice ended the tenancy, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1003; Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1002(a)(i); Wyo. Stat. § 34-2-128
Good to know
  • A tenant who stays after the lease ends must get a written notice to leave at least 3 days before you file (W.S. 1-21-1002(a)(i), 1-21-1003). A lease isn’t renewed by the tenant staying or by your taking rent: that makes only a tenancy by sufferance (W.S. 34-2-128).
  • These are the words of the court’s Notice to Quit Premises (FED Form 01) for a holdover; its instructions say not to change them.
  • Serve it by leaving a copy with the tenant, or, if they can’t be found, at their usual place of abode or business. The server must be 18 or older and signs the Affidavit for Service of Notice to Quit (FED Form 02) before a notary. File on the 4th day after service or later.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (FED Forms 00–16)

Everything to copy onto the court’s Complaint for Forcible Entry and Detainer, with the steps and deadlines after it.

When
The notice to quit was served at least 3 days ago and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1003; Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1004; Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1005; Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1008; Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1013
Good to know
  • Wyoming’s circuit courts use the Judicial Branch’s FED forms, so NOHM doesn’t write the complaint: copy these values onto FED Form 04, swear to it before the clerk or a notary, and file it. The forms are at wyocourts.gov.
  • Wyoming has no statute on counting the 3 days; NOHM refuses a filing date before the 4th day after service.

Notice that a business is represented by its owner Court

Lets an LLC, corporation or partnership that owns the rental appear in the eviction without a lawyer.

When
Your business owns the rental and an owner, shareholder, member or partner will handle the case.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1017(a)
Good to know
  • A business entity may represent itself in an eviction without a lawyer only if an owner, shareholder, member or partner represents it, it owns a majority interest in the property, and it gives the court and all parties this written notice, signed by every owner, shareholder, member or partner (W.S. 1-21-1017(a)). A property manager who isn’t an owner can’t.
  • It doesn’t apply if you seek a judgment over the amount in W.S. 1-21-201 ($6,000). If a lawyer later appears for the business, the tenant is entitled to a continuance (W.S. 1-21-1017(a)(iii)).
  • The court has no form for this notice. File it with the complaint and serve a copy on each tenant.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • No Wyoming statute requires a move-in checklist. It’s good practice: it shows the unit’s condition when you deduct for damage beyond reasonable wear and tear or for cleaning (W.S. 1-21-1208(a)).
  • Wyoming requires the rental agreement, and a written notice when the deposit is taken, to say whether any part of the deposit is nonrefundable (W.S. 1-21-1207; NOHM’s notice). Any lack of hot water, heat or other basics must be agreed in writing by both of you (W.S. 1-21-1202(a)). Homes built before 1978 need the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Notice of nonrefundable deposit

The written notice, when you take the deposit, of whether any part of it is nonrefundable.

When
When you take the deposit.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1207
Good to know
  • The rental agreement must say whether any part of the deposit is nonrefundable, and the renter must also get written notice of it when you take the deposit (W.S. 1-21-1207). Give this notice even when none of it is nonrefundable.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1205(a)(iii)
Good to know
  • Wyoming sets no notice period or hours for entry: follow your lease. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • A renter may not unreasonably refuse entry for repairs, inspection or showing the unit (W.S. 1-21-1205(a)(iii)). If they do, that is a violation you can serve a 3-day notice to quit for (W.S. 1-21-1002(a)(vi)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Wyoming statute sets notice for a rent increase, or caps rent. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date: that is NOHM’s practice, not the law.
  • A month-to-month tenancy exists only by an express written agreement (W.S. 34-2-129): change the rent as it provides. A lease that ended isn’t renewed by your taking rent (W.S. 34-2-128).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the lease ends, or 15 days after you get the tenant’s new address, whichever is later (30 more days if the unit is damaged).
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1208(a)
Good to know
  • Deliver or mail the balance with a written itemization of deductions and the reasons, within 30 days after the rental agreement ends or 15 days after you get the renter’s new mailing address, whichever is later; 30 days more if the unit is damaged (W.S. 1-21-1208(a)). The renter should tell you within 30 days where to send it. NOHM warns at 30 days.
  • A deposit for utilities, identified separately, has its own timeline: refund within 10 days of proof the utilities are paid (W.S. 1-21-1208(b)).
  • No interest is owed, and there is no cap. Unreasonably failing to comply lets the renter recover the full deposit and court costs (W.S. 1-21-1208(c)). Damages beyond the deposit carry 10% annual interest (W.S. 1-21-1211(b)).

Notice of abandoned property

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Wyo. Stat. § 1-21-1210(a)
Good to know
  • After you regain lawful possession, you may dispose of trash and property you reasonably believe is hazardous, perishable or valueless right away; property left is presumed valueless and abandoned (W.S. 1-21-1210). For anything of value, give this notice.
  • The notice is served on the day it’s mailed certified to an address the renter gave you in writing for this, served under Rule 4 with a copy delivered personally, or published in a county newspaper (W.S. 1-21-1210(a)(i)). The court’s version is FED Form 16.
  • With no written answer in 7 days, it is conclusively abandoned. If the renter answers in writing, hold it longer: the statute says 7 more days but then refers to “the additional fifteen (15) day period”; hold it 15 days after their answer to be safe (W.S. 1-21-1210(a)(iii)).
  • The renter pays reasonable storage and removal costs before taking it back; you aren’t responsible for loss while it’s stored (W.S. 1-21-1210(b)–(c)).
