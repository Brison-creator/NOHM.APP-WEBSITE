Vermont 30-day notice of termination for breach

Ends the tenancy at least 30 days after the tenant gets it, for breaking a material term of the lease.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenThe tenant broke a material lease term (other than rent).
  • The law9 V.S.A. § 4467(b)(1); 9 V.S.A. § 4467(f); 9 V.S.A. § 4451(1)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Vermont gives no right to cure a breach: the tenancy ends on the date stated.
  • For criminal activity, illegal drug activity or acts of violence that threaten other residents’ health or safety, 14 days’ notice is enough (9 V.S.A. § 4467(b)(2)); ask a lawyer before using it.
  • A termination for something other than rent within 90 days after a government notice that the home doesn’t meet health or safety rules is presumed retaliation (§ 4465).
  • The days run from “actual notice”: hand delivery, or mail to the last known address, which is presumed received three days after mailing by first-class or certified mail (9 V.S.A. § 4451(1)). NOHM adds those 3 days when it’s mailed. Vermont law has no e-mail service for these notices.
  • Start the eviction within 60 days after the termination date, or the notice won’t support a judgment (9 V.S.A. § 4467(k)). Several valid notices on different grounds can all support the case (§ 4467(i)).
