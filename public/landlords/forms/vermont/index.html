Notices before court.

14-day notice of termination for nonpayment

Ends the tenancy for unpaid rent at least 14 days after the tenant gets it, unless they pay the rent due first.

When
Rent is past due (Vermont has no grace period).
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4467(a); 9 V.S.A. § 4467(f); 9 V.S.A. § 4451(1)
Good to know
  • The tenant can stop the termination by paying, or offering, the rent due through the end of the rental period in which they pay, before the termination date (9 V.S.A. § 4467(a)). After you file, they can stop the case once in 12 months by paying all rent due, interest and costs into court before the writ is executed (12 V.S.A. § 4773).
  • Taking part of the rent doesn’t waive the termination (§ 4467(a)); taking rent after the termination doesn’t dismiss the case (§ 4467(j)).
  • The days run from “actual notice”: hand delivery, or mail to the last known address, which is presumed received three days after mailing by first-class or certified mail (9 V.S.A. § 4451(1)). NOHM adds those 3 days when it’s mailed. Vermont law has no e-mail service for these notices.
  • Start the eviction within 60 days after the termination date, or the notice won’t support a judgment (9 V.S.A. § 4467(k)). Several valid notices on different grounds can all support the case (§ 4467(i)).
  • For a nonpayment case the complaint must include the Declaration of Compliance with the CARES Act (court form 100-00031): for a covered property, a 30-day notice is needed.

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov

30-day notice of termination for breach

Ends the tenancy at least 30 days after the tenant gets it, for breaking a material term of the lease.

When
The tenant broke a material lease term (other than rent).
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4467(b)(1); 9 V.S.A. § 4467(f); 9 V.S.A. § 4451(1)
Good to know
  • Vermont gives no right to cure a breach: the tenancy ends on the date stated.
  • For criminal activity, illegal drug activity or acts of violence that threaten other residents’ health or safety, 14 days’ notice is enough (9 V.S.A. § 4467(b)(2)); ask a lawyer before using it.
  • A termination for something other than rent within 90 days after a government notice that the home doesn’t meet health or safety rules is presumed retaliation (§ 4465).
  • The days run from “actual notice”: hand delivery, or mail to the last known address, which is presumed received three days after mailing by first-class or certified mail (9 V.S.A. § 4451(1)). NOHM adds those 3 days when it’s mailed. Vermont law has no e-mail service for these notices.
  • Start the eviction within 60 days after the termination date, or the notice won’t support a judgment (9 V.S.A. § 4467(k)). Several valid notices on different grounds can all support the case (§ 4467(i)).

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov

60- or 90-day notice of termination for no cause

Ends a monthly tenancy for no cause: 60 days’ notice, or 90 after two years.

When
You want a monthly tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4467(c)(1); 9 V.S.A. § 4467(e); 9 V.S.A. § 4467(f)
Good to know
  • With no written rental agreement and rent paid monthly: 60 days, or 90 days for a tenant who has lived there more than two years (9 V.S.A. § 4467(c)(1)). NOHM gives 90 days from 24 full months on, so it is never short.
  • With a written lease, the notice ends it at the end of its term: at least 30 days before the term ends, or 60 days after two years (§ 4467(e)). NOHM uses the longer no-written-lease notice, which is never shorter; set the lease’s end date if it is later.
  • Weekly rent: 21 days with no written agreement (§ 4467(c)(2)), 7 days with a written week-to-week agreement (§ 4467(e)). Sale of the building (no written agreement): 30 days (§ 4467(d)). Sharing the landlord’s own home: 15 days monthly, 7 weekly (§ 4467(h)).
  • No separate demand for possession: once the tenancy ends, a tenant who stays without your express consent can be sued for possession (§ 4468).
  • The days run from “actual notice”: hand delivery, or mail to the last known address, which is presumed received three days after mailing by first-class or certified mail (9 V.S.A. § 4451(1)). NOHM adds those 3 days when it’s mailed. Vermont law has no e-mail service for these notices.
  • Start the eviction within 60 days after the termination date, or the notice won’t support a judgment (9 V.S.A. § 4467(k)). Several valid notices on different grounds can all support the case (§ 4467(i)).

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov

Court papers.

Complaint for ejectment Court

The eviction filing for the Superior Court: that the tenant holds the home unlawfully after the tenancy ended, with the rental agreement and the notice attached.

When
The termination date in your notice has passed and the tenant hasn’t left (file within 60 days).
The law
12 V.S.A. § 4852; 9 V.S.A. § 4468; 9 V.S.A. § 4467(k)
Good to know
  • File it in the Superior Court, Civil Division, for the county (unit) where the rental is. Attach a copy of the rental agreement, if any, and of the notice that ended the tenancy (12 V.S.A. § 4852).
  • Use the Judiciary’s Summons and Notice of Appearance (form 100-00268); a sheriff serves it, and you file the Sheriff’s Return of Service (100-00261). The tenant has 21 days to answer. The Judiciary also has a general complaint form (100-00050), which you may use instead of this one.
  • For a case based in whole or part on unpaid rent, file the Declaration of Compliance with the CARES Act (form 100-00031) with the complaint: the form says it is required.
  • You may ask the court to order rent paid into court while the case goes on (12 V.S.A. § 4853a), by motion with an affidavit (Civil Division Motion, 100-00053); it is heard on 14 days’ notice, and failing to pay as ordered leads to judgment for immediate possession.
  • If you win, the writ of possession issues the day judgment is entered, and the sheriff puts you in possession no sooner than 14 days after serving it (§ 4854). Attorney’s fees only where a written rental agreement provides for them.
  • Start the case within 60 days after the termination date in the notice, or the notice can’t support a judgment (9 V.S.A. § 4467(k)). Whether an LLC needs a lawyer wasn’t confirmed; ask the court.

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov, legislature.vermont.gov, vtcourts.gov

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Vermont doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: only damage beyond normal wear and tear can come out of the deposit (9 V.S.A. § 4461(b)), and this form records the home’s condition.
  • Before the lease is signed, give the flood hazard disclosure in a separate written document (9 V.S.A. § 4466): NOHM has it.
  • For a home built before 1978, give the Health Department’s approved lead materials and a copy of your most recent lead-safe maintenance (RRPM) compliance statement before the lease (18 V.S.A. § 1759(d)(1)), plus the federal lead-based paint disclosure.
  • You may not charge an application fee, and may not require a Social Security number; accept a government ID or an ITIN (9 V.S.A. § 4456a). A lease may not ban possessing cannabis or its non-smoked use in the unit (§ 4468b).

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov, legislature.vermont.gov

Flood hazard disclosure (FEMA flood area)

The separate written disclosure Vermont requires before a lease: whether any part of the rental is in a FEMA mapped special flood hazard area.

When
At or before the lease is signed, as its own document.
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4466(a)
Good to know
  • Required since June 17, 2024 (Act 181): disclose before entering the lease whether any part of the premises is in a FEMA mapped special flood hazard area, in a separate written document given at or before signing, substantially in the form the Department of Housing and Community Development prescribes (9 V.S.A. § 4466(a)).
  • This form follows the Department’s model form (Rev. 9/2024): the map number and date, the answer, your certification that you checked the map yourself, and the signatures. The Department’s form also has its own notice to tenants about flood insurance; NOHM’s follows it in substance. You may use the Department’s form instead.
  • Look the address up on FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov) before you answer.

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov, floodready.vermont.gov

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter.
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4460
Good to know
  • Give at least 48 hours’ notice, and enter only between 9 A.M. and 9 P.M. (9 V.S.A. § 4460(b)). Pick a window inside those hours.
  • Entry on notice is only to inspect, make repairs, alterations or improvements, supply agreed services, or show the unit. Any other time needs the tenant’s consent, which they may not unreasonably withhold (§ 4460(a)).
  • Without consent or notice only when you reasonably believe there is imminent danger to a person or property (§ 4460(c)).

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 60 days before the new rent starts.
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4455(b)
Good to know
  • A rent increase takes effect on the first day of a rental period at least 60 days after the tenant gets actual notice (9 V.S.A. § 4455(b)). Mailed notice is presumed received 3 days after mailing (§ 4451(1)): if you mail it, allow 63 days.
  • In age-restricted housing, give the Department of Housing and Community Development’s form to the Department and every affected resident at least 60 days ahead, or the increase can’t be enforced (§ 4468a).
  • Vermont has no rent cap. You can’t change the terms of a tenancy to retaliate (§ 4465(a)).

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 14 days after the tenant vacates (or you discover they have).
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4461
Good to know
  • Return the deposit with this written statement within 14 days after the tenant vacated, if they told you the date, or after you discover they vacated or abandoned the home (9 V.S.A. § 4461(c)). For a seasonal rental that isn’t a primary residence, 60 days.
  • Hand-deliver it, or mail it to the tenant’s last known address (§ 4461(d)).
  • Miss the 14 days and you forfeit the right to keep any of it; if that is willful, you owe double what was wrongfully withheld, plus attorney’s fees and costs (§ 4461(e)).
  • Vermont sets no cap or interest, but a town may (Burlington, Brattleboro and Winooski have deposit rules; § 4461(g)). On a sale, transfer the deposit and give the tenant the new landlord’s name and address (§ 4461(f)).

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
9 V.S.A. § 4462(c)
Good to know
  • Use it when a tenant abandoned the home: you reasonably believe they no longer live there full time, rent isn’t current, and you made reasonable efforts to learn their intentions (9 V.S.A. § 4462(a)). Mail it to their last known address (§ 4462(c)(1)).
  • Keep the property in a safe, dry, secured place; trash, garbage and refuse may be thrown out. Unclaimed after 60 days, it becomes yours (§ 4462(c)(2)).
  • No notice is needed if the tenant gave you actual notice that they were leaving, or left at the end of the rental agreement (§ 4462(d)).
  • After an eviction, property may be disposed of without notice 15 days after the writ is served or you are restored to possession, whichever is later (12 V.S.A. § 4854a).

Read the law: legislature.vermont.gov, legislature.vermont.gov
