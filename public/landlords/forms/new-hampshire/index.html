Notices before court.

Demand for rent and 7-day eviction notice

Demands the back rent and gives 7 days’ notice to leave; the tenant can still pay everything before the hearing to stay.

When
Rent is due and in arrears.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:2, II(a); N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:3, II–IV; N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:5; N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:9
Good to know
  • New Hampshire uses two papers, the Demand for Rent (court form NHJB-3040-D) and the Eviction Notice (NHJB-3041-D), which may be served together (RSA 540:4, 540:5, I). NOHM’s notice is both.
  • Your notice must carry the same information as the court’s Eviction Notice form, NHJB-3041-D (RSA 540:5, II), which the court revised on October 8, 2026. NOHM couldn’t read that revision: compare it at courts.nh.gov before you serve.
  • The tenant can stop the eviction by paying everything listed in the box before the hearing, up to 3 times in 12 months; you then file the receipt with the court and send the tenant a copy (RSA 540:9). What a tenant paid your utility to prevent a shutoff isn’t unpaid rent (RSA 540:2, VI).
  • If you take the arrears after you file, tell the tenant in writing that you’ll proceed with the eviction anyway, or the payment can create a new tenancy (RSA 540:13, VII): NOHM’s Notice of intent to proceed.
  • Anyone may serve it, in hand or by leaving it at the tenant’s last and usual place of abode; no mail. Keep a true attested copy and sign an affidavit of service (it needn’t be sworn): the court needs both (RSA 540:5, I). The days are calendar days; a weekend doesn’t extend them (RSA 21:35).
  • After the date passes, get the Landlord and Tenant Writ from the circuit court (the eviction filing worksheet). No other demand is needed (RSA 540:12).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, courts.nh.gov

30-day eviction notice (lease term broken)

Ends the tenancy in 30 days because the tenant broke a material term of the lease, stated in the notice.

When
The tenant failed to comply with a material term of the lease.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:2, II(c); N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:3, II–III; N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:5
Good to know
  • Name the lease term and exactly how it was broken: the notice must state the reason with specificity (RSA 540:3, III).
  • Substantial damage to the premises, or behavior that harms the health or safety of other tenants or the landlord, needs only 7 days (RSA 540:2, II(b), (d); 540:3, II). NOHM’s notice is the 30-day one; the court’s Eviction Notice form has a box for each.
  • There’s no chance to cure. For “other good cause” based on what the tenant, family or guests did, first give the written warning (RSA 540:2, III): NOHM’s form.
  • You can’t evict a tenant solely because they or a household member were a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking (RSA 540:2, VII).
  • Your notice must carry the same information as the court’s Eviction Notice form, NHJB-3041-D (RSA 540:5, II), which the court revised on October 8, 2026. NOHM couldn’t read that revision: compare it at courts.nh.gov before you serve.
  • Anyone may serve it, in hand or by leaving it at the tenant’s last and usual place of abode; no mail. Keep a true attested copy and sign an affidavit of service (it needn’t be sworn): the court needs both (RSA 540:5, I). The days are calendar days; a weekend doesn’t extend them (RSA 21:35).
  • After the date passes, get the Landlord and Tenant Writ from the circuit court (the eviction filing worksheet). No other demand is needed (RSA 540:12).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

30-day eviction notice (other good cause)

Ends the tenancy in 30 days for a legitimate business or economic reason, stated in the notice.

When
You need the rental back for a business or economic reason.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:2, II(e), V; N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:3, II–III; N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:5
Good to know
  • Most rentals are restricted property, ended only for a reason the law lists. A legitimate business or economic reason is good cause, and needn’t be anything the tenant did (RSA 540:2, V). Write the reason in the notice’s details.
  • Nonrestricted property (a single-family house whose owner owns no more than 3, or a unit in an owner-occupied building of 4 or fewer) can be ended for any reason on 30 days’ notice (RSA 540:1-a, 540:2, I).
  • Since July 1, 2026, a lease of 12 months or more (or renewed to 12 months) may end at its term if you give written notice at least 60 days before the term ends, and you file within 6 months after it expires (RSA 540:2, II(i)). For that, serve this notice 60 days ahead. It’s a no-fault termination, not an eviction, for tenant screening (RSA 540:2, VIII).
  • A tenant’s refusal of a rent increase you gave 30 days’ written notice of is good cause (RSA 540:2, IV).
  • A tenant who complained to a code authority, or used RSA 540-A, may have a retaliation defense (RSA 540:13-a).
  • Your notice must carry the same information as the court’s Eviction Notice form, NHJB-3041-D (RSA 540:5, II), which the court revised on October 8, 2026. NOHM couldn’t read that revision: compare it at courts.nh.gov before you serve.
  • Anyone may serve it, in hand or by leaving it at the tenant’s last and usual place of abode; no mail. Keep a true attested copy and sign an affidavit of service (it needn’t be sworn): the court needs both (RSA 540:5, I). The days are calendar days; a weekend doesn’t extend them (RSA 21:35).
  • After the date passes, get the Landlord and Tenant Writ from the circuit court (the eviction filing worksheet). No other demand is needed (RSA 540:12).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

Written warning (before an eviction for conduct)

Warns the tenant that if their conduct happens again it will be grounds for eviction.

When
Before an eviction for “other good cause” based on what the tenant, their family or guests did.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:2, III
Good to know
  • Required before an eviction notice for other good cause that rests on the actions or inactions of the tenant, their family or guests (RSA 540:2, III).
  • Serve it in hand, by leaving it at the tenant’s last and usual place of abode, or by certified mail, and keep a copy.
  • If the conduct happens again, give the 30-day eviction notice, and say in it that this warning was given.

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Landlord and Tenant Writ) Court

What the court’s Landlord and Tenant Writ asks for, filled in from the lease, with where to get it and every step and deadline after.

When
The eviction notice expired and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:12; N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:13; N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:14
Good to know
  • The Landlord and Tenant Writ is the court’s own form, issued by the circuit court’s District Division; NOHM doesn’t write it. Copy these values onto it.
  • Bring the expired eviction notice (and, for rent, the demand for rent), each with its attested copy and affidavit of service.
  • The court revised its procedure on October 8, 2026 (answers due 5 days after the return day; judgment 7 days after the notice of judgment). Follow the court’s current filing and service instructions at courts.nh.gov.
  • A limited liability company, corporation or partnership with 5 or fewer members may appear through a member or employee, with a written authorization for each appearance (RSA 540:30): NOHM’s form.
  • Never lock the tenant out, remove their property or cut off utilities yourself (RSA 540-A:3).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, courts.nh.gov, courts.nh.gov

Notice of intent to proceed after accepting payment

Tells the tenant you took their payment while the eviction case is pending, and are still going ahead with it.

When
You accept a payment of the arrears after filing.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:13, VII
Good to know
  • While the possessory action is pending, you may accept payment of the arrears without creating a new tenancy only if you tell the tenant in writing that you intend to proceed with the eviction anyway (RSA 540:13, VII). Give this when you take the payment.
  • In a case for unpaid rent alone, a tenant who pays everything RSA 540:9 lists before the hearing (rent, $15, lawful lease charges, filing and service costs) gets the case dismissed: this notice doesn’t change that.

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

Written authorization to represent a company (RSA 540:30) Court

Lets a member or employee of a small company landlord appear for it in an eviction case.

When
Before each court appearance, if the landlord is an LLC, corporation or partnership with 5 or fewer members.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:30
Good to know
  • A person of good character who isn’t a lawyer, but is a member or employee of an LLC, corporation or partnership with 5 or fewer members, may represent it in the circuit court in a case under RSA 540, with the organization’s written authorization (RSA 540:30).
  • Each appearance needs a new authorization: make one for every hearing.

Read the law: gc.nh.gov

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in; the tenant has 5 days to return it.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540-A:6, I(b)–(c)
Good to know
  • When you take a deposit, you must tell the tenant in writing that conditions needing repair or correction should be listed and given to you within 5 days of occupancy (RSA 540-A:6, I(b)–(c)). This form does it.
  • Exempt: an owner renting a single-family home who owns no other rental property, or units in an owner-occupied building of 5 units or fewer, unless the tenant is 60 or older (RSA 540-A:5, I).
  • Also give the signed deposit receipt (the next form) unless the tenant paid by personal, bank or agency check.
  • Before taking an application fee, disclose its amount in writing and any background or credit check you require (RSA 540-A:3, VIII). Let the tenant pay rent at least one way other than electronically (RSA 540-A:3, X).
  • Owners of restricted property file with the town or city clerk the name, address and phone of an in-state agent for service of process, within 30 days of becoming the owner (RSA 540:1-b).
  • A home built before 1978 also needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

Security deposit receipt

The signed receipt for the deposit: how much, where it’s held, and the tenant’s 5 days to list repairs.

When
When you receive a security deposit.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540-A:6, I(b)
Good to know
  • Give it at once on receiving a deposit. It isn’t needed when the tenant pays by personal, bank or agency check, but the 5-day notice still is (RSA 540-A:6, I(c)).
  • The deposit can’t be more than one month’s rent or $100, whichever is greater (RSA 540-A:6, I(a)).
  • Hold it in trust, not mixed with your own money, in a New Hampshire bank account (or post a bond with the town or city clerk) (RSA 540-A:6, II). After a year it earns interest at the account’s savings rate (RSA 540-A:6, IV).
  • If you sell the property, turn the deposit over and tell the tenant by registered or certified mail who holds it (RSA 540-A:6, III).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540-A:3, IV–V
Good to know
  • New Hampshire sets no number of hours: you need the tenant’s consent, after notice adequate under the circumstances (RSA 540-A:3, IV–V). NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • For a bed bug evaluation, give 48 hours’ written notice (RSA 540-A:3, V-b).
  • Without consent you may enter only to make emergency repairs, or with a court order (RSA 540-A:3, IV, V-d).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540:2, IV
Good to know
  • Give written notice of the amount and effective date at least 30 days ahead. If the tenant then refuses the increase, that is good cause for eviction (RSA 540:2, IV).
  • During a fixed-term lease, the rent changes only as the lease allows.
  • New Hampshire has no rent cap. A rent increase to retaliate for a complaint can be a defense in an eviction (RSA 540:13-a).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540-A:7
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the tenancy ends, return the deposit with any interest due, less the itemized deductions (RSA 540-A:7, I).
  • Describe each repair with particularity and enclose your evidence that it was or will be done: receipts, estimates, bills or invoices (RSA 540-A:7, I). Give the period of each rent claim (RSA 540-A:7, II).
  • A deposit held a year or more earns interest at the bank account’s savings rate (RSA 540-A:6, IV).
  • Getting it wrong costs twice the deposit plus interest, less what you paid and what’s owed (RSA 540-A:8, I(b)). If the tenant gave no forwarding address, a deposit unclaimed 6 months after the tenancy ends becomes yours (RSA 540-A:8, II).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 540-A:3, VII
Good to know
  • After a tenant vacates, voluntarily or by eviction, store their property with reasonable care for 7 days; they may take it without paying rent or storage. After 7 days you may dispose of it without notice (RSA 540-A:3, VII). This notice isn’t required, but it heads off disputes.
  • If you believe the tenant abandoned the home without saying so, the statute’s own Notice of Property Abandonment (RSA 540-A:4, XII) gives you a presumption of abandonment: leave it at the premises and send it by certified mail. It must be printed in 12-point type, which NOHM’s papers don’t yet print, so NOHM doesn’t draw it.
  • Never remove a tenant’s property while they still have possession, except through the court (RSA 540-A:3, III).

Read the law: gc.nh.gov, gc.nh.gov
