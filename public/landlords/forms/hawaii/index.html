Notices before court.

10-calendar-day notice for failure to pay rent

Demands the rent due within 10 calendar days of receipt, or the rental agreement ends; a copy goes to a mediation center.

When
Rent is past due (Hawaii has no grace period).
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-68(a)–(c); Haw. Rev. Stat. § 1-29
Good to know
  • Since February 5, 2026, the notice must carry everything HRS § 521-68(b) lists, the warning in bold. NOHM prints each; write each tenant’s phone, and email and mailing address if you have them, and the mediation center’s name in the blanks.
  • Give a copy to a state-funded mediation center the same day, and keep proof: the court complaint must include the center’s verification, or your statement of how you sent it (HRS § 521-68(c)–(d)). On O‘ahu: The Mediation Center of the Pacific, (808) 521-6767, Landlordtenant@mediatehawaii.org, 1301 Young Street, 2nd Floor, Honolulu 96814. Each island’s form names its own center.
  • Post it on the unit (received that day) or mail it (received 2 business days after the postmark). The court’s complaint asks which of the two you used, so NOHM offers only those.
  • If mediation is scheduled within the 10 days and the tenant takes part, you can’t file until 20 calendar days after they received the notice, unless they don’t attend or cancel. If they schedule it, you must take part.
  • The mediation parts apply from February 5, 2026 through February 4, 2028; the 10 calendar days stay after that.
  • HRS § 1-29 moves a last day that is a Sunday or a holiday; NOHM also moves a Saturday, so the date is never early.
  • The Judiciary’s fillable form (RG-AC-508) is the courts’ version of this notice. Tenancies under the Maui wildfire proclamations have a separate 15-day notice (HRS § 521-84): ask a lawyer.

10-day notice of a rule violation

Names the rule broken, in the statute’s form: if it continues after the date, the rental agreement can end.

When
The tenant broke a house rule they were told about when they signed.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-72(a); Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-52; Haw. Rev. Stat. § 1-29
Good to know
  • This is the form HRS § 521-72(a) sets out, for a rule you adopted under HRS § 521-52 and brought to the tenant’s attention when they entered the rental agreement. Name the rule and how it was broken.
  • For failing to keep the unit clean and safe or follow housing laws (HRS § 521-51), use the 10-day notice of noncompliance in the forms list.
  • No time to remedy is required when the breach causes or threatens damage to a person, or violates HRS § 521-51(1) or (6): ask a lawyer about ending the agreement without one.
  • If the breach continues or recurs after the date, you may bring a summary proceeding for possession within 30 days after that (HRS § 521-72(b)).
  • The law names no way to serve it: hand it to the tenant, or mail it (NOHM adds 2 business days).

45-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on any day at least 45 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-71(a)
Good to know
  • Any day at least 45 days out; it needn’t end a rental period (HRS § 521-71(a)).
  • Demolition, conversion to a condominium, or conversion to transient vacation rental use needs 120 days’ notice (§ 521-71(c)). A tenancy shorter than month-to-month: 10 days (§ 521-71(d)). A fixed-term lease ends on its date.
  • No demand for possession is needed after the tenancy ends. If the tenant stays, file for summary possession within the first 60 days of the holdover, or a month-to-month tenancy at the old rent begins; you may recover up to twice the monthly rent, prorated daily, for each day they stay (HRS § 521-71(e)).
  • A notice given to evade the 45-day rent-increase rule is void (§ 521-71(f)), and you can’t terminate to retaliate (HRS § 521-74).
  • The law names no way to serve it; hand it to the tenant and keep proof.

10-day notice of noncompliance (tenant’s duties)

Tells the tenant how they’re failing a duty under HRS § 521-51 and gives at least 10 days from receipt to fix it.

When
The tenant isn’t keeping the unit clean and safe, is damaging it, or breaks a housing law.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-69(a); Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-51
Good to know
  • For a material noncompliance with the tenant’s duties in HRS § 521-51. After the time to remedy, you may end the agreement and sue for possession only if the noncompliance is with § 521-51(1), building and housing laws materially affecting health and safety; otherwise, fix it and bill the tenant, and the bill is rent due with the next rent (HRS § 521-69(a)).
  • No time to remedy is required when the noncompliance causes or threatens irremediable damage to a person or property: ask a lawyer.
  • Give at least 10 days after the tenant receives it. If you can’t serve the tenant, you may post it in a conspicuous place on the unit (§ 521-69(a)). Mailed, allow a few days for delivery.
  • For a broken house rule, use the 10-day rule-violation notice instead (HRS § 521-72).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Act 278 Complaint, 1DC08, 1DC50 Summons)

Hawaii’s district courts use the Judiciary’s summary possession forms: this lists them, fills in what to write on the complaint from the lease, and sets out the steps and deadlines.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid or left.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 666-6; Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-68(d); Haw. Rev. Stat. § 666-11; DCRCP 12(a)
Good to know
  • The form numbers are the First Circuit’s (O‘ahu). The other circuits have their own versions (2DC on Maui, 3DC on Hawai‘i Island, 5DC on Kaua‘i, from search results; not opened): get yours from your island’s landlord forms page.
  • A company (an LLC or corporation) must be represented by a lawyer licensed in Hawaii; an individual landlord may represent themselves.
  • Nonpayment: attach the 10-day notice and the mediation center’s verification of receiving it (or your affirmation of how you sent it), and say where mediation stands (HRS § 521-68(d)).
  • If rent is disputed, either side can ask the court to order the tenant to pay it into court as it comes due; if the tenant doesn’t, you get judgment for possession (HRS § 666-21).
  • The Judiciary’s Act 278 landlord information sheet and FAQ explain the mediation rules.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-42(a)
Good to know
  • Required: before the tenant first moves in, inventory the home and its furnishings and appliances in writing; you and each tenant sign duplicate copies, and each tenant gets one (HRS § 521-42(a)).
  • Without it, the home at move-out is presumed to be in the condition it was in at move-in (§ 521-42(a)), which makes damage hard to charge to the deposit.
  • Give the owner and manager disclosure, with your general excise tax number, at or before the start: it’s the next form.
  • If you live off the island where the unit is, name an agent who lives on that island in the written rental agreement (HRS § 521-43(f)).
  • Give the tenant a copy of the lease, a written receipt for rent when it’s paid (for a check, when the tenant asks), and your rules when they sign (HRS §§ 521-43(d)–(e), 521-52(a)).
  • At the start you may collect only the first month’s rent and the security deposit (HRS § 521-44(b)).
  • Built before 1978: the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

Owner and manager disclosure

The written disclosure Hawaii requires at the start: who manages the home, who accepts rent, notices and legal papers for the owner, and your general excise tax number.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, and again when any of it changes.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-43(a); Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-43(h)
Good to know
  • Required in writing at or before the start of the tenancy, and kept current (HRS § 521-43(a)). If you don’t, the person who signs the rental agreement for you becomes your agent (§ 521-43(b)); not fixing it within 10 days of the tenant’s demand can cost $100 and fees (§ 521-67).
  • Landlords must give all tenants their general excise tax number, for the low-income tax credit (§ 521-43(h)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-53(b)
Good to know
  • At least two days’ notice, and only during reasonable hours (HRS § 521-53(b)); in an emergency, or where it’s impracticable, you may enter sooner.
  • Don’t use entry to harass the tenant. Otherwise you may enter only by court order, if the tenant appears to have abandoned the unit, or while they’re away as reasonably necessary (§§ 521-53(c), 521-70(b)).
  • Repeated unreasonable entry lets the tenant end the agreement, and can bring an injunction and a fine (HRS § 521-73).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 45 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-21(d)
Good to know
  • A month-to-month rent can’t go up without written notice given 45 consecutive days before the increase takes effect (HRS § 521-21(d)); a tenancy shorter than month-to-month needs 15 days (§ 521-21(e)).
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Hawaii has no statewide rent cap. You can’t raise rent to retaliate (HRS § 521-74), and a termination notice meant to evade this rule is void (§ 521-71(f)).
  • A late charge can’t be more than 8 per cent of the rent due (§ 521-21(f)).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 14 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-44(c)
Good to know
  • Within 14 days after the rental agreement ends, return the deposit, or notify the tenant in writing of what you keep and why, with written evidence of the costs: estimates, invoices or receipts (HRS § 521-44(c)). Miss it and you can’t keep any of it.
  • Mailed to an address the tenant gave you, with proof of mailing, postmarked before midnight of the 14th day, it is presumed on time.
  • A tenant who wrongfully quit (gone 20 or more days without notice or rent) isn’t owed this notice, and you may keep the whole deposit (§ 521-44(c)–(d)).
  • The deposit can’t exceed one month’s rent, plus a pet deposit of up to one month’s rent for a tenant with a pet (never for an assistance animal) (§ 521-44(b)).
  • Wrongful, wilful retention can cost three times the amount kept (§ 521-44(h)).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Haw. Rev. Stat. § 521-56
Good to know
  • Applies when the tenant has wrongfully quit, or left under a notice to quit or at the end of the term, and abandoned property you determine in good faith to be of value (HRS § 521-56(a)). Property of no value you may dispose of (§ 521-56(c)).
  • Before you sell or donate it, mail this notice to the tenant’s forwarding address, an address they gave for notices, or their previous known address. You may sell it only after advertising the sale in a daily paper in the circuit for at least three consecutive days, or donate it to a charity; not until 15 days after mailing.
  • You may also store it at the tenant’s expense. Hold sale proceeds, after rent and costs, in trust for the tenant for 30 days (§ 521-56(b)).
