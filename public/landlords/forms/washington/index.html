Notices before court.

14-day notice to pay rent or vacate

The statute’s own form: pay the rent due in 14 days, or move out.

When
Rent is past due (any time after it’s due).
The law
RCW 59.12.030(3); RCW 59.18.057(1); RCW 59.12.040; RCW 59.18.650(2)(a); RCW 1.12.040
Good to know
  • Washington requires this notice in substantially the form RCW 59.18.057 sets; NOHM prints its words. The Attorney General has it in other languages at www.atg.wa.gov/landlord-tenant (RCW 59.18.058).
  • Serve it any time after the rent is due (RCW 59.12.030(3)). Fill in where to pay: the form names the landlord and the address.
  • When any copy is mailed, five more days are allowed before you can file, so NOHM’s date is 19 days out. Mail it from within Washington. Certified mail isn’t required for notices served on or after June 11, 2026.
  • Prove service with a declaration or affidavit of the person who served it.
  • Payments go to rent first (RCW 59.18.283). A late fee can’t be charged for rent paid within 5 days of its due date (RCW 59.18.170(2)), and a judgment can include no more than $75 in late fees (RCW 59.18.410(1)).
  • A tenant whose main income is monthly government assistance that arrives after the due date can ask to move the due date up to 5 days later (RCW 59.18.170(3)).
  • Some cities (Seattle, Tacoma and others) and King County add their own rules; NOHM hasn’t covered them.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, atg.wa.gov

10-day notice to comply or vacate

States the breach and its facts: fix it within 10 days, or move out.

When
The tenant broke a material lease term or a duty the law sets (not one for money).
The law
RCW 59.12.030(4); RCW 59.18.650(2)(b); RCW 59.18.650(6); RCW 59.12.040; RCW 1.12.040
Good to know
  • The breach must be of a material term of the lease, a material program requirement of subsidized housing, or a tenant obligation the law imposes, and not an obligation for money damages (RCW 59.18.650(2)(b)).
  • State the facts in detail: the notice must let the tenant respond and prepare a defense (RCW 59.18.650(6)). A subtenant in possession must be served too (RCW 59.12.030(4)).
  • Waste, nuisance, unlawful activity, or substantial or repeated and unreasonable interference allows a 3-day notice to quit with no chance to cure (RCW 59.18.650(2)(c)). Four or more violations in 12 months, each with a written warning notice, allow a 60-day notice to end the tenancy at the end of the term (RCW 59.18.650(2)(n)). Ask a lawyer for those.
  • When a copy is mailed, NOHM adds the five days RCW 59.12.040 allows.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov

90-day notice to end a tenancy for cause

Ends a tenancy for a cause the law allows, with its facts, at least 90 days out.

When
The initial or fixed term is ending, you or family will move in, you’re selling, or another cause RCW 59.18.650 lists.
The law
RCW 59.18.650(1)–(2); RCW 59.18.650(6); RCW 59.12.040; RCW 1.12.040
Good to know
  • Washington has no no-cause ending: a landlord may end a periodic tenancy or refuse to continue one only for a cause RCW 59.18.650 lists (RCW 59.18.650(1)(a)). Write the cause and its facts in the reason.
  • NOHM gives 90 days, which covers the causes that need 60 or 90: the end of an initial lease of 6 to 12 months that rolls into month-to-month, or of a fixed term of 12 months or more that doesn’t roll over (60 days, RCW 59.18.650(1)(b)–(c)); the owner or family moving in, or the owner selling a single-family home (90 days, RCW 59.18.650(2)(d)–(e)); another legitimate business reason (60 days before the end of the period, RCW 59.18.650(2)(m)).
  • Demolition, substantial rehabilitation, a change of use or a condominium conversion needs 120 days (RCW 59.18.200(2)); don’t use this notice for them.
  • A fixed term can’t be ended early for the owner moving in, a sale or demolition unless you and the tenant agree in writing and the tenant has at least 60 days to leave (RCW 59.18.650(5)).
  • Washington has no separate demand for possession: this notice is the demand. If the tenant stays after the date, file for unlawful detainer.
  • Ending a tenancy without a lawful cause costs the greater of the tenant’s damages or three months’ rent (RCW 59.18.650(4)). Local just-cause and relocation rules (Seattle, Tacoma, Burien and others) can be stricter; NOHM hasn’t covered them.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov

Court papers.

Eviction summons and complaint for unlawful detainer Court

The statute’s Eviction Summons (Residential) and your complaint, for the superior court.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid, complied or left.
The law
RCW 59.18.365; RCW 59.12.070; RCW 59.12.030; RCW 59.18.650; RCW 59.18.410
Good to know
  • Washington has no statewide court form for an eviction. The summons is in the words RCW 59.18.365(3) sets; the complaint states the facts, describes the premises and, for unpaid rent, the amount (RCW 59.12.070). File in the superior court of the county where the rental is (RCW 59.12.050).
  • Set the response date at least 7 and no more than 30 days after the summons is served (RCW 59.12.070). Have the summons and complaint served by someone other than you, after the notice has run out; you may serve before filing (say so on the summons).
  • If the tenant responds, ask the court for an order to show cause; the hearing is 7 to 30 days after it is served (RCW 59.18.370), and the court may order the writ of restitution there (RCW 59.18.380). The sheriff serves the writ and waits 3 days to execute it (RCW 59.18.390); in a rent case, not until 5 court days after judgment (RCW 59.18.410(2)).
  • You need a bond only for a writ before final judgment (RCW 59.18.380). Give the sheriff the statutory Request for Storage of Personal Property form to serve with the writ (RCW 59.18.312(6)); NOHM doesn’t draw it.
  • A judgment can include rent, damages, late fees due under the lease up to $75 in all, costs and attorney’s fees (RCW 59.18.410(1)); no attorney’s fees on a default, or when the rent awarded is no more than the greater of two months’ rent or $1,200 (RCW 59.18.290(3)). Low-income tenants have a right to an appointed lawyer (RCW 59.18.640).
  • A company (an LLC or a corporation) needs a lawyer in superior court (Dutch Village Mall v. Pelletti, 2011). NOHM read only summaries of that case; ask the clerk or a lawyer.
  • Attach the notice, the proof you served it, and the rental agreement.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
RCW 59.18.260
Good to know
  • You may not collect a deposit unless the rental agreement is in writing and you give the tenant this checklist at the start of the tenancy, signed and dated by both of you, with a copy for the tenant; without it, you are liable to the tenant for the amount of the deposit (RCW 59.18.260). Describe condition and cleanliness on every row.
  • Deductions can’t be taken for anything whose condition wasn’t documented here (RCW 59.18.280(1)(c)).
  • Put the deposit in a trust account in Washington, give a written receipt, and tell the tenant in writing the name, address and location of the depository (RCW 59.18.270). On the tenant’s written request, deposits, fees and last month’s rent can be paid in installments (RCW 59.18.610).
  • Also at signing: the fire safety and smoke detection notice, signed by both of you (RCW 59.18.060(12)); the Department of Health’s mold information (RCW 59.18.060(14)); the landlord’s name and address (RCW 59.18.060(16)); for leases entered into after December 31, 2026, the flood disclosure (NOHM’s form, RCW 59.18.060(13)); and the federal lead-based paint disclosure for homes built before 1978.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov

Flood disclosure

The three things the law says to tell a tenant about flood risk and insurance.

When
At lease signing, for leases entered into after December 31, 2026.
The law
RCW 59.18.060(13)
Good to know
  • Required for leases entered into after December 31, 2026 (RCW 59.18.060(13)(b)). Give it with the lease, and keep the tenant’s signed copy.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter (1 day to show the unit).
The law
RCW 59.18.150(6)
Good to know
  • Give at least two days’ written notice (one day to show the unit to prospective or actual purchasers or tenants), and enter only at reasonable times (RCW 59.18.150(6)). The window you give is the earliest and latest possible times of entry.
  • The notice must give a phone number the tenant can call to object or reschedule: put it in your address and phone.
  • You may enter to inspect, make necessary or agreed repairs, alterations or improvements, supply services, or show the unit to purchasers, mortgagees, tenants, workers or contractors (RCW 59.18.150(1)). No notice is needed in an emergency or after abandonment (RCW 59.18.150(5)).

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov

Rent and fee increase notice

The statute’s notice of a rent increase: the new total, and that it’s within the state limit or which exemption you claim.

When
At least 90 days before the new rent starts.
The law
RCW 59.18.140(3)(a)
Good to know
  • Give at least 90 days’ written notice, and the increase can’t take effect before the rental agreement’s term ends (RCW 59.18.140(3)(a)); 30 days for a subsidized tenancy whose rent is based on income (RCW 59.18.140(3)(b)). Serve it as RCW 59.12.040 says.
  • Washington requires this notice in substantially the form RCW 59.18.720(3) sets; NOHM prints its words. It counts rent and every other recurring or periodic charge together.
  • No increase in the first 12 months of a tenancy, and in any 12 months no more than 7% plus inflation or 10%, whichever is less (RCW 59.18.700(1)). The Department of Commerce publishes the figure each year (reported as 9.683% for 2026 and 10% for 2027; NOHM couldn’t confirm either from the statute). NOHM stops anything over 10%.
  • An exemption must be one RCW 59.18.710 lists, with its facts stated or documents attached. Owner-occupancy exemptions don’t apply to a REIT, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member.
  • A tenant can demand the excess be fixed, or end the tenancy with 20 days’ notice before the increase; overcharging can cost up to three months of the excess and penalties from the Attorney General (RCW 59.18.700). Seattle and other cities may require more notice.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, commerce.wa.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
RCW 59.18.280(1)
Good to know
  • Give it, with any refund and the documentation, within 30 days after the rental agreement ends and the tenant vacates; if the tenant abandoned the home, within 30 days after you learn of it (RCW 59.18.280(1)).
  • You can’t deduct for ordinary wear and tear, for carpet cleaning unless documented wear beyond ordinary, for anything the move-in checklist didn’t document, or more than the cost of repairing the damaged part (RCW 59.18.280(1)(c)).
  • Late, or without the checklist, you owe the whole deposit; an intentional refusal can cost up to twice the deposit (RCW 59.18.280(2), 59.18.260). Sue for damages beyond the deposit within 3 years (RCW 59.18.280(3)(c)).
  • Nonrefundable fees count as a deposit unless the written lease says they’re nonrefundable (RCW 59.18.285).

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
A tenant who owed rent abandoned the home and left property.
The law
RCW 59.18.310(2)
Good to know
  • This is for a tenant who defaulted in rent and, by words or actions, showed they won’t resume the tenancy (RCW 59.18.310(1)). Mail it first class to the last known address and any other address you know (RCW 59.18.310(2)).
  • The sale or disposal can be no sooner than 45 days after the notice is mailed or delivered; 7 days if everything left is worth $250 or less, except personal papers, family pictures and keepsakes (RCW 59.18.310(2)). Hold any excess from a sale for a year.
  • After a writ of restitution, RCW 59.18.312 governs instead (storage on the tenant’s request; 30 days’ notice of sale). For a sole tenant who died, RCW 59.18.595.

Read the law: app.leg.wa.gov, app.leg.wa.gov
