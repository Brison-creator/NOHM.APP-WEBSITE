Washington 14-day notice to pay rent or vacate

The statute’s own form: pay the rent due in 14 days, or move out.

At a glance.

  • WhenRent is past due (any time after it’s due).
  • The lawRCW 59.12.030(3); RCW 59.18.057(1); RCW 59.12.040; RCW 59.18.650(2)(a); RCW 1.12.040
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Washington requires this notice in substantially the form RCW 59.18.057 sets; NOHM prints its words. The Attorney General has it in other languages at www.atg.wa.gov/landlord-tenant (RCW 59.18.058).
  • Serve it any time after the rent is due (RCW 59.12.030(3)). Fill in where to pay: the form names the landlord and the address.
  • When any copy is mailed, five more days are allowed before you can file, so NOHM’s date is 19 days out. Mail it from within Washington. Certified mail isn’t required for notices served on or after June 11, 2026.
  • Prove service with a declaration or affidavit of the person who served it.
  • Payments go to rent first (RCW 59.18.283). A late fee can’t be charged for rent paid within 5 days of its due date (RCW 59.18.170(2)), and a judgment can include no more than $75 in late fees (RCW 59.18.410(1)).
  • A tenant whose main income is monthly government assistance that arrives after the due date can ask to move the due date up to 5 days later (RCW 59.18.170(3)).
  • Some cities (Seattle, Tacoma and others) and King County add their own rules; NOHM hasn’t covered them.
