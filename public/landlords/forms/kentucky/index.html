Notices before court.

7-day notice of nonpayment and intent to terminate

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid and the lease ends if it isn’t paid within 7 days of getting the notice.

When
Rent is past due (Kentucky has no grace period).
The law
KRS 383.660(2); KRS 383.560(3)(c); KRS 446.030(1)(a)
Good to know
  • These rules apply where the city or county adopted the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (KRS 383.500). There is no official list; advocates list Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville. Ask your city or county.
  • Outside those areas no statute sets a notice to pay rent: the lease decides. The court’s complaint form (AOC-216) still asks the date you gave written notice to vacate, so serve this notice anyway; NOHM never gives less than 7 days.
  • Rent is due without demand when the lease says; Kentucky has no grace period (KRS 383.565(2)). The 7th day moves to the next business day if it falls on a weekend or legal holiday (KRS 446.030(1)(a)).
  • Taking rent while knowing of the default waives your right to end the lease for it, unless you both agree otherwise after the default (KRS 383.675).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or send it by registered or certified mail (KRS 383.560(3)(c)); regular mail and posting aren’t ways the Act allows. NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.

15-day notice of breach and termination

Says what the tenant did wrong; the lease ends on the date in it, at least 15 days out, unless they fix it first.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease (other than rent).
The law
KRS 383.660(1); KRS 383.560(3)(c); KRS 446.030(1)(a)
Good to know
  • These rules apply where the city or county adopted the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (KRS 383.500). There is no official list; advocates list Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville. Ask your city or county.
  • The statute sets the termination date at least 14 days after receipt, and also says the agreement ends if the breach isn’t remedied in 15 days (KRS 383.660(1)). NOHM uses 15 days, which meets both, and lets the tenant remedy it up to that date.
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the notice must specify the acts and omissions that are the breach.
  • If substantially the same act or omission recurs within 6 months, the Act lets you end the agreement on at least 14 days’ written notice specifying the breach and the date, with no chance to remedy it (KRS 383.660(1)). NOHM’s notice always offers the remedy; giving more than the law requires is allowed.
  • A tenant who refuses you lawful access can be given this notice too (KRS 383.700(1)).
  • A notice to vacate substantially based on acts that violated a tenant’s protective order, or led to one, is a defense to eviction (KRS 383.300(3)(b)).
  • Outside the Act’s areas no statute sets this notice: the lease decides. The complaint form (AOC-216) has an “Other violation of lease” box and asks the date of your written notice to vacate.
  • Hand it to the tenant, or send it by registered or certified mail (KRS 383.560(3)(c)); regular mail and posting aren’t ways the Act allows. NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rental date at least 30 days after the tenant gets it.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
KRS 383.695(2); KRS 383.195
Good to know
  • These rules apply where the city or county adopted the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (KRS 383.500). There is no official list; advocates list Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville. Ask your city or county.
  • Outside those areas a tenancy at will ends on one month’s written notice requiring the tenant to remove (KRS 383.195). NOHM’s 30 days ending a rental period is at least a month in nearly every case; if the date it gives is before the same day next month, pick the next period end.
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ written notice (KRS 383.695(1)). A tenancy that began when a written lease ended needs 10 days’ written notice, and none if the tenant fails to pay rent within 10 days after it is due (KRS 383.695(3)).
  • If the tenant stays willfully and not in good faith, you may recover up to 3 months’ rent or three times your actual damages, whichever is greater, and reasonable attorney’s fees (KRS 383.695(4)).
  • You can’t end a tenancy in retaliation (KRS 383.705): a tenant’s complaint within the year before can make the court presume it was.
  • A lease for a fixed term ends on its own date; for a tenant who stays, use NOHM’s notice to vacate before you file.
  • Hand it to the tenant, or send it by registered or certified mail (KRS 383.560(3)(c)); regular mail and posting aren’t ways the Act allows. NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
KRS 383.200(3); KRS 383.695(4)
Good to know
  • Kentucky sets no period for this notice, but the court’s complaint form (AOC-216) asks the date you gave the tenant written notice to vacate: give one before you file. NOHM fills in 7 days.
  • NOHM’s rent, breach and month-to-month notices are written notices to vacate too. Use this one when a lease for a fixed term ran out, or a tenancy ended another way.
  • Outside the Act’s areas, after a lease of less than a year ends you may recover possession without demand or notice if you file within 30 days after it ends; if you don’t, you can’t file until 60 days after it ended. For a lease of a year or more it is 90 days, then one year (KRS 383.160). The notice is good practice either way.
  • Hand it to the tenant or send it by registered or certified mail, and keep proof.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (AOC-216, AOC-215, AOC-220) Court

Everything the forcible detainer complaint (AOC-216) asks for, filled in from the lease, with where to get each court form and the steps and deadlines after filing.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
KRS 383.200(3); KRS 383.210; KRS 383.240; KRS 383.245; KRS 383.255
Good to know
  • Kentucky’s District Courts use the AOC’s forcible detainer forms. File AOC-216 filled in from this worksheet; this worksheet isn’t filed.
  • AOC-216 is sworn: sign it in front of the clerk or a notary. It certifies you are the owner or the attorney.
  • A business (an LLC or a corporation) must have an attorney file the complaint: Kentucky’s Court of Appeals has held one filed by a non-lawyer, a property manager included, is void. NOHM read only summaries of those cases.
  • Under the Act a notice by mail counts when it is mailed by registered or certified mail (KRS 383.560(3)(c)); NOHM counts from the date you enter.

Move-in.

Move-in damage listing

The list of damage already in the home, with what each would cost to repair, signed by you and the tenant before the deposit is paid.

When
Before the tenant pays the deposit: list the damage and its estimated repair cost, let them inspect, and both sign.
The law
KRS 383.580(2)
Good to know
  • Required where the Act applies: give the tenant this listing of existing damage, with the estimated cost of repairing each, before they pay the deposit; let them inspect; both sign (KRS 383.580(2)). Without it, and the move-out listing, you may not keep any of the deposit (§ 383.580(4)). This is a rule of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, which applies only where the city or county adopted it (KRS 383.500): Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville, by advocates’ lists (there is no official one). Ask your city or county.
  • Outside the Act’s areas it isn’t required; it is still good practice.
  • Also required under the Act, in writing at or before the start of the tenancy: who manages the home and who receives legal papers for the owner (KRS 383.585), and where the separate deposit account is, with its number (§ 383.580(1)). Use NOHM’s two disclosures.
  • Statewide: a lease can’t penalize a tenant for calling the police or emergency help (KRS 383.302). Under the Act a lease can’t make the tenant waive their rights, confess judgment, pay your attorney’s fees, or excuse you from liability (KRS 383.570).
  • A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

Disclosure of manager and owner

Tells the tenant in writing who manages the home and who receives legal papers and notices for the owner.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, and again whenever it changes.
The law
KRS 383.585
Good to know
  • Required in writing at or before the start of the tenancy, and kept current (KRS 383.585(1)–(2)). This is a rule of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, which applies only where the city or county adopted it (KRS 383.500): Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville, by advocates’ lists (there is no official one). Ask your city or county.
  • Anyone who fails to give it becomes your agent for service of process and for receiving notices and demands (KRS 383.585(3)).

Notice of security deposit account

Tells the tenant where the separate account holding their deposit is, and its number.

When
When you take a security deposit, before the tenant moves in.
The law
KRS 383.580(1)
Good to know
  • Required where the Act applies: keep every deposit in an account used only for deposits, at a bank or other lending institution regulated by Kentucky or a federal agency, and tell the tenant where the account is and its number (KRS 383.580(1)). This is a rule of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, which applies only where the city or county adopted it (KRS 383.500): Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville, by advocates’ lists (there is no official one). Ask your city or county.
  • Without the separate account, and the move-in and move-out damage listings, you may not keep any part of the deposit (KRS 383.580(4)).
  • Kentucky caps no deposit and requires no interest on it.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter.
The law
KRS 383.615(3)
Good to know
  • Give at least 2 days’ notice and enter only at reasonable times, except in an emergency or where notice is impracticable (KRS 383.615(2)–(3)). This is a rule of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, which applies only where the city or county adopted it (KRS 383.500): Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville, by advocates’ lists (there is no official one). Ask your city or county.
  • Outside the Act’s areas no statute sets entry notice: your lease decides. NOHM uses 2 days anyway.
  • Don’t abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant (KRS 383.615(3)). If the tenant is away more than 7 days, you may enter at times reasonably necessary (§ 383.670(2)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Kentucky statute sets the notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date: under the Act a month-to-month can’t be ended on less (KRS 383.695(2)), and elsewhere on less than a month (§ 383.195).
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • No statewide rent cap; local governments can’t control rents on private property (KRS 65.875).
  • Under the Act you can’t raise rent to retaliate against a tenant (KRS 383.705).

Move-out.

Move-out damage listing and deposit statement

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
When the tenant moves out: inspect, list the damage with its estimated repair cost, and both sign.
The law
KRS 383.580(3)
Good to know
  • Required where the Act applies: when the tenant leaves, inspect the home and list any damage you’ll charge against the deposit, with the estimated cost of repairing it; the tenant may inspect, and you both sign. A tenant who disagrees lists the items they dissent from and signs that (KRS 383.580(3)). This is a rule of the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, which applies only where the city or county adopted it (KRS 383.500): Louisville/Jefferson County, Lexington/Fayette County, Oldham and Pulaski Counties, and cities including Covington, Newport, Florence, Bellevue, Dayton, Georgetown, Shelbyville and Barbourville, by advocates’ lists (there is no official one). Ask your city or county.
  • Without this listing, the move-in listing and the separate account, you may not keep any of the deposit (KRS 383.580(4)). The tenant can sue only over the items they dissented from (§ 383.580(5)).
  • Kentucky sets no deadline to return it. If a refund is due, send notice of the amount to the tenant’s last known address; if they don’t respond within 60 days, you may take it from the account and keep it (KRS 383.580(7)). If they left without paying the last month’s rent and don’t ask for the deposit, you may apply it to the debt after 30 days (§ 383.580(6)).
  • Outside the Act’s areas no statute governs deposits: the lease does. This statement is still good practice.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • No Kentucky statute covers a tenant’s belongings left behind. NOHM suggests holding them at least 14 days after this notice; the date is yours to set.
  • Use it only after the tenancy has ended and the tenant has left: never while they still have possession. Photograph the property before you move it.
  • The squatter law that lets an owner move an unlawful occupant’s belongings to the property line doesn’t apply to tenants or former tenants (KRS 383.290).
