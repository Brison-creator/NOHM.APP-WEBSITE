Notices before court.

14-day notice of possible eviction (rent not paid)

The notice Minnesota requires before an eviction for unpaid rent or charges: the amounts, who to pay, and 14 days to pay or move.

When
Rent or another charge under the lease is due and unpaid (no grace period by statute).
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subd. 1a; Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subd. 1(d); Minn. Stat. § 645.15
Good to know
  • Under Payment, give the name and address of the person authorized to receive rent and fees for you: the law requires it.
  • Date the notice the day you deliver or mail it: the 14 days run from delivery or mailing, and the notice tells the tenant they run “from the date of this notice”. Mail counts from the day you mail it, so NOHM adds no days.
  • Some cities (Minneapolis and St. Paul among them) require a longer notice; follow the longer one.
  • Attach a copy of this notice to your eviction complaint: without it the court must dismiss the case and expunge it (Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subd. 1(d)).
  • After you file, the tenant can still pay what’s owed, with interest, costs and a $5 attorney’s fee, until possession is delivered (§ 504B.291).
  • If your lease requires a digital payment platform, keep another way to pay: a tenant can’t be evicted or charged a late fee while both are down (§ 504B.118).

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov

Notice of lease violation (the lease sets the terms)

Tells the tenant which lease term they broke and when you’ll end the lease, on the notice the lease itself sets.

When
The tenant broke a lease term that the lease lets you evict for.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.285, subd. 1; Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subd. 3
Good to know
  • Minnesota sets no notice before an eviction for breach of the lease: your lease decides. Follow any notice, and any chance to fix it, that the lease gives, and set the date to match.
  • You can evict for a breach only if the lease has a clause giving you the right to evict for that conduct. Describe the lease clause broken, what the tenant did and when: your complaint must identify them, and the eviction clause (Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subd. 3).
  • Illegal drugs, prostitution, unlawful firearms or stolen property on the premises end the tenant’s right to possession with no notice (§ 504B.171), and an expedited hearing is available.
  • You can’t penalize or end a tenancy for the tenant’s conduct away from the premises unless it is a crime of violence against a tenant, guest, landlord or staff, or a conviction for one (§ 504B.171, subd. 2a).
  • If you serve a notice within 90 days after the tenant used a legal right (a complaint to a government agency, a repair request), you must prove it wasn’t retaliation (Minn. Stat. § 504B.285, subd. 2).

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov

Notice to quit (one rental period, month-to-month)

Ends a tenancy at will with written notice of at least the time between rent due dates.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.135; Minn. Stat. § 504B.147, subd. 3; Minn. Stat. § 645.15
Good to know
  • For monthly rent the notice is one month; for weekly rent, one week; never more than three months (Minn. Stat. § 504B.135).
  • The statute doesn’t require the date to end a rental period. Older court decisions (NOHM didn’t check them) may; ending it on the day before rent is due is the safest choice.
  • Under a lease, your notice to quit or not to renew can’t be shorter than the notice the lease asks of the tenant (§ 504B.147, subd. 3). To enforce an automatic renewal clause, remind the tenant in writing 15 to 30 days before their notice is due (§ 504B.145).
  • Minnesota has no separate demand for possession: a lease that ran out needs no notice, and a tenancy at will ends with this one (§ 504B.285, subd. 1). Attach it to your complaint (§ 504B.321, subd. 3).
  • No statute says how to serve it; hand delivery is the surest. NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.
  • If you serve a notice within 90 days after the tenant used a legal right (a complaint to a government agency, a repair request), you must prove it wasn’t retaliation (Minn. Stat. § 504B.285, subd. 2).

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov

Court papers.

Eviction action complaint Court

The eviction filing, with the facts and attachments Minnesota requires, and your verification.

When
The 14-day notice or the notice to quit has run, the lease has ended, or the tenant breached it, and they haven’t left.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.285, subd. 1; Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subds. 1, 1a, 3
Good to know
  • File it in the district court of the county where the rental is (Housing Court in Hennepin and Ramsey). The court issues the summons, with the statements on legal help and financial help that the law requires.
  • Attach the lease and its addenda; for nonpayment, the 14-day notice (without it the court must dismiss the case and expunge it); and any notice to quit (Minn. Stat. § 504B.321, subds. 1(d), 3). The court must dismiss and expunge a case that doesn’t comply (subd. 5).
  • Never name a tenant’s minor child as a defendant (§ 504B.213).
  • The appearance is 7 to 14 days after the summons issues. Serve the summons and complaint at least 7 days before it by personal service, or substitute service at the home; mail and post only after two tries on different days, one between 6 and 10 p.m.; file the affidavits of service at least 3 days before. If you usually email or text the tenant, also send them a dated electronic notice of the hearing at least 7 days before (§ 504B.332).
  • If you win, the writ of recovery issues, usually stayed up to 7 days; the tenant has 15 days to appeal; the officer gives 24 hours to leave (§§ 504B.345, 504B.365, 504B.371).
  • In a nonpayment case the tenant can pay what’s owed, with interest, costs and a $5 attorney’s fee, until possession is delivered, unless you also claimed a material breach (§ 504B.291).
  • A management agent or other non-lawyer may bring the case for you, but not try it to a jury or appeal it (§ 481.02, subd. 3). Hennepin and Ramsey ask for a Power of Authority (HOU103), and a company may need a lawyer to sign there (not confirmed by NOHM).
  • The court’s own form, HOU102 Eviction Action Complaint, covers the same ground; NOHM couldn’t open the court’s site to compare it. Sign the verification in front of a notary.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Minnesota doesn’t require a move-in checklist; it is good practice, and helps if the deposit is disputed.
  • Minnesota does require you to tell the tenant, at the start of the tenancy or within 14 days after they move in, that they may ask for an initial inspection, and before move-out, that they may ask for a move-out inspection and be there (Minn. Stat. § 504B.182). NOHM has the notice.
  • Other papers Minnesota requires: the name and address of the manager and of the owner or agent for service, in the lease or in writing before the tenancy starts, and posted (§ 504B.181; NOHM has it); any outstanding inspection or condemnation orders, before the tenant signs or pays (§ 504B.195); a written lease for a building of 12 or more units, and a copy to each signer (§§ 504B.111, 504B.115); on the lease’s first page, the start and end dates, any prorated rent, the total monthly payment and whether utilities are included (§§ 504B.120, 504B.146); with pet fees, that they don’t apply to service or support animals (§ 504B.113); a receipt for cash rent (§ 504B.118); and the federal lead disclosure for homes built before 1978.

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov

Notice of the right to an inspection

Tells the tenant they may ask for an inspection at move-in, or at move-out and be there for it.

When
At the start of the tenancy or within 14 days after move-in; again before move-out.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.182
Good to know
  • Give the move-in notice at the start of the tenancy, or within 14 days after the tenant moves in; give the move-out notice, in writing, before the tenant moves out (Minn. Stat. § 504B.182).
  • A move-out inspection is held no earlier than 5 days before the tenant moves out. You and the tenant may agree to dated photos or video instead.
  • Not giving these notices can cost the amount you withhold from the deposit, plus interest (§ 504B.178, subd. 4).

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov

Disclosure of manager and owner

Names who manages the rental and who accepts legal papers for the owner, as Minnesota requires before you can sue for rent or possession.

When
In the lease, or in writing before the tenancy starts; and posted on the premises.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.181, subd. 1
Good to know
  • Give it to the tenant in writing, in the lease or before the tenancy starts, and post it in a conspicuous place on the premises. Without it you can’t sue for rent or possession until it is given (Minn. Stat. § 504B.181, subd. 4).
  • Post a notice too that the Attorney General’s statement of landlord and tenant rights is available (§ 504B.181).
  • Update it when the manager, owner or agent changes.

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.211, subd. 2
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ notice, with a time or a window of time, and enter only between 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. unless the tenant agrees otherwise (Minn. Stat. § 504B.211). The tenant can’t waive this.
  • A reasonable business purpose includes showing the unit to prospective tenants (during the notice period), buyers or insurers; maintenance; a code inspection; a disturbance; a reasonable belief of a lease violation in the unit; an unauthorized occupant; or a vacated unit.
  • Without notice you may enter only to prevent injury or damage, check on someone’s safety, or comply with an ordinance on unlawful activity; if the tenant isn’t home, leave a written note saying you entered. A violation can cost up to $500 each (§ 504B.211, subds. 4–6).

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • Minnesota sets no general notice for a rent increase. For a month-to-month, give at least what ending the tenancy would take, one rental period (Minn. Stat. § 504B.135); NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date.
  • Under a lease, change the rent only as the lease allows, and never on shorter notice than the lease asks of the tenant to quit (§ 504B.147, subd. 3).
  • Minnesota has no statewide cap; a city may control rents only with the voters’ approval (§ 471.9996). St. Paul has rent stabilization: check it if your rental is there.
  • A rent increase in retaliation is a defense to an eviction (§ 504B.285, subd. 3).

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 21 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.178, subd. 3
Good to know
  • Within three weeks after the tenancy ends and you have the tenant’s mailing address or delivery instructions, return the deposit with 1% simple interest, or give this statement of the specific reasons for keeping any of it. Mailing it first class within that time counts. If the building was condemned, it is 5 days (Minn. Stat. § 504B.178, subd. 3).
  • You must prove each deduction. Not giving the statement, or not giving the inspection notices (§ 504B.182), costs the amount withheld plus interest, on top of the deposit; keeping it in bad faith can add up to $500 (subds. 4, 7).
  • A tenant can’t use the deposit as the last month’s rent, except on a month-to-month with no notice served (subd. 8). Minnesota sets no cap on deposits.

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov

Notice of sale of abandoned property

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
The tenant abandoned the home and left things behind: at least 14 days before you sell them.
The law
Minn. Stat. § 504B.271, subd. 1
Good to know
  • Give it at least 14 days before the sale: in person, or by first class AND certified mail (the 14 days start when you mail it); and post it on the premises at least two weeks before (Minn. Stat. § 504B.271).
  • The sale can be no sooner than 28 days after you learn of the abandonment, or it reasonably appears the tenant abandoned the home, whichever is later.
  • If the tenant asks in writing for their property, return it within 24 hours (48 hours, not counting weekends and holidays, if you stored it elsewhere). Withholding it can cost up to twice their actual damages or $1,000.
  • After an eviction, don’t use this: the officer’s procedure applies (§ 504B.365, subd. 3).

Read the law: revisor.mn.gov, revisor.mn.gov
