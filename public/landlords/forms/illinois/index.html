Notices before court.

5-day notice to pay rent

Demands the rent owed: pay it in full within 5 days, or the lease is terminated.

When
Rent is past due (Illinois has no grace period).
The law
735 ILCS 5/9-209; 735 ILCS 5/9-211; 5 ILCS 70/1.11
Good to know
  • The Illinois Supreme Court’s Notice of Termination for Unpaid Rent (ATJ 3502.3) is the courts’ version of this notice; NOHM’s carries the statute’s required words, the FULL PAYMENT sentence printed prominently (735 ILCS 5/9-209).
  • Demand rent only. From January 1, 2027, you can’t charge a fee for an eviction notice (765 ILCS 705/35(c)(4)).
  • If the tenant pays the full amount within the period, the lease goes on. A partial payment doesn’t stop the notice unless you agree in writing to continue the lease.
  • Mailed certified or registered: the period starts when it’s delivered, so NOHM adds 3 days. Post it only if no one is living there.
  • Whoever serves it fills out the Supreme Court’s Affidavit of Service of a Demand or Notice (ATJ 3507.3); you attach it to the eviction complaint.
  • If it isn’t paid, file the eviction the day after the period ends; no further notice or demand is needed (735 ILCS 5/9-209).
  • Chicago, suburban Cook County and other cities may require more.

10-day notice of termination (lease violation)

Ends the lease for a default in its terms and tells the tenant to leave in 10 days.

When
The tenant broke a lease term other than paying rent.
The law
735 ILCS 5/9-210; 735 ILCS 5/9-211; 5 ILCS 70/1.11
Good to know
  • This is the form of notice 735 ILCS 5/9-210 sets out. Describe the default so it reads in the sentence “in consequence of your default in ___ of the premises”, for example “keeping a dog, against the lease’s no-pets clause,”.
  • Illinois gives no right to cure. If your lease or a local ordinance (Chicago’s, for one) gives the tenant one, the notice must say so and give at least 10 days to fix it; ask a lawyer, or use the Supreme Court’s form (ATJ 3503.3), which has a box for it.
  • You or your agent must sign it (735 ILCS 5/9-210). Whoever serves it fills out the Affidavit of Service of a Demand or Notice (ATJ 3507.3).
  • Using the premises for a felony or Class A misdemeanor lets you void the lease; the tenant then has 5 days after a written notice to vacate (735 ILCS 5/9-120). Ask a lawyer.
  • Watch for defenses: domestic or sexual violence (735 ILCS 5/9-106.2), immigration status (§ 9-106.3), and retaliation (765 ILCS 721).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy at the end of a rental period at least 30 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
735 ILCS 5/9-207(b); 735 ILCS 5/9-211
Good to know
  • A tenancy for any term under a year other than week-to-week ends on 30 days’ written notice (735 ILCS 5/9-207(b)). The Illinois courts’ instructions start the period at the beginning of the next rental month, so NOHM ends it on the last day of a rental period at least 30 days out.
  • Week-to-week: 7 days (§ 9-207(a)). Year-to-year: 60 days before the end of the year (§ 9-205).
  • No further demand is needed before you file once this notice ends the tenancy (735 ILCS 5/9-208), and none when a fixed-term lease runs out (§ 9-213), so NOHM has no separate demand for possession in Illinois.
  • To claim double rent from a tenant who wilfully holds over, you need a written demand for possession (735 ILCS 5/9-202): ask a lawyer.
  • Local law may require more. From the Illinois courts’ instructions (the ordinances weren’t re-read): Chicago, 60 days if the tenant has lived there more than 6 months but less than 3 years, 120 days for more than 3 years; suburban Cook County, 60 days before a lease you won’t renew ends.
  • You can’t refuse to renew to retaliate against a tenant (765 ILCS 721/5).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (ATJ 3508.4, 3510.4, 3507.3, Eviction Order 3601.7)

Illinois courts use the Supreme Court’s statewide eviction forms: this lists them, fills in what to write on the complaint from the lease, and sets out the steps and deadlines.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
735 ILCS 5/9-106; 735 ILCS 5/9-109.6; 735 ILCS 5/9-117; Ill. S. Ct. R. 101(b)(2)
Good to know
  • Print the forms from the Illinois courts’ approved eviction forms page and copy the values from this worksheet. Every Illinois court must accept them, and the judge uses the statewide Eviction Order (735 ILCS 5/9-109.6).
  • If a corporation or LLC owns the property, it must hire a lawyer; a property manager can’t file for the owner (Illinois courts’ instructions).
  • Don’t name a minor as a defendant: the whole case is dismissed and sealed, and naming one willfully can cost $1,000 (735 ILCS 5/9-106(b)–(d)).
  • Attorney’s fees can be asked for only if the lease provides for them, local law allows them, and a licensed attorney represents you (the complaint form).
  • Cook County cases start in the Early Resolution Program, and Chicago and suburban Cook County add their own rules.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Illinois doesn’t require a move-in checklist; a signed record of the condition helps when you itemize damage from the deposit (765 ILCS 710/1).
  • Since January 1, 2026, attach the Illinois Department of Human Rights’ Summary of Rights for Safer Homes as the first page of every written lease and renewal, and have each tenant sign the acknowledgment on each page (765 ILCS 752/20).
  • Give the radon disclosure and the flood disclosure: they’re the next two forms.
  • A written lease must say that using the premises for a felony or Class A misdemeanor lets you void the lease (735 ILCS 5/9-120(a)).
  • From January 1, 2027, every non-optional fee must be on the first page of the lease, with whether utilities are included in the rent, and many fees are banned (765 ILCS 705/35).
  • Charging for master-metered utilities needs the allocation formula in writing first (765 ILCS 740/5).
  • Built before 1978: the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

Radon disclosure

The Disclosure of Information on Radon Hazards to Tenants, in the words Illinois law sets out.

When
With the lease application or before the lease is signed, with IEMA’s “Radon Guide for Tenants”.
The law
420 ILCS 46/26
Good to know
  • Give it, with the Illinois Emergency Management Agency’s pamphlet “Radon Guide for Tenants” and copies of any records or reports showing a radon hazard in the unit, at the tenant’s application or before the lease is signed (420 ILCS 46/26(a)).
  • It applies to leases entered on or after January 1, 2024, and not to units on the third story or higher above ground (420 ILCS 46/20, 46/26(g)).
  • Initial each line that applies, by hand; the tenant does the same, and both of you sign.
  • You can’t deduct radon testing or mitigation from the deposit (420 ILCS 46/30(c)).

Flood disclosure

Whether the home is in a FEMA flood zone and has flooded, in the form Illinois law sets out.

When
Before the lease or a renewal is signed; put it in the lease too.
The law
765 ILCS 705/25
Good to know
  • Since January 1, 2025, tell the tenant in writing, before they sign, whether the home is in a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area and, as far as you know, whether and how often it has flooded; for a garden, basement or first-floor unit, its flooding in the last 10 years (765 ILCS 705/25(b)–(c)).
  • The same disclosure must be in the written lease or renewal, signed by both of you (765 ILCS 705/25).
  • If you don’t, the tenant may end the lease within 30 days, with a refund within 15 days, and recover damages (705/25(e)).
  • Search FEMA’s flood map by address before you answer.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • Illinois has no statewide law on notice before entry; your lease decides. NOHM suggests at least 48 hours.
  • Local ordinances may set it: Chicago, Evanston, Oak Park and suburban Cook County require 2 days, Urbana 24 hours (from secondary sources; the ordinances weren’t re-read).
  • In an emergency you may need to go in right away; tell the tenant as soon as you can.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Illinois statute sets the notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date; ending a month-to-month itself takes 30 days’ notice (735 ILCS 5/9-207(b)).
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Illinois has no rent cap, and local governments can’t control rents (50 ILCS 825/5).
  • You can’t raise rent to retaliate against a tenant (765 ILCS 721/5); doing so can cost two months’ rent or twice the damages.
  • Chicago may require more notice (from secondary sources; not re-read).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
765 ILCS 710/1(a)
Good to know
  • To keep any of the deposit for damage, give this statement, with paid receipts or copies, within 30 days after the tenant moves out or their right of possession ends, whichever is later (765 ILCS 710/1(a)). Without it, return the whole deposit within 45 days after they move out.
  • Deliver it in person, by postmarked mail to their last known address, or by email to a verified address they gave you.
  • If you give estimates, send the paid receipts within 30 days after the statement. If you did the work yourself, you may include the reasonable cost of your labor. With no receipts, attach the cost list and your verified statement of why you can’t produce them (710/1(b)).
  • Where the lease sets a cost for cleaning or repairing a component, refer to that amount and attach that part of the lease.
  • With 25 or more units in a building or complex, interest is owed on deposits held more than 6 months (765 ILCS 715): enter it on the form.
  • Refusing in bad faith can cost twice the deposit, with costs and attorney’s fees (710/1(c)). You can’t deduct radon testing or mitigation (420 ILCS 46/30(c)).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • Illinois has no statewide law for property a tenant leaves behind; this letter isn’t a statutory notice. NOHM fills in 14 days to hold it.
  • After an eviction order, ask the sheriff’s office about removing personal property from the unit.
  • Chicago’s ordinance has its own rules. Ask a lawyer before you dispose of anything of value.
