Notices before court.

3-day notice to pay rent or vacate

Demands the rent, or the home back, at least 3 days after it’s delivered: the notice before an eviction suit.

When
Rent is past due (a late fee only once it’s two full days late).
The law
Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a); Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(f-3); Tex. Prop. Code § 24.0042
Good to know
  • A written lease can set a shorter or longer notice period (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a)); follow it.
  • A tenant who was never late before the month of this notice must get a notice to pay rent or vacate, as this one is. A tenant who was late before may get either it or a plain notice to vacate.
  • Days are calendar days, not counting the day it’s delivered; if the last day is a Saturday, Sunday or state or federal holiday, the period runs to the next day that isn’t (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.0042).
  • Deliver it one of the ways § 24.005(f-3) allows: by mail (first class, registered, certified, or a delivery service); inside the home in a conspicuous place; by hand to a tenant 16 or older; or electronically if you both agreed in writing. Posting on the outside of the door no longer counts (since January 1, 2026). NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed, for delivery.
  • If federal law requires a longer notice (the CARES Act’s 30 days for a property with a federally backed mortgage or subsidy), you may still file after this notice, but the writ of possession can’t be served until the federal period has run (§ 24.005(c-1)).

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

3-day notice to vacate (lease default)

Demands the home back at least 3 days after it’s delivered, for a default under the lease.

When
The tenant broke a lease term (other than rent).
The law
Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a); Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(f-3); Tex. Prop. Code § 24.0042
Good to know
  • Texas gives no right to cure a lease default and sets no list of breaches: the lease decides. If it promises a chance to cure or a longer notice, follow it (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a)).
  • Within 6 months after a tenant’s good-faith repair request or complaint, an eviction can be retaliation unless it rests on a ground § 92.332 allows (delinquent rent, intentional damage, threats and others).
  • A conviction for public indecency on the premises lets you end the lease by written notice within 6 months; possession returns on the 10th day after the notice (§ 91.003).
  • Days are calendar days, not counting the day it’s delivered; if the last day is a Saturday, Sunday or state or federal holiday, the period runs to the next day that isn’t (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.0042).
  • Deliver it one of the ways § 24.005(f-3) allows: by mail (first class, registered, certified, or a delivery service); inside the home in a conspicuous place; by hand to a tenant 16 or older; or electronically if you both agreed in writing. Posting on the outside of the door no longer counts (since January 1, 2026). NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed, for delivery.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

1-month notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy one month after the notice, or on a later date it names.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Tex. Prop. Code § 91.001(a)–(b); Tex. Gov’t Code § 311.014
Good to know
  • A month-to-month ends one month after the notice, or on a later date it names; it needn’t end a rental period (Tex. Prop. Code § 91.001(b), (d)). A week-to-week ends after as many days as the rent period (§ 91.001(c)).
  • A lease signed by both of you can set a different notice, or none (§ 91.001(e)); NOHM uses the lease’s if it’s longer.
  • If the tenant stays after the date, you must still give a written 3-day notice to vacate before filing (§ 24.005(a)): NOHM’s demand for possession is that notice.
  • A buyer at foreclosure gives a tenant who is current on rent at least 30 days’ written notice to vacate (§ 24.005(b)).
  • Section 91.001 sets no way to serve it; NOHM uses the ways § 24.005(f-3) allows for the 3-day notice.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a); Tex. Prop. Code § 24.002(b); Tex. Prop. Code § 24.0042
Good to know
  • A tenant who holds over after the lease’s term ends must get at least 3 days’ written notice to vacate before you file (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a)), unless the lease sets a shorter or longer period. The demand for possession must be in writing (§ 24.002(b)).
  • For a month-to-month, end the tenancy first with the one-month notice (§ 91.001); this notice then follows if the tenant stays.
  • Days are calendar days, not counting the day it’s delivered; a last day on a weekend or a state or federal holiday moves to the next day (§ 24.0042). Deliver it one of the ways § 24.005(f-3) allows.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov, tcss.legis.texas.gov

Demand to vacate for attorney’s fees (10 days before filing)

The written demand that lets you recover attorney’s fees in an eviction suit when your lease doesn’t provide for them.

When
At least 10 days before you file, if your written lease has no attorney’s-fee clause.
The law
Tex. Prop. Code § 24.006(a)
Good to know
  • Optional. You need it to recover attorney’s fees only if your written lease doesn’t entitle you to them (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.006(a)–(b)).
  • Send it by registered mail, or by certified mail, return receipt requested, at least 10 days before you file the suit.
  • It doesn’t replace the 3-day notice to vacate (§ 24.005); give that too.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

Court papers.

Sworn petition for eviction Court

The eviction filing for the justice court: the sworn petition with everything Rule 510.6 requires, and the military status statement.

When
The 3-day notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid or left.
The law
Tex. Prop. Code § 24.00505; Tex. Prop. Code § 24.0041; Tex. R. Civ. P. 510.6
Good to know
  • File it in the justice court for the precinct where the rental is (Tex. Prop. Code § 24.0041). No statewide form is required: many courts hand out the Texas Justice Court Training Center’s “Petition: Eviction Case”, and a court can’t require more than the rules do (§ 24.00506). This petition has what Rule 510.6 asks for; check the court’s current requirements, since the rewritten Rule 510 was read from the Supreme Court’s amended order and its final approval wasn’t found.
  • It must be sworn: sign it before a notary, or sign the statement that it is true and correct under penalty of perjury (Rule 510.2), as the verification here does.
  • Name every tenant on the written lease who lives there (Rule 510.6(d)), and list every home and work address you know of for them in the county: the court needs them to allow alternative service.
  • Only a sheriff or constable serves it (Rule 510.8). The trial is 10 to 21 days after you file, and at least 4 days after service (§ 24.0051(g)).
  • You, your agent or your property manager may represent you, and so may an owner, officer or employee of an LLC or corporation: no lawyer is needed in justice court (§ 24.011; Rule 510.3(b)).
  • Rent claims are capped at $20,000 (Rule 510.6(e)). The writ of possession can’t issue before the 6th day after judgment (§ 24.0061(b)); the tenant has 5 days to appeal (§ 24.005107).
  • The filing fee is set by the county; ask the court. File the military status statement too; the court can’t give a default judgment without it.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov, txcourts.gov, greggcounty.texas.gov

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Texas doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: no deduction may be made for normal wear and tear (Tex. Prop. Code § 92.104(b)), and this form records the home’s condition.
  • Give the flood disclosure at or before signing (§ 92.0135): NOHM has it. Give the tenant a copy of the lease within 3 business days after signing (§ 92.024).
  • Put these in the lease in underlined or bold print where you use them: the repair remedies (§ 92.056(g)), a surrender-notice condition on the deposit refund (§ 92.103(b)), a rekeying charge (§ 92.156(e)), the landlord’s lien (§ 54.043), and the early-termination statements on family violence, military deployment, and sexual offenses or stalking (§§ 92.016(f), 92.017(g), 92.0161(g)).
  • Rekey the locks within 7 days after each tenant moves out (§ 92.156(a)); test the smoke alarms at the start of the tenancy (§§ 92.255, 92.258). Disclose the owner and any off-site manager on request (§ 92.201), and give a 24-hour emergency number (§ 92.020).
  • Give your tenant selection criteria at application (§ 92.3515). A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

Flood disclosure notice

The notice Texas requires with every lease: whether you know the home is in a 100-year floodplain, and whether it has flooded in the last five years.

When
At or before signing the lease; you and the tenant both sign it.
The law
Tex. Prop. Code § 92.0135
Good to know
  • Give it with the lease, as a lease paragraph, an addendum, or this separate paper, at or before the lease is signed; you and the tenant must both sign it (Tex. Prop. Code § 92.0135(e)).
  • Leases under 30 days don’t need it (§ 92.0135(a-1)).
  • If you don’t give it and the tenant suffers a substantial flood loss, the tenant may end the lease (§ 92.0135(f)).
  • Check FEMA’s flood map for the address before you answer.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • No Texas statute sets notice or hours for entry; your lease does. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • In an emergency you may need to go in right away; tell the tenant as soon as you can.

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Texas statute sets notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date; for a month-to-month, a change in effect ends the old tenancy, which takes a month’s notice (Tex. Prop. Code § 91.001), so give at least a month (NOHM’s reading, not a rule the statute states).
  • During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Texas has no rent cap; a city can control rents only in a housing emergency from a disaster, with the governor’s approval (Tex. Loc. Gov’t Code § 214.902).
  • You can’t raise rent within 6 months after a tenant’s good-faith repair request or complaint in retaliation (Tex. Prop. Code § 92.331).

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov, tcss.legis.texas.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenant surrenders the home, once you have their forwarding address in writing.
The law
Tex. Prop. Code §§ 92.103, 92.104
Good to know
  • Refund the deposit with a written description and itemized list of all deductions on or before the 30th day after the tenant surrenders the home (Tex. Prop. Code §§ 92.103(a), 92.104(c)). You don’t have to until the tenant gives you a forwarding address in writing (§ 92.107).
  • No deduction for normal wear and tear (§ 92.104(b)). If the tenant owes rent and doesn’t dispute it, the itemized list isn’t required, but it is still the safe practice.
  • Mail postmarked by the 30th day is on time (§ 92.1041). You may send it by e-mail if you and the tenant have e-mailed each other before (§ 92.113).
  • Keeping a deposit in bad faith costs $100, three times what was wrongfully withheld, and attorney’s fees; not itemizing in bad faith forfeits your right to keep any of it. Missing the 30 days is presumed bad faith (§ 92.109).
  • No cap, interest or separate account. Keep accurate records (§ 92.106).

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • Texas has no general statute on property left behind: your lease’s terms on abandoned property govern. NOHM fills in 30 days to hold it, as a courtesy.
  • Seizing a tenant’s property for unpaid rent (the landlord’s lien) has its own rules: an underlined or bold lease clause, items you may not take, a notice left in the home, and a sale notice mailed 30 days ahead by first class and certified mail (Tex. Prop. Code §§ 54.041–54.045). Ask a lawyer before you do it.
  • If a sole tenant dies, follow § 92.014(c) for their property. After a writ of possession, the officer handles the tenant’s property (§ 24.0062).

Read the law: tcss.legis.texas.gov, tcss.legis.texas.gov
