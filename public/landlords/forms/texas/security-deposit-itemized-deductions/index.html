Texas security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenWithin 30 days after the tenant surrenders the home, once you have their forwarding address in writing.
  • The lawTex. Prop. Code §§ 92.103, 92.104
  • StageMove-out

What to know.

  • Refund the deposit with a written description and itemized list of all deductions on or before the 30th day after the tenant surrenders the home (Tex. Prop. Code §§ 92.103(a), 92.104(c)). You don’t have to until the tenant gives you a forwarding address in writing (§ 92.107).
  • No deduction for normal wear and tear (§ 92.104(b)). If the tenant owes rent and doesn’t dispute it, the itemized list isn’t required, but it is still the safe practice.
  • Mail postmarked by the 30th day is on time (§ 92.1041). You may send it by e-mail if you and the tenant have e-mailed each other before (§ 92.113).
  • Keeping a deposit in bad faith costs $100, three times what was wrongfully withheld, and attorney’s fees; not itemizing in bad faith forfeits your right to keep any of it. Missing the 30 days is presumed bad faith (§ 92.109).
  • No cap, interest or separate account. Keep accurate records (§ 92.106).
