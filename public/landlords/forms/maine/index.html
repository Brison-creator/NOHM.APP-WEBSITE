Notices before court.

7-day notice to quit (rent 7 days or more in arrears)

Ends a tenancy at will in 7 days for rent 7 days or more in arrears, unless the tenant pays it all first; form CV-256 goes with it.

When
Rent is 7 days or more in arrears.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6002(1)(C); 14 M.R.S. § 6002(2); 14 M.R.S. § 6001(7); M.R. Civ. P. 6(a)
Good to know
  • Serve it only once the tenant is 7 days or more in arrears (14 M.R.S. § 6002(1)(C)), and state the amount 7 or more days in arrears as of the date of the notice. An unintentional clerical error in the amount doesn’t invalidate it (§ 6002(2)(B)).
  • If the tenant pays the full rent due before the notice expires, the notice is void. After it expires, paying all arrears, the rent due to date and your filing and service fees before the writ issues reinstates the tenancy. Payment or a written assurance of payment through general assistance counts as cash (14 M.R.S. § 6002(1)).
  • Attach form CV-256 from the Judicial Branch website (courts.maine.gov) to the notice: the law requires it with every notice to terminate (14 M.R.S. § 6001(7)). You don’t write anything on it. Serve it again with the summons and complaint.
  • Try at least 3 times, in good faith, to hand it to the tenant before you mail it first class and leave it at the home (14 M.R.S. § 6002). A notice served by a sheriff, constable or other authorized server can be proved by their return (§ 6001(8)). Maine adds no days for mailing; NOHM adds 3.
  • A written lease: serve the notice the lease requires; where the lease says nothing about ending it, this notice applies (14 M.R.S. § 6001(1-B)).
  • A late fee may be charged only after rent is 15 days late, up to 4% of a month’s rent, and only if you told the tenant in writing at the start (14 M.R.S. § 6028). Collect it separately.
  • No separate demand is needed: once this notice expires, you may file for forcible entry and detainer (14 M.R.S. § 6002).

7-day notice to quit for cause

Ends a tenancy at will in 7 days for a ground the statute lists, such as substantial damage, a nuisance or violence; form CV-256 goes with it.

When
The tenant did one of the things 14 M.R.S. § 6002(1) lists.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6002(1); 14 M.R.S. § 6002(2); 14 M.R.S. § 6001(7); M.R. Civ. P. 6(a)
Good to know
  • The 7-day notice is only for the grounds 14 M.R.S. § 6002(1) lists, which you must be able to show by affirmative proof: the tenant, their family or an invitee caused substantial damage they haven’t repaired; caused or permitted a nuisance, made the unit unfit for human habitation, or violated the law regarding the tenancy; the tenant is a perpetrator of domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking against another tenant; the tenant or a guest committed or threatened violence or sexual assault against another tenant, a guest, you or your employee or agent; or the occupant isn’t authorized. A tenant who changes the lock and won’t give you a key can also be given 7 days (§ 6025(3)).
  • Name the specific ground in the details: the notice must state it (14 M.R.S. § 6002(2)).
  • For any other breach of a tenancy at will, use the 30-day notice. A 30-day and a 7-day notice may be combined in one notice (14 M.R.S. § 6002).
  • A victim of domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking may not be evicted for an incident of it at the premises treated as a nuisance, damage or lease violation (14 M.R.S. § 6001(6)).
  • No separate demand is needed: once this notice expires, you may file for forcible entry and detainer (14 M.R.S. § 6002).
  • Attach form CV-256 from the Judicial Branch website (courts.maine.gov) to the notice: the law requires it with every notice to terminate (14 M.R.S. § 6001(7)). You don’t write anything on it. Serve it again with the summons and complaint.
  • Try at least 3 times, in good faith, to hand it to the tenant before you mail it first class and leave it at the home (14 M.R.S. § 6002). A notice served by a sheriff, constable or other authorized server can be proved by their return (§ 6001(8)). Maine adds no days for mailing; NOHM adds 3.
  • A written lease: serve the notice the lease requires; where the lease says nothing about ending it, this notice applies (14 M.R.S. § 6001(1-B)).

30-day notice to quit (tenancy at will)

Ends a tenancy at will on at least 30 days’ written notice, no reason needed; form CV-256 goes with it.

When
You want a tenant at will out, no fault needed.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6002; 14 M.R.S. § 6001(7); M.R. Civ. P. 6(a)
Good to know
  • At least 30 days; the date needn’t end a rental period. If the tenant has paid rent through a later date, the notice must expire on or after that date (14 M.R.S. § 6002).
  • The tenant may waive the 30 days in writing, but only at the time the notice is given. Money you receive after giving the notice doesn’t undo it until a writ of possession issues (§ 6002).
  • No separate demand is needed: when the notice expires, the tenant is liable to forcible entry and detainer without further notice, unless they paid, after the notice, rent that accrued after the tenancy ended (14 M.R.S. § 6002).
  • A written lease that has ended: you may file without notice within 7 days after it ends (14 M.R.S. § 6001(1)). After that, NOHM treats the tenant as a tenant at will and gives this 30-day notice, the safer reading.
  • Within 6 months after a tenant takes one of the actions 14 M.R.S. § 6001(3) protects, an eviction is presumed retaliatory, unless it is for a § 6002(1) ground or a lease violation.
  • Attach form CV-256 from the Judicial Branch website (courts.maine.gov) to the notice: the law requires it with every notice to terminate (14 M.R.S. § 6001(7)). You don’t write anything on it. Serve it again with the summons and complaint.
  • Try at least 3 times, in good faith, to hand it to the tenant before you mail it first class and leave it at the home (14 M.R.S. § 6002). A notice served by a sheriff, constable or other authorized server can be proved by their return (§ 6001(8)). Maine adds no days for mailing; NOHM adds 3.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (CV-007, CV-034, CV-256) Court

Everything the Judicial Branch’s complaint for forcible entry and detainer asks for, filled in from the lease, with where to get each form and the steps and deadlines.

When
The notice to quit expired and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6001; 14 M.R.S. § 6004; 14 M.R.S. § 6005; M.R. Civ. P. 80D
Good to know
  • Maine’s District Courts use the Judicial Branch’s forms. Fill in the complaint (CV-007) from this worksheet; this worksheet isn’t filed.
  • Attach form CV-256 to the summons and complaint, as you did to the notice to quit (14 M.R.S. § 6004(2)).
  • Bring the lease, the notice to quit with its CV-256, your proof of service, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Maine doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: you can’t deduct normal wear and tear from the deposit (14 M.R.S. § 6033(1)), and this form records the home’s condition.
  • Before the tenant signs or pays a deposit, Maine requires: the radon notice in the form the Department of Health and Human Services prescribes, with the date and result of the latest test (§ 6030-D); written notice of the smoking policy, with the tenant’s written acknowledgment (§ 6030-E; NOHM’s smoking policy disclosure); the energy efficiency disclosure statement, signed by you both, where the tenant pays for energy (§ 6030-C); and the total price disclosure, signed by you both, where there are recurring fees (§ 6030-J).
  • At the start of the tenancy: written notice that a late fee of up to 4% of a month’s rent may be charged, if you’ll charge one (§ 6028(3)); and whether an adjacent unit is infested with or being treated for bedbugs (§ 6021-A).
  • Give a written receipt for every cash rent or deposit payment (§ 6022). The deposit can’t be more than 2 months’ rent (§ 6032). A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Smoking policy disclosure

Tells the tenant in writing whether smoking is prohibited, allowed, or allowed only in some areas, and records their acknowledgment.

When
Before the tenant signs the lease or pays a deposit.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6030-E
Good to know
  • Required: before a tenant enters into a lease or pays a deposit, tell them in writing whether smoking is prohibited on the property, allowed on all of it, or allowed only in limited areas (and which), and get their written acknowledgment (14 M.R.S. § 6030-E). It can be in the lease instead.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6025
Good to know
  • Give reasonable notice and enter only at reasonable times; 24 hours is presumed reasonable (14 M.R.S. § 6025(2)). The tenant can’t waive this (§ 6025(4)).
  • You may enter to inspect, make necessary or agreed repairs, decorations, alterations or improvements, supply services, or show the home to buyers, mortgagees, tenants, workers or contractors (§ 6025(1)). For bedbugs, the notice must give the reasons for and scope of the access (§ 6021-A).
  • Entering without it can cost the greater of actual damages or $250, plus attorney’s fees after a contested hearing (§ 6025(3)). An emergency, including an animal whose welfare is at risk, allows entry without 24 hours’ notice.
  • A tenant who changes the lock and won’t give you a key can be given a 7-day notice to quit (§ 6025(3)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 45 days before the new rent starts; 75 days for an increase of 10% or more.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6015
Good to know
  • Rent, or a mandatory recurring fee, may be increased only on at least 45 days’ written notice; 75 days’ for an increase of 10% or more, counting every increase in the last 12 months (14 M.R.S. § 6015). A waiver of the notice is void. Fill in the rent of 12 months ago if it changed since.
  • The 75-day rule doesn’t apply to deed-restricted affordable housing or a rent subsidy program’s housing.
  • No increase while the home violates the warranty of habitability, unless the tenant caused it (14 M.R.S. § 6016).
  • During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it. Maine has no statewide cap, and doesn’t stop cities from adding rent rules: Portland has its own.

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 21 days after a tenancy at will ends and you get the home back; under a written lease, the time it states, up to 30 days.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6033(2)
Good to know
  • Return the deposit, or this written statement itemizing why you kept any of it with the rest of the deposit, within 21 days after a tenancy at will ends or the home is surrendered and accepted, whichever is later; under a written lease, within the time it states, up to 30 days (14 M.R.S. § 6033(2)). Mailing it to the tenant’s last known address complies.
  • No deduction for normal wear and tear (§ 6033(1)). Missing the deadline forfeits your right to keep any of it (§ 6033(3)); wrongful retention costs double the amount, plus fees, after the tenant gives 7 days’ notice of a suit (§ 6034).
  • Keep the deposit in a bank account, apart from your own money and beyond your creditors’ reach; on request, tell the tenant the bank and the account number (§ 6038).
  • The deposit rules don’t apply to a unit in a building of 5 units or fewer where you live in one (§ 6037).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
14 M.R.S. § 6013
Good to know
  • Use it after the tenant has vacated, or after a judgment for you. Mail it first class with proof of mailing (a certificate of mailing) to the tenant’s last known address, with an itemized list of the items and containers of items (14 M.R.S. § 6013(2)(B)).
  • If the tenant still lives there, the notice goes to the rental unit, and can’t give less than 7 days after mailing or 48 hours after the writ is served, whichever is longer (§ 6013(2)(A)).
  • Claimed within 7 days: release it without asking for payment, and keep it at least 14 days after the notice. Not claimed: you may make its release depend on paying the arrears, damages and storage, sell it for a reasonable fair market price and apply the proceeds (any balance goes to the Treasurer of State), or dispose of property with no reasonable fair market value (§ 6013(3)–(5)).
  • The tenant may waive this in writing at or after vacating (§ 6013(6)).
