Notices before court.

7-business-day notice to pay rent or the lease ends

States the rent and late fees owed; if they aren’t paid within 7 business days after the tenant receives it, the lease ends.

When
Rent is unpaid when due (Alabama has no grace period).
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(b); Ala. Code § 35-9A-141(3); Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(d)
Good to know
  • Rent is payable without demand when the lease says. Alabama sets no grace period: you may deliver this once rent is unpaid when due.
  • The seven business days run from when the tenant receives it, not when you send it, and no Alabama law presumes a mailed notice was received (Ala. Code § 35-9A-144(e)). Hand it over and keep proof; NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.
  • The Act doesn’t define “business day”. NOHM skips Saturdays, Sundays and state and federal holidays (Ala. Code § 1-3-8), which can only make the date later. In Baldwin and Mobile Counties, Mardi Gras is also a holiday: if the count crosses it, add a day.
  • A tenant can cure a breach no more than twice in any 12 months unless you agree in writing (Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(d)).
  • Accepting rent knowing of the default waives your right to end the lease for it, unless you agree otherwise after the breach (Ala. Code § 35-9A-424).
  • Keep a copy and your proof of delivery: you attach them to the court’s default-judgment affidavit (Form C-25B).
  • Alabama has no separate demand for possession: if the rent isn’t paid, the lease ends on the date in this notice and you can file Form C-59.

7-business-day notice to remedy a lease violation

Says what the tenant did wrong; if it isn’t remedied within 7 business days after they receive it, the lease ends.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease, or a duty that affects health and safety.
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(a); Ala. Code § 35-9A-141(3); Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(d)
Good to know
  • Use it for a material noncompliance with the lease, or a noncompliance with the tenant’s duties (Ala. Code § 35-9A-301) that materially affects health and safety. Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do.
  • Some breaches can’t be cured and end the lease on a 7-day notice instead (Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(d)): illegal drugs, illegal firearm use, criminal assault on the premises, or a repeat within 6 months of a breach already cured. An intentional misrepresentation on the application can’t be cured either (§ 35-9A-421(a)). NOHM doesn’t draw that notice; ask a lawyer.
  • A tenant can cure a breach no more than twice in any 12 months unless you agree in writing (Ala. Code § 35-9A-421(d)).
  • A tenant who refuses you lawful access can be served this notice (Ala. Code § 35-9A-442(a)).
  • The seven business days run from when the tenant receives it, not when you send it, and no Alabama law presumes a mailed notice was received (Ala. Code § 35-9A-144(e)). Hand it over and keep proof; NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.
  • The Act doesn’t define “business day”. NOHM skips Saturdays, Sundays and state and federal holidays (Ala. Code § 1-3-8), which can only make the date later. In Baldwin and Mobile Counties, Mardi Gras is also a holiday: if the count crosses it, add a day.
  • Some county court pages say 14 days for other violations; the statute says seven business days.

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rental date at least 30 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out; Alabama needs no reason.
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-441(b); Ala. Code § 35-9A-141(3)
Good to know
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ written notice instead (Ala. Code § 35-9A-441(a)). A lease with a fixed term ends on its own; no notice is needed.
  • Alabama has no separate demand for possession: if the tenant stays after the date, file Form C-59 (Ala. Code § 35-9A-441(c)). If the holdover is willful and not in good faith, you may also recover up to three months’ rent or your actual damages, whichever is greater, and reasonable attorney’s fees.
  • Alabama has no statewide just-cause rule, but you can’t end a tenancy to retaliate against a tenant (Ala. Code § 35-9A-501).
  • NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it, for delivery time.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Forms C-59, C-25B)

What to write on the court’s Statement of Claim for eviction (Form C-59), filled in from the lease, with the steps and deadlines that follow.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-461; Ala. Code § 35-9A-421; Ala. Code § 35-9A-441(c); Ala. R. Civ. P. 12(dc), 55(dc), 62(dc)
Good to know
  • Alabama evictions are filed on the court’s own Form C-59, not a complaint you write. Print it from the court’s site and copy NOHM’s values onto it.
  • An individual landlord can file without a lawyer. A company (an LLC or a corporation) needs an Alabama attorney: a court held a complaint filed for a company by a non-lawyer void (Ex parte Williams, 89 So. 3d 135 (Ala. Civ. App. 2011), read from a summary NOHM couldn’t check against the official reporter).
  • Filing and service fees are set by each county; Montgomery County’s page lists $350 plus service fees per defendant, and asks you to bring a copy of the notice (a court web page, not law).
  • Don’t accept rent after the lease terminates: the default form asks you to swear you haven’t.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Alabama doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: signed by both of you, it is your record of the home’s condition when the deposit comes back.
  • Before the tenancy starts, give the tenant in writing the name and business address of the manager and of the owner or the owner’s agent for notices (Ala. Code § 35-9A-202(a)): NOHM’s disclosure form does it.
  • Give the tenant any rules and regulations when they sign, or when you adopt them; otherwise they can’t be enforced (Ala. Code § 35-9A-302(b)(6)).
  • For a home built before 1978, give the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet (42 U.S.C. § 4852d).

Disclosure of manager and owner

Tells the tenant in writing who manages the home and who receives notices and legal papers for the owner.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, and again whenever it changes.
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-202
Good to know
  • Required at or before the start of the tenancy (Ala. Code § 35-9A-202(a)). Keep it current (§ 35-9A-202(b)).
  • If it isn’t given, the person who signed the lease for the landlord becomes the landlord’s agent for service of process and for notices and demands (Ala. Code § 35-9A-202(c)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter.
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-303(c)
Good to know
  • Give at least 2 days’ notice and enter only at reasonable times (Ala. Code § 35-9A-303(c)). Posting a note on the tenant’s main entry door, with the time and purpose, is a permitted way to give it.
  • A general notice or schedule given apart from the lease, more than 2 days ahead, covers repairs, maintenance, pest control and health or safety service; the tenant may also agree to less notice (§ 35-9A-303(d)). A tenant’s repair request is consent to enter to make it (§ 35-9A-303(e)).
  • In an emergency you may enter without consent (§ 35-9A-303(b)). Showing the home in its last 4 months without consent needs a separate general notice the tenant signed, and only with the prospective tenant or buyer.
  • Abusing access can cost you an injunction, termination or actual damages (Ala. Code § 35-9A-442(b)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Alabama statute sets the notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date: a month-to-month can only be changed by ending it on 30 days’ notice to a rental date (Ala. Code § 35-9A-441(b)), so that is the safe minimum.
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Alabama has no rent cap, and cities and counties can’t control rents (Ala. Code § 11-80-8.1(b)).
  • You can’t raise rent to retaliate against a tenant who complained to a code agency or to you, or joined a tenants’ union (Ala. Code § 35-9A-501(a)).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 60 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-201
Good to know
  • Mail it within 60 days after the tenancy ends and the tenant gives back possession (Ala. Code § 35-9A-201(b)–(c)). First-class mail to the address the tenant gave in writing is enough (§ 35-9A-201(e)).
  • No forwarding address? Mail it first class to the tenant’s last known address, or if none, to the premises (§ 35-9A-201(d)).
  • Deduct only accrued rent and damage from the tenant’s breach of their duties, each on its own line.
  • Miss the 60 days and you owe the tenant double the original deposit (§ 35-9A-201(f)).
  • A deposit or check the tenant doesn’t claim within 90 days is forfeited (§ 35-9A-201(d)).
  • The deposit can’t be more than one month’s rent, except for pets, changes to the premises or added liability risk (§ 35-9A-201(a)). No interest or separate account is required.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Ala. Code § 35-9A-423(d)
Good to know
  • Alabama doesn’t require this notice. Property left in the unit more than 14 days after the tenancy terminates may be disposed of (Ala. Code § 35-9A-423(d)); NOHM counts the 14 days from this notice, which is never earlier.
  • A unit is also considered abandoned if its electric service has been off for seven days in a row (§ 35-9A-423(e)).
  • Alabama abolished distress for rent: you can’t hold the tenant’s things for unpaid rent (Ala. Code § 35-9A-425).
  • Photograph the property before you move or dispose of it.
