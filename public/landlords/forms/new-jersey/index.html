Notices before court.

Demand for payment of rent (optional)

Asks for the rent due. New Jersey needs no notice before filing for nonpayment, so this is a courtesy.

When
Rent is past due (5 business days’ grace for some seniors and benefit recipients).
The law
N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1(a); N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2
Good to know
  • New Jersey requires no notice before you file for nonpayment of rent, unless your lease does: the lease decides (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2). This demand is a courtesy, served the ways the statute allows for a notice to quit.
  • Seniors on Social Security or a government pension, and tenants on SSDI, SSI or Work First New Jersey, get 5 business days’ grace for rent due on the 1st, with no late fee in that time (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-6.1, 6.3).
  • Your complaint can claim late fees or attorney’s fees only if the written lease calls them “additional rent”, and never against Section 8 or public-housing tenants (court form CN 11252).
  • The tenant can end the case by paying the rent and costs any time before trial (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-9), and within 3 business days after a lockout for nonpayment; you must accept it and tell the court in writing within 2 business days (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-10.16a): NOHM’s form.
  • Rent a tenant used to keep a utility on, after a shutoff notice for your own nonpayment, isn’t unpaid rent (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1(a)).
  • No judgment for possession until you’re registered under the Landlord Identity Law (N.J.S.A. 46:8-33).

Notice to cease

Tells the tenant, in detail, what conduct to stop: the first step before a notice to quit for conduct or habitual late rent.

When
The tenant is disorderly, breaks your rules or a lease covenant, or habitually pays late.
The law
N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1(b), (d), (e)(1), (j); N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2
Good to know
  • New Jersey requires a written notice to cease before a notice to quit for disorderly conduct (b), breaking reasonable rules the tenant accepted in writing (d), breaking a reasonable lease covenant with a right of reentry (e), or habitual late rent (j) (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1).
  • The law sets no period: give a time that is reasonable for the conduct (Truth in Renting). Describe the conduct in detail.
  • If the tenant keeps it up, serve the notice to quit (NOHM’s form): 3 days for disorderly conduct; one month for rules, covenants or habitual late payment (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2).
  • Willful or grossly negligent damage to the premises needs no notice to cease: serve the 3-day notice to quit directly.
  • The statute doesn’t say how to serve a notice to cease; NOHM uses the notice-to-quit methods (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2).

One-month notice to quit (owner-occupied, 2 rental units or fewer)

Ends a month-to-month tenancy in a building you live in with no more than 2 rental units; nowhere else.

When
You live in the building, it has 2 or fewer rental units, and you want a month-to-month tenant out.
The law
N.J.S.A. 2A:18-56(b); N.J.S.A. 2A:18-53(a)
Good to know
  • Use this only if you live in the building and it has no more than 2 rental units (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1). For every other rental, New Jersey doesn’t let a tenancy end because the lease ran out or you want it back: you need one of the Anti-Eviction Act’s causes, each with its own notice (NOHM’s notice to quit). Ask a lawyer.
  • A month-to-month tenancy needs 1 month’s notice to quit; year-to-year, 3 months; another term, one term’s notice (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-56). NOHM couldn’t confirm whether the date must end a rental period: the day before a rent due date is the safe choice.
  • Serve it in person or leave it with a family member over 14 at the tenant’s home: certified mail isn’t listed for this notice (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-53(a)).
  • You may not end or refuse to renew a tenancy in reprisal for a complaint or tenant organizing (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-10.10).

Notice to quit (Anti-Eviction Act cause)

The written demand and notice for delivery of possession the Anti-Eviction Act requires before an eviction for any cause but unpaid rent.

When
The tenant kept up the conduct after a notice to cease, damaged the premises, or you’re an owner of 3 units or fewer moving in.
The law
N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1; N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2
Good to know
  • It must specify the cause in detail (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2). Attach it, and the notice to cease, to your complaint.
  • Periods before you may file: 3 days for disorderly conduct or damage; one month for rules, lease covenants or habitual late payment; two months for an owner of 3 units or fewer moving in, and not before a written lease expires (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.2).
  • Other causes (drug, assault, theft and trafficking offenses: 3 days; code-violation closings, retirement from residential use, condominium conversion, refused lease changes) have their own conditions and periods: talk to a lawyer.
  • Serve it in person, leave it at the tenant’s home with a family member over 14, or send it by certified mail, then regular mail if the certified letter isn’t claimed.
  • You may not serve it in reprisal for a complaint or tenant organizing (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-10.10).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (CN 11252, CN 10822, CN 12770, CN 12842) Court

What the Judiciary’s mandatory landlord-tenant forms ask for, filled in from the lease, with where to get them and every step after.

When
Rent is unpaid, or a notice to quit ran out, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
N.J.S.A. 2A:18-53; N.J.S.A. 2A:18-57; N.J.S.A. 2A:42-10.16; N.J.S.A. 46:8-33
Good to know
  • New Jersey’s courts require their own forms for a residential eviction: the verified complaint (CN 11252), the summons (CN 10822), the Landlord Case Information Statement (CN 12770) and the certification of lease and registration (CN 12842). NOHM doesn’t write a complaint: copy these values onto those forms.
  • A corporation or LLC must be represented by a New Jersey attorney in the Landlord/Tenant Section (CN 10289).
  • Without a current landlord registration, no judgment for possession (N.J.S.A. 46:8-33).

Notice to the court of payment after a lockout Court

Tells the court the tenant paid all the rent due within 3 business days after the warrant was posted or the lockout, so the case is dismissed.

When
Within 2 business days after a tenant paid in full after a lockout for nonpayment.
The law
N.J.S.A. 2A:42-10.16a
Good to know
  • For 3 business days after a warrant for removal is posted or a lockout is executed for nonpayment, the tenant may pay the rent due; you must accept all of it, with no late fee beyond the one in your application for the warrant (N.J.S.A. 2A:42-10.16a(a)–(b)).
  • Within 2 business days after the payment, give the court this written notice, and the tenant a copy. The court then dismisses the case with prejudice.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • New Jersey doesn’t require a move-in checklist, but a signed one is the best proof of the home’s condition when you itemize the deposit.
  • Truth in Renting: give each new tenant the Department of Community Affairs’ current Truth in Renting statement at or before move-in, and keep a copy posted where all your tenants can see it (N.J.S.A. 46:8-46; nj.gov/dca). It applies unless the building has 2 units or fewer, or 3 or fewer with you living there (N.J.S.A. 46:8-44). Up to $100 a violation.
  • Give each new tenant a copy of your landlord registration certificate when the tenancy starts (N.J.S.A. 46:8-29).
  • Before the lease is signed or renewed, tell the tenant in writing whether the home is in a FEMA 100-year or 500-year floodplain and whether you know it has flooded, and put the statute’s flood-insurance sentence in the lease (N.J.S.A. 46:8-50; DCA’s model Flood Risk Notice). Used as a lease rider it must be in 12-point type, which NOHM’s papers don’t yet print, so NOHM doesn’t draw it.
  • Within 30 days of taking a deposit, give the tenant the deposit’s bank notice (the next form) (N.J.S.A. 46:8-19(c)).
  • For a home built before 1978, give a new tenant your valid lead-safe certification (N.J.S.A. 52:27D-437.16), and the federal lead-based paint disclosure. Once the state publishes its model statement, a Lead in Drinking Water Disclosure is due before a lease or renewal too.
  • An application fee can’t be more than $50, indexed each year (N.J.S.A. 46:8-18.1).

Security deposit bank notice

Tells the tenant where their deposit is held: the bank, the type of account, its interest rate and the amount.

When
Within 30 days of taking the deposit or moving it; with each annual interest payment; within 30 days of a sale.
The law
N.J.S.A. 46:8-19(c)
Good to know
  • Give it within 30 days after you receive the deposit, within 30 days after you move it, at each annual interest payment, and within 30 days after the property is sold (N.J.S.A. 46:8-19(c)).
  • Owners of 10 or more units keep deposits in an insured New Jersey money market fund or a variable-rate account; fewer, in an interest-bearing account at an insured New Jersey bank. The deposit stays the tenant’s money, held in trust (N.J.S.A. 46:8-19).
  • Without this notice, the tenant may by written notice have the deposit plus 7% interest applied to rent, and owe no further deposit.
  • The deposit can’t be more than one and a half months’ rent; any increase in a year, no more than 10% of the current deposit (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.2). An owner-occupied building of 2 rental units or fewer is exempt unless the tenant gives 30 days’ written notice invoking the law (N.J.S.A. 46:8-26).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
N.J.A.C. 5:10-5.1(c)
Good to know
  • No New Jersey statute sets notice for entry. The housing code asks occupants of a multiple dwelling (3 or more units) to allow access on reasonable notice, ordinarily one day, for inspections and repairs; immediately in a safety or structural emergency (N.J.A.C. 5:10-5.1(c)). NOHM asks for a day everywhere.
  • Otherwise, a landlord generally may not enter without the tenant’s consent or a court judgment (Truth in Renting). Entering while the tenant is out can be an unlawful entry (N.J.S.A. 2A:39-1).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No New Jersey statute sets a rent increase or its notice. To raise a month-to-month rent, end the current term with a notice to quit and offer the tenancy at the new rent: a tenant can be evicted for not paying an increase only after a valid notice to quit and notice of increase, and only if the increase isn’t unconscionable and complies with any rent ordinance (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1(f); Truth in Renting).
  • NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date. Courts may expect a full rental period’s notice; NOHM couldn’t confirm how long a “valid” notice is.
  • About 100 municipalities have rent control ordinances: check yours. During a fixed-term lease, raise the rent only at renewal.
  • Manufactured home park sites: no more than 3.5% in 12 months (N.J.S.A. 52:27D-287.13).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the lease ends, return the deposit with its interest, less charges the lease allows, itemized, by personal delivery or registered or certified mail (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1).
  • Faster: 5 business days for a tenant displaced by fire, flood, condemnation or evacuation; 15 business days after a domestic-violence lease termination. Nothing may be deducted while the tenant stays in possession.
  • Withholding wrongfully costs double the amount, with court costs and possibly attorney’s fees (N.J.S.A. 46:8-21.1).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
N.J.S.A. 2A:18-72 to 2A:18-74
Good to know
  • Only after the warrant for removal was executed and possession restored, or the tenant gave written notice of voluntarily giving up possession, and you reasonably believe they left the property with no intent to claim it (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-72).
  • Mail it certified (return receipt) or first class with a certificate of mailing, to the last known address and any other you know, marked “Please Forward” (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-73). The date must be at least 30 days after delivery or 33 after mailing, whichever comes first: NOHM uses 33 days after mailing. A manufactured or mobile home needs 75 or 78 days, and notice to the Motor Vehicle Commission and lienholders.
  • If the tenant responds, they get 15 more days; if not, the property is conclusively abandoned (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-76).
  • After a sale, deduct your costs and rent owed that the deposit didn’t cover, and send the tenant the rest with an itemized accounting; if they can’t be found, deposit it with the Superior Court (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-80). Getting it wrong can cost twice the tenant’s actual damages (N.J.S.A. 2A:18-82).
