Notices before court.

Demand for payment of rent (no notice required)

An optional written demand for the rent. South Dakota requires no notice before you file.

When
Rent is past due; you can file once it is unpaid 3 days after the due date.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-1(4)
Good to know
  • South Dakota repealed its 3-day notice to quit in 2024 (SL 2024, ch 75): no notice is required before you file. You can file once the rent is unpaid for 3 days after it was due (S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-1(4)).
  • Counting the 3 days: the due date doesn’t count (S.D. Codified Laws § 2-14-14). NOHM also skips Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, as the court rule for periods under 11 days does (§ 15-6-6(a)), and waits until the third day has passed: the safe reading.
  • This demand is optional: a courtesy, or the notice your lease promises. Letting the tenant pay by a date is your choice, not a right the statute gives; give the time your lease sets, if it sets one.
  • South Dakota’s law sets no way to serve it. Put it in writing, hand it to the tenant or mail it, and keep proof; NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed.

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov

Notice of lease violation and termination

Ends the lease for a use or act it forbids. The lease sets any notice period or chance to fix it.

When
The tenant broke a lease term (other than rent).
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-18; S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-1(7)
Good to know
  • South Dakota sets no notice period and no chance to fix a lease violation: your lease decides. Give the notice and any chance to cure your lease promises; NOHM leaves the date to you.
  • For repairs the tenant is bound to make, you must first ask for them and allow a reasonable time before you end the lease (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-18(2)).
  • You can’t evict a tenant because they called the police or emergency help about domestic abuse, unlawful sexual behavior or stalking, or because they or someone in their household was the victim (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-18.1).
  • A notice not based on a breach of the lease, given within 180 days after a tenant’s good-faith code complaint, written repair notice or tenant-union activity, can be retaliation (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-27).
  • A false disability or service-animal claim lets you evict and claim a damage fee of up to $1,000 (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-36).
  • South Dakota’s law sets no way to serve it. Put it in writing, hand it to the tenant or mail it, and keep proof; NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed.

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov, sdlegislature.gov

1-month notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy at the end of a rental month, on at least a month’s notice.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-15; S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-4
Good to know
  • The notice must be at least as long as the rental period, up to one month, and end at the end of a period (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-15). A week-to-week tenancy needs one week’s notice, ending at the end of a week.
  • If the tenant stays after the date and you accept rent, the tenancy renews on the same terms (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-14). Don’t take rent for any time after the date.
  • A fixed-term lease ends on its own date. Once the tenant holds over you may file; no notice or demand is required (S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-1(4)). A written notice to quit and demand for possession lets you claim double damages for willful holding over (§ 21-3-8): NOHM’s demand for possession is that paper.
  • A mobile home lot that is being put to another use needs 90 days’ notice to vacate (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-31).
  • South Dakota’s law sets no way to serve it. Put it in writing, hand it to the tenant or mail it, and keep proof; NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed.

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 21-3-8; S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-1(4)
Good to know
  • Not required before you file: since 2024 you may sue once a tenant holds over after the lease ends (S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-1(4)). Give it anyway if you want double damages: they need notice to quit and a demand of possession (§ 21-3-8), and the complaint must claim them (§ 21-16-10).
  • South Dakota sets no period for it. NOHM fills in 3 days; give more if you like.
  • Don’t accept rent for time after the lease ended: accepting it renews the tenancy on the same terms, for up to a year (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-14).

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov, sdlegislature.gov, sdlegislature.gov

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (UJS-232, UJS-111, UJS-112) Court

Everything the Unified Judicial System’s eviction forms ask for, filled in from the lease, with where to get each form and the steps after filing.

When
Rent is 3 days late, the lease ended, or the tenant broke it, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-1; S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-6; S.D. Codified Laws § 21-16-7
Good to know
  • South Dakota’s courts use the Unified Judicial System’s eviction forms. File those, filled in from this worksheet: a clerk can turn away a homemade complaint.
  • Get them one by one, or all in the landlord’s “Print All” packet, from the UJS self-help page.
  • A corporation or LLC must file through an attorney (the UJS forms’ instructions; S.D. Codified Laws § 15-39-47).
  • Bring the lease, any notice you gave and how, and the rent ledger to the hearing.

Read the law: ujs.sd.gov, ujs.sd.gov, sdlegislature.gov, sdlegislature.gov

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • South Dakota doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: the deposit can only be kept to restore the home to its condition at the start of the tenancy, ordinary wear and tear excepted (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-24), and this form records that condition.
  • Written disclosures: if you know the home was ever used to manufacture methamphetamine, you must tell the tenant before they rent it (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-30). A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.
  • A lease for more than one year must be in writing and signed (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-5).
  • A security deposit can’t be more than one month’s rent, unless special conditions pose a danger to the premises and you both agree to more (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-6.1).

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-32
Good to know
  • Give it in writing at least 24 hours ahead, unless your lease sets another way or time (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-32).
  • The law requires the notice to give the date, a time window during normal business hours, the purpose, and a way for the tenant to ask to reschedule. Pick a window between about 8 a.m. and 5 p.m.; the notice tells the tenant to contact you to reschedule.
  • In an emergency, or where notice is impracticable, you may enter without it.

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-13
Good to know
  • A month-to-month’s rent or terms change on written notice given at least 30 days before the end of the month, taking effect when the month ends (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-13). Start the new rent on a rent due date.
  • The tenant may end the tenancy on the first of the next month by telling you within 15 days of getting the notice.
  • During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • South Dakota has no rent cap. A rent increase above fair market value after a tenant’s complaint or repair request can be retaliation (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-27).

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 21 days after the tenancy ends and the tenant gives you a mailing address or delivery instructions.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-24
Good to know
  • Return the deposit, or send this statement of the specific reasons for keeping any of it, within 21 days after the tenancy ends and you receive the tenant’s mailing address or delivery instructions (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-24, as amended effective July 1, 2026; it was two weeks).
  • Keep only what is reasonably needed for unpaid rent and other money due under an agreement, and to restore the home to its condition at move-in, ordinary wear and tear excepted.
  • If the tenant asks, give an itemized accounting of what you withheld within 45 days after the tenancy ends.
  • Missing these steps forfeits your right to keep any of the deposit; bad faith can add punitive damages of up to $200.
  • No interest or separate account is required. The deposit cap is one month’s rent (§ 43-32-6.1).

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
S.D. Codified Laws §§ 43-32-25, 43-32-26
Good to know
  • South Dakota doesn’t require this notice. Giving one heads off disputes.
  • Property worth $500 or less in all may be disposed of 10 days after the tenant has quit the home (S.D. Codified Laws § 43-32-25). Worth more: store it at least 30 days; you have a lien for handling and storage (§ 43-32-26). NOHM fills in 30 days, which works for both.
  • Use it only after the tenant has quit the home, never while they still have possession. Photograph the property before you move it.

Read the law: sdlegislature.gov
