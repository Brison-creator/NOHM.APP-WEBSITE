Notices before court.

3-day notice to pay rent or quit

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid: pay it within 3 days, or the rental agreement ends and they must move.

When
Rent is past due (Montana has no grace period).
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-422(2); Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-108(1); Mont. Code Ann. § 1-1-306
Good to know
  • Montana has no grace period: rent is due without demand on the day the rental agreement sets (MCA 70-24-201(3)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it with a certificate of mailing (USPS Form 3817) or by certified mail: mailed, it counts as given 3 days after mailing (MCA 70-24-108(1)(d)). Email works only to an address the tenant gave in the rental agreement, and counts from a read receipt or a reply that isn’t automatic. Montana doesn’t allow taping it to the door or leaving it with someone else.
  • If the tenant pays in full within the 3 days, the tenancy goes on. Taking the full rent after that waives the nonpayment; taking part of it doesn’t (MCA 70-24-423).
  • Montana needs no other demand before you file: if the tenant stays after the rental agreement ends, file the complaint for possession (MCA 70-24-429(1)).

14-day notice of noncompliance

Tells the tenant what they did wrong: remedy it within 14 days, or the rental agreement ends.

When
The tenant broke the rental agreement (other than rent).
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-422(1)(a), (d); Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-108(1); Mont. Code Ann. § 1-1-306
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the law requires the notice to specify it.
  • Some breaches have shorter notices (MCA 70-24-422): an unauthorized pet or person living there, or verbal abuse of the landlord, 3 days with the chance to remedy; damage to the premises, or conduct that puts the premises or neighbors at risk (drugs, gang activity, weapons), 3 days to terminate; the same breach again within 6 months of a notice, 5 days. NOHM’s notice is the 14-day one; the courts’ “Notice to Vacate” form letter (courts.mt.gov/Forms/landlord) has a box for each of the others.
  • A tenant who refuses lawful access or changes a lock without giving you a key gets a 24-hour notice to correct first, then a 3-day notice to terminate (MCA 70-24-424).
  • Taking rent doesn’t waive a breach other than nonpayment (MCA 70-24-423).
  • For a month-to-month tenancy you can use the 30-day notice instead, with no reason (MCA 70-24-422(7)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it with a certificate of mailing (USPS Form 3817) or by certified mail: mailed, it counts as given 3 days after mailing (MCA 70-24-108(1)(d)). Email works only to an address the tenant gave in the rental agreement, and counts from a read receipt or a reply that isn’t automatic. Montana doesn’t allow taping it to the door or leaving it with someone else.
  • Montana needs no other demand before you file: if the tenant stays after the rental agreement ends, file the complaint for possession (MCA 70-24-429(1)).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on any date at least 30 days out, no reason needed.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out.
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-441(2)–(3); Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-108(1); Mont. Code Ann. § 1-1-306
Good to know
  • Any date at least 30 days out works; it needn’t end a rental period (MCA 70-24-441(3)). A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ written notice (MCA 70-24-441(1)).
  • No reason is needed, but you may not end a tenancy to retaliate for a complaint (MCA 70-24-431).
  • A tenant who stays on after a lawful 30-day notice can owe up to 3 months’ rent or three times your damages, whichever is more (MCA 70-24-429(3)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it with a certificate of mailing (USPS Form 3817) or by certified mail: mailed, it counts as given 3 days after mailing (MCA 70-24-108(1)(d)). Email works only to an address the tenant gave in the rental agreement, and counts from a read receipt or a reply that isn’t automatic. Montana doesn’t allow taping it to the door or leaving it with someone else.
  • Montana needs no other demand before you file: if the tenant stays after the rental agreement ends, file the complaint for possession (MCA 70-24-429(1)).

Court papers.

Complaint for possession Court

The eviction filing: asks the court for possession of the rental, the rent owed and costs.

When
The notice ran out, or the lease ended, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-427; Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-429; Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-422; Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-441
Good to know
  • File it with the justice court of the county where the rental is (or a city or district court). Attach the rental agreement if it’s written, and the notice you gave.
  • Montana’s courts publish an optional complaint in their Action for Possession packet (courts.mt.gov/Forms/landlord); this one asks for the same things. The packet’s instructions about a hearing within 20 days are out of date: the law now says 10 business days.
  • The clerk issues the summons; the sheriff or another levying officer serves it with the complaint. You can’t hand them to the tenant yourself.
  • The tenant has 5 business days after service, not counting the day of service, to answer (MCA 70-24-429(4)). The hearing must be held within 10 business days after the tenant appears or the answer date, or 5 business days where you ended the lease for drugs, gang activity, weapons or other dangerous conduct (MCA 70-24-427(2)(a)).
  • The court rules within 5 days after the hearing. If you win, it issues a writ of possession and a writ of assistance at once, and the sheriff carries out the writ of assistance within 5 business days (MCA 70-24-427(4)).
  • No bond is required. The winner may be awarded reasonable attorney fees and costs (MCA 70-24-442).
  • In justice court, a member with a majority interest may represent an LLC (MCA 25-31-601(3), 35-8-301(5)). Otherwise, and in district court, plan on a lawyer for a company.
  • Never lock the tenant out or shut off utilities to get them out (MCA 70-24-428).

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-25-206
Good to know
  • If you take a security deposit, Montana requires this statement, signed by you or your agent, given to each tenant when the lease is signed or the tenancy starts. Without it you can’t charge the deposit for damage or cleaning unless you prove it by clear and convincing evidence (MCA 70-25-206).
  • If the tenant asks in writing, give them a copy of the last tenant’s list of damage and cleaning charges for this home (MCA 70-25-206(1)).
  • At or before the start of the tenancy, also tell the tenant in writing the name and address of the person managing the premises, and of the owner or the person authorized to accept service and notices for the owner (MCA 70-24-301).
  • Check that the smoke and carbon monoxide detectors work when the tenancy starts (MCA 70-24-303(1)(g)).
  • To give notices by email, the rental agreement must let the tenant choose email; you can’t make an email address a condition of renting (MCA 70-24-202(4)).
  • When the lease ends, give the tenant the property-left-behind rules of MCA 70-24-430 in plain language (MCA 70-24-430(9)).
  • A home built before 1978 also needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-312(3)
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ notice and enter only at reasonable times, unless it’s an emergency or notice is impracticable (MCA 70-24-312(3)(a)).
  • Besides the usual ways of giving notice, you may post this on the main entry door of the unit (MCA 70-24-312(3)(b)).
  • Don’t use the right of entry to harass the tenant. A tenant who refuses lawful access gets a 24-hour notice to correct, then a 3-day notice to terminate (MCA 70-24-424).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Montana statute sets the notice for a rent increase. A month-to-month tenancy can be ended on 30 days’ written notice (MCA 70-24-441), so NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date.
  • During a fixed-term lease, the rent changes only as the lease allows.
  • Montana has no rent cap, and cities and counties can’t control rents (MCA 7-1-111(26)).
  • You may not raise rent to retaliate for a complaint or a tenants’ union (MCA 70-24-431).

Move-out.

Notice of cleaning required

Tells the tenant what cleaning is left undone; they have 24 hours to do it before you charge the deposit for it.

When
Before you deduct cleaning from the deposit.
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-25-201(3)
Good to know
  • You can’t deduct cleaning from the deposit until you’ve given this notice; the tenant then has 24 hours to do the cleaning (MCA 70-25-201(3)).
  • Give it as any notice (in hand, or mail with a certificate of mailing or certified mail: served 3 days after mailing). If the tenant left without notice, you may leave it in a conspicuous place in the unit and tell them by email, phone or text (MCA 70-25-201(3)(c)).
  • Not needed where the rental agreement was already terminated and your claim is pending in court.
  • Montana’s courts publish an optional “Letter Additional Cleaning” for the same purpose (courts.mt.gov/Forms/landlord).

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-25-202(1)
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the tenancy ends (or the home is surrendered and accepted, if sooner), give the tenant this list with the refund (MCA 70-25-202(1)(a)). If there’s no damage, no cleaning and no unpaid rent, and the tenant shows the utilities are paid, return the whole deposit within 10 days (MCA 70-25-202(1)(b)).
  • Deduct cleaning only after the 24-hour cleaning notice (MCA 70-25-201(3)), and nothing for any purpose the law doesn’t list (MCA 70-25-201(4)).
  • Without this list you lose the right to keep any of the deposit for damage or cleaning (MCA 70-25-203). Withholding wrongfully makes you liable for the amount withheld, and you carry the burden of proving the damage (MCA 70-25-204).
  • Montana has no deposit cap and no interest requirement. A fee for cleaning and damage, whatever it’s called, is a deposit (MCA 70-25-101(4)).
  • These rules don’t apply where the rental agreement was terminated and your claim is pending in court (MCA 70-25-202(2)).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Mont. Code Ann. § 70-24-430
Good to know
  • If the tenancy ended by court order, the property is considered abandoned and you may dispose of it right away (MCA 70-24-430(1)(a)). Otherwise you need clear and convincing evidence the tenant abandoned it, and 48 hours since you got that evidence; then trash and anything hazardous, perishable or valueless can go at once (MCA 70-24-430(1)(b)).
  • Inventory and store what you reasonably believe is valuable, with reasonable care, and mail this notice with a certificate of mailing or by certified mail to the tenant’s last-known address. The date must be at least 10 days after mailing (MCA 70-24-430(2)–(3)).
  • Items labeled “rent to own” or “leased” may be disposed of only after the owner confirms its lien (MCA 70-24-430(1)(c)).
  • After a sale, deduct the reasonable costs of notice, storage, labor and sale and any rent or damages owed, and send the tenant the rest with an itemized accounting; if they can’t be found, deposit it with the county treasurer (MCA 70-24-430(8)).
