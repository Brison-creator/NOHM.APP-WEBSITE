Notices before court.

14-day notice to quit for nonpayment of rent

Ends the tenancy 14 days after the tenant gets it unless the rent is paid; goes with the state’s required form.

When
Rent is due and unpaid (Massachusetts has no grace period).
The law
M.G.L. c. 186, § 12; M.G.L. c. 186, § 11; M.G.L. c. 186, § 31(a)
Good to know
  • Attach the state’s “Form to Accompany Residential Notice to Quit” (M.G.L. c. 186, § 31), with the part on any repayment agreement filled in. The court won’t accept your summons and complaint without proof you delivered it. Get the current PDF at mass.gov/notice-to-quit-accompanying-form: NOHM couldn’t open that page to check its version.
  • A tenant at will who hasn’t had a nonpayment notice to quit in the last 12 months can stop the termination by paying the full rent due within 10 days after getting this notice. A tenant under a written lease can pay all rent due, with interest and costs, up to the day the answer is due. NOHM’s notice gives 14 days to pay; if the tenant pays in that time, the tenancy goes on.
  • Massachusetts sets no grace period before this notice: serve it once rent is due and unpaid.
  • Massachusetts law doesn’t say how to serve a notice to quit: it runs from when the tenant receives it, and you must prove that. A constable’s or sheriff’s return is the usual proof; NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.
  • If the 14th day is a weekend or holiday, NOHM moves the date to the next business day. Massachusetts moves a tenant’s payment deadline that falls on a Sunday or legal holiday to the next business day (M.G.L. c. 4, § 9).
  • If the tenant shows the court a pending application for emergency rental assistance, the court must pause the case until it is decided (M.G.L. c. 239, § 15).

Notice to quit for breach of the lease

Ends a written lease for a breach, on the notice and terms the lease itself sets.

When
The tenant broke a term of a written lease that lets you end it.
The law
M.G.L. c. 239, § 1
Good to know
  • Massachusetts has no statutory notice for a lease violation: your written lease decides. Use the notice period, and any chance to fix the breach, that the lease’s termination clause gives, and set the date to match.
  • A tenant at will (no written lease) can’t be ended for a breach this way. Use the notice to quit for a tenancy at will (M.G.L. c. 186, § 12).
  • Using the premises for illegal drugs, prostitution, illegal gaming, illegal weapons or explosives lets you void the lease, with no time to cure (M.G.L. c. 139, § 19).
  • A termination notice, other than for rent, within 6 months after the tenant reported a code violation, joined a tenants’ union or used another legal right is presumed to be a reprisal (M.G.L. c. 186, § 18).
  • Massachusetts law doesn’t say how to serve a notice to quit: it runs from when the tenant receives it, and you must prove that. A constable’s or sheriff’s return is the usual proof; NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.

Read the law: malegislature.gov

Notice to quit a tenancy at will (a rental period, at least 30 days)

Ends a tenancy at will on the last day of a rental period, at least 30 days after the tenant gets it.

When
You want to end a tenancy at will (month to month), no fault needed.
The law
M.G.L. c. 186, § 12; M.G.L. c. 186, § 13
Good to know
  • For monthly rent the notice is a full rental period, and at least 30 days, from when the tenant receives it; for weekly rent, 30 days. If rent is payable every three months or less often, it is three months (M.G.L. c. 186, § 12).
  • The statute doesn’t say the date must end a rental period; Massachusetts practice (from court decisions NOHM didn’t check) is that it should. NOHM ends it on the last day of a rental period, which satisfies both readings.
  • Massachusetts has no separate demand for possession: this notice is the demand. A written lease that ran out on its own date needs no notice before summary process (M.G.L. c. 239, § 1).
  • You may offer a new tenancy on different terms, such as a new rent, in this notice without affecting it (M.G.L. c. 186, § 12).
  • You can’t file until that same period has passed after the tenant receives the notice (M.G.L. c. 186, § 13). A court may stay a no-fault eviction up to 6 months, or 12 if the tenant is disabled or 60 or older (M.G.L. c. 239, § 9).
  • A notice within 6 months after the tenant used a legal right is presumed to be a reprisal (M.G.L. c. 186, § 18).
  • Massachusetts law doesn’t say how to serve a notice to quit: it runs from when the tenant receives it, and you must prove that. A constable’s or sheriff’s return is the usual proof; NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.

Read the law: malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Summary Process Summons and Complaint, TC0030)

What to write on the Trial Court’s summary process forms, what goes with them, and the steps and deadlines after you file.

When
The notice to quit has run, or the lease has ended, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
M.G.L. c. 239, §§ 1–5; M.G.L. c. 186, § 13; M.G.L. c. 186, § 31(b)
Good to know
  • Massachusetts courts take summary process on their own Summary Process Summons and Complaint, which you get from the clerk (reported: only a sample is online; NOHM couldn’t open mass.gov). NOHM doesn’t write a complaint for you; this sheet tells you what to put on the court’s.
  • The timing is set by the Uniform Summary Process Rules, which NOHM could read only in search excerpts: serve the summons and complaint no later than the 7th day and no earlier than the 30th day before the entry day; entry days are Mondays; the answer is due the Monday after entry; the case is listed for the second Thursday after entry. Courts now also hold a first court event (mediation) under standing orders. Check the current rules with the clerk.
  • A constable or sheriff usually serves the summons and complaint.
  • TC0030 is the Trial Court’s e-form; NOHM couldn’t read its text.
  • A company (LLC or corporation) must appear through a Massachusetts lawyer (from case law as reported; NOHM didn’t read it).
  • Before a default, the court will ask for a Servicemembers Civil Relief Act affidavit (federal law).
  • The Superior Court also hears summary process, usually only where damages over $25,000 are likely (M.G.L. c. 239, § 2).

Notice of satisfaction of judgment Court

Tells the court that a judgment or agreement in a case only for unpaid rent has been paid, as the law requires.

When
Within 14 days after a nonpayment judgment or agreement is paid.
The law
M.G.L. c. 239, § 16(k)
Good to know
  • The law requires it only in a case solely for nonpayment of rent: file it with the court that entered the judgment or agreement, not more than 14 days after it is paid (M.G.L. c. 239, § 16(k)).
  • If the clerk has a form of their own for it, use theirs. Send the tenant a copy.

Read the law: malegislature.gov

Move-in.

Statement of condition (move-in)

The signed statement of the home’s condition, with the notice the law requires, that goes with a security deposit.

When
Give it at move-in; the tenant has 15 days to return it.
The law
M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(2)(c)
Good to know
  • Required when you take a security deposit: give it when you receive the deposit or within 10 days after the tenancy starts, whichever is later, signed by you or your agent (M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(2)(c)).
  • List all damage existing in the premises, including any code violation certified by a board of health or building official or found by a court.
  • If the tenant returns a separate signed list of damage, within 15 days give them a copy with your signed agreement, or a clear statement of disagreement, attached.
  • You can’t deduct from the deposit for damage on this statement or the tenant’s list (§ 15B(4)). Keep a record of damage and repairs for each unit, open to the tenant, until 2 years after the tenancy ends (§ 15B(2)(d)).
  • Other papers Massachusetts requires: a signed deposit receipt when you take the deposit, and a bank receipt within 30 days (NOHM has both); a receipt for last month’s rent, saying the tenant is owed 5% interest on it (or the lower rate the bank paid) and should give you a forwarding address (§ 15B(2)(a)); a statement of interest each year (§ 15B(3)(b)); a copy of the signed lease within 30 days (§ 15D); the lead law notification, the latest lead inspection report or letter of compliance, and the tenant certification, before the tenancy starts (c. 111, § 197A; NOHM didn’t check the Department of Public Health’s form titles); the federal lead disclosure for homes built before 1978; your name and contact posted, and the Department of Public Health’s notice of occupants’ rights (105 CMR 410.400); and your fire insurance details within 15 days if the tenant asks (§ 21).
  • Up front you may take only the first month’s rent, the last month’s rent, a deposit of no more than one month’s rent, and the cost of a new lock and key (§ 15B(1)(b)).

Read the law: malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov, law.cornell.edu

Security deposit receipt

The signed receipt Massachusetts requires when you take a security deposit.

When
When the tenant pays the deposit.
The law
M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(2)(b)
Good to know
  • Give it when you take the deposit, signed by whoever received it (M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(2)(b)).
  • Put the deposit in a separate, interest-bearing account in a Massachusetts bank, and give the bank receipt within 30 days (§ 15B(3)(a)). Give the statement of condition too (§ 15B(2)(c)).
  • The deposit can be no more than the first month’s rent (§ 15B(1)(b)).

Read the law: malegislature.gov

Security deposit bank receipt

Tells the tenant where the deposit is held: the bank and its location, the amount and the account number.

When
Within 30 days after you receive the deposit.
The law
M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(3)(a)
Good to know
  • Give it within 30 days after you receive the deposit. If you don’t, the tenant is entitled to the deposit back right away (M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(3)(a)).
  • The account must be separate and interest-bearing, in a bank located in Massachusetts, and out of reach of your creditors.

Read the law: malegislature.gov

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(1)(a)
Good to know
  • Massachusetts sets no advance notice for entry; your lease may. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • Enter only for the purposes the statute lists: to inspect, make repairs or show the home; under a court order; if it appears abandoned; or, near the end of the tenancy, to check for damage.
  • Entering for other reasons, or too often, can breach the tenant’s quiet enjoyment, with damages of at least three months’ rent (M.G.L. c. 186, § 14).

Read the law: malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • Massachusetts sets no notice for a rent increase. For a tenant at will, a new rent is a new tenancy: give at least what a notice to quit would need, a full rental period and at least 30 days, starting on a rent day (M.G.L. c. 186, § 12 lets a notice to quit offer the new terms). NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date.
  • During a lease, change the rent only as the lease allows.
  • Massachusetts has no rent cap, and cities and towns can’t control rents (M.G.L. c. 40P).
  • A rent increase within 6 months after the tenant used a legal right is presumed to be a reprisal (M.G.L. c. 186, § 18).

Read the law: malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(4)
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the tenancy ends, return the deposit with its interest, less only what the law allows, and give this list, signed under the pains and penalties of perjury, with the estimates, bills, invoices or receipts for each repair (M.G.L. c. 186, § 15B(4)).
  • You can’t deduct for damage listed on the statement of condition or on the tenant’s own list at move-in.
  • Interest is 5% a year, or the lower rate the bank paid (§ 15B(3)(b)).
  • Miss a step (the separate bank account, this sworn list within 30 days, the return within 30 days) and you lose the right to keep any of it; some failures cost three times the deposit, plus interest, costs and attorney’s fees (§ 15B(6), (7)).
  • The deposit can be no more than the first month’s rent (§ 15B(1)(b)). Vacation rentals of 100 days or less are exempt (§ 15B(9)).

Read the law: malegislature.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • Massachusetts has no statute for property a tenant leaves after moving out on their own. NOHM fills in 30 days to pick it up; it isn’t a statutory period. Store it safely, and photograph it before you move it.
  • After an eviction, don’t use this: the sheriff or constable stores the tenant’s belongings with a licensed public warehouser (M.G.L. c. 239, § 4).
  • Within 3 days after you know, or should know, the tenant has left, check the home for animals left behind and notify an animal control or police officer right away (M.G.L. c. 186, § 30).

Read the law: malegislature.gov, malegislature.gov
