Massachusetts eviction filing worksheet (Summary Process Summons and Complaint, TC0030)

What to write on the Trial Court’s summary process forms, what goes with them, and the steps and deadlines after you file.

Updated November 7, 2026

The facts.

  • WhenThe notice to quit has run, or the lease has ended, and the tenant hasn’t left.
  • The lawM.G.L. c. 239, §§ 1–5; M.G.L. c. 186, § 13; M.G.L. c. 186, § 31(b)
  • StageCourt papers

What to know.

  • Massachusetts courts take summary process on their own Summary Process Summons and Complaint, which you get from the clerk (reported: only a sample is online; NOHM couldn’t open mass.gov). NOHM doesn’t write a complaint for you; this sheet tells you what to put on the court’s.
  • The timing is set by the Uniform Summary Process Rules, which NOHM could read only in search excerpts: serve the summons and complaint no later than the 7th day and no earlier than the 30th day before the entry day; entry days are Mondays; the answer is due the Monday after entry; the case is listed for the second Thursday after entry. Courts now also hold a first court event (mediation) under standing orders. Check the current rules with the clerk.
  • A constable or sheriff usually serves the summons and complaint.
  • TC0030 is the Trial Court’s e-form; NOHM couldn’t read its text.
  • A company (LLC or corporation) must appear through a Massachusetts lawyer (from case law as reported; NOHM didn’t read it).
  • Before a default, the court will ask for a Servicemembers Civil Relief Act affidavit (federal law).
  • The Superior Court also hears summary process, usually only where damages over $25,000 are likely (M.G.L. c. 239, § 2).
