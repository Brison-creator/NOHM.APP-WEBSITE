Notices before court.

10-day demand for rent

Demands all past-due rent: if it isn’t paid within 10 days, the lease term is forfeited.

When
Rent is past due and your lease has no clause ending it for nonpayment.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-3; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-26(b); N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-33
Good to know
  • G.S. 42-3 fills a gap: use it when your lease has no clause ending the tenancy for unpaid rent (so the courts’ complaint form instructions and the UNC School of Government read it). If your lease has one, you proceed under the lease, and must still demand possession before you file (G.S. 42-26(a)).
  • Make the demand clear: pay all past-due rent, and say you intend to use the statute’s forfeiture (Snipes v. Snipes). A lease line saying a demand is always in effect doesn’t count.
  • No rule sets how the 10 days are counted. NOHM counts 10 full days after the day of the demand, so file on the 11th day or later; it adds 3 days when you mail it.
  • Until judgment, the tenant can stop the case by paying the rent due and the court costs (G.S. 42-33).
  • Late fees: only if the lease provides them, after rent is 5 days late, at most $15 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is greater (G.S. 42-46(a)).
  • No statute sets how to deliver it. Hand it over or mail it, and keep a dated record.

Notice of lease violation

Tells the tenant about a breach; North Carolina leaves the rest to the lease.

When
The tenant broke a lease condition that the lease says ends the tenancy.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-26(a)(2); N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-43(b)

The statute doesn’t set this notice’s exact words: check it before you serve one.

Good to know
  • Your lease decides: it must say the tenancy ends on that breach (G.S. 42-26(a)(2)). Then demand possession before you file (G.S. 42-26(a)).
  • For a breach of the tenant’s duties in G.S. 42-43(a) (keeping it clean and safe, the plumbing, the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, damage), notify the tenant in writing, except in an emergency (G.S. 42-43(b)).
  • The lease may say that taking partial rent doesn’t waive the breach (G.S. 42-26(c)).
  • Criminal activity is its own expedited case, with no notice first (G.S. 42-59 to 42-68); ask a lawyer.

7-day notice to quit (month-to-month)

Ends a month-to-month tenancy at the end of a rental period, with at least 7 days’ notice.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-14
Good to know
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 2 days’ notice, a year-to-year one a month before the end of the year, and a manufactured home space 60 days before the end of the period (G.S. 42-14).
  • If the tenant stays after the date, demand possession in writing before you file (G.S. 42-26(a)): NOHM has the demand for possession.
  • A subsidized or federally covered tenancy (VAWA, Section 8, public housing) may need more.
  • No statute sets how to deliver it. NOHM adds 3 days when you mail it.

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-26(a)
Good to know
  • Every summary ejectment needs a demand that the tenant surrender the premises before you file (G.S. 42-26(a)); the complaint says you made it. The statute sets no waiting period: NOHM fills in 3 days.
  • Use it after the lease ran out, after your notice to quit ended a periodic tenancy, after the 10-day rent demand ran, or when the tenant broke a lease condition that ends the tenancy.
  • No statute says how to deliver it. Hand it over or mail it, and keep a dated record.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (AOC-CVM-201)

The courts’ Complaint in Summary Ejectment and the papers after it, where to get them, the values from your lease to copy onto them, and the steps and deadlines.

When
The 10-day demand, the lease or the notice to quit ran out, and the tenant hasn’t left after your demand for possession.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-26; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-28; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-29; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 7A-216; N.C. Gen. Stat. § 7A-232
Good to know
  • The Administrative Office of the Courts’ Complaint in Summary Ejectment (AOC-CVM-201) is what clerks and magistrates expect. The law allows a complaint in any clear form (G.S. 7A-216), but NOHM fills in this worksheet for you to copy onto the court’s form. The worksheet isn’t filed.
  • Don’t change an AOC form in a way that keeps its official look; if you change it, mark it as changed and say what changed (G.S. 7A-232).
  • NOHM read the complaint form’s 2012 revision (the courts’ site wouldn’t open); a search result shows a July 2024 revision NOHM couldn’t read. Check the boxes against the current form.
  • Name the owner as the plaintiff, not the managing agent (the UNC School of Government, citing Rule 17(a)). An agent with actual knowledge of the facts may sign.
  • The writ forms (AOC-CV-401, Writ of Possession; AOC-CV-412, for a judgment more than 30 days old) and when clerks issue the writ (in practice, after the 10-day appeal period) come from search results NOHM couldn’t open: ask the clerk.
  • Fees you may recover (G.S. 42-46): a complaint-filing fee if the tenant cures before judgment, a court-appearance fee if you win, and court costs; attorney’s fees only under a written lease and only if you prevail.
  • Bring the lease, the demands and your record of them, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • North Carolina doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It’s good practice: a signed record of the home’s condition settles deposit disputes.
  • Within 30 days after the lease begins, tell the tenant where the deposit is held (G.S. 42-50): NOHM has that notice.
  • Smoke alarms, and carbon monoxide alarms where there’s a fossil-fuel heater, appliance or fireplace or an attached garage, must work at the start of each tenancy, with new batteries (G.S. 42-42(a)(5), (7)).
  • If you bill for water and know it exceeds a maximum contaminant level, say so (G.S. 42-42(a)(6)). If you require renter’s insurance, you can’t name the insurer (G.S. 42-46(l)).
  • Homes built before 1978: give the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Notice of where the deposit is held

Tells the tenant the bank where their deposit is held in trust, or the insurer that bonds it.

When
Within 30 days after the lease term begins.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-50
Good to know
  • Keep the deposit in a trust account with a licensed and federally insured depository institution or trust institution in North Carolina, or furnish a bond from a North Carolina-licensed insurer (G.S. 42-50).
  • Tell the tenant within 30 days after the lease term begins (G.S. 42-50). Willfully failing to can void your right to keep any of the deposit (G.S. 42-55).
  • The deposit can’t exceed two weeks’ rent for a week-to-week tenancy, one and a half months’ rent for month-to-month, or two months’ rent for longer terms (G.S. 42-51(b)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • No North Carolina statute sets notice for entry; your lease decides. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • In an emergency you may need to go in right away; tell the tenant as soon as you can.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No North Carolina statute sets notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date; for a month-to-month, that is more than the 7-day notice to quit (G.S. 42-14).
  • During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Cities and counties can’t regulate rents (G.S. 42-14.1). An eviction substantially in response to a tenant’s protected act within the last 12 months can be defended as retaliation (G.S. 42-37.1).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get possession back.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-52
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get possession back, itemize any damage in writing and mail or deliver it, with the rest of the deposit (G.S. 42-52).
  • If you can’t work out the full claim in 30 days, send an interim accounting within 30 days and a final one within 60 (G.S. 42-52).
  • If you don’t know the tenant’s address, apply the deposit after 30 days and hold the balance at least six months for them to collect (G.S. 42-52).
  • Willfully failing to follow the deposit, bond or notice rules voids your right to keep any of it, and can cost damages and attorney’s fees (G.S. 42-55).
  • The deposit act covers anyone in the business of renting or managing homes, except single rooms (G.S. 42-56); transient occupancies and vacation rentals are outside the chapter (G.S. 42-14.6, 42-39(a1)).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 42-25.9(g)
Good to know
  • For property left after the sheriff executed a writ of possession. Hold it 7 days; release it if the tenant asks during that time (G.S. 42-25.9(g)).
  • To sell it, mail the tenant written notice by first-class mail to the last known address at least 7 days before the sale, with its date, time and place, and that any surplus after unpaid rent, damages, storage fees and sale costs goes to the tenant on request within 7 days, then to the county (G.S. 42-25.9(g)). Fill in the sale below and NOHM adds that notice.
  • Property worth less than $500 in all is abandoned 5 days after the writ is executed (G.S. 42-25.9(h)). Property worth $750 or less may go to a nonprofit that stores it 30 days, with the notice G.S. 42-25.9(d) requires.
  • A tenant who left without a writ: property is abandoned 10 or more days after you post a notice of suspected abandonment inside and outside the home and get no response (G.S. 42-25.9(e)).
