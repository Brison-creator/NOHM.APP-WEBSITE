Notices before court.

7-day notice to quit for unpaid rent

Gives the tenant 7 days to pay the rent in full, or the tenancy ends; goes with the court’s MED-600 sheet.

When
Rent is unpaid when due (Alaska has no grace period).
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.220(b); Alaska Stat. § 09.45.090(a)(1), (c); Alaska Stat. §§ 09.45.100–.105; Alaska Civil Rule 6(a)
Good to know
  • Give the tenant the court’s MED-600 information sheet with this notice: it is required with every notice to quit, and the eviction complaint asks you to confirm you gave it (Administrative Bulletin 98 ¶ 3(a)). Without it the judge may continue the hearing up to 2 days. Print it from public.courts.alaska.gov/web/forms/docs/med-600.pdf.
  • Mailed by registered or certified mail, the law adds 3 days to the period (AS 09.45.090(c)); NOHM counts them.
  • The law requires the date and time the tenancy ends (AS 09.45.105); NOHM states 11:59 p.m. on the last day.
  • Only one written notice is needed for any one default. You may accept a partial payment and extend the date for the eviction (AS 34.03.220(b)); otherwise accepting rent knowing of the default can waive your right to end the tenancy for it (AS 34.03.240).
  • Rent paid in advance: you can’t file until the prepaid period runs out (AS 09.45.130).
  • For a property with a federally backed mortgage or in a federal housing program, the CARES Act requires 30 days’ notice to vacate for nonpayment, and the court requires form CIV-731 with every complaint.
  • NOHM’s calendar doesn’t know Seward’s Day (last Monday in March) or Alaska Day (October 18): if the last day falls on one, move it a day later (AS 44.12.010).

10-day notice to quit for a lease violation

Says what the tenant did wrong and how to fix it within 10 days, or the tenancy ends; goes with the MED-600 sheet.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease, or a duty that affects health and safety.
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.220(a)(2); Alaska Stat. § 09.45.090(a)(2), (c); Alaska Stat. §§ 09.45.100–.105; Alaska Civil Rule 6(a)
Good to know
  • Describe both the violation and exactly what the tenant must do to fix it: the law requires both (AS 09.45.105(1)(A)–(B)).
  • Give the tenant the court’s MED-600 information sheet with this notice: it is required with every notice to quit, and the eviction complaint asks you to confirm you gave it (Administrative Bulletin 98 ¶ 3(a)). Without it the judge may continue the hearing up to 2 days. Print it from public.courts.alaska.gov/web/forms/docs/med-600.pdf.
  • Mailed by registered or certified mail, the law adds 3 days to the period (AS 09.45.090(c)); NOHM counts them.
  • The law requires the date and time the tenancy ends (AS 09.45.105); NOHM states 11:59 p.m. on the last day.
  • If substantially the same breach recurs within 6 months of one you gave notice of, the lease can end on 5 days’ notice with no chance to fix it (AS 34.03.220(a)(2); court form CIV-727’s “No Remedy” box).
  • Shorter notices apply to deliberate damage over $400 and to illegal activity (24 hours to 5 days, court forms CIV-728 and CIV-729), and to utilities shut off for the tenant’s nonpayment (5 days, CIV-726). A tenant who refuses lawful access can be given 10 days’ notice (AS 34.03.300(a)). NOHM doesn’t draw those; the court’s forms are free.
  • NOHM’s calendar doesn’t know Seward’s Day (last Monday in March) or Alaska Day (October 18): if the last day falls on one, move it a day later (AS 44.12.010).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rental due date at least 30 days out; goes with the MED-600 sheet.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out; Alaska needs no reason.
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.290(b); Alaska Stat. § 09.45.090(a)(2)(F), (c); Alaska Stat. §§ 09.45.100–.105
Good to know
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 14 days’ written notice instead, and only while rent is current (AS 34.03.290(a)).
  • Give the tenant the court’s MED-600 information sheet with this notice: it is required with every notice to quit, and the eviction complaint asks you to confirm you gave it (Administrative Bulletin 98 ¶ 3(a)). Without it the judge may continue the hearing up to 2 days. Print it from public.courts.alaska.gov/web/forms/docs/med-600.pdf.
  • Mailed by registered or certified mail, the law adds 3 days to the period (AS 09.45.090(c)); NOHM counts them.
  • The law requires the date and time the tenancy ends (AS 09.45.105); NOHM states 11:59 p.m. on the last day.
  • Alaska has no statewide just-cause rule, but you can’t end a tenancy to retaliate against a tenant (AS 34.03.310). For some of the reasons the retaliation law allows, the tenant gets the longer of 30 days or the lease’s notice period (AS 09.45.090(a)(2)(D)).
  • A lease with a fixed term that has run out still needs a notice to quit before you can file (AS 34.03.290(c); AS 09.45.100(a)): use NOHM’s notice to quit for a holdover.

Notice to quit (holdover)

The notice to quit Alaska requires before filing when a tenant stays after the lease ended.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.290(c); Alaska Stat. §§ 09.45.100–.105
Good to know
  • Alaska requires a notice to quit before you can file to evict, even when a lease term has simply run out (AS 09.45.100(a); AS 34.03.290(c)).
  • The law sets no number of days for it; NOHM fills in 7. If you mail it, the law adds 3 days for mail (AS 09.45.090(c)): give at least 10.
  • Serve it in hand, leave it at the premises if the tenant is absent, or send it by registered or certified mail (AS 09.45.100(c)), with the court’s MED-600 sheet (Administrative Bulletin 98 ¶ 3(a)).
  • The law requires the date and time the tenant must leave (AS 09.45.105); NOHM states 11:59 p.m. on the date.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (CIV-730, CIV-731, CIV-732, CIV-105)

What to write on the court’s Complaint for Forcible Entry and Detainer and the papers filed with it, filled in from the lease, with the steps and deadlines that follow.

When
The notice to quit ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Alaska Stat. §§ 09.45.060–.160; Alaska Civil Rule 85; Administrative Bulletin 98
Good to know
  • Use the court’s own complaint, CIV-730: a complaint you write must still carry the court form’s notice about dismissal word for word (Civil Rule 85(a)(1)). NOHM fills in what to write on it.
  • An individual owner can file without a lawyer. A corporation, a property manager, or anyone acting under a power of attorney must have an attorney sign the complaint (court booklet CIV-720; AS 22.20.040). The booklet doesn’t name LLCs; treat one like a corporation.
  • Bring the original notice to quit, your record of serving it, and the lease.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.020(e)
Good to know
  • Alaska lets you require a premises condition statement (and a contents inventory for a furnished home) in the lease, by reference or as an attachment; signed by both of you, it becomes part of the rental agreement (AS 34.03.020(e)).
  • Give the tenant in writing, at or before the start: who manages the home and who receives notices for the owner (AS 34.03.080; NOHM’s disclosure form), and the terms on which you may keep any of the deposit (AS 34.03.070(c)).
  • The lease must require the tenant to tell you of an absence of more than 7 days (AS 34.03.150). Post the rules prominently on the premises (AS 34.03.130(a)).
  • Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors must be working (AS 34.03.100(a)(7)). For a home built before 1978, give the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Disclosure of manager and owner

Tells the tenant in writing who manages the home and who receives notices and legal papers for the owner.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, and again whenever it changes.
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.080
Good to know
  • Required at or before the start of the tenancy, and kept current (AS 34.03.080(a)–(b)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.140(c)
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ notice, enter only at reasonable times, and with the tenant’s consent (AS 34.03.140(c)).
  • You may enter to inspect, make repairs or improvements, supply services, remove your own property not covered by the lease, or show the home to buyers, lenders, tenants, workers or contractors (AS 34.03.140(a)). In an emergency you may enter without consent (§ 34.03.140(b)); also during an absence of more than 7 days (§ 34.03.230(b)).
  • Abusing access, or refusing it, has remedies under AS 34.03.300.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Alaska statute sets the notice for a rent increase. The Department of Law’s handbook says to give at least 30 days in a month-to-month, effective on a rental due date; NOHM does the same (official guidance, not statute).
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Alaska has no rent cap. You can’t raise rent to retaliate against a tenant, though an increase for higher costs or improvements may be allowed (AS 34.03.310).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 14 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back (30 days if you deduct for damage, or no proper notice was given).
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.070(b), (g)
Good to know
  • Mail it within 14 days after the tenancy ends and the tenant gives back possession, if the tenancy was ended by a proper notice. You have 30 days if you deduct for damage, or if the tenant didn’t give proper notice (AS 34.03.070(g)).
  • Don’t know the tenant’s mailing address but know how to reach them? Make a reasonable effort to deliver it (AS 34.03.070(g)).
  • Wilfully failing to return it or itemize it can cost up to twice the amount withheld (AS 34.03.070(d)).
  • The deposit and prepaid rent can’t exceed two months’ rent unless the rent is over $2,000 a month; a pet deposit up to one month’s rent is accounted for separately (AS 34.03.070(a), (h)). Keep deposits in a trust account where practicable (§ 34.03.070(c)). No interest is required.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Alaska Stat. § 34.03.260(a)
Good to know
  • Give at least 15 days after you deliver or mail it (AS 34.03.260(a)). Mark in the list which items you’ll sell at public sale and which you’ll destroy or dispose of: the law asks the notice to say.
  • Store the property in a place of safekeeping with reasonable care. You may charge storage: on the premises, no more than its fair rental value; in commercial storage, the actual charge (AS 34.03.260(b)).
  • If the tenant answers in writing within the time, they get 15 more days to remove it, and pay the storage (AS 34.03.260(c)).
  • A public sale follows the execution-sale procedure of AS 09.35.140, which NOHM didn’t read: ask the court or a lawyer before you sell.
  • Violating this section deliberately or negligently can cost actual damages and as much again (AS 34.03.260(d)).
