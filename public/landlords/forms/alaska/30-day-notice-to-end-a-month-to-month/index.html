Alaska 30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rental due date at least 30 days out; goes with the MED-600 sheet.

At a glance.

  • WhenYou want a month-to-month tenant out; Alaska needs no reason.
  • The lawAlaska Stat. § 34.03.290(b); Alaska Stat. § 09.45.090(a)(2)(F), (c); Alaska Stat. §§ 09.45.100–.105
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 14 days’ written notice instead, and only while rent is current (AS 34.03.290(a)).
  • Give the tenant the court’s MED-600 information sheet with this notice: it is required with every notice to quit, and the eviction complaint asks you to confirm you gave it (Administrative Bulletin 98 ¶ 3(a)). Without it the judge may continue the hearing up to 2 days. Print it from public.courts.alaska.gov/web/forms/docs/med-600.pdf.
  • Mailed by registered or certified mail, the law adds 3 days to the period (AS 09.45.090(c)); NOHM counts them.
  • The law requires the date and time the tenancy ends (AS 09.45.105); NOHM states 11:59 p.m. on the last day.
  • Alaska has no statewide just-cause rule, but you can’t end a tenancy to retaliate against a tenant (AS 34.03.310). For some of the reasons the retaliation law allows, the tenant gets the longer of 30 days or the lease’s notice period (AS 09.45.090(a)(2)(D)).
  • A lease with a fixed term that has run out still needs a notice to quit before you can file (AS 34.03.290(c); AS 09.45.100(a)): use NOHM’s notice to quit for a holdover.
