Nevada 5-day notice of unlawful detainer

The notice to surrender that starts a summary eviction for any reason but unpaid rent.

Page updated November 7, 2026

At a glance.

  • WhenA 5-day notice to perform ran out, a no-cause notice or the lease term ended, and the tenant is still there.
  • The lawNev. Rev. Stat. § 40.254; Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.280(1)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • This is the second notice of a summary eviction for any reason but unpaid rent: after a 5-day notice to perform that wasn’t complied with, a 3-day notice for nuisance or waste, a 30-day (or 7-day) no-cause notice, or the end of the lease term (NRS 40.254, 40.251).
  • It must name the court and tell the tenant they may contest it by an affidavit filed before the court’s close of business on the 5th judicial day after service, or ask for a stay of up to 10 days (NRS 40.254); NOHM’s does.
  • Serve it the way NRS 40.280 requires: by a sheriff, constable, licensed process server or an attorney’s agent. You can’t serve it yourself.
  • Clark County’s justice courts don’t count Fridays as judicial days (their forms say so). NOHM counts Monday to Friday: in Clark County, count Monday to Thursday yourself and use that later date.
  • Reno Justice Court expects its own current form and may dismiss a case on another (Administrative Order 2025-01): for a Washoe County rental, use the court’s form.
  • After the 5 judicial days, if the tenant hasn’t left or filed an answer, file your court’s complaint for summary eviction (the eviction filing worksheet).
