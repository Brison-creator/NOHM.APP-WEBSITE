What to know.

  • Nevada requires every rental agreement to include a signed record of the inventory and condition of the premises in the tenant’s exclusive control (NRS 118A.200(3)(k)). Sign it with the tenant and attach it to the lease.
  • The agreement must also state, among other things, the rent as a single figure that includes every mandatory fee, the deposits and how they’re refunded, late and dishonored-check charges, who pays which utilities, your inspection rights, and how to report a nuisance or a code violation (NRS 118A.200(3), (6)).
  • A house or a building of 4 or fewer units whose lease isn’t signed by a licensed property manager needs the disclosure of NRS 118A.200(4) at the top of the first page, in type twice the size of any other.
  • In writing, at or before the start of the tenancy: the names and addresses of the manager, of a person in Nevada authorized to accept service and notices, and of the owner, and a phone number for emergencies answered by someone within 60 miles (NRS 118A.260). Tell a prospective tenant if the home is in foreclosure (NRS 118A.275).
  • Rent paid weekly or more often: to use the short 4-day notice later, have the tenant sign at move-in that you told them the short-term eviction rules (NRS 40.253(2)).
  • A home built before 1978 also needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure.
