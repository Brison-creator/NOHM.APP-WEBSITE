Notices before court.

7-day notice to pay rent or quit

Tells the tenant to pay the rent or surrender the home within 7 judicial days, and how to contest it in court.

When
Rent is past due (a late fee only 3 days after the due date).
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.253(1)–(3); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.2512(1)(b); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.280(1)
Good to know
  • In the notice’s details, enter the justice court for the township where the rental is, with its address (for example, Las Vegas Justice Court and its address). The law requires the notice to identify it (NRS 40.253(3)(a)); left blank, the notice prints a line to write it on.
  • Clark County’s justice courts don’t count Fridays as judicial days (their forms say so). NOHM counts Monday to Friday, so in Clark County count the judicial days Monday to Thursday yourself and give that later date.
  • Reno Justice Court expects its own current notice form and may dismiss a case that uses another (Administrative Order 2025-01). For a Washoe County rental, serve the court’s form (washoecounty.gov/rjc/forms).
  • A sheriff, constable, licensed process server or an attorney’s agent must serve it; you can’t serve it yourself (NRS 40.280(1)). The server signs a statement of the date and manner of service with their badge or license number; an attorney’s agent’s needs the attorney’s declaration (NRS 40.280(5)).
  • You may serve it any time after rent is due. If the tenant then pays the rent, you can’t refuse it because collection fees, attorney’s fees or other costs are unpaid (NRS 40.253(11)).
  • Rent paid by the week or less, in a tenancy of 45 days or less, may use a 4-day notice instead (NRS 40.253(1)(b)); NOHM doesn’t draw it.
  • Keep the notice private: it may not be made available for public inspection (NRS 40.2545(5)). Workers affected by a government shutdown have protections (NRS 40.253(12)).
  • If the tenant doesn’t pay, leave, or file an answer by the deadline, file your court’s complaint for summary eviction (the eviction filing worksheet).

5-day notice to perform lease condition or quit

Tells the tenant what they did wrong: remedy it within 5 judicial days, or the rental agreement may be terminated.

When
The tenant broke the rental agreement (other than rent).
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.2516(1); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 118A.430(1); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.251(1)(b)(2); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.280(1)
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the law requires the notice to specify it.
  • The statute says 5 days; the courts’ own forms count 5 judicial days, and so does NOHM, which is never earlier.
  • Subletting against the lease, waste, an unlawful business, a nuisance or a drug violation on the premises gets a 3-day notice to quit with no chance to cure (NRS 40.2514). NOHM doesn’t draw it; the courts’ forms do.
  • Then, if the tenant stays, serve the 5-day notice of unlawful detainer (NRS 40.254), NOHM’s next form.
  • A lease can’t shorten these notice periods (NRS 40.252).
  • Clark County’s justice courts don’t count Fridays as judicial days (their forms say so). NOHM counts Monday to Friday, so in Clark County count the judicial days Monday to Thursday yourself and give that later date.
  • A sheriff, constable, licensed process server or an attorney’s agent must serve it; you can’t serve it yourself (NRS 40.280(1)). The server signs a statement of the date and manner of service with their badge or license number; an attorney’s agent’s needs the attorney’s declaration (NRS 40.280(5)).

30-day no-cause notice to quit

Ends a month-to-month (or other periodic) tenancy at least 30 days out, no reason needed.

When
You want a periodic tenant out.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.251(1)(b)(1); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.251(2)–(6); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.280(1)
Good to know
  • Any date at least 30 calendar days after service works; it needn’t end a rental period. A week-to-week tenancy needs at least 7 days, and the age and disability extension doesn’t apply to it (NRS 40.251(1)(b), (2)).
  • The notice must tell the tenant about the age, disability and shutdown protections (NRS 40.251(5)); NOHM’s does.
  • After the date passes, serve the 5-day notice of unlawful detainer (NRS 40.254) to begin a summary eviction.
  • After a foreclosure, a new owner gives at least 60 days after a notice of change of ownership (NRS 40.255(2)).
  • No reason is needed, but you may not end a tenancy to retaliate (NRS 118A.510).
  • A sheriff, constable, licensed process server or an attorney’s agent must serve it; you can’t serve it yourself (NRS 40.280(1)). The server signs a statement of the date and manner of service with their badge or license number; an attorney’s agent’s needs the attorney’s declaration (NRS 40.280(5)).

5-day notice of unlawful detainer

The notice to surrender that starts a summary eviction for any reason but unpaid rent.

When
A 5-day notice to perform ran out, a no-cause notice or the lease term ended, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.254; Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.280(1)
Good to know
  • This is the second notice of a summary eviction for any reason but unpaid rent: after a 5-day notice to perform that wasn’t complied with, a 3-day notice for nuisance or waste, a 30-day (or 7-day) no-cause notice, or the end of the lease term (NRS 40.254, 40.251).
  • It must name the court and tell the tenant they may contest it by an affidavit filed before the court’s close of business on the 5th judicial day after service, or ask for a stay of up to 10 days (NRS 40.254); NOHM’s does.
  • Serve it the way NRS 40.280 requires: by a sheriff, constable, licensed process server or an attorney’s agent. You can’t serve it yourself.
  • Clark County’s justice courts don’t count Fridays as judicial days (their forms say so). NOHM counts Monday to Friday: in Clark County, count Monday to Thursday yourself and use that later date.
  • Reno Justice Court expects its own current form and may dismiss a case on another (Administrative Order 2025-01): for a Washoe County rental, use the court’s form.
  • After the 5 judicial days, if the tenant hasn’t left or filed an answer, file your court’s complaint for summary eviction (the eviction filing worksheet).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (your justice court’s summary eviction forms) Court

Everything your justice court’s complaint for summary eviction asks for, filled in from the lease, with where to get the form and the steps after.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid, left or answered.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.253(5)–(6); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.254(2); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.385
Good to know
  • Each Nevada justice court publishes its own summary-eviction forms, and Reno Justice Court may dismiss a case filed on other versions (Administrative Order 2025-01). So NOHM doesn’t write the complaint: copy these values onto your court’s current form.
  • The complaint is an affidavit or declaration under penalty of perjury; Henderson’s must be typed.
  • A company landlord may file the complaint through its agent, but plan on a lawyer if the tenant contests it.
  • Clark County’s justice courts don’t count Fridays as judicial days (their forms say so). NOHM counts Monday to Friday: in Clark County, count Monday to Thursday yourself and use that later date.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 118A.200(3)(k)
Good to know
  • Nevada requires every rental agreement to include a signed record of the inventory and condition of the premises in the tenant’s exclusive control (NRS 118A.200(3)(k)). Sign it with the tenant and attach it to the lease.
  • The agreement must also state, among other things, the rent as a single figure that includes every mandatory fee, the deposits and how they’re refunded, late and dishonored-check charges, who pays which utilities, your inspection rights, and how to report a nuisance or a code violation (NRS 118A.200(3), (6)).
  • A house or a building of 4 or fewer units whose lease isn’t signed by a licensed property manager needs the disclosure of NRS 118A.200(4) at the top of the first page, in type twice the size of any other.
  • In writing, at or before the start of the tenancy: the names and addresses of the manager, of a person in Nevada authorized to accept service and notices, and of the owner, and a phone number for emergencies answered by someone within 60 miles (NRS 118A.260). Tell a prospective tenant if the home is in foreclosure (NRS 118A.275).
  • Rent paid weekly or more often: to use the short 4-day notice later, have the tenant sign at move-in that you told them the short-term eviction rules (NRS 40.253(2)).
  • A home built before 1978 also needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 118A.330(3)
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ notice and enter only at reasonable times during normal business hours, unless it’s an emergency or the tenant expressly agrees to shorter notice or another time for that entry (NRS 118A.330(3)).
  • Don’t use the right of entry to harass the tenant.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 60 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 118A.300
Good to know
  • Serve it at least 60 days before the first increased payment; 30 days for a periodic tenancy shorter than a month (NRS 118A.300).
  • Serve it the way NRS 40.280 provides (NRS 118A.190(2)).
  • During a fixed-term lease, the rent changes only as the lease allows. State the rent as one figure that includes every mandatory fee (NRS 118A.200(6)).
  • Nevada has no rent cap. You may not raise rent to retaliate (NRS 118A.510).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 118A.242(4)
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the tenancy ends, give the tenant this written accounting and the rest of the deposit, in hand where the rent is paid or by mail (NRS 118A.242(4)).
  • Keeping it wrongfully costs the whole deposit, plus up to that much again as the court decides (NRS 118A.242(6)).
  • The deposit (with any surety bond and last month’s rent) can’t be more than 3 months’ rent. Only a reasonable cleaning charge may be nonrefundable (NRS 118A.242(1), (8)). No interest is required.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Nev. Rev. Stat. § 118A.460(1)
Good to know
  • Store the property safely for 30 days after the abandonment, eviction or end of the rental period; you’re liable only for negligent or wrongful acts in storing it (NRS 118A.460(1)(a)).
  • Then you may dispose of it if you made reasonable efforts to find the tenant, mailed them this notice, and 14 days have passed since (NRS 118A.460(1)(b)). Vehicles go through the abandoned-vehicle law (NRS ch. 487).
  • For 5 days after an eviction or lockout, give the tenant a reasonable chance to retrieve essentials: medication, baby formula, basic clothing, personal care items (NRS 118A.460(3)).
