Nevada 30-day no-cause notice to quit

Ends a month-to-month (or other periodic) tenancy at least 30 days out, no reason needed.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenYou want a periodic tenant out.
  • The lawNev. Rev. Stat. § 40.251(1)(b)(1); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.251(2)–(6); Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.280(1)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Any date at least 30 calendar days after service works; it needn’t end a rental period. A week-to-week tenancy needs at least 7 days, and the age and disability extension doesn’t apply to it (NRS 40.251(1)(b), (2)).
  • The notice must tell the tenant about the age, disability and shutdown protections (NRS 40.251(5)); NOHM’s does.
  • After the date passes, serve the 5-day notice of unlawful detainer (NRS 40.254) to begin a summary eviction.
  • After a foreclosure, a new owner gives at least 60 days after a notice of change of ownership (NRS 40.255(2)).
  • No reason is needed, but you may not end a tenancy to retaliate (NRS 118A.510).
  • A sheriff, constable, licensed process server or an attorney’s agent must serve it; you can’t serve it yourself (NRS 40.280(1)). The server signs a statement of the date and manner of service with their badge or license number; an attorney’s agent’s needs the attorney’s declaration (NRS 40.280(5)).
