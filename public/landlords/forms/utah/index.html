Notices before court.

3-business-day notice to pay or vacate

Demands the rent and other amounts due, or the home back, within 3 business days after service.

When
Rent is past due (any day after it’s due).
The law
Utah Code § 78B-6-802(1)(c); Utah Code § 78B-6-805(1)
Good to know
  • You may serve it any time after the rent is due (Utah Code § 78B-6-802(1)(c)); Utah has no grace period by statute.
  • Business days: start counting with the business day after service; weekends and legal holidays (Utah’s include Pioneer Day, July 24, and Good Friday) don’t count.
  • The courts’ own form is 1001EV, “Three Day Notice to Pay or to Vacate”.
  • Serve it as Utah Code § 78B-6-805(1) allows: in person; by registered or certified mail (or an equivalent) to the tenant’s home; with a person of suitable age and discretion there if the tenant is out; or, if no one is there, posted in a conspicuous place on the property. The statute doesn’t require mailing a second copy after leaving or posting it, but the court’s return of service form asks about one; mailing one too is good practice. NOHM adds 3 days when you send it by mail.
  • For a property covered by the CARES Act, its 30-day notice to vacate applies before removal, but the tenant still gets only three business days to pay (§ 78B-6-802(5)).

3-day notice to comply with lease or vacate

Says what lease term the tenant broke: comply within 3 calendar days, or leave.

When
The tenant broke a lease term (other than rent).
The law
Utah Code § 78B-6-802(1)(h); Utah Code § 78B-6-805(1); Utah Code § 68-3-7
Good to know
  • Serve it on the tenant and on any subtenant living there (Utah Code § 78B-6-802(1)(h)). The courts’ form is 1005EV.
  • Some breaches get a 3-calendar-day notice to quit with no chance to comply: assigning or subletting against the lease, or waste (§ 78B-6-802(1)(d), forms 1020EV, 1025EV); an unlawful business (1030EV); a nuisance (1015EV, 1015.5EV); a criminal act on the premises (1040EV); and a violation that can’t be brought into compliance (§ 78B-6-802(2), 1035EV). Ask a lawyer before using one; since H.B. 591 (May 6, 2026), abating a nuisance by eviction has its own action (§ 78B-6a-303).
  • Serve it as Utah Code § 78B-6-805(1) allows: in person; by registered or certified mail (or an equivalent) to the tenant’s home; with a person of suitable age and discretion there if the tenant is out; or, if no one is there, posted in a conspicuous place on the property. The statute doesn’t require mailing a second copy after leaving or posting it, but the court’s return of service form asks about one; mailing one too is good practice. NOHM adds 3 days when you send it by mail.

15-day notice to vacate (month-to-month)

Ends a month-to-month (or other periodic) tenancy at the end of a rental period, served at least 15 calendar days before.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Utah Code § 78B-6-802(1)(b)(i); Utah Code § 78B-6-805(1)
Good to know
  • A month-to-month, a week-to-week, or any tenancy with periodic rent ends at the end of a period on 15 calendar days’ notice (Utah Code § 78B-6-802(1)(b)(i)). The courts’ form is 1045EV.
  • A tenancy at will ends on 5 calendar days’ notice (§ 78B-6-802(1)(b)(ii), form 1050EV).
  • A fixed-term lease ends at the end of its term without notice (§ 78B-6-802(1)(a)): you may file once the tenant holds over. For a periodic tenancy this notice is the only notice the law uses, so NOHM has no separate demand for possession.
  • Utah has no just-cause rule, and cities can’t control rents or fees (§ 57-20-1).
  • Serve it as Utah Code § 78B-6-805(1) allows: in person; by registered or certified mail (or an equivalent) to the tenant’s home; with a person of suitable age and discretion there if the tenant is out; or, if no one is there, posted in a conspicuous place on the property. The statute doesn’t require mailing a second copy after leaving or posting it, but the court’s return of service form asks about one; mailing one too is good practice. NOHM adds 3 days when you send it by mail.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (1100EV, 1105EV, 1044XX) Court

Everything the Utah State Courts’ eviction forms ask for, filled in from the lease, with the forms and the steps after filing.

When
The notice ran out, or the lease ended, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Utah Code § 78B-6-807; Utah Code § 78B-6-810; Utah Code § 78B-6-811
Good to know
  • Utah’s district courts use the courts’ approved eviction forms (the Online Court Assistance Program). File those, filled in from this worksheet; the worksheet isn’t filed.
  • The courts say their self-help pages haven’t been updated for H.B. 591 (May 6, 2026); check the form versions before you file.
  • Bring the lease, every notice and its return of service, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Utah Code § 57-22-4(6)
Good to know
  • Before signing the lease, Utah requires one of three things: a written inventory of the unit’s condition (normal wear and tear excepted), a form like this one for the tenant to complete and return within a reasonable time after moving in, or a walkthrough inspection (Utah Code § 57-22-4(6)).
  • Before taking an application fee, give a written good-faith estimate of the rent and each fixed non-rent cost, the type of each usage-based cost, the date the unit is available, your screening criteria, and how an applicant gets money back if the terms differ (§ 57-22-4(3)).
  • At or before move-in, give in writing the owner’s name, address and phone (or the manager’s, or an agent for notices), a signed copy of the lease, and the rules (§ 57-22-4(7)). Any non-refundable part of the deposit must be stated in writing when the deposit is taken (§ 57-17-2).
  • A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Utah Code § 57-22-4(2)
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ notice, unless the lease says otherwise (Utah Code § 57-22-4(2)).
  • A renter may not unreasonably deny you entry to make repairs (§ 57-22-5(2)(c)). Utah sets no hours or emergency rule; come at reasonable times.

The notice of entry in full · Read the law: le.utah.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Utah statute sets notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date; a month-to-month can be ended on 15 days’ notice before a period ends (Utah Code § 78B-6-802(1)(b)), so 30 days is more than that.
  • Adding a new fee to a month-to-month takes 15 days’ notice (§ 57-22-4(5)(b)). During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Utah has no rent cap, and cities and counties can’t control rents or fees (§ 57-20-1).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Utah Code § 57-17-3
Good to know
  • No later than 30 days after the tenant vacates and returns possession, mail or deliver to their last known address (or send electronically by a means they gave you) the deposit’s balance, the balance of any prepaid rent, and this itemized notice (Utah Code § 57-17-3(2)).
  • Deduct only rent, damage beyond reasonable wear and tear, other costs and fees the lease provides for, and cleaning (§ 57-17-3(1)). A non-refundable part must have been stated in writing when the deposit was taken (§ 57-17-2).
  • If the tenant serves the statutory “Tenant’s Notice to Provide Deposit Disposition”, you have 5 business days to comply (§ 57-17-3(3), (5)); after that, failing to costs the full deposit and prepaid rent, a $100 penalty, and attorney fees for bad faith (§ 57-17-5).
  • No cap, interest or separate account.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Utah Code § 78B-6-816(2)
Good to know
  • Post a copy in a conspicuous place and send one by first class mail to the tenant’s last known address (Utah Code § 78B-6-816(2)(b)). The tenant may get it back within 15 calendar days by paying the costs of inventory, moving and storage.
  • After 15 calendar days in storage with no reasonable effort to recover it, you may sell it at a public sale (mail notice of the sale at least 5 calendar days before) or donate it if that is commercially reasonable; pay any surplus to the tenant or as unclaimed property (§ 78B-6-816(9), (11)).
  • Up to 15 more days for documented domestic violence, hospitalization or death (§ 78B-6-816(7)). Hazardous items, pets, garbage and perishables needn’t be stored; vehicles have their own rules.
  • Property left after an order of restitution is handled the same way (§ 78B-6-812(4)(d)).

Declaration of abandonment

The statutory declaration that lets you retake a home the tenant appears to have abandoned, unless they dispute it in writing within 24 hours.

When
Rent is unpaid and the tenant seems to have left for good.
The law
Utah Code § 78B-6-815(2)
Good to know
  • A home is presumed abandoned when the tenant gave no notice of an absence, rent is 15 days past due, and nothing shows anyone living there but their belongings; or when rent is unpaid and their belongings are gone (Utah Code § 78B-6-815(1)).
  • Serve it the way the eviction notices are served (§ 78B-6-805). The 24 hours don’t count Saturdays, Sundays or holidays the courts are closed.
  • If the tenant doesn’t dispute it in writing at your contact address within the 24 hours, you may retake possession. Then handle what they left with the notice of abandoned property (§ 78B-6-816).
