Notices before court.

3-day notice to pay rent

Tells the tenant the rental agreement ends if the rent isn’t paid within 3 days (weekends and holidays not counted).

When
Rent is due and unpaid (Mississippi has no grace period).
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-13(5)(a); Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-7(1)(k); Miss. Code Ann. § 1-3-67
Good to know
  • Mississippi sets no grace period before this notice: serve it once rent is due and unpaid.
  • Email or text counts only if the tenant agreed in writing to be notified that way; NOHM has a consent form. NOHM adds 3 days if you mail it.
  • Even after a judgment for nonpayment, the tenant can pay everything the judgment awards by the court’s move-out date, and you must accept full payment in good faith (§ 89-8-39(4)).
  • At the hearing the judge must follow the rental agreement you and the tenant signed (§ 89-8-13(5)(b)).
  • Since 2022, residential evictions in Mississippi are under Miss. Code Ann. §§ 89-8-31 to 89-8-45. File a copy of this notice with your complaint (§ 89-8-33(b)).

14-day notice of breach

Says what the tenant did wrong: remedy it within 14 days, or the rental agreement ends.

When
The tenant broke the rental agreement or a tenant’s duty under the law (other than rent).
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-13(3); Miss. Code Ann. § 1-3-67
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the law requires the notice to specify it.
  • If substantially the same breach happens again within 6 months, you can end the agreement on 14 days’ written notice with no chance to cure (§ 89-8-13(3)(b)). For a violation that materially affects health or safety, no notice is required (§ 89-8-19(4)).
  • You can’t end it for a condition you or your family or guests caused (§ 89-8-13(3)(c)).
  • Since 2022, residential evictions in Mississippi are under Miss. Code Ann. §§ 89-8-31 to 89-8-45. File a copy of this notice with your complaint (§ 89-8-33(b)).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a date at least 30 days after the notice.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-19(3); Miss. Code Ann. § 1-3-67
Good to know
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ written notice instead (Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-19(2)). The date doesn’t have to end a rental period.
  • A lease with a fixed term ends on its own date (§ 89-8-17(1)); for a tenant who stays after it, give the 3-day notice of holding over (§ 89-8-17(2)).
  • If the tenant stays after the date, you may file to evict (§ 89-8-31(b)).
  • You may raise the rent after a rental agreement ends, but not mainly to retaliate against the tenant (§ 89-8-17(2)).
  • Since 2022, residential evictions in Mississippi are under Miss. Code Ann. §§ 89-8-31 to 89-8-45. File a copy of this notice with your complaint (§ 89-8-33(b)).

Notice of holding over (3 days)

Tells a tenant who stayed after the lease expired that you will file to evict no earlier than 3 days later.

When
A lease with a fixed term expired and the tenant is still there.
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-17(2); Miss. Code Ann. § 1-3-67
Good to know
  • For a lease with a fixed term that has expired: tell the tenant they are holding over, and that you will file no earlier than 3 days after this notice, not counting intermediate weekends and holidays (Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-17(2)). File a copy with your complaint (§ 89-8-33(b)).
  • A month-to-month ended with the 30-day notice needs no further notice: that notice goes with your complaint.
  • Unless your lease says otherwise, you may charge double rent for the time a tenant stays after you notified them to leave (§ 89-8-45).
  • Email or text counts only if the tenant agreed to it in writing.

Court papers.

Complaint for residential eviction Court

The eviction filing, sworn, with the summons in the words the law requires for the clerk to issue.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid, remedied the breach or left.
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-31; Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-33; Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-35
Good to know
  • File in justice court, county court or circuit court in the county where the rental is (Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-7(1)(b)). Justice court has a limit on money claims (§ 9-11-9; NOHM didn’t check the amount). The courts post sample forms only; none is required.
  • Attach a copy of the written notice you delivered: the notice of breach or to pay rent, the 30-day notice, or the notice of holding over (§ 89-8-33(b)). Swear to the complaint before the clerk or a notary.
  • The last page is the summons, in the words § 89-8-35 requires since the 2025 amendments, for the clerk to fill in and issue; the courts’ sample summons is older and lacks two sentences. If the clerk uses their own, check it has the same words. It is served under the Mississippi Rules of Court.
  • One adjournment can’t be longer than 10 days, and the whole case can’t go past 30 days after filing, unless you and the tenant agree (§ 89-8-41). A judgment is signed the same business day (§ 89-8-37).
  • The judge orders the tenant out in 7 days unless an emergency justifies a shorter or longer time. After that date, ask for a warrant for removal; law enforcement must remove the occupants, not just post the warrant. The tenant then has 72 hours of reasonable access to take their belongings (§ 89-8-39).
  • In a nonpayment case the tenant can still pay everything the judgment awards by the move-out date, and you must accept full payment in good faith (§ 89-8-39(4)).
  • NOHM found no rule letting a non-lawyer appear for a company; if your rental is owned by an LLC or corporation, use a lawyer.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Mississippi doesn’t require a move-in checklist; it is good practice, and helps if the deposit is disputed.
  • Mississippi requires no other written disclosure at move-in, apart from the federal lead disclosure for homes built before 1978. If you want to send notices by email or text, get the tenant’s written consent (NOHM has the form).
  • A lease can’t include a confession of judgment, or excuse you from liability for your own willful misconduct (Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-5).

Consent to notices by email or text

The tenant’s written agreement to be notified by email or text message, which Mississippi requires before a notice counts that way.

When
At lease signing, or any time the tenant agrees.
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-13(3), (5); Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-17(2)
Good to know
  • A notice of breach, a notice to pay rent or a notice of holding over counts by email or text only if the tenant agreed in writing to be notified that way (Miss. Code Ann. §§ 89-8-13, 89-8-17). Have every adult tenant sign.
  • The 30-day notice to end a month-to-month isn’t on that list: deliver it in writing.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • Mississippi has no statute on entry; your lease decides. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours’ notice.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • Mississippi sets no notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date. For a month-to-month, the clean way is to end it with the 30-day notice and offer new terms.
  • During a lease, change the rent only as the lease allows. After it expires you may raise the rent, but not mainly to retaliate against the tenant (Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-17(2)).
  • Mississippi has no rent cap.

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 45 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-21(3)
Good to know
  • Return what is left no later than 45 days after the tenancy ends, the tenant gives back possession and asks for it (Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-21(3)). Itemize every amount you keep in this written notice.
  • Keeping any of it wrongly and in bad faith can cost up to $200 on top of the tenant’s actual damages (§ 89-8-21(4)).
  • Mississippi sets no cap on deposits and requires no interest.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-39(3)
Good to know
  • For the 72 hours after law enforcement executes the warrant for removal, give the tenant reasonable access to take their belongings; after that you may move what is left to the curb, a garbage area or a place you both agree on (Miss. Code Ann. § 89-8-39(3)).
  • If the tenant moved out by the court’s move-out date and left things, you may dispose of them without further notice (§ 89-8-39(1)).
  • Without a court case, Mississippi has no statute on property left behind: hold it longer (NOHM suggests 30 days) and change the date.
  • A federal court in northern Mississippi reportedly found parts of the state’s property-disposal scheme unconstitutional (news reports; NOHM didn’t read the decision). Be cautious, and photograph everything.
