Nebraska complaint for restitution of premises

The eviction filing: asks the court to restore the rental to you, with the facts and notice the law requires.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenThe notice ran out, or the lease ended, and the tenant hasn’t left.
  • The lawNeb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1441; Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1437(3); Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1446
  • StageCourt papers
  • KindCourt paper

What to know.

  • File it with the clerk of the county court (or district court) of the county where the rental is. Nebraska has no statewide complaint form (the Judicial Branch says none is available), so NOHM writes it with the four things § 76-1441(1) requires.
  • The clerk issues the summons with the time and place of trial. It must be served within 3 days of issuance, not counting nonjudicial days, and is returnable within 5 (§ 76-1442). If diligent efforts to serve it fail, it may be posted on the front door and mailed first class, with your affidavit (the next form); then you can’t get a money judgment (§ 76-1442.01, § 76-1446).
  • Trial is held 10 to 14 days after the summons issues, by the judge without a jury (§ 76-1446). The tenant may answer on or before the appearance day; if they don’t appear, the court tries the case as though they were there (§ 76-1444, § 76-1445).
  • If you win, ask for the writ of restitution (the praecipe); it directs the sheriff or constable to restore possession on a date not more than 10 days after it issues (§ 76-1446).
  • Claims for rent or damages are other causes of action: the tenant can ask in writing for them to be answered and tried separately (§ 76-1441(1)).
  • No bond is needed to file. A tenant who appeals stays the writ only by depositing the judgment and costs or an appeal bond, and paying rent monthly into court (§ 76-1447).
  • Except in small claims court, a corporation or LLC landlord must be represented by an attorney (Nebraska Judicial Branch).
  • Never lock the tenant out or cut off utilities (§ 76-1430).
