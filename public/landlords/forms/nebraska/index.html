Notices before court.

7-day notice to pay rent or quit

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid: pay it within 7 calendar days, or the rental agreement ends.

When
Rent is past due (Nebraska has no grace period).
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1431(2); Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1413(2)(c)
Good to know
  • Nebraska has no grace period: rent is due without demand when the rental agreement says (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1414(3)).
  • Accepting rent knowing of the default waives your right to terminate for it (§ 76-1433): taking a late payment after the 7 days generally ends this notice.
  • The 7 days are calendar days. If the last one falls on a weekend or court holiday, NOHM moves it to the next business day; Nebraska’s time rule (§ 25-2221) may not reach a notice, and later is the safe side.
  • Hand it to the tenant or mail it; the statute adds no days for mail, NOHM adds 3 for delivery. Email works only if the tenant affirmatively consented after getting the statements § 76-1413(7) lists, and you can’t require it as a condition of renting (§ 76-1413(9)); if you don’t get the receipt the law asks for, mail it (§ 76-1413(11)). Nebraska has no posting on the door.
  • Nebraska needs no other demand before you file: a tenant who stays after the rental agreement ends is sued for possession (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1437(3)).
  • A rental the Act doesn’t cover (a hotel, employee housing, a lease of land for 5 years or more) uses a 3-day notice to leave instead (§ 25-21,221).

14-day notice to remedy a breach (30 days to end)

Tells the tenant what they did wrong: remedy it within 14 days, or the rental agreement ends at least 30 days after they got the notice.

When
The tenant materially broke the rental agreement (other than rent).
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1431(1); Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1413(2)(c)
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the notice must specify it.
  • The same breach again within 6 months of this notice: 14 days’ written notice terminates the agreement, with no chance to remedy (§ 76-1431(1)).
  • Violent criminal activity, sale of a controlled substance, or another threat to health or safety on the premises: 5 days’ written notice, with no right to cure (§ 76-1431(4)). Not against a tenant who is the victim and sought a protective order or reported it to the police (§ 76-1431(5)). NOHM’s notice is the 14-day one.
  • A tenant who refuses lawful access can be given this notice (§ 76-1438(1)). A rule is enforceable only if the tenant had notice of it when the lease was signed (§ 76-1422(6)).
  • Hand it to the tenant or mail it; the statute adds no days for mail, NOHM adds 3 for delivery. Email works only if the tenant affirmatively consented after getting the statements § 76-1413(7) lists, and you can’t require it as a condition of renting (§ 76-1413(9)); if you don’t get the receipt the law asks for, mail it (§ 76-1413(11)). Nebraska has no posting on the door.
  • Nebraska needs no other demand before you file: a tenant who stays after the rental agreement ends is sued for possession (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1437(3)).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rental date at least 30 days out, no reason needed.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out.
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1437(2); Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1413(2)(c)
Good to know
  • The date must be a periodic rental date at least 30 days out (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1437(2)). A week-to-week tenancy needs 7 days’ written notice (§ 76-1437(1)).
  • No reason is needed, but you may not end a tenancy to retaliate for a complaint or tenants’ union activity (§ 76-1439).
  • A tenant whose holdover is willful and not in good faith can owe up to 3 months’ rent or three times your damages, whichever is more, and your attorney’s fees (§ 76-1437(3)).
  • Hand it to the tenant or mail it; the statute adds no days for mail, NOHM adds 3 for delivery. Email works only if the tenant affirmatively consented after getting the statements § 76-1413(7) lists, and you can’t require it as a condition of renting (§ 76-1413(9)); if you don’t get the receipt the law asks for, mail it (§ 76-1413(11)). Nebraska has no posting on the door.
  • Nebraska needs no other demand before you file: a tenant who stays after the rental agreement ends is sued for possession (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1437(3)).

Court papers.

Complaint for restitution of premises Court

The eviction filing: asks the court to restore the rental to you, with the facts and notice the law requires.

When
The notice ran out, or the lease ended, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1441; Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1437(3); Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1446
Good to know
  • File it with the clerk of the county court (or district court) of the county where the rental is. Nebraska has no statewide complaint form (the Judicial Branch says none is available), so NOHM writes it with the four things § 76-1441(1) requires.
  • The clerk issues the summons with the time and place of trial. It must be served within 3 days of issuance, not counting nonjudicial days, and is returnable within 5 (§ 76-1442). If diligent efforts to serve it fail, it may be posted on the front door and mailed first class, with your affidavit (the next form); then you can’t get a money judgment (§ 76-1442.01, § 76-1446).
  • Trial is held 10 to 14 days after the summons issues, by the judge without a jury (§ 76-1446). The tenant may answer on or before the appearance day; if they don’t appear, the court tries the case as though they were there (§ 76-1444, § 76-1445).
  • If you win, ask for the writ of restitution (the praecipe); it directs the sheriff or constable to restore possession on a date not more than 10 days after it issues (§ 76-1446).
  • Claims for rent or damages are other causes of action: the tenant can ask in writing for them to be answered and tried separately (§ 76-1441(1)).
  • No bond is needed to file. A tenant who appeals stays the writ only by depositing the judgment and costs or an appeal bond, and paying rent monthly into court (§ 76-1447).
  • Except in small claims court, a corporation or LLC landlord must be represented by an attorney (Nebraska Judicial Branch).
  • Never lock the tenant out or cut off utilities (§ 76-1430).

Affidavit of diligent efforts and service by posting and mailing Court

Your sworn statement that the summons couldn’t be served the usual way, so it was posted on the door and mailed.

When
Diligent efforts to serve the summons failed, and it was posted and mailed instead.
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1442.01
Good to know
  • Use it only after diligent efforts to serve the summons in the usual ways (§§ 25-505.01 to 25-516.01) failed. Then a copy is posted on the front door of the unit and mailed by first-class mail to the tenant’s last-known address (§ 76-1442.01).
  • With service this way, the court can give you possession but no money judgment (§ 76-1446).
  • Sign it in front of a notary, and file it with the court.

Praecipe for writ of restitution (CC 3:24) Court

Asks the clerk to issue the writ that has the sheriff or constable put you back in possession.

When
The court entered judgment for restitution of the premises.
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1446
Good to know
  • The writ issues at your request after judgment, and restores possession on a date not more than 10 days after it issues (§ 76-1446).
  • This follows the Nebraska Supreme Court’s form CC 3:24, Praecipe for Writ of Restitution/Execution. If your clerk asks for that form itself, it’s at supremecourt.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/CC-3-24.pdf.
  • Remove property left on the premises only as the Disposition of Personal Property Landlord and Tenant Act allows (§ 76-1446; the property form).

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Nebraska doesn’t require a move-in checklist, but a signed one is the best proof of the home’s condition when you itemize the deposit. Keep it with the lease.
  • At or before the start of the tenancy, tell the tenant in writing the name and address of the person managing the premises, and of an owner or the person authorized to accept service and notices for the owner (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1417).
  • Give the tenant instructions for testing the smoke detector, with the battery working at move-in (§ 81-5,144(2)); have a working carbon monoxide alarm on each habitable floor and give the tenant its batteries (§ 76-606).
  • Give the tenant your rules when the lease is signed: a rule is enforceable only if they had notice of it then (§ 76-1422(6)).
  • To send notices by email, get the tenant’s affirmative consent after the statements § 76-1413(7) requires (the right to withdraw it, the notices it covers, paper copies, how to update the address); it can’t be a condition of renting.
  • The deposit can’t be more than one month’s rent, plus a pet deposit of up to a quarter month’s rent (§ 76-1416(1)).
  • A home built before 1978 also needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1423(3)
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ written notice to each unit, with the purpose and a reasonable time window, and enter only at reasonable times, except in an emergency or where it’s impracticable (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1423(3)).
  • Don’t use the right of entry to harass the tenant. Abuse of access can cost at least one month’s rent (§ 76-1438(2)).
  • During an absence of more than 7 days you may enter when reasonably necessary (§ 76-1432(2)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Nebraska statute sets the notice for a rent increase. A month-to-month tenancy can be ended on 30 days’ written notice to a rental date (§ 76-1437(2)), so NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date.
  • During a fixed-term lease, the rent changes only as the lease allows.
  • Nebraska has no rent cap, and cities can’t enact rent control (§ 13-331).
  • You may not raise rent to retaliate for a complaint or tenants’ union activity (§ 76-1439).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 14 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 76-1416(2)
Good to know
  • Deliver or mail the balance and the itemization within 14 days after the tenancy ends. With no address from the tenant, mail them first class to the last-known mailing address (§ 76-1416(2)).
  • If the mail comes back, or the refund goes unclaimed for a year, it’s abandoned property to report to the State Treasurer.
  • Keeping it wrongfully costs the deposit, court costs and attorney’s fees; if willful and not in good faith, also one month’s rent or twice the deposit, whichever is less (§ 76-1416(3)).
  • The deposit can’t be more than one month’s rent, plus a pet deposit of up to a quarter month’s rent (§ 76-1416(1)). No interest is required.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Neb. Rev. Stat. § 69-2303; Neb. Rev. Stat. § 69-2304; Neb. Rev. Stat. § 69-2305(1)
Good to know
  • Give this notice within 6 months after the lease expired or you discovered the abandonment, whichever is later (§ 69-2303(2)(d)).
  • The claim date must be at least 7 days after you hand it to the tenant, or 14 days after you mail it (first class, postage prepaid, to the last-known address, and to any other address where they’re likelier to get it) (§ 69-2303(2)(c), (3)).
  • Send the same notice to anyone else you believe owns the property (§ 69-2305(2)).
  • Store the property with reasonable care, on the premises or elsewhere; you may charge reasonable storage costs (§ 69-2306, § 69-2310).
  • If it isn’t claimed, sell it at a public sale after advertising it once a week for two weeks; if you reasonably believe it’s worth less than $2,000, you may keep or dispose of it. Pay the net proceeds to the State Treasurer within 30 days (§ 69-2308).
  • If the tenant asks in writing within 14 days of moving out, give them an itemized demand for your removal and storage costs within 5 days; they then have 72 hours to remove the property (§ 69-2311).
