New York 14-day rent demand

Demands the rent owed, or the home back, within 14 days, with the Good Cause Eviction Law notice attached.

At a glance.

  • WhenRent is unpaid; if it’s 5 days late, send the late-rent notice by certified mail first.
  • The lawN.Y. Real Prop. Acts. Law § 711(2); N.Y. Real Prop. Acts. Law § 735(1); N.Y. Real Prop. Acts. Law § 702(1); N.Y. Real Prop. Law § 231-c(1); N.Y. Gen. Constr. Law § 25-a(1)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • First, if rent isn’t received within 5 days of the due date, send the tenant a written notice of nonpayment by certified mail (Real Prop. Law § 235-e(d)); NOHM has it as the 5-day late-rent notice. Without it, the tenant has a defense to the case.
  • You can’t serve it yourself: someone 18 or older who isn’t a party to the case must (UCS Landlord’s Guide). Leave it with someone, or post it, only after a reasonable try at handing it over; the courts’ guide reads that as two tries at different times.
  • Attach the Good Cause Eviction Law notice, filled in to say whether the premises are covered and, if exempt, why (Real Prop. Law § 231-c). Get it from the courts’ Landlord’s Guide or New York State Homes and Community Renewal.
  • When service by leaving it with someone or posting it is complete isn’t settled for a rent demand; NOHM adds 3 days when you serve it that way.
  • If the tenant pays all the rent due before the hearing, you must accept it and the case ends (RPAPL § 731(4)).
  • Late fees: only after rent is 5 days late, and at most $50 or 5% of the monthly rent, whichever is less (Real Prop. Law § 238-a(2)). They can’t go in this demand.
  • Where the Good Cause Eviction Law applies, unpaid rent is good cause only if it didn’t come from an unreasonable rent increase (Real Prop. Law § 216(1)(a)).
  • New York City’s Housing Court, and rent-stabilized units, have rules of their own; use a lawyer there.
