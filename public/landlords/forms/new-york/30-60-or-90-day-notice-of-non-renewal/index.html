New York 30, 60 or 90-day notice of non-renewal

Tells the tenant you won’t renew, or ends a month-to-month, with the notice the time they’ve lived there calls for.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenYou won’t renew the lease, or want to end a month-to-month tenancy.
  • The lawN.Y. Real Prop. Law § 226-c(1)(a); N.Y. Real Prop. Law § 226-c(2); N.Y. Real Prop. Law § 232-a; N.Y. Real Prop. Law § 231-c(1)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Real Prop. Law § 226-c(2) sets the notice by the longer of how long the tenant has lived there and the lease’s term: under a year, 30 days; one to two years, 60; two years or more, 90. NOHM counts from the lease and ends the tenancy on the last day of a rental period, to be safe.
  • Given late, the tenancy goes on under its existing terms until the notice period has run from the day you gave it (§ 226-c(1)(a)).
  • Where the Good Cause Eviction Law applies (New York City and the cities, towns and villages that opted in), a covered tenancy can end only for a reason in Real Prop. Law § 216; say which on the attached notice. Check whether your locality opted in.
  • In New York City, the notice must be served the way a notice of petition is (Real Prop. Law § 232-a); NOHM’s ways of serving are those (RPAPL § 735), and NOHM adds 3 days when you leave it with someone or post it.
  • A fixed-term lease ends on its date with no notice to terminate, but this non-renewal notice is still required (§ 226-c).
  • Taking rent after the date can create a new tenancy, and a judge may dismiss your case (UCS Landlord’s Guide).
  • New York requires no separate demand for possession: if the tenant stays after the date, you may bring a holdover proceeding (RPAPL § 711(1)).
