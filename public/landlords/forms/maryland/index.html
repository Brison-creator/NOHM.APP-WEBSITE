Notices before court.

10-day notice of intent to file (DC-CV-115)

The Judiciary’s required notice that you’ll file for unpaid rent if it isn’t paid within 10 days; served with form DC-CV-115.

When
Rent is past due (Maryland has no grace period).
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-401(c); Md. Code, Gen. Prov. § 1-302
Good to know
  • The law requires this notice to be in a form created by the Maryland Judiciary: DC-CV-115 (RP § 8-401(c)(2)). Fill in DC-CV-115 with these amounts and dates and serve it with NOHM’s notice, which carries its words and works out the deadline. Get it at https://www.mdcourts.gov/sites/default/files/court-forms/dccv115.pdf.
  • Notice occurs when it is sent by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, affixed to the door, or, if the tenant chose it, sent by e-mail, text or tenant portal with proof of transmission (RP § 8-401(c)(2)). A lease can’t require the tenant to accept it electronically (§ 8-208(d)(9)).
  • Wait the full 10 days before you file. A Sunday or legal holiday at the end moves the last day to the next day (Gen. Prov. § 1-302); NOHM moves a Saturday too, to be safe. Your complaint (DC-CV-082) must state the date you gave this notice (§ 8-401(c)(3)).
  • Rent can be demanded once it is unpaid when due; Maryland has no grace period (RP § 8-401(a)).
  • After a judgment the tenant may still redeem the home by paying all past-due amounts the court found, with court costs and fees, before the eviction is carried out, unless they had three judgments for unpaid rent in the 12 months before (RP § 8-401(h)).
  • Baltimore City, Montgomery County, Prince George’s County and Takoma Park have their own rules that may add to these; check yours.

Read the law: mgaleg.maryland.gov, mdcourts.gov

30-day notice of breach of lease

Tells a tenant under a lease for a stated term that they are in breach and you want the home back, 30 days before you can file.

When
The tenant breached a lease that lets you repossess for a breach.
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-402.1(a)(1)(i); Md. Code, Gen. Prov. § 1-302
Good to know
  • Only for a lease for a stated term that lets you repossess the home if the tenant breaches it (RP § 8-402.1(a)). For a month-to-month, end it with the 60-day notice instead.
  • If the breach shows a clear and imminent danger of the tenant, or someone there with the tenant’s consent, doing serious harm to themselves, other tenants, you, your property or representatives, or anyone else on the property, the law allows 14 days’ notice. NOHM’s notice gives 30.
  • The court will order possession only if it finds the breach was substantial and warrants an eviction (RP § 8-402.1(b)(1)).
  • Taking payment after this notice isn’t a waiver of it, unless you agree to that in writing (RP § 8-402.1(d)(1)).
  • Maryland’s law doesn’t say how to deliver this notice. Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class with a certificate of mailing (NOHM adds 3 days), and keep a copy: the court’s complaint form asks for the date and has you attach it.
  • Baltimore City, Montgomery County, Prince George’s County and Takoma Park have their own rules that may add to these; check yours.

Read the law: mgaleg.maryland.gov

60-day notice to end a tenancy

Ends a month-to-month, or a lease at the end of its term, on 60 days’ written notice before it expires.

When
You want the tenant out when the month or the lease ends, no fault needed.
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-402(c)(2)(i)
Good to know
  • A month-to-month, or a written lease for a stated term of more than a week, needs 60 days’ written notice before the tenancy expires; a fixed lease doesn’t end on its own (RP § 8-402(c)(2)(i)). A tenancy from year to year needs 90 days before the year ends; week to week, 7 days with a written lease, 21 without.
  • No separate demand is needed: if the tenant stays, this notice is the one the holdover complaint (DC-CV-080) asks for and has you attach (RP § 8-402(b)).
  • Taking rent after the notice isn’t a waiver of it, unless you agree to that in writing (RP § 8-402(c)(5)). A lease can’t set a shorter notice (§ 8-208(d)(5)).
  • A holdover tenant owes your actual damages, at least the rent for the time held over (RP § 8-402(a)).
  • Maryland’s law doesn’t say how to deliver this notice. Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class with a certificate of mailing (NOHM adds 3 days), and keep a copy: the court’s complaint form asks for the date and has you attach it.
  • Baltimore City, Montgomery County, Prince George’s County and Takoma Park have their own rules that may add to these; check yours.

Read the law: mgaleg.maryland.gov

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (DC-CV-115, DC-CV-082, DC-CV-080, DC-CV-085) Court

Everything the Maryland Judiciary’s notice and complaint forms ask for, filled in from the lease, with where to get each form and the steps and deadlines to the eviction.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid or left.
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-401; Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-402; Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-402.1; Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-407
Good to know
  • Maryland’s District Court uses the Judiciary’s forms: DC-CV-115 before a rent case, then DC-CV-082 (the court requires its carbonless multi-part form, from any District Court location), DC-CV-080 or DC-CV-085. Fill them in from this worksheet; this worksheet isn’t filed.
  • Each complaint is affirmed under the penalties of perjury: check every amount against the ledger.
  • A rental license is required in some counties and cities; where it is, the complaint must give it (Real Prop. § 8-406).
  • In a failure-to-pay-rent case anyone may represent you (Bus. Occ. & Prof. § 10-206(b)(1)). In a holdover or breach case a business may need a lawyer; the Judiciary says only that some entities must have one, and NOHM couldn’t confirm the rule. Ask the clerk.
  • Baltimore City, Montgomery County, Prince George’s County and Takoma Park have their own rules that may add to these.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-203.1(a)(1)
Good to know
  • The tenant may ask, by certified mail within 15 days after moving in, for a joint inspection and a written list of existing damage (Real Prop. § 8-203.1(a)(1)). Doing it at move-in, with this form, meets that.
  • The lease must include the deposit receipt with the tenant’s rights under the deposit law (Real Prop. § 8-203.1(a)) and a copy of the current Maryland Tenants’ Bill of Rights from the Department of Housing and Community Development (§ 8-208(c)(4)), and must state the home’s condition and who is responsible for heat, gas, electricity, water and repairs (§ 8-208(c)). A landlord who offers 5 or more units must use a written lease (§ 8-208(a)).
  • Give the State’s mold pamphlet when the lease is signed, and ask the tenant to sign that they received it (Real Prop. § 8-220(b)).
  • A lead “affected property” must be registered with the Department of the Environment and have a current inspection certificate at each change of tenant (Env. § 6-811).
  • The deposit can’t be more than one month’s rent (Real Prop. § 8-203(b)); at move-in you may ask for no more than the deposit and the first month’s rent (§ 8-208(d)(11)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-221
Good to know
  • Give written notice at least 24 hours ahead with the date, the approximate time and the specific purpose, and enter only 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., Monday through Saturday, unless the tenant agrees in writing to another time or to shorter notice (Real Prop. § 8-221).
  • Deliver it by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing (only if it will arrive at least 24 hours ahead), by affixing it to the door, or electronically if the tenant chose that, with proof of transmission (§ 8-221(c)).
  • In an emergency you may enter without it. A tenant who reports a code violation must let you in within 24 hours to fix it (§ 8-221(f)).

Read the law: mgaleg.maryland.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 60 days before the new rent starts on a month-to-month; 90 days on a lease of more than a month.
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-209
Good to know
  • Give written notice before any rent increase: at least 60 days for a tenancy of more than a week up to a month (month to month), 90 days for a lease term of more than a month; 7 days for a week-to-week with a written lease, 21 without (Real Prop. § 8-209).
  • Send it by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, or, if the tenant chose it, by e-mail, text or tenant portal with proof of transmission. A lease application can’t require the tenant to choose electronic notice (§ 8-209).
  • Not needed if you’ve already given notice to end the tenancy (§ 8-402(c)). No statewide cap; Montgomery County, Prince George’s County and Takoma Park have their own rent stabilization laws.

Read the law: mgaleg.maryland.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 45 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Md. Code, Real Prop. § 8-203
Good to know
  • Within 45 days after the tenancy ends, return the deposit with its interest, less damages rightfully withheld, and send this list of damages with the cost of each by first-class mail to the tenant’s last known address (Real Prop. § 8-203(e), (g)).
  • Interest is owed on a deposit of $50 or more held 6 months or more, for each full month, at the greater of the 1-year Treasury rate on the first business day of each year or 1.5% a year. The Department of Housing and Community Development posts the rates and a calculator you may rely on (§ 8-203(e), (m)–(n)).
  • Deduct only unpaid rent, damage from a breach of the lease, and damage beyond ordinary wear and tear (§ 8-203(f)). Attach the invoices or receipts, or an estimate followed by the final invoice and any refund within 30 days after the repair (§ 8-203(j)–(k)).
  • No list in time, or a move-out inspection the tenant asked for and didn’t get, forfeits your right to keep any of it for damages; withholding it without a reasonable basis can cost up to 3 times the amount withheld plus attorney’s fees (§ 8-203(e)(4), (f), (g)(2)).
  • A tenant who was evicted or abandoned the home must ask for it by first-class mail within 45 days; you then have 45 days (§ 8-203(h)).

Read the law: mgaleg.maryland.gov

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • No statewide statute covers a tenant’s belongings left behind; your county or city may have rules. NOHM suggests holding them at least 14 days after this notice; the date is yours to set.
  • A lease can’t let you take the tenant’s belongings unless the lease has ended and the tenant abandoned them without formal legal process (Real Prop. § 8-208(d)(6)). Taking possession any way but a warrant the sheriff executes, or the tenant’s abandonment or surrender, is unlawful (§ 8-216).
  • When a warrant is executed, the sheriff removes the tenant’s belongings; the final notice of pending eviction warns the tenant they could lose what they leave (Real Prop. §§ 8-401(f), 8-407).
  • Use it only after the tenant has left: never while they still have possession. Photograph the property before you move it.

Read the law: mgaleg.maryland.gov, mgaleg.maryland.gov
