Maryland 30-day notice of breach of lease

Tells a tenant under a lease for a stated term that they are in breach and you want the home back, 30 days before you can file.

At a glance.

  • WhenThe tenant breached a lease that lets you repossess for a breach.
  • The lawMd. Code, Real Prop. § 8-402.1(a)(1)(i); Md. Code, Gen. Prov. § 1-302
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Only for a lease for a stated term that lets you repossess the home if the tenant breaches it (RP § 8-402.1(a)). For a month-to-month, end it with the 60-day notice instead.
  • If the breach shows a clear and imminent danger of the tenant, or someone there with the tenant’s consent, doing serious harm to themselves, other tenants, you, your property or representatives, or anyone else on the property, the law allows 14 days’ notice. NOHM’s notice gives 30.
  • The court will order possession only if it finds the breach was substantial and warrants an eviction (RP § 8-402.1(b)(1)).
  • Taking payment after this notice isn’t a waiver of it, unless you agree to that in writing (RP § 8-402.1(d)(1)).
  • Maryland’s law doesn’t say how to deliver this notice. Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class with a certificate of mailing (NOHM adds 3 days), and keep a copy: the court’s complaint form asks for the date and has you attach it.
  • Baltimore City, Montgomery County, Prince George’s County and Takoma Park have their own rules that may add to these; check yours.
