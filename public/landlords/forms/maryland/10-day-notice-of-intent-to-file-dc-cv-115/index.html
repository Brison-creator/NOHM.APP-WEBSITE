Maryland 10-day notice of intent to file (DC-CV-115)

The Judiciary’s required notice that you’ll file for unpaid rent if it isn’t paid within 10 days; served with form DC-CV-115.

At a glance.

  • WhenRent is past due (Maryland has no grace period).
  • The lawMd. Code, Real Prop. § 8-401(c); Md. Code, Gen. Prov. § 1-302
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • The law requires this notice to be in a form created by the Maryland Judiciary: DC-CV-115 (RP § 8-401(c)(2)). Fill in DC-CV-115 with these amounts and dates and serve it with NOHM’s notice, which carries its words and works out the deadline. Get it at https://www.mdcourts.gov/sites/default/files/court-forms/dccv115.pdf.
  • Notice occurs when it is sent by first-class mail with a certificate of mailing, affixed to the door, or, if the tenant chose it, sent by e-mail, text or tenant portal with proof of transmission (RP § 8-401(c)(2)). A lease can’t require the tenant to accept it electronically (§ 8-208(d)(9)).
  • Wait the full 10 days before you file. A Sunday or legal holiday at the end moves the last day to the next day (Gen. Prov. § 1-302); NOHM moves a Saturday too, to be safe. Your complaint (DC-CV-082) must state the date you gave this notice (§ 8-401(c)(3)).
  • Rent can be demanded once it is unpaid when due; Maryland has no grace period (RP § 8-401(a)).
  • After a judgment the tenant may still redeem the home by paying all past-due amounts the court found, with court costs and fees, before the eviction is carried out, unless they had three judgments for unpaid rent in the 12 months before (RP § 8-401(h)).
  • Baltimore City, Montgomery County, Prince George’s County and Takoma Park have their own rules that may add to these; check yours.
