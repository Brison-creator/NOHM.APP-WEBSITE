North Dakota 3-day notice of intention to evict (lease violation)

The written notice North Dakota requires before you file to evict for breaking a material term of a written lease.

At a glance.

  • WhenThe tenant violated a material term of the written lease.
  • The lawN.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-01(8); N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-02; N.D. Cent. Code § 1-02-15
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Only for a material term of a written lease (§ 47-32-01(8)). The statute gives no chance to fix it.
  • A tenant who unreasonably disturbs other tenants’ peaceful enjoyment can be taken to court with no notice first (§ 47-32-01(7)), and the court can’t stay that eviction (§ 47-32-04).
  • You can’t serve it yourself. The sheriff, or another adult who isn’t a party to or interested in the case, serves it as a summons is served and signs a declaration of service. Only if the tenant can’t be found (try at least once between 6 and 10 p.m.) may the sheriff or a process server post it on the premises (§ 47-32-02).
  • Counting (the courts’ instructions): start with the day after service and count 3 calendar days; if the third day is a Saturday, Sunday or North Dakota holiday, go to the next day that isn’t one. File after that.
