North Dakota 3-day notice of intention to evict (holding over)

The written notice North Dakota requires before you file against a tenant who stayed after the lease, or your notice, ended it.

At a glance.

  • WhenThe lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
  • The lawN.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-01(4); N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-02
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • A tenant who holds over after the lease ended, or after your termination notice ran, gets this 3-day notice before you file (§ 47-32-01(4), § 47-32-02).
  • You can’t serve it yourself. The sheriff, or another adult who isn’t a party to or interested in the case, serves it as a summons is served and signs the proof of service. Only if the tenant can’t be found (try at least once between 6 and 10 p.m.) may the sheriff or a process server post it on the premises.
  • Counting (the courts’ instructions): start the day after service and count 3 calendar days; if the third day is a Saturday, Sunday or North Dakota holiday, go to the next day that isn’t one. NOHM fills in that day.
