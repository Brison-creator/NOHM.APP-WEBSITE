Notices before court.

3-day notice of intention to evict (rent unpaid)

The written notice North Dakota requires before you file to evict for unpaid rent: 3 days, then you may file.

When
Rent is still unpaid 3 days after it was due.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-01(4); N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-02; N.D. Cent. Code § 1-02-15
Good to know
  • Rent must be unpaid 3 days after it was due before you serve it (§ 47-32-01(4)).
  • You can’t serve it yourself. The sheriff, or another adult who isn’t a party to or interested in the case, serves it as a summons is served and signs a declaration of service. Only if the tenant can’t be found (try at least once between 6 and 10 p.m.) may the sheriff or a process server post it on the premises (§ 47-32-02).
  • Counting (the courts’ instructions): start with the day after service and count 3 calendar days; if the third day is a Saturday, Sunday or North Dakota holiday, go to the next day that isn’t one. File after that.
  • The statute gives the tenant no right to pay and stay; the courts’ self-help form offers the choice to pay the total due or move out. If you accept full payment, don’t file.

3-day notice of intention to evict (lease violation)

The written notice North Dakota requires before you file to evict for breaking a material term of a written lease.

When
The tenant violated a material term of the written lease.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-01(8); N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-02; N.D. Cent. Code § 1-02-15
Good to know
  • Only for a material term of a written lease (§ 47-32-01(8)). The statute gives no chance to fix it.
  • A tenant who unreasonably disturbs other tenants’ peaceful enjoyment can be taken to court with no notice first (§ 47-32-01(7)), and the court can’t stay that eviction (§ 47-32-04).
  • You can’t serve it yourself. The sheriff, or another adult who isn’t a party to or interested in the case, serves it as a summons is served and signs a declaration of service. Only if the tenant can’t be found (try at least once between 6 and 10 p.m.) may the sheriff or a process server post it on the premises (§ 47-32-02).
  • Counting (the courts’ instructions): start with the day after service and count 3 calendar days; if the third day is a Saturday, Sunday or North Dakota holiday, go to the next day that isn’t one. File after that.

One-month notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy with at least one calendar month’s written notice.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-15(2); N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-15(5)
Good to know
  • A lease that turned month-to-month when its term ran out (§ 47-16-06 or § 47-16-06.1) can end only on the last day of a month (§ 47-16-15(5)), so NOHM ends every one on the last day of a rental period. If rent isn’t due on the 1st, pick the last day of a calendar month.
  • The lease may ask for longer notice in writing (§ 47-16-15(2)). A week-to-week tenancy needs a week’s notice before the end of the week (§ 47-16-15(1)).
  • If the tenant stays after the date, serve the 3-day notice of intention to evict before you file (§ 47-32-02): NOHM has it, for a tenant holding over.
  • The statute names no way to deliver it. NOHM adds 3 days when you mail it.

3-day notice of intention to evict (holding over)

The written notice North Dakota requires before you file against a tenant who stayed after the lease, or your notice, ended it.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-01(4); N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-02
Good to know
  • A tenant who holds over after the lease ended, or after your termination notice ran, gets this 3-day notice before you file (§ 47-32-01(4), § 47-32-02).
  • You can’t serve it yourself. The sheriff, or another adult who isn’t a party to or interested in the case, serves it as a summons is served and signs the proof of service. Only if the tenant can’t be found (try at least once between 6 and 10 p.m.) may the sheriff or a process server post it on the premises.
  • Counting (the courts’ instructions): start the day after service and count 3 calendar days; if the third day is a Saturday, Sunday or North Dakota holiday, go to the next day that isn’t one. NOHM fills in that day.

Court papers.

Complaint for eviction Court

The eviction filing for the district court: the facts the statute asks for, the notice you served, and what you ask the court for.

When
The 3-day notice of intention to evict ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-01; N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-02; N.D. Cent. Code § 47-32-04
Good to know
  • North Dakota has no mandatory eviction forms: the courts’ self-help packet isn’t official, and courts needn’t accept it. NOHM writes the complaint from the statute and the packet’s outline.
  • File it with a summons in the district court of the county where the rental is. The filing fee is $160 (the courts’ instructions, June 2026).
  • The summons sets the hearing 3 to 15 days after it’s issued. Serve it at least 3 days before the hearing if it’s handed to the tenant in the county, otherwise at least 7 (§ 47-32-02). The sheriff or another adult who isn’t a party serves it.
  • Posting the summons on the door is allowed only if the tenant can’t be found after at least one try between 6 and 10 p.m., with your affidavit saying so, and a copy mailed to the tenant’s last known address (§ 47-32-02).
  • A judicial referee may hear the case; either side may ask in writing for a judge within 7 days of getting the notice of hearing. The tenant answers at the hearing.
  • Judgment is for immediate restitution of the premises; for hardship the court may stay it up to 5 days, but not for a disturbance of the peace (§ 47-32-04). The sheriff carries out the writ.
  • Only a lawyer licensed in North Dakota can file for a company (an LLC or a corporation); papers a non-lawyer signs for one are void (the courts’ eviction page).
  • Only rent and profits, or damages from the tenant’s possession, may be joined with the eviction; the tenant may counterclaim only as a setoff (§ 47-32-04).
  • No verification is required. Attach the lease and the notice of intention to evict with its proof of service.

Move-in.

Statement of condition of premises

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
When you enter the rental agreement: both of you sign it.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-07.2
Good to know
  • North Dakota requires it: give the tenant a statement of the condition of the premises when you enter the rental agreement, and both of you sign it (§ 47-16-07.2).
  • The deposit goes in a federally insured, interest-bearing account for the tenant, and can’t exceed one month’s rent (two in limited cases); a pet deposit is capped at the greater of $2,500 or two months’ rent, and none for an assistance animal (§ 47-16-07.1).
  • A lease that asks the tenant to give more than a month’s notice must say so with a space the tenant initials (§ 47-16-15(4)).
  • An automatic renewal clause in a lease of two months or more needs a written reminder at least 30 days before the lease ends (§ 47-16-06.1): NOHM has it.
  • Homes built before 1978: give the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-07.3
Good to know
  • North Dakota sets no number of hours: notify the tenant and get their consent to a time certain, unless that’s impractical (§ 47-16-07.3(2)). NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • If the tenant doesn’t object after notice of a time certain, consent is presumed. Give the notice in person, by posting it in a conspicuous place in or about the unit for a reasonable time, or any other way that actually reaches the tenant.
  • In an emergency, or if you reasonably believe the tenant abandoned the unit or is in substantial violation of the lease, you may enter at any time (§ 47-16-07.3(1)). Don’t use access to harass.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the end of the month before the new rent starts.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-07
Good to know
  • In a month-to-month lease, give written notice at least 30 days before the month ends; the new rent starts when the month ends (§ 47-16-07). NOHM counts 31 days to the new rent’s due date, so the notice is always in time.
  • Any reasonable way that actually tells the tenant counts (§ 47-16-07).
  • After a change of terms, the tenant may end the tenancy at the end of a month on 25 days’ notice (§ 47-16-15(3)).
  • North Dakota has no rent cap, and cities can’t control rents (§ 47-16-02.1).

Automatic renewal reminder

The written reminder of the lease’s automatic renewal clause, without which the clause can’t be enforced.

When
At least 30 days before the lease expires, if it renews automatically.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-06.1
Good to know
  • In a lease of two months or more, an automatic renewal clause is enforceable only if you remind the tenant of it in writing, delivered personally or by first-class mail, at least 30 days before the lease expires (§ 47-16-06.1).
  • Without the reminder, the lease expires and turns into a month-to-month tenancy on its latest terms.

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant gives back possession.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-07.1(3)
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the lease ends and the tenant gives back possession, deliver or mail the itemized list, the amount due and a written notice of any amount still due you or the refund due them, to the last address the tenant gave you (§ 47-16-07.1(3)).
  • Pay the deposit’s interest with it, unless the tenant lived there less than 9 months.
  • Withholding any of it without reasonable justification costs treble damages (§ 47-16-07.1(4)).
  • Money the tenant doesn’t claim within a year is reported as unclaimed property (§ 47-30.2-04).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
N.D. Cent. Code § 47-16-30.1
Good to know
  • North Dakota requires no notice: property worth $2,500 or less in all may be kept and disposed of 28 or more days after you learn, or it reasonably appears, that the tenant left (§ 47-16-30.1). Giving notice heads off disputes. NOHM counts the 28 days from this notice, the safe way.
  • You keep what a sale brings, and may take storage and moving costs beyond it from the deposit. After an eviction and the special execution, you have a lien on what you moved for reasonable storage and moving costs.
  • Property worth more than $2,500: no statute; ask a lawyer.
