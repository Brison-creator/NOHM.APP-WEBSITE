Tennessee 30-day notice to remedy a breach, or the lease ends

Says what the tenant did wrong: remedy it within 30 days, or the rental agreement ends. Good in every county.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenThe tenant broke a lease term (other than rent).
  • The lawTenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-505(a)(1)–(3); Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-7-109(a)(1)(B), (b); Tenn. Code Ann. § 1-3-102
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Tennessee has two sets of rules. The Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA) applies in Anderson, Blount, Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Knox, Madison, Maury, Montgomery, Rutherford, Sevier, Shelby, Sullivan, Sumner, Washington, Williamson and Wilson counties (those over 75,000 people at the 2010 census, Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-102(a); the list follows from the census, not from the statute's text, so ask the county clerk if unsure). Every other county follows § 66-7-109.
  • NOHM gives 30 days, with a chance to remedy, so the notice is good in every county. URLTA counties allow 14 days after receipt, to remedy or, for a breach that can’t be remedied, to end the agreement (§ 66-28-505(a)(2)–(3)); other counties allow 14 days to remedy damage beyond normal wear and tear, and require 30 days for every other default (§ 66-7-109(a)(1)(B), (b)).
  • Shorter notices exist for serious cases; ask a lawyer before using them: 7 days for the same breach again within 6 months (URLTA, § 66-28-505(a)(2)(B)); 3 days for a violent act, a real and present danger, a hazardous condition, or an unauthorized occupant who won’t leave (URLTA, § 66-28-517, from the day the tenant receives it); 3 days outside URLTA counties for a violent act, drug-related criminal activity or a real danger by a tenant who is not disabled (§ 66-7-109(d)), or an unauthorized occupant (§ 66-7-109(f)).
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the law requires the notice to specify it.
  • If the tenant must make repairs to remedy it, the notice must say they need your written authorization first; this one does.
  • Written notice goes to the tenant’s last known address or the one in the lease (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-106(b)); in URLTA counties, to the email the tenant gave in the lease, if they gave one (§ 66-28-108). The period runs from receipt: NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed. A last day on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next day (§ 1-3-102).
