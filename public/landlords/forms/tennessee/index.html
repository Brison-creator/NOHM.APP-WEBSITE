Notices before court.

14-day notice to pay rent, or the lease ends

Demands the rent due: if it isn’t paid within 14 days after the tenant gets the notice, the rental agreement ends.

When
Rent is past due (a late fee only after the 5-day grace period).
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-505(a)(1)–(2); Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-7-109(a)(1)(A), (a)(2); Tenn. Code Ann. § 1-3-102
Good to know
  • Tennessee has two sets of rules. The Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA) applies in Anderson, Blount, Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Knox, Madison, Maury, Montgomery, Rutherford, Sevier, Shelby, Sullivan, Sumner, Washington, Williamson and Wilson counties (those over 75,000 people at the 2010 census, Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-102(a); the list follows from the census, not from the statute's text, so ask the county clerk if unsure). Every other county follows § 66-7-109.
  • Both sets give 14 days to pay: in URLTA counties from when the tenant receives the notice (§ 66-28-505(a)(2)); elsewhere, notice of 14 days for rent in arrears upon demand (§ 66-7-109(a)(1)(A)). This notice meets both.
  • If the same breach happens again within 6 months, the next notice can be shorter: 7 days in URLTA counties (§ 66-28-505(a)(2)(B)); elsewhere 14 days, with no chance to cure (§ 66-7-109(a)(2)).
  • In URLTA counties a lease can waive this notice, if the waiver is in 12-point bold type or larger; the 5-day grace period still applies (§ 66-28-505(b)). Outside them, this notice doesn’t apply to a rental period under 14 days (§ 66-7-109(c)).
  • Accepting rent without reservation, knowing of the default, can waive the termination (§ 66-28-508).
  • Written notice goes to the tenant’s last known address or the one in the lease (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-106(b)); in URLTA counties, to the email the tenant gave in the lease, if they gave one (§ 66-28-108). The period runs from receipt: NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed. A last day on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next day (§ 1-3-102).

30-day notice to remedy a breach, or the lease ends

Says what the tenant did wrong: remedy it within 30 days, or the rental agreement ends. Good in every county.

When
The tenant broke a lease term (other than rent).
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-505(a)(1)–(3); Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-7-109(a)(1)(B), (b); Tenn. Code Ann. § 1-3-102
Good to know
  • Tennessee has two sets of rules. The Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA) applies in Anderson, Blount, Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Knox, Madison, Maury, Montgomery, Rutherford, Sevier, Shelby, Sullivan, Sumner, Washington, Williamson and Wilson counties (those over 75,000 people at the 2010 census, Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-102(a); the list follows from the census, not from the statute's text, so ask the county clerk if unsure). Every other county follows § 66-7-109.
  • NOHM gives 30 days, with a chance to remedy, so the notice is good in every county. URLTA counties allow 14 days after receipt, to remedy or, for a breach that can’t be remedied, to end the agreement (§ 66-28-505(a)(2)–(3)); other counties allow 14 days to remedy damage beyond normal wear and tear, and require 30 days for every other default (§ 66-7-109(a)(1)(B), (b)).
  • Shorter notices exist for serious cases; ask a lawyer before using them: 7 days for the same breach again within 6 months (URLTA, § 66-28-505(a)(2)(B)); 3 days for a violent act, a real and present danger, a hazardous condition, or an unauthorized occupant who won’t leave (URLTA, § 66-28-517, from the day the tenant receives it); 3 days outside URLTA counties for a violent act, drug-related criminal activity or a real danger by a tenant who is not disabled (§ 66-7-109(d)), or an unauthorized occupant (§ 66-7-109(f)).
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the law requires the notice to specify it.
  • If the tenant must make repairs to remedy it, the notice must say they need your written authorization first; this one does.
  • Written notice goes to the tenant’s last known address or the one in the lease (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-106(b)); in URLTA counties, to the email the tenant gave in the lease, if they gave one (§ 66-28-108). The period runs from receipt: NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed. A last day on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next day (§ 1-3-102).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy at the end of a rental period, at least 30 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-512(b)
Good to know
  • Tennessee has two sets of rules. The Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (URLTA) applies in Anderson, Blount, Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Knox, Madison, Maury, Montgomery, Rutherford, Sevier, Shelby, Sullivan, Sumner, Washington, Williamson and Wilson counties (those over 75,000 people at the 2010 census, Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-102(a); the list follows from the census, not from the statute's text, so ask the county clerk if unsure). Every other county follows § 66-7-109.
  • In URLTA counties a month-to-month ends on written notice given at least 30 days before the rent date it names (§ 66-28-512(b)); a week-to-week, 10 days (§ 66-28-512(a)). Other counties have no statute: the common law’s rule is one full rental period, ending at the end of a period (not confirmed from a statute). NOHM’s 30 days, ending a rental period, meets both.
  • No notice to quit or demand for possession is needed before you file, other than the detainer warrant itself (Tenn. Code Ann. § 29-18-113). This notice, or the end of a lease’s term, is the only notice the law uses, so NOHM has no separate demand.
  • If the tenant stays without your consent, you may sue for possession, back rent and reasonable attorney’s fees, and more if the holdover is willful (§ 66-28-512(c)).
  • A tenant 55 or older, current on rent, in a qualifying federally assisted senior facility being redeveloped, needs 60 days (§ 66-28-523; § 66-7-109(i)).
  • Written notice goes to the tenant’s last known address or the one in the lease (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-106(b)); in URLTA counties, to the email the tenant gave in the lease, if they gave one (§ 66-28-108). The period runs from receipt: NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed. A last day on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next day (§ 1-3-102).

Court papers.

Detainer warrant and complaint for unlawful detainer Court

The eviction filing: your complaint, and the detainer warrant in the words Tennessee’s statute sets, for the General Sessions clerk to issue.

When
The rental agreement ended (by a notice, or its term ran out) and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 29-18-104; Tenn. Code Ann. § 29-18-107; Tenn. Code Ann. § 29-18-112
Good to know
  • File in the General Sessions Court of the county where the rental is (Tenn. Code Ann. § 29-18-107). Tennessee has no statewide eviction form: most clerks issue the detainer warrant on their own form. Ask yours; copy these facts onto it, or file this complaint with it if the clerk accepts it.
  • You must give a bond, with good security, for the costs and damages of a wrongful suit before the warrant issues (§ 29-18-111); the clerk collects the costs (in Sevier County, for example, $250.50 plus $52 for each added defendant).
  • A sheriff, deputy, constable or private process server serves it. After three tries on different dates, it may be posted on the door and mailed first class at least 6 days before the trial date, but then only for possession, not money (§ 29-18-115(e)). The trial is at least 6 days after service (§ 29-18-117); a postponement is 7 days at most unless the parties agree (§ 29-18-118, Pub. Ch. 755 (2024)).
  • No written answer is needed in General Sessions; the judge hears it without a jury, decides possession, and works out the rent, interest and damages owed (§§ 29-18-119, 29-18-125).
  • No writ of possession issues until 10 days after the judgment (§ 29-18-126). The tenant may appeal to circuit court within 10 days, but only by posting a bond, cash or letter of credit, or two good sureties, for a year’s rent (§ 29-18-130(b)(2)(A), Pub. Ch. 755 (2024)).
  • No statute requires the complaint to be sworn; county clerks’ forms vary, so sign it as your clerk asks. County clerks say an LLC or corporation must file through an attorney (not confirmed as a statewide rule).
  • Bring the lease, the notice and how you gave it, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Tennessee doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: it records the home’s condition for the damage listing at move-out.
  • In URLTA counties, give the tenant in writing, at or before move-in: who manages the home and who accepts legal notices, with their addresses, and a maintenance phone, email or online portal (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-302(a), Pub. Ch. 907, for agreements from January 1, 2025); that you don’t provide insurance for the tenant’s belongings (§ 66-28-201(a)); and where the deposit account is (§ 66-28-301(h)). NOHM’s landlord disclosures form has all three.
  • A lease clause waiving the 14-day rent notice must be in 12-point bold type or larger (§ 66-28-505(b)); a clause making a no-show tenant waive objections to the move-out inspection must be in the lease (§ 66-28-301(b)(1)(A)).
  • Davidson County landlords register each rental with the codes department (§ 66-28-107). A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.
  • From January 1, 2027, a lease entered, amended or renewed generally can’t ban a tenant’s lawful firearms (Pub. Ch. 606 (2026); its exact text wasn’t read).

Landlord disclosures (URLTA counties)

The written disclosures the URLTA requires at the start of a tenancy: the manager, the owner or agent for notices, maintenance contact, insurance, and the deposit account.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, with the lease.
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-302(a); Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-201(a); Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-301(h)
Good to know
  • Required in URLTA counties; harmless anywhere. Give it at or before the start of the tenancy, and keep it current (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-302(b)).
  • The manager and maintenance disclosure applies to agreements entered, amended or renewed from January 1, 2025 (Pub. Ch. 907 (2024)).
  • Name the bank and where it is; the account number isn’t required (§ 66-28-301(h)). A deposit not kept in its own account, with no damage listing at move-out, can’t be kept at all (§ 66-28-301(c)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-403
Good to know
  • Tennessee sets no notice period for most entries. To show the home to new tenants in the last 30 days of the lease, the lease must allow it and you must give at least 24 hours’ notice (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-403(e)(5)). NOHM suggests 24 hours every time.
  • In an emergency you may enter without consent (§ 66-28-403(b)); also during a tenant’s absence of more than 7 days, at times reasonably necessary (§ 66-28-507(b)). Don’t use entry to harass (§ 66-28-403(d)).
  • Outside URLTA counties no statute governs entry; your lease does.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Tennessee statute sets notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date; in URLTA counties a month-to-month ends on 30 days’ notice before a rent date (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-512(b)), so give at least that.
  • During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Tennessee has no rent cap, and cities and counties can’t control rents (§ 66-35-102(a)).
  • In URLTA counties you can’t raise rent to retaliate for a complaint or a tenant’s use of their remedies (§ 66-28-514).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
After the tenant moves out: list the damage at the move-out inspection, and send the refund notice.
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-301
Good to know
  • This is the URLTA’s procedure; outside URLTA counties no deposit statute was found, and itemizing is still the safe practice.
  • Within 5 days after the tenant’s written notice to vacate you may tell them of their right to be at the move-out inspection; inspect the day they leave or within 4 days after. Together you list the damage and its estimated cost and both sign; a tenant who disagrees says in writing which items (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-301(b)(1)).
  • If the tenant left without notice, abandoned the home, was evicted, or didn’t come, list the damage yourself, and send them a copy by certificate of mailing if they ask in writing (§ 66-28-301(b)(2)).
  • Damage found later counts only if found before the earlier of 30 days after they vacate or 7 days after a new tenant moves in (§ 66-28-301(g)).
  • Tennessee sets no deadline to return the deposit. NOHM suggests sending this within 30 days. Send the refund notice to the last known or reasonably determinable address; after 60 days with no response you may keep the deposit (§ 66-28-301(f)).
  • A deposit not kept in its own account at a regulated bank, with no damage listing, can’t be kept at all (§ 66-28-301(a), (c)). No cap; no interest.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-405(c)
Good to know
  • In URLTA counties, store a tenant’s belongings at least 30 days after you take possession of them; then you may sell or dispose of them, apply the proceeds to what’s owed, and hold any balance for 6 months (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-405(c)). Before you re-enter an abandoned home, post and mail the notice of abandonment.
  • After a writ of possession is executed, the tenant’s property is set out on the premises; don’t disturb it for 48 hours, then it may be discarded (Tenn. Code Ann. § 29-18-127(b)).
  • Outside URLTA counties no statute on property left behind was found; this notice is a courtesy that heads off disputes.
  • Photograph the property before you move it.

Notice of abandonment (URLTA counties)

The notice the URLTA requires before you re-enter a home you believe the tenant abandoned: 10 days to contact you.

When
Rent is 15 days past due and the tenant appears to have moved out.
The law
Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-405(b)
Good to know
  • Rent unpaid 15 days past the due date, with other facts showing the tenant has moved out for good, is evidence of abandonment; so is an unexplained absence of 30 days without paying rent (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-405(a)–(b)).
  • Post it at the home and send it by regular mail, postage prepaid, to the tenant at the home’s address, the same day. It must give a phone number and a mailing address where you can be reached (§ 66-28-405(b)(2)–(3)).
  • If the tenant doesn’t contact you within 10 days, you may re-enter. If they tell you they intend to stay, you must go to court instead (§ 66-28-405(b)(4)).
  • Store their belongings at least 30 days after you take them (§ 66-28-405(c)).
