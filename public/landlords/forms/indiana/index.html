Notices before court.

10-day notice to pay rent or vacate

Tells the tenant to pay the rent due within 10 days of getting it, or move out.

When
Rent is past due (Indiana has no grace period).
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-1-6; Ind. Code § 32-31-1-7; Ind. Code § 32-31-1-9; Ind. Trial Rule 6(A)
Good to know
  • This is the form of notice Ind. Code § 32-31-1-7 sets out. If the tenant pays the rent in full before the notice period ends, the lease isn’t terminated (§ 32-31-1-6).
  • Your lease can set a different period (§ 32-31-1-6(1)).
  • Indiana provides no mail service for this notice: hand it to the tenant; if they can’t be found, to a person living there, explaining what it says; if no one is there, fix it to a conspicuous part of the premises (§ 32-31-1-9).
  • Some tenancies need no notice (§ 32-31-1-8), but the courts advise giving one when in doubt; NOHM always does.
  • Demand rent only; late fees only if the written lease provides for them (Indiana Small Claims Manual). If you’ve accepted late rent before, first tell the tenant in writing that late payment will be treated as a breach.
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

Notice of noncompliance (lease violation)

Tells the tenant what they’re doing wrong and gives them a reasonable time to fix it.

When
The tenant broke a lease term or a tenant duty (not rent).
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-7-7(b); Ind. Code § 32-31-7-5
Good to know
  • Indiana has no statute for a notice to end a lease for a breach other than rent: your lease decides (its forfeiture clause). Pick the date yourself; at least 10 days is a fair time to fix most breaches.
  • This notice is also the notice Ind. Code § 32-31-7-7(b) requires before you sue a tenant for not meeting their duties (following codes, keeping the unit clean, not damaging it, following reasonable rules, keeping smoke detectors working), with a reasonable time to fix it.
  • Waste, a crime by a tenant or guest affecting others’ health or safety, or materially false information on the application: you can ask the court for an emergency possessory order, heard within 3 business days (Ind. Code § 32-31-6). Ask a lawyer.
  • Unpaid rent isn’t a violation for this notice: use the 10-day notice to pay rent or vacate.

One-month notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy at the end of a rental period at least a month out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-1-1(a); Ind. Code § 32-31-1-4; Ind. Code § 32-31-1-9
Good to know
  • A tenancy at will ends on one month’s written notice delivered to the tenant (Ind. Code § 32-31-1-1), and a periodic tenancy of 3 months or less on notice equal to the interval between periods (§ 32-31-1-4). The statute doesn’t say the date must end a rental period, but that is how the interval reads; NOHM ends it on the last day of one. A month can be 28 to 31 days and the courts’ manual says at least 30, so NOHM counts 31.
  • Week-to-week: 7 days (§ 32-31-1-4). Year-to-year: 3 months before the year ends, in the statute’s own form (§§ 32-31-1-3, 32-31-1-5).
  • A fixed-term lease ends on its date, and no notice is needed (§ 32-31-1-8); nor is a separate demand for possession after this notice runs.
  • Not renewing a lease at the end of its term isn’t retaliation (Ind. Code § 32-31-8.5-5(b)(1)).
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (CCA-EV-0321-8001)

Indiana’s statewide small claims eviction form: this names it, fills in what to write on it from the lease, and sets out the steps and deadlines.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Ind. Code § 33-29-2-4(b); Ind. Small Claims Rules 2, 3, 4, 16; Ind. Code § 32-30-3-10
Good to know
  • The Small Claims Complaint and Clerk’s Notice of Claim for Possession, Rent Due and Damages is approved statewide by the Indiana Supreme Court’s Coalition for Court Access; some counties (Marion, for one) use their own. Ask the clerk which to file.
  • In small claims a company may be represented by counsel, its owner, or a designated full-time employee with the certificate and affidavit Small Claims Rule 8(C) requires. A building manager can’t sue in their own name: the landlord must bring it.
  • Waste, a crime by a tenant or guest affecting others’ safety, or lies on the application: an emergency possessory order is a separate, faster petition (Ind. Code § 32-31-6). Unpaid rent isn’t a ground for it.
  • Bring the lease, the notice and how you served it, and the rent ledger.
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-5-7(a)
Good to know
  • When you deliver the unit, the tenant must acknowledge in writing that it has a functional smoke detector (Ind. Code § 32-31-5-7(a)); this form records it. The room-by-room checklist isn’t required, but the Small Claims Manual advises a walk-through and photos.
  • Give the owner and manager disclosure at or before the start: it’s the next form.
  • If the lowest floor, basement included, is at or below the 100-year flood elevation, say clearly in the rental agreement that the structure is in a flood plain (Ind. Code § 32-31-1-21(b)).
  • Built before 1978: the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

Owner and manager disclosure

The written disclosure Indiana requires at the start: who in Indiana manages the home, and who accepts notices and legal papers for the owner.

When
At or before the start of the rental agreement.
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-3-18(a)
Good to know
  • Required in writing at or before the start of the rental agreement (Ind. Code § 32-31-3-18(a)). Both people must live in Indiana; the agent must be reasonably accessible to the tenant.
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-5-6(g)
Good to know
  • Indiana requires reasonable written or oral notice and entry at reasonable times, but sets no number of hours (Ind. Code § 32-31-5-6(g)). NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • In an emergency that threatens the occupants’ safety or your property you may enter without notice; without consent, only by court order or if the tenant abandoned or surrendered the unit (§ 32-31-5-6(f)).
  • Don’t use entry to harass the tenant. It applies to rental agreements entered into or renewed after June 30, 1999.
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-5-4
Good to know
  • Unless a written rental agreement provides otherwise, give at least 30 days’ written notice before modifying the rental agreement, a rent increase included (Ind. Code § 32-31-5-4).
  • Indiana has no rent cap, and local governments can’t regulate rents (Ind. Code § 32-31-1-20(b)).
  • Raising rent to what comparable rentals charge isn’t retaliation (Ind. Code § 32-31-8.5-5(b)(2)).
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 45 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Ind. Code §§ 32-31-3-12, 32-31-3-14
Good to know
  • Mail the itemized list of damages, with a check or money order for the rest of the deposit, within 45 days after the tenancy ends and you get possession back (Ind. Code §§ 32-31-3-12(a), 32-31-3-14).
  • You’re not liable until the tenant gives you, in writing, a mailing address to send it to.
  • List the estimated cost of repair for each damaged item. Deduct only damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, rent owed (and rent for leaving early), and utility or sewer charges you paid for the tenant (§ 32-31-3-13).
  • Miss it and you’ve agreed no damages are due: the whole deposit must be returned, and you can owe the amount withheld plus attorney’s fees (§§ 32-31-3-15, -16).
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.

Notice of order to remove property left behind

Tells the tenant the court ordered their property removed, and where it will be stored: personally served before you move it.

When
The court awarded you possession and ordered the tenant’s property removed.
The law
Ind. Code § 32-31-4-2; Ind. Code § 32-31-4-3
Good to know
  • Property a reasonable person would conclude the tenant left when they vacated and surrendered possession is abandoned, and you aren’t liable for it (Ind. Code § 32-31-4-2(a)–(b)); a lease can’t define abandonment differently.
  • Otherwise, ask the court that awarded you possession for an order allowing removal of the tenant’s property (§ 32-31-4-2(d)). You may deliver it to a warehouse or a court-approved storage facility only after this notice, of the order and of the facility’s identity and location, is personally served on the tenant at their last known address (§ 32-31-4-3(a)). Attach a copy of the order.
  • Exempt property (§ 32-31-4-1: among it medical items, tools of the trade, a week’s clothing, blankets and children’s school items) must be released without payment (§ 32-31-4-3(b)).
  • The storage facility may sell property not claimed within 45 days after the tenant receives this notice (§ 32-31-4-5, as amended in 2025).
  • Never take or keep a tenant’s property to make them pay (Ind. Code § 32-31-5-5(a)).
  • Read from an unofficial copy of the Indiana Code current to 2024 (the official site couldn’t be opened), checked against the 2025 and 2026 session laws.
