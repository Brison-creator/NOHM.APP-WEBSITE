Notices before court.

5-day notice to vacate (rent not paid)

After you dissolve the lease for unpaid rent, tells the lessee to leave within 5 days of delivery.

When
Rent is past due and you have ended the lease (Louisiana has no grace period).
The law
La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701; La. Civ. Code art. 2704; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4703; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 5059
Good to know
  • Louisiana gives no statutory time to pay: when rent isn’t paid when due, the lessor may dissolve the lease (La. Civ. Code art. 2704) and then send this notice. If you’d rather give the lessee a chance to pay first, the Civil Code allows a notice to perform within a reasonable time, with a warning that the lease will otherwise be dissolved (art. 2015); NOHM’s notice doesn’t do that.
  • Rent is due at the beginning of each period unless the lease says otherwise (La. Civ. Code art. 2703); there is no grace period.
  • The 5 days leave out legal holidays because the period is under 7 days (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 5059(C)). Whether Saturdays are legal holidays (La. R.S. 1:55) wasn’t confirmed, so NOHM skips Saturdays and Sundays too: the longer, safer count.
  • Many Louisiana leases contain the lessee’s written waiver of the notice to vacate. If yours does, you may file the rule to show cause as soon as the lessee’s right of occupancy ends (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701).
  • Hand it to the lessee and keep proof of delivery. You may attach it to a door of the home only if the home is abandoned or closed, or you don’t know where the lessee is (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4703). The Code names no mail delivery, so NOHM doesn’t offer it.
  • Under an oral lease, a written demand for the rent that gives the correct amount makes the lessee liable for your attorney fees if it isn’t paid within 20 days after delivery (La. R.S. 9:3259). It can go by certified mail, by hand, or tacked on the door.

5-day notice to vacate (lease broken)

After you dissolve the lease for a failure to perform it, tells the lessee to leave within 5 days of delivery.

When
The lessee broke the lease (other than rent) and you have ended it.
The law
La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701; La. Civ. Code art. 2719; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4703; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 5059
Good to know
  • A lessee must pay the rent, use the home as a prudent administrator for its purpose, and return it as delivered except for normal wear and tear (La. Civ. Code art. 2683). When a party fails to perform, the other may dissolve the lease (art. 2719).
  • A lease can’t be dissolved when the lessee has rendered a substantial part of the performance and the part not rendered doesn’t substantially impair your interest (La. Civ. Code art. 2014). For a smaller breach, a notice to perform within a reasonable time, warning that the lease will otherwise be dissolved, is the safer first step (art. 2015).
  • In a building of 6 or more units (other than an owner-occupied building of 10 or fewer), you may not give a notice to vacate because domestic abuse occurred on the premises and the victim is a lessee or a member of the lessee’s household (La. R.S. 9:3261.1).
  • The 5 days leave out legal holidays because the period is under 7 days (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 5059(C)). Whether Saturdays are legal holidays (La. R.S. 1:55) wasn’t confirmed, so NOHM skips Saturdays and Sundays too: the longer, safer count.
  • Many Louisiana leases contain the lessee’s written waiver of the notice to vacate. If yours does, you may file the rule to show cause as soon as the lessee’s right of occupancy ends (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701).
  • Hand it to the lessee and keep proof of delivery. You may attach it to a door of the home only if the home is abandoned or closed, or you don’t know where the lessee is (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4703). The Code names no mail delivery, so NOHM doesn’t offer it.

10-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month lease at the end of a month, given at least 10 calendar days before; it is also the notice to vacate.

When
You want a month-to-month lessee out, no fault needed.
The law
La. Civ. Code art. 2728(2); La. Civ. Code art. 2729; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701
Good to know
  • A month-to-month lease ends by written notice given at least 10 calendar days before the end of the month (La. Civ. Code arts. 2728(2), 2729). The notice ends the lease at the end of that month; one given late ends it at the end of the next month for which it is timely.
  • Other terms: a lease by periods longer than a month needs 30 calendar days before the period ends; by the week, 5 calendar days (La. Civ. Code art. 2728). A lease with no agreed term is month to month (art. 2680).
  • This notice is also the notice to vacate (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701): when the month ends you may file the rule to show cause without another one.
  • A lease with a fixed term ends on its own date without notice (La. Civ. Code art. 2720), but you still need a notice to vacate (NOHM’s, given up to 30 days before the term ends) unless the lease waives it (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701). If the lessee stays a week after the term without your objection, the lease is reconducted month to month (La. Civ. Code arts. 2721, 2723).
  • No just-cause rule or rent control: a lessor’s rights can be limited only by state law (La. R.S. 9:3258).
  • Hand it to the lessee and keep proof of delivery. You may attach it to a door of the home only if the home is abandoned or closed, or you don’t know where the lessee is (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4703). The Code names no mail delivery, so NOHM doesn’t offer it.

Notice to vacate (5 days)

The written notice to vacate a lessee must get before an eviction, when the lease ran out or ended another way.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 5059
Good to know
  • Louisiana requires a written notice to vacate before every eviction, including when a fixed-term lease simply runs out, unless the lease waives it in writing (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701). It must allow at least 5 days from delivery; NOHM doesn’t count Saturdays, Sundays or legal holidays (art. 5059), the safer reading.
  • For a fixed term you may give it up to 30 days before the term ends (art. 4701). Give it before the lessee has stayed a week past the term without your objection: that reconducts the lease month to month (La. Civ. Code art. 2721).
  • NOHM’s 5-day rent and lease-violation notices, and its month-to-month termination, are notices to vacate already. Use this one for a lease that ran out, or for an occupant who isn’t a lessee (5 days, art. 4702).
  • Hand it to the lessee and keep proof of delivery. Attach it to a door of the home only if the home is abandoned or closed, or you don’t know where the lessee is (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4703).

20-day written demand for rent (oral lease)

Demands the past-due rent in writing; under an oral lease, if it isn’t paid in 20 days the lessee owes your attorney fees for collecting it.

When
Rent is past due under an oral lease.
The law
La. R.S. 9:3259
Good to know
  • For oral leases only (La. R.S. 9:3259(C)). If the lessee doesn’t pay within 20 days after delivery of a written demand that correctly sets out the rent due and owing, they are liable for reasonable attorney fees for collecting it once you win a judgment on it (§ 9:3259(A)).
  • Get the amount right: list only the rent that has become due and delinquent.
  • Deliver it by certified mail to the lessee’s last known address, by hand, or by tacking it on the door of the leased premises (La. R.S. 9:3259(B)).
  • This demand is about money. To get the home back, the lessor dissolves the lease and gives the 5-day notice to vacate.

Court papers.

Petition for eviction (rule to show cause) Court

The eviction filing: your petition stating the grounds, and the order for the judge to sign that cites the lessee to show cause why they should not deliver possession.

When
The notice to vacate ran out (or the lease waives it) and the lessee hasn’t left.
The law
La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4731; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4732; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4733
Good to know
  • File it in the court for the home’s location: a justice of the peace court hears evictions of residential tenants whatever the rent, and so do city and parish courts; the district court can too (La. Code Civ. Proc. arts. 4912(A)(1), 4844). Ask the clerk which court your address goes to, and its fee.
  • Louisiana has no statewide eviction forms; some courts have their own, and if yours does, use it with these facts. In a justice of the peace court no written pleading is required, and you may state the claim orally (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4917(A)).
  • The judge signs the order and fixes the day: no earlier than the third day after the rule is served (art. 4732(A)). The sheriff, constable or marshal serves it; a justice of the peace court may serve by certified mail; if the home is abandoned or closed or the lessee can’t be found, it may be attached to a door (art. 4703).
  • If the lessee doesn’t answer or appear, or you prove your case, the court renders the judgment of eviction immediately, effective for at least 90 days (art. 4732(B)). If they don’t leave within 24 hours, the court issues the warrant for possession (art. 4733). Their appeal stops it only if they answered under oath with an affirmative defense and filed the appeal and bond within 24 hours (art. 4735).
  • The rule is for possession. Whether rent can be claimed in the same filing wasn’t confirmed, so the petition reserves it (art. 4705): sue for it separately.
  • No verification or bond is required of you. Whether a company may appear without a lawyer wasn’t confirmed: ask the court.
  • Bring the lease, the notice to vacate and your proof of delivering it, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Louisiana doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: you may keep the deposit only to remedy the lessee’s default or unreasonable wear (La. R.S. 9:3251(A)), and the lessee must return the home as it was delivered, except for normal wear and tear (La. Civ. Code art. 2683(3)). This form records its condition.
  • Louisiana requires no move-in disclosure by statute. A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.
  • Lease terms to know: a waiver of the notice to vacate must be written in the lease (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701); a waiver of the warranty against vices or defects has limits (La. Civ. Code art. 2699); and in covered buildings a lease can’t limit or penalize calling the police or emergency help (La. R.S. 9:3261.1).
  • No cap on the deposit, and no interest or separate account is required.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • No Louisiana statute sets notice or hours for a lessor’s entry; your lease may. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours, as a courtesy.
  • The Civil Code still limits you: during the lease you may not make alterations to the home (La. Civ. Code art. 2690); you may make a repair that can’t wait until the lease ends, even if it inconveniences the lessee (art. 2693); and you warrant the lessee’s peaceful possession (art. 2700).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Louisiana statute sets the notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date.
  • A month-to-month lease can be ended only by written notice 10 calendar days before the month ends (La. Civ. Code art. 2728), so a lessee who won’t accept the new rent can be given that notice. During a fixed-term lease, change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • No rent cap, and parishes and cities can’t limit a lessor’s rights (La. R.S. 9:3258).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within one month after the lease ends.
The law
La. R.S. 9:3251(A)
Good to know
  • Return the deposit within one month after the lease ends, less what is reasonably necessary to remedy the lessee’s default or unreasonable wear to the home, with an itemized statement of what you kept and why (La. R.S. 9:3251(A)).
  • Since Act 63 of 2026 the itemized statement may follow up to 15 days after that month; when the change took effect wasn’t confirmed, and the refund itself is still due within the month, so NOHM sends both within the month.
  • Send it to the forwarding address the lessee must give you when the lease ends (La. R.S. 9:3251(A)).
  • Willfully failing to return it costs the amount wrongly kept plus $300 or twice that amount, whichever is greater; not paying within 30 days after the lessee’s written demand counts as willful (La. R.S. 9:3252(A)).
  • The one-month rule doesn’t apply when the lessee abandons the home without the notice required, or before the lease ends (La. R.S. 9:3251(C)). No cap on the deposit, and no interest is required.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • No Louisiana statute covers a lessee’s belongings left behind. NOHM suggests holding them at least 14 days after this notice; the date is yours to set.
  • After the notice to vacate, you may take possession of a home you reasonably believe the lessee abandoned (keys returned, furnishings removed, no one living there) without going to court (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4731(B)); in a parish under a federal disaster declaration, not for 30 days after it.
  • With a warrant for possession, the officer clears the home of any property in it (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4734).
  • Your privilege on the lessee’s movables for the rent is enforced by a court seizure, not by keeping them yourself (La. Civ. Code arts. 2707, 2710).
  • Photograph the property before you move it.
