Louisiana petition for eviction (rule to show cause)

The eviction filing: your petition stating the grounds, and the order for the judge to sign that cites the lessee to show cause why they should not deliver possession.

What to know.

  • File it in the court for the home’s location: a justice of the peace court hears evictions of residential tenants whatever the rent, and so do city and parish courts; the district court can too (La. Code Civ. Proc. arts. 4912(A)(1), 4844). Ask the clerk which court your address goes to, and its fee.
  • Louisiana has no statewide eviction forms; some courts have their own, and if yours does, use it with these facts. In a justice of the peace court no written pleading is required, and you may state the claim orally (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4917(A)).
  • The judge signs the order and fixes the day: no earlier than the third day after the rule is served (art. 4732(A)). The sheriff, constable or marshal serves it; a justice of the peace court may serve by certified mail; if the home is abandoned or closed or the lessee can’t be found, it may be attached to a door (art. 4703).
  • If the lessee doesn’t answer or appear, or you prove your case, the court renders the judgment of eviction immediately, effective for at least 90 days (art. 4732(B)). If they don’t leave within 24 hours, the court issues the warrant for possession (art. 4733). Their appeal stops it only if they answered under oath with an affirmative defense and filed the appeal and bond within 24 hours (art. 4735).
  • The rule is for possession. Whether rent can be claimed in the same filing wasn’t confirmed, so the petition reserves it (art. 4705): sue for it separately.
  • No verification or bond is required of you. Whether a company may appear without a lawyer wasn’t confirmed: ask the court.
  • Bring the lease, the notice to vacate and your proof of delivering it, and the rent ledger.
