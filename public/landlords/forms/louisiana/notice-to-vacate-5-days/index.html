Louisiana notice to vacate (5 days)

The written notice to vacate a lessee must get before an eviction, when the lease ran out or ended another way.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenThe lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
  • The lawLa. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701; La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 5059
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Louisiana requires a written notice to vacate before every eviction, including when a fixed-term lease simply runs out, unless the lease waives it in writing (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4701). It must allow at least 5 days from delivery; NOHM doesn’t count Saturdays, Sundays or legal holidays (art. 5059), the safer reading.
  • For a fixed term you may give it up to 30 days before the term ends (art. 4701). Give it before the lessee has stayed a week past the term without your objection: that reconducts the lease month to month (La. Civ. Code art. 2721).
  • NOHM’s 5-day rent and lease-violation notices, and its month-to-month termination, are notices to vacate already. Use this one for a lease that ran out, or for an occupant who isn’t a lessee (5 days, art. 4702).
  • Hand it to the lessee and keep proof of delivery. Attach it to a door of the home only if the home is abandoned or closed, or you don’t know where the lessee is (La. Code Civ. Proc. art. 4703).
