Notices before court.

Demand for rent (no waiting period)

Demands the rent that is due; Missouri needs only the demand before you file for rent and possession.

When
Rent is due and unpaid (wait out any forfeiture time your lease sets).
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.020; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.060; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.050
Good to know
  • Missouri requires a demand for the rent, not a notice period: any demand made after rent is due counts (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.060). The month-to-month notice isn’t needed first (§ 535.020). Pick a reasonable date to pay.
  • If your written lease says it is forfeited for nonpayment “for a specified time”, wait until that time has run before you file (§ 535.050).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or leave it at their home with a family member over 15 (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 1.190). Missouri’s statutes don’t provide for mailing it.
  • The tenant can stop the case by paying the rent and costs at the hearing, and stop the eviction by paying the judgment and costs before it becomes final (§§ 535.040, 535.160).
  • A company (LLC or corporation) must appear in court through a lawyer in Missouri (a 2014 Missouri Supreme Court decision, as reported; NOHM didn’t read it).

10-day notice to vacate (lease violation)

Ten days’ notice to leave for breaking the written lease, assigning it without consent, or damaging the property.

When
The tenant violated a condition of the written lease, subleased without your written consent, or committed waste.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.040; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.030; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 506.060.1
Good to know
  • This is for a tenant whose term is two years or less, or at will or by sufferance (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.030). The statute gives no chance to cure; your lease may.
  • § 441.020 covers gaming devices, a bawdyhouse or brothel, and the illegal possession, sale or distribution of controlled substances on the premises. For drugs you must prove the use in court, but you aren’t liable for relying on a law enforcement agency’s written notice.
  • You can’t evict a tenant because they are a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault or stalking, except as § 441.920 allows.
  • If the tenant hasn’t left after the 10 days, serve the written demand for possession, then file for unlawful detainer (§ 534.030).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or leave it at their home with a family member over 15 (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 1.190). Missouri’s statutes don’t provide for mailing it.

1-month notice to vacate (month-to-month)

Ends a month-to-month tenancy on a rent-paying date at least one month after the tenant receives it.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, no fault needed.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.060.4(1); Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.060.1
Good to know
  • Missouri ends a month-to-month on a rent-paying date at least one month after the tenant receives the notice (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.060.4(1)). NOHM sets the last day before that rent day, so the tenancy ends as the rent day begins.
  • A year-to-year tenancy needs notice at least 60 days before the year ends (§ 441.050); a lease that ends on a set date needs none (§ 441.070).
  • If the tenant stays, serve a written demand for possession before you file for unlawful detainer (§ 534.030).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or leave it at their home with a family member over 15 (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 1.190). Missouri’s statutes don’t provide for mailing it.

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 534.030.1; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 534.050
Good to know
  • A tenant who holds over after the tenancy ends, and after a written demand for possession, is guilty of unlawful detainer (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 534.030.1). Serve this demand after the lease, the 10-day notice or the month-to-month notice has ended the tenancy.
  • Missouri sets no waiting period after the demand; NOHM fills in 3 days. Give more if you like.
  • Serve it by handing it to the person in possession, or leaving it with someone over 15 who lives there or is in charge; if no one is there, post it on the premises (§ 534.050). Anyone but a sheriff or other officer must swear to the return: sign it before a notary.
  • You don’t need this demand to file for rent and possession; the demand for rent is enough there.

Court papers.

Statement for rent and possession Court

The filing for unpaid rent: the terms of the rental, the rent due, and your demand for it, verified by affidavit.

When
You demanded the rent and it hasn’t been paid.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.020; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.060
Good to know
  • File it with the associate circuit judge of the county where the rental is (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.020). Sign the affidavit before a notary or the clerk. Missouri has no statewide form; some circuits post their own (Boone County’s, for one).
  • The court date is within 21 business days after the summons issues, unless you agree in writing to a later one; the summons is served at least 4 days before it. If it is served by posting and mailing (which you may do yourself, at least 10 days before the court date), the court can give possession but no money judgment on a default (§ 535.030).
  • The tenant can stop the case by paying the rent and costs at the hearing (§ 535.040). After judgment they have 10 days to ask to set it aside or for a new trial; then it is final (§ 535.030.4). The officer delivers possession within 5 days after receiving the execution (§ 535.040.1). A tenant who appeals must post a bond for the rent (§ 535.110).
  • If the officer hasn’t delivered possession within 7 days of receiving the writ, you may, within 60 days after the judgment and with a law enforcement officer present, change the locks and remove the tenant’s property (§ 441.060.5).
  • If your written lease is forfeited for nonpayment only after a set time, wait until it runs before you file (§ 535.050).
  • A company (LLC or corporation) must appear in court through a lawyer in Missouri (a 2014 Missouri Supreme Court decision, as reported; NOHM didn’t read it). Bills to change that weren’t enacted.

Complaint for unlawful detainer Court

The sworn filing to recover possession from a tenant who stayed after the tenancy ended and a written demand.

When
The tenancy ended, you served a written demand for possession, and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 534.030.1; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 534.070.1; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.040; Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.060.4(1)
Good to know
  • File it with the associate circuit judge of the county where the rental is (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 534.060). It must be signed and sworn to: sign the affidavit before a notary or the clerk (§ 534.070.1).
  • The court date is within 21 business days after the summons issues, and the summons is served at least 4 days before it (§§ 534.070.2, 534.090.1). The execution for possession issues no sooner than 10 days after the judgment (§ 534.350).
  • Keep copies of the notice that ended the tenancy and of the written demand, with its sworn return.
  • A company (LLC or corporation) must appear in court through a lawyer in Missouri (a 2014 Missouri Supreme Court decision, as reported; NOHM didn’t read it). Bills to change that weren’t enacted.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Missouri doesn’t require a move-in checklist; it is good practice, and helps if the deposit is disputed.
  • Papers Missouri requires: a written disclosure if you know the home was used to make methamphetamine (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.236); to charge for carpet cleaning, a lease clause setting the amounts and telling the tenant they may owe actual costs beyond ordinary wear and tear (§ 535.300); written notice of the move-out inspection (NOHM has it); and the federal lead disclosure for homes built before 1978.
  • The deposit can be no more than two months’ rent, held in a federally insured bank or credit union (§ 535.300).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • Missouri has no statute on entry or notice before it; your lease decides. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • Never enter to remove the tenant or their things, or change the locks, without a court order: that is forcible entry and detainer (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.233).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • Missouri sets no notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date. For a month-to-month, the clean way is the 1-month notice to vacate, offering a new tenancy at the new rent.
  • During a lease, change the rent only as the lease allows.
  • No city or county in Missouri may regulate the amount of rent (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.043.1).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.300
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the tenancy ends, return the full deposit, or send this list with the balance. Mailing them to the tenant’s last known address is enough (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.300).
  • Carpet cleaning can be deducted only if the lease set the amounts and told the tenant they may owe actual costs beyond ordinary wear and tear; give a receipt for the actual cost within 30 days.
  • Keeping any of it wrongfully costs twice the amount withheld. Interest on the deposit is yours; a pet deposit isn’t a security deposit; the tenant can’t use the deposit as rent.

Notice of belief of abandonment

The notice in the statute’s words when you believe a tenant has abandoned the home: 10 days to answer before you may remove what is left.

When
Rent has been unpaid for 30 days and you believe the tenant has moved out.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.065
Good to know
  • Use it only when you reasonably believe the tenant has moved out and doesn’t mean to return, and the rent has been due and unpaid for 30 days (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 441.065).
  • Post it on the door AND mail it by first class AND certified mail, return receipt requested, to the tenant’s last known address, on the date of the notice. The 10 days run from when you have done both.
  • If the tenant doesn’t pay the rent or answer in writing within those 10 days, the premises are abandoned and you may remove and dispose of what is left, without liability to the tenant.
  • This is not for a tenant who is still living there: removing a tenant or their things without a court order is forcible entry and detainer (§ 441.233).

Notice of move-out inspection

Tells the tenant when you’ll inspect after the tenancy ends, so they can be there.

When
Before you inspect the home after the tenancy ends.
The law
Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.300.5
Good to know
  • Give the tenant reasonable written notice of the date and time, at their last known address or in person; they may be there (Mo. Rev. Stat. § 535.300.5).
