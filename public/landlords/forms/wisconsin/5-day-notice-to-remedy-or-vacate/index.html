Wisconsin 5-day notice to remedy or vacate

Fix the breach within 5 days or move out (30 days for a lease of more than a year).

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenThe tenant broke a lease term or damaged the property.
  • The lawWis. Stat. § 704.17; Wis. Stat. § 704.21
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • NOHM counts the 5 days without Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, the longer reading, and gives 30 for a lease of more than one year.
  • The agency guide says a repeat breach within a year allows a 14-day notice with no chance to cure, and criminal or drug-related activity a 5-day notice to vacate with required contents (Wis. Stat. § 704.17(3m)). NOHM doesn’t draw those.
  • A lease can’t let you end a tenancy because the tenant, or someone living with them, was the victim of a crime there (Wis. Admin. Code § ATCP 134.08(9)).
  • The ways of serving are the ones Wis. Stat. § 704.21 is understood to allow; NOHM couldn’t confirm them from the statute, or whether mailing adds days.

The statute doesn’t set this notice’s exact words: check it before you serve one.
