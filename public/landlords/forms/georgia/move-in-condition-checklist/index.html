Georgia move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenBefore the tenant pays the security deposit; both of you sign it.
  • The lawO.C.G.A. § 44-7-33(a), (c)
  • StageMove-in

What to know.

  • Before the tenant pays a security deposit, give them a comprehensive list of any existing damage. They may inspect the premises; you both sign it, or they dissent in writing; they keep it permanently (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-33(a), (c)).
  • Within 3 business days after the tenant moves out, inspect and make a list of any damage; the tenant may inspect within 5 business days (§ 44-7-33(b)). Without both lists you can’t keep any of the deposit (§ 44-7-35).
  • A natural person who, with spouse and minor children, owns 10 or fewer rental units and manages them without a paid manager is exempt from the lists and the escrow rule (§ 44-7-36). The lists are still good practice.
  • Give in writing, at or before the start of the tenancy, the names and addresses of the owner of record (or an agent for notices) and of the manager (§ 44-7-3).
  • If the living space flooded 3 or more times in the 5 years before the lease, tell the tenant in writing before signing (§ 44-7-20).
  • Tell the tenant in writing where the deposit is held in escrow (§ 44-7-31): use the escrow notice. The deposit can’t be more than two months’ rent on a lease made or renewed on or after July 1, 2024 (§ 44-7-30.1).
  • An owner out of state renting a single-family home or duplex must hire a Georgia-licensed broker, with someone in the state for tenants to deal with (§ 44-7-25). The federal lead-paint disclosure applies to homes built before 1978.
