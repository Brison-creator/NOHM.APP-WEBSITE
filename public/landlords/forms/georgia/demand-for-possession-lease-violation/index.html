Georgia demand for possession (lease violation)

Georgia has no notice to cure: where the lease ends the tenancy for a violation, you demand possession.

Page updated November 7, 2026

At a glance.

  • WhenThe tenant broke the lease, and the lease says that ends the tenancy (after any cure period it gives).
  • The lawO.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a); O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(d)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Georgia has no statutory notice to cure a lease violation, and no waiting period: your lease decides whether a violation ends the tenancy, and whether the tenant gets time to fix it first. If the lease gives a cure period, give it before you serve this.
  • Name the lease term that was broken, what happened, and the lease clause that ends the tenancy for it.
  • The statute sets no time to leave; NOHM lets you pick the date.
  • Public housing: the demand may go with the federal termination notice, in a separate writing (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(b)).
  • Post it in a sealed envelope on the door AND send it by every other way your lease says notices go (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(d)). Both are required for leases made or renewed on or after July 1, 2024.
  • If the tenant doesn’t pay or leave, you make a dispossessory affidavit under oath, before a magistrate, the court’s clerk or a notary, in the magistrate court of the county where the property is (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a), (c)).
