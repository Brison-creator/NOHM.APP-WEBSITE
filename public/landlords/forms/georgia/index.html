Notices before court.

3-business-day notice to vacate or pay

Demands all past-due rent, late fees, utilities and other charges within 3 business days, or the premises.

When
Rent is past due, on a lease made or renewed on or after July 1, 2024.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(c); O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(d)
Good to know
  • This notice is for leases made or renewed on or after July 1, 2024. For an older lease never renewed, use a demand for possession instead (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a)).
  • Georgia doesn’t define “business day” for this notice. NOHM skips Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, the reading that never gives too few days.
  • Georgia sets no grace period or late-fee cap: your lease does.
  • Once the case is served, the tenant can end it by paying all the rent claimed plus the cost of the dispossessory warrant within 7 days; you must accept that only once in any 12 months (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-52).
  • Post it in a sealed envelope on the door AND send it by every other way your lease says notices go (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(d)). Both are required for leases made or renewed on or after July 1, 2024.
  • If the tenant doesn’t pay or leave, you make a dispossessory affidavit under oath, before a magistrate, the court’s clerk or a notary, in the magistrate court of the county where the property is (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a), (c)).

Demand for possession (lease violation)

Georgia has no notice to cure: where the lease ends the tenancy for a violation, you demand possession.

When
The tenant broke the lease, and the lease says that ends the tenancy (after any cure period it gives).
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a); O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(d)
Good to know
  • Georgia has no statutory notice to cure a lease violation, and no waiting period: your lease decides whether a violation ends the tenancy, and whether the tenant gets time to fix it first. If the lease gives a cure period, give it before you serve this.
  • Name the lease term that was broken, what happened, and the lease clause that ends the tenancy for it.
  • The statute sets no time to leave; NOHM lets you pick the date.
  • Public housing: the demand may go with the federal termination notice, in a separate writing (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(b)).
  • Post it in a sealed envelope on the door AND send it by every other way your lease says notices go (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(d)). Both are required for leases made or renewed on or after July 1, 2024.
  • If the tenant doesn’t pay or leave, you make a dispossessory affidavit under oath, before a magistrate, the court’s clerk or a notary, in the magistrate court of the county where the property is (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a), (c)).

60-day notice to end a tenancy at will

Ends a tenancy with no fixed term on 60 days’ notice.

When
You want a month-to-month (a tenancy at will) to end, no fault needed.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-7
Good to know
  • A tenancy with no fixed term (month-to-month included) is a tenancy at will: the landlord must give 60 days’ notice to end it, the tenant 30 (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-7). The date doesn’t have to end a rental period.
  • The statute doesn’t say how to deliver it. Hand it over or mail it certified and keep proof; NOHM adds 3 days for the mail.
  • A fixed-term lease ends on its own date with no notice required by statute.
  • A tenant who stays after the date is holding over: give a demand for possession (sealed envelope on the door and the lease’s method) before you file the dispossessory affidavit (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a), (d)).
  • You can’t end a tenancy to retaliate within 3 months after a tenant’s protected act (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-24).

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a); O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(d)
Good to know
  • Georgia requires a demand for possession before you file a dispossessory affidavit, when a tenant holds over after the term, is a tenant at will or at sufferance, or (on a lease made before July 1, 2024 and not renewed) hasn’t paid rent (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a)).
  • The statute sets no waiting period: if the tenant refuses, you may file at once. NOHM fills in 3 days; give more if you like.
  • Post it in a sealed envelope on the door AND send it by every way your lease says notices go (§ 44-7-50(d)).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (MAG 30-02, MAG 30-08) Court

What to put on the magistrate court’s dispossessory affidavit, where to get the forms, and the deadlines after you file.

When
The notice or demand ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid or left.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50; O.C.G.A. § 44-7-51; O.C.G.A. §§ 44-7-53 to 44-7-56
Good to know
  • File the dispossessory affidavit in the magistrate court of the county where the property is (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(a)), on the Council of Magistrate Court Judges’ statewide form, MAG 30-02. Most counties take it through their e-filing portal; counties brand the form.
  • You swear to the affidavit before a magistrate, the court’s clerk or deputy clerk, or a notary (§ 44-7-50(a), (c)).
  • A company (an LLC or a corporation) may be represented in magistrate court by a full-time employee or officer it designates on MAG 11-02 (the form cites O.C.G.A. § 15-10-43(i) and Uniform Magistrate Court Rule 31; NOHM read the form, not the rule). In superior or state court, use a lawyer.
  • Don’t cut off the tenant’s heat, cooling, light or water while the case is pending (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-14.1).
  • From January 1, 2027, a court may restrict public access to dispossessory records in some cases (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-50(e), SB 406).

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Before the tenant pays the security deposit; both of you sign it.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-33(a), (c)
Good to know
  • Before the tenant pays a security deposit, give them a comprehensive list of any existing damage. They may inspect the premises; you both sign it, or they dissent in writing; they keep it permanently (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-33(a), (c)).
  • Within 3 business days after the tenant moves out, inspect and make a list of any damage; the tenant may inspect within 5 business days (§ 44-7-33(b)). Without both lists you can’t keep any of the deposit (§ 44-7-35).
  • A natural person who, with spouse and minor children, owns 10 or fewer rental units and manages them without a paid manager is exempt from the lists and the escrow rule (§ 44-7-36). The lists are still good practice.
  • Give in writing, at or before the start of the tenancy, the names and addresses of the owner of record (or an agent for notices) and of the manager (§ 44-7-3).
  • If the living space flooded 3 or more times in the 5 years before the lease, tell the tenant in writing before signing (§ 44-7-20).
  • Tell the tenant in writing where the deposit is held in escrow (§ 44-7-31): use the escrow notice. The deposit can’t be more than two months’ rent on a lease made or renewed on or after July 1, 2024 (§ 44-7-30.1).
  • An owner out of state renting a single-family home or duplex must hire a Georgia-licensed broker, with someone in the state for tenants to deal with (§ 44-7-25). The federal lead-paint disclosure applies to homes built before 1978.

Security deposit escrow notice

Tells the tenant in writing where the security deposit is held.

When
When you take the security deposit.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-31
Good to know
  • Keep the deposit in an escrow account used only for deposits, at a bank or lending institution regulated by Georgia or the United States, and tell the tenant in writing where it is (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-31).
  • Georgia also allows a surety bond instead (§ 44-7-32); NOHM hasn’t read that section.
  • Exempt: a natural person who, with spouse and minor children, owns 10 or fewer rental units and manages them without a paid manager (§ 44-7-36).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • No Georgia statute sets notice, hours or reasons for a landlord’s entry; your lease does. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours’ notice and daytime hours.
  • In an emergency you may need to go in right away; tell the tenant as soon as you can.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 60 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Georgia statute sets notice for a rent increase; during a fixed-term lease the rent changes only as the lease allows. A tenancy at will (a month-to-month) can be ended only on 60 days’ notice (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-7), so NOHM gives 60 days to change its rent: NOHM’s practice, not a statute.
  • No statewide cap. Georgia bars local rent control (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-19; NOHM didn’t re-read it).
  • No rent increase within 3 months after a tenant’s protected act (§ 44-7-24).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-34
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after you get possession back, return the deposit, or send this written statement of the exact reasons for keeping any of it, by first-class mail to the tenant’s last known address (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-34).
  • You may keep: unpaid rent or late fees, rent lost to the tenant’s abandoning the premises, unpaid utilities and pet fees, the cost of repairs or cleaning the tenant contracted for, and actual damages beyond ordinary wear and tear (after mitigating).
  • Make the move-out list of damage within 3 business days after the tenant leaves; the tenant may inspect it within 5 business days (§ 44-7-33(b)).
  • If a refund comes back and you can’t find the tenant, it becomes yours 90 days after you mailed it.
  • Keeping it improperly costs three times the amount withheld plus attorney’s fees, and failing to follow the rules forfeits your right to keep any of it (§ 44-7-35). The 30-day rule applies even to an exempt small landlord (§ 44-7-36).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
O.C.G.A. § 44-7-55(c)
Good to know
  • Georgia sets no notice, storage or sale procedure: once the writ of possession is executed, the tenant’s property is regarded as abandoned (O.C.G.A. § 44-7-55(c)). Holding it, and this notice, are NOHM’s courtesy, not the law’s.
  • For a tenant who left without an eviction, Georgia has no statute on their belongings; ask a lawyer before you dispose of anything.
  • Photograph the property before you move or dispose of it.
