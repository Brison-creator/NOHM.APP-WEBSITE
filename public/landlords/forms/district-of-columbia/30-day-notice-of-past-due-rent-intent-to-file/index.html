District of Columbia 30-day notice of past due rent (intent to file)

The statute’s notice of the rent owed, with a ledger: pay in full and stay, or the landlord may file.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenAt least $600 in rent is past due.
  • The lawD.C. Code § 42-3505.01(a-1); 14 DCMR 4300.7; 14 DCMR 4300.22; D.C. Code § 16-1501(b)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • D.C. Law 26-80 (eff. Dec. 31, 2025) cut the wait before filing to 10 days, but the words the statute prescribes for this notice still say “within 30 days of this notice”, and so does the regulation (14 DCMR 4300.6–4300.7). NOHM uses the longer, safer 30 days: don’t file before the 31st day after service.
  • Don’t serve it unless the tenant owes at least $600 in rent; you can’t file for less (D.C. Code §§ 42-3505.01(a-1)(1), 16-1501(b)).
  • Deliver it both ways: by certified mail (or a tracked delivery service), return receipt requested, AND by hand delivery to the unit or posting on the front door. If you post it, take a photograph with a readable timestamp: the court will ask for it (§ 42-3505.01(a-1)(3)). NOHM counts from the later delivery, adding 3 days for the mail.
  • Attach the ledger of charges and payments for the months behind.
  • The tenant can stay by paying the full balance, and, once a case is filed, by paying it in full before the eviction (redemption).
  • D.C. notices must be served in English and Spanish, and also in the tenant’s primary language if you know (or should know) it is another language D.C. law covers (14 DCMR 4300.22; D.C. Code § 42-3206). NOHM prints English: serve a Spanish translation with it.
  • To file, you’ll also need a valid rental registration or claim of exemption and a current basic business license for rental housing (D.C. Code § 16-1501(c)).
  • A corporation or partnership (treat an LLC the same) can’t appear in the Landlord and Tenant Branch without a lawyer who is a member of the D.C. Bar (L&T Rule 9(b)).
