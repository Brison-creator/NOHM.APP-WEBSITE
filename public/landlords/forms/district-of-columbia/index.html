Notices before court.

30-day notice of past due rent (intent to file)

The statute’s notice of the rent owed, with a ledger: pay in full and stay, or the landlord may file.

When
At least $600 in rent is past due.
The law
D.C. Code § 42-3505.01(a-1); 14 DCMR 4300.7; 14 DCMR 4300.22; D.C. Code § 16-1501(b)
Good to know
  • D.C. Law 26-80 (eff. Dec. 31, 2025) cut the wait before filing to 10 days, but the words the statute prescribes for this notice still say “within 30 days of this notice”, and so does the regulation (14 DCMR 4300.6–4300.7). NOHM uses the longer, safer 30 days: don’t file before the 31st day after service.
  • Don’t serve it unless the tenant owes at least $600 in rent; you can’t file for less (D.C. Code §§ 42-3505.01(a-1)(1), 16-1501(b)).
  • Deliver it both ways: by certified mail (or a tracked delivery service), return receipt requested, AND by hand delivery to the unit or posting on the front door. If you post it, take a photograph with a readable timestamp: the court will ask for it (§ 42-3505.01(a-1)(3)). NOHM counts from the later delivery, adding 3 days for the mail.
  • Attach the ledger of charges and payments for the months behind.
  • The tenant can stay by paying the full balance, and, once a case is filed, by paying it in full before the eviction (redemption).
  • D.C. notices must be served in English and Spanish, and also in the tenant’s primary language if you know (or should know) it is another language D.C. law covers (14 DCMR 4300.22; D.C. Code § 42-3206). NOHM prints English: serve a Spanish translation with it.
  • To file, you’ll also need a valid rental registration or claim of exemption and a current basic business license for rental housing (D.C. Code § 16-1501(c)).
  • A corporation or partnership (treat an LLC the same) can’t appear in the Landlord and Tenant Branch without a lawyer who is a member of the D.C. Bar (L&T Rule 9(b)).

30-day notice to correct or vacate

States the violation, the lease provision or regulation, and what to do to correct it within 30 days.

When
The tenant is violating a substantial lease obligation (not rent) within the last 6 months.
The law
D.C. Code § 42-3505.01(b); 14 DCMR 4301.1–4301.8; 14 DCMR 4300.8; 14 DCMR 4300.22; 14 DCMR 4300.25
Good to know
  • Describe the violation in enough detail for the tenant to understand it, name the lease provision or Housing Regulation that creates the obligation, and say exactly what the tenant must do to correct it (14 DCMR 4301.4).
  • Only a substantial obligation in a valid lease or in the Housing Regulations counts, never the rent (14 DCMR 4301.2). You can’t serve this notice for a violation more than 6 months old (4301.3).
  • Fill in the Rental Accommodations Division’s address and phone (dhcd.dc.gov) and the Office of Human Rights’ contact information (ohr.dc.gov) on the lines in the statements: the regulation requires both (4301.4(d), 4301.6(b)).
  • Within 5 business days after serving it, file a copy with the Rent Administrator, with a certification of how the tenant was served (14 DCMR 4300.8).
  • Serve it in person; if the tenant has left the District or can’t be found, on a person over 16 living there; or, after at least two tries at different times of day, by posting it (with a timestamped photograph) and mailing it first-class within 3 calendar days (14 DCMR 4300.25–4300.26). NOHM adds 3 days for the mail.
  • A violent or dangerous crime has its own 10-day notice to vacate, and an illegal act a court has found has a 30-day notice to vacate (D.C. Code § 42-3505.01(c)). Ask a lawyer before using either.
  • D.C. notices must be served in English and Spanish, and also in the tenant’s primary language if you know (or should know) it is another language D.C. law covers (14 DCMR 4300.22; D.C. Code § 42-3206). NOHM prints English: serve a Spanish translation with it.
  • A corporation or partnership (treat an LLC the same) can’t appear in the Landlord and Tenant Branch without a lawyer who is a member of the D.C. Bar (L&T Rule 9(b)).

Notice to vacate (only for a cause D.C. law lists)

D.C. doesn’t let a landlord end a tenancy because the lease is over; possession is recovered only for a cause the law lists.

When
Only for a listed cause, such as the owner’s own use; ask a lawyer first.
The law
D.C. Code § 42-3505.01(a)(1); D.C. Code § 42-3202(b); 14 DCMR 4300.3

The statute doesn’t set this notice’s exact words: check it before you serve one.

Good to know
  • Don’t use this to end a tenancy because a lease is over: in D.C. that isn’t a ground for eviction.
  • The causes the law allows include an illegal act a court has found (30 days), the owner’s own use (90 days), a sale to a buyer for their own use (90 days, after the tenant’s purchase rights), substantial rehabilitation (120 days), and demolition or ending housing use (180 days), each with its own conditions (D.C. Code § 42-3505.01(c)–(i)). Ask a lawyer first.
  • There’s no separate demand for possession in D.C.
  • D.C. notices must be served in English and Spanish, and also in the tenant’s primary language if you know (or should know) it is another language D.C. law covers (14 DCMR 4300.22; D.C. Code § 42-3206). NOHM prints English: serve a Spanish translation with it.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Forms 1A, 1B, 1C, 1S) Court

What to put on the Superior Court’s mandatory complaint forms, where to get them, and the deadlines after you file.

When
The notice’s 30 days have run and the tenant hasn’t paid or corrected the violation.
The law
D.C. Code § 16-1501; D.C. Code § 16-1502; Super. Ct. L&T R. 3, 9, 14-II, 16; D.C. Code § 42-3505.01a
Good to know
  • A complaint for possession must be filed on the court’s Form 1A, 1B or 1C, verified under oath before a notary, with a prepared Form 1S (L&T Rule 3(a)). The clerk won’t take a homemade complaint.
  • A corporation or partnership can’t appear in the Landlord and Tenant Branch without a member of the D.C. Bar (L&T Rule 9(b)); the rule doesn’t name LLCs, so treat an LLC the same.
  • You can’t file a rent case for less than $600, or any case without a valid rental registration or claim of exemption and a current basic business license for rental housing (D.C. Code § 16-1501(b)–(c)).
  • NOHM counts the notice’s 30 days before filing: the statute now says 10 for a rent case, but the notice’s prescribed words and the regulation still say 30.
  • The court’s Supplement for rent cases dates from 2022 and still lists pandemic-era items; ask the clerk which it still requires.
  • The rule on serving the summons (L&T Rule 4) was amended in 2026 to follow the RENTAL Act; NOHM couldn’t read the order. Check with the clerk.

Final notice of the eviction date (21 days)

The notice D.C. requires at least 21 days before the eviction: the date, the warning about belongings, and the phone numbers.

When
The court issued the writ and the Marshals gave you an eviction date.
The law
D.C. Code § 42-3505.01a(b)
Good to know
  • Deliver it at least 21 days before the eviction date, all three ways: by phone or electronic message (email or text), by first-class mail to the rental unit, and by posting it conspicuously at the unit (D.C. Code § 42-3505.01a(b)(1)). Without it, the court may stop the eviction (§ 42-3505.01a(g)).
  • Fill in the phone numbers of the U.S. Marshals Service and the Landlord and Tenant Branch.
  • Serve it in Spanish too, with the tenant’s primary language if it is another one D.C. law covers.
  • On the eviction day you must also post and mail the day-of-eviction notice, with the text of § 42-3505.01a(c)–(d); NOHM doesn’t have that text.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • D.C. doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It’s good practice: it is your evidence of damage beyond ordinary wear and tear when the deposit comes back.
  • When a prospective tenant applies, give the Rent Administrator’s disclosure form with its documents: the rent, rent-control status, registration, business license, code violations, the deposit and its interest, any indoor mold in the past 3 years not professionally remediated, the Office of the Tenant Advocate’s Tenant Bill of Rights, and the voter registration packet (D.C. Code § 42-3502.22(b)). Tell each tenant where the full disclosure file is kept.
  • At the start of every tenancy, give a copy of 14 DCMR chapter 3 (the landlord-tenant housing regulations) and §§ 101 and 106 of chapter 1 (14 DCMR 300.1).
  • State the security deposit terms in the lease or the receipt (14 DCMR 308.6). The deposit can be no more than the first full month’s rent, charged once, held in an interest-bearing escrow account at a D.C. financial institution (308.2–308.3).
  • A late fee is allowed only if the written lease states its maximum (D.C. Code § 42-3505.31(b)).
  • For rent-controlled units, the notice of the maximum increase for elderly and disabled tenants (§ 42-3502.08). For a new building exempt from the tenant’s purchase rights, the exemption notice before the lease (§ 42-3404.02b(d)).
  • Lead: the federal disclosure for homes built before 1978; D.C. adds its own lead disclosures, which NOHM hasn’t confirmed.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter.
The law
D.C. Code § 42-3505.51
Good to know
  • Written notice at least 48 hours ahead, unless the tenant agrees in writing to less. Enter between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m., not on a Sunday or federal holiday, unless the tenant agrees to another time (D.C. Code § 42-3505.51).
  • Email or text counts as written notice; if the tenant doesn’t acknowledge it in writing, give a paper notice.
  • In an emergency you may enter without notice.

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 61 days before the new rent starts.
The law
D.C. Code § 42-3509.04(b)
Good to know
  • An increase takes effect only on the first rent day more than 60 calendar days after the notice is given (D.C. Code § 42-3509.04(b)): NOHM requires 61 days.
  • For a rent-controlled unit the increase must also meet § 42-3502.08: the unit registered, licensed and in substantial compliance with the housing code; no more than once in 12 months; and for an occupied unit no more than the adjustment of general applicability plus 2%, and never over 10%. The notice must give the current and new rent, the utilities, a summary of the tenant’s rights and a list of help sources.
  • No increase during a lease that fixes the rent. Whether the 60 days reach rent-control-exempt units isn’t settled in the text; NOHM applies them to every unit.

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 45 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
14 DCMR 309 and D.C. Code § 42-3502.17(e)
Good to know
  • Within 45 days after the tenancy ends, either return the deposit and interest, or tell the tenant in writing, personally or by certified mail, what you’ll withhold; then refund the balance with this itemized statement within 30 days of that notice (14 DCMR 309.1–309.2). Sending this statement with the refund within 45 days does both.
  • Since August 14, 2026 the notice of unpaid amounts must include photographs or other documentation, a statement of the tenant’s right to dispute, and your contact information; the tenant has 30 days to dispute and you 10 days to answer (D.C. Code § 42-3502.17(e)). Ask the tenant for a forwarding mail or email address when the tenancy ends. Email is allowed for this notice.
  • No deduction for ordinary wear and tear, including breakage or malfunction from age (§ 42-3502.17(c)). Unpaid invoiced late fees may be deducted (§ 42-3505.31(d)).
  • Interest at the statement savings rate for each six-month period is due on a tenancy of 12 months or more (14 DCMR 311). Enclose the list of rates.
  • Missing the deadline makes the tenant prima facie entitled to the whole deposit; bad faith costs triple (14 DCMR 309.3, 309.5).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
D.C. Code § 42-3505.01a
Good to know
  • Only after an eviction carried out by the U.S. Marshals. D.C. has no procedure for belongings of a tenant who leaves without one, and a landlord may not remove a tenant’s property except as § 42-3505.01a allows.
  • Store it with reasonable care, give at least 16 hours of access between 8 a.m. and 6 p.m. over no more than 2 days, and charge nothing for it. After 7 days (not counting Sundays and federal holidays) it is abandoned: remove or dispose of it, never by putting it outdoors except at a licensed disposal facility or receptacle (D.C. Code § 42-3505.01a).
  • NOHM fills in the date 7 days after the eviction, not counting Sundays and federal holidays.
