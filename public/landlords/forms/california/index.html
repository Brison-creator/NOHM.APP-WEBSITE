Notices before court.

3-day notice to pay rent or quit

Demands the rent due within 3 court days, or the home back, and elects to forfeit the lease.

When
Rent is past due (California has no grace period by statute).
The law
Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(2); Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1162(a); Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1174(a)
Good to know
  • Serve it any time within one year after the rent was due (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(2)). If someone else lives there as a subtenant, serve them too.
  • Under PAYMENT give the name, phone number and address of the person to pay, and the usual days and hours they take payment in person; or a bank account within five miles of the rental; or an electronic transfer already set up (§ 1161(2)).
  • A new owner or manager who hasn’t given the tenant the owner and manager disclosures (Cal. Civ. Code § 1962) can’t serve this notice for rent that came due before they did.
  • You can’t charge the tenant a fee for serving, posting or delivering this notice (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(6)).
  • Whether the law adds days when a copy is mailed with substituted or posted service is unsettled; NOHM adds 5 days when a copy is mailed (as Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 does for papers mailed in a case), which only makes the date later.
  • Cities and counties with rent control or just-cause ordinances (Los Angeles, San Francisco, Oakland, Berkeley, San Jose, Santa Monica and others) may require more words, a copy to a rent board, or a different notice. NOHM doesn’t cover local ordinances.

3-day notice to perform covenant or quit

Requires the tenant to fix a lease violation within 3 court days, or give the home back.

When
The tenant broke a lease term that can still be performed.
The law
Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(3); Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1162(a); Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1174(a); Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2(c)
Good to know
  • Name the covenant and exactly what the tenant must do to perform it.
  • Under the Tenant Protection Act, a curable violation needs this notice first; if it isn’t cured in time, a 3-day notice to quit without a chance to cure may follow (Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2(c)).
  • Some breaches can’t be cured: assigning or subletting against the lease, committing waste, a nuisance, or using the premises for an unlawful purpose. They allow a 3-day notice to quit (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(4)), which NOHM doesn’t draw.
  • You can’t charge the tenant a fee for serving, posting or delivering this notice (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(6)).
  • Whether the law adds days when a copy is mailed with substituted or posted service is unsettled; NOHM adds 5 days when a copy is mailed (as Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 does for papers mailed in a case), which only makes the date later.
  • Cities and counties with rent control or just-cause ordinances (Los Angeles, San Francisco, Oakland, Berkeley, San Jose, Santa Monica and others) may require more words, a copy to a rent board, or a different notice. NOHM doesn’t cover local ordinances.

30- or 60-day notice to end a tenancy

Ends a periodic tenancy: 60 days if a tenant has lived there a year or more, 30 if less, with just cause after 12 months.

When
You want to end a month-to-month; check the Tenant Protection Act and local rules first.
The law
Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.1(b)–(c), (f), (h); Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2

The statute doesn’t set this notice’s exact words: check it before you serve one.

Good to know
  • The notice is 60 days if any tenant has lived there a year or more, 30 days if less (Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.1(b)–(c)); 30 days also for a qualifying sale to a buyer who will live there (§ 1946.1(d)). The date doesn’t have to end a rental period.
  • After 12 months (24 for a tenant joined by new adults), the Tenant Protection Act applies unless the home is exempt: the notice must state the just cause, and a no-fault cause (owner move-in, withdrawal from the market, a government order, demolition or substantial remodel) needs one month’s rent paid within 15 days, or waived, and the notice must say so. A notice that doesn’t strictly comply is void (Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2(a), (b), (d)).
  • A single-family home or condo owned by individuals (not a REIT, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member) is exempt only if the lease gave the tenant the statute’s exemption notice (Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2(e)(8)).
  • California has no separate demand for possession: after this notice runs, or a fixed-term lease ends, you file the unlawful detainer (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1161(1), (5)).
  • For a property with a federally backed mortgage or in a federal housing program, the CARES Act requires 30 days’ notice for nonpayment.
  • You can’t charge for serving the notice (Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.1(i)).
  • Cities and counties with rent control or just-cause ordinances (Los Angeles, San Francisco, Oakland, Berkeley, San Jose, Santa Monica and others) may require more words, a copy to a rent board, or a different notice. NOHM doesn’t cover local ordinances.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (UD-100, SUM-130, CM-010)

What to write on the Judicial Council’s unlawful detainer complaint, summons and cover sheet, filled in from the lease, with the steps and deadlines that follow.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §§ 1166, 1167, 1169, 1170.5, 1174; Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §§ 715.010–715.020; Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2
Good to know
  • Use the Judicial Council’s forms: the summons (SUM-130) and cover sheet (CM-010) are mandatory, and the complaint form (UD-100) asks every question the statute does. NOHM fills in what to write on them.
  • Don’t use UD-101 (Plaintiff’s Mandatory Cover Sheet): the COVID-19 law it rests on is no longer in the code. Ask the clerk if your court still lists it.
  • An individual landlord can file without a lawyer. A company generally must appear through an attorney (case law NOHM didn’t read), and verifies the complaint on a different form.
  • Cities with rent control or just-cause ordinances add steps; UD-100 asks about them (item 17). NOHM doesn’t cover local ordinances.
  • Filing fees vary with the amount claimed; ask the clerk.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • California doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice, signed by both of you. Photos are required: for tenancies beginning on or after July 1, 2025, photograph the unit immediately before, or at the start of, the tenancy (Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5(g)(1)).
  • In the lease, give the name, phone and address of each manager and of the owner or agent for notices and service, and who, where and how rent is paid (Cal. Civ. Code § 1962).
  • Unless the home is exempt, the lease (or a signed notice) must carry the Tenant Protection Act notice in the statute’s words, in at least 12-point type (Cal. Civ. Code § 1946.2(f)(3)). A single-family home or condo exempt under § 1946.2(e)(8) must have the statute’s exemption notice in the lease instead. Put these in the lease itself.
  • Other required notices: Megan’s Law database notice (Civ. Code § 2079.10a), flood hazard (Gov. Code § 8589.45), bed bugs (Civ. Code § 1954.603), known mold (Health & Safety Code § 26147), pest control service (Civ. Code § 1940.8), a pending demolition permit (§ 1940.6), former ordnance nearby (§ 1940.7), shared utility meters (§ 1940.9), the offer of positive rent reporting (§ 1954.07), and a working stove and refrigerator for leases from January 1, 2026 (§ 1941.1). For a home built before 1978, the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Cal. Civ. Code § 1954(d)(1)
Good to know
  • Give it in writing, with the date, approximate time and purpose; enter only during normal business hours (Cal. Civ. Code § 1954(b), (d)(1)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, leave it with someone of suitable age at the home, or leave it on, near or under the usual entry door where they’ll find it: 24 hours is presumed reasonable. If you mail it, mail it at least 6 days ahead.
  • To show the home to a buyer, notice can be oral if you told the tenant in writing within the last 120 days that it’s for sale (§ 1954(d)(2)). No notice is needed in an emergency, if the tenant agrees at the time, or after they’ve abandoned it (§ 1954(e)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Cal. Civ. Code § 827(b)
Good to know
  • An increase of 10% or less needs at least 30 days’ notice; more than 10% (counted against the lowest rent in the past 12 months) needs at least 90 days (Cal. Civ. Code § 827(b)(2)–(3)). Mailed, add 5 days (Code Civ. Proc. § 1013).
  • For a home the Tenant Protection Act covers, the increase over any 12 months can’t exceed 5% plus the regional cost-of-living change, or 10%, whichever is lower, of the lowest rent in the past 12 months, in no more than two steps (Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12(a)). NOHM stops anything over 10%; check the lower figure for your region.
  • Exempt from the cap: housing first occupied within the last 15 years, an owner-occupied duplex, and a single-family home or condo owned by individuals whose lease has the exemption notice (§ 1947.12(d)).
  • City rent control (Los Angeles, San Francisco, Oakland and others) may allow less; NOHM doesn’t cover it. No retaliatory increases (Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 21 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5(h)
Good to know
  • Send it, with any refund, no later than 21 days after the tenant vacates (Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5(h)(1)).
  • If the deposit or rent was paid electronically, return the refund electronically to the account the tenant names, unless you agree otherwise; tell the tenant of that right near the end of the tenancy. For several adult tenants, one check payable to all of them unless they all agree otherwise.
  • Attach receipts, invoices and photos for each repair or cleaning deduction; for your own work, the time spent and the hourly rate. Not needed if the deductions total $125 or less (§ 1950.5(h)(2)–(4)).
  • Take photos after the tenant leaves, before and after repairs and cleaning (§ 1950.5(g)(2)). Before the tenancy ends, offer the tenant an initial inspection (§ 1950.5(f)).
  • Keeping a deposit in bad faith can cost up to twice the deposit plus actual damages (§ 1950.5(m)). Since July 1, 2024, a deposit is capped at one month’s rent (two for a small landlord with no more than two properties and four units). Nothing may be “nonrefundable.” Some cities may require interest on deposits (NOHM didn’t check local law).

Notice of right to reclaim abandoned property

The statute’s own notice to a former tenant: what they left, where to claim it, and the date after which it may be disposed of.

When
After the tenant vacated and left things behind.
The law
Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1983–1984
Good to know
  • This is the form Cal. Civ. Code § 1984(a) sets out, word for word.
  • The last day to claim must be at least 15 days after you hand it over, or 18 days after you mail it (Cal. Civ. Code § 1983(b)). Mail it first class to the tenant’s last known address, and if you mail it, send one copy to the vacated premises too; you may also email it if the tenant gave you an email (§ 1983(c)).
  • Property worth $700 or more is sold at a public sale after publication in a newspaper; under $700 you may keep, sell or destroy it (Cal. Civ. Code § 1988). Store it with reasonable care meanwhile.
  • Property that belongs to someone other than the tenant takes a different notice (Cal. Civ. Code § 1985). After a writ of possession, the rules in Code Civ. Proc. § 1174(f)–(i) apply. NOHM doesn’t draw those.
