New Mexico 3-day notice of nonpayment of rent

Tells the resident the rent owed: pay it in full within 3 days, or the owner may end the rental agreement and file to evict.

At a glance.

  • WhenRent is unpaid when due (New Mexico has no grace period).
  • The lawN.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-33(D); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-33(H); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-13(D)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • Serve it any day after rent is unpaid when due; New Mexico has no grace period (§ 47-8-15(B)).
  • Under “Payment”, say how you’ll accept it (cash, money order, cashier’s or certified check, or personal check) and where. If the tenant tenders the full amount that way before the 3 days end, you can’t file for nonpayment (§ 47-8-33(D)).
  • This is the courts’ Form 4-901 NMRA, with its tenant-rights statements. Keep two copies: attach one to the petition.
  • Write on it the date you hand it over, mail it or post it: that date is the notice’s effective date (§ 47-8-13(D)).
  • A last day on a weekend or federal holiday moves to the next day that isn’t one (§ 47-8-33(H)). Whether weekends in between also don’t count (NMSA § 12-2A-7(E)) isn’t settled; NOHM counts calendar days. NOHM adds 3 days when you mail it.
  • If the mail comes back, or the last known address is the unit the tenant left, serve another notice at any other address the tenant gave you (§ 47-8-13(F)).
  • Late fees: only if the lease provides them, at most 5% of the rent for each period in default, and you must give notice of the fee by the end of the next rental period (§ 47-8-15(D)).
  • A rent payment can’t be put toward deposits or damages unless you both agreed in writing (§ 47-8-15(G)).
