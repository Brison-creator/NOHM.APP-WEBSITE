Notices before court.

3-day notice of nonpayment of rent

Tells the resident the rent owed: pay it in full within 3 days, or the owner may end the rental agreement and file to evict.

When
Rent is unpaid when due (New Mexico has no grace period).
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-33(D); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-33(H); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-13(D)
Good to know
  • Serve it any day after rent is unpaid when due; New Mexico has no grace period (§ 47-8-15(B)).
  • Under “Payment”, say how you’ll accept it (cash, money order, cashier’s or certified check, or personal check) and where. If the tenant tenders the full amount that way before the 3 days end, you can’t file for nonpayment (§ 47-8-33(D)).
  • This is the courts’ Form 4-901 NMRA, with its tenant-rights statements. Keep two copies: attach one to the petition.
  • Write on it the date you hand it over, mail it or post it: that date is the notice’s effective date (§ 47-8-13(D)).
  • A last day on a weekend or federal holiday moves to the next day that isn’t one (§ 47-8-33(H)). Whether weekends in between also don’t count (NMSA § 12-2A-7(E)) isn’t settled; NOHM counts calendar days. NOHM adds 3 days when you mail it.
  • If the mail comes back, or the last known address is the unit the tenant left, serve another notice at any other address the tenant gave you (§ 47-8-13(F)).
  • Late fees: only if the lease provides them, at most 5% of the rent for each period in default, and you must give notice of the fee by the end of the next rental period (§ 47-8-15(D)).
  • A rent payment can’t be put toward deposits or damages unless you both agreed in writing (§ 47-8-15(G)).

Read the law: gaar.com, nmlegis.gov, supremecourt.nmcourts.gov

7-day notice of noncompliance

Describes the breach with its dates and facts: fix it within 7 days, or the rental agreement ends.

When
The resident broke the rental agreement (not rent), within the last 30 days or since you learned of it.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-33(A); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-33(C); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-33(H); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-13(D)
Good to know
  • For the first material breach of the lease (other than rent), or a breach of the tenant’s duties under § 47-8-22 that materially affects health and safety.
  • Describe what happened, with the dates and specific facts: the court can grant relief only on the grounds in the notice (§ 47-8-33(G)).
  • Serve it within 30 days of the breach, or of when you learned of it, or it isn’t effective (§ 47-8-33(C)).
  • A second material breach within 6 months of the first gets a 7-day notice with no chance to fix it (§ 47-8-33(B); Form 4-902’s “Second notice”). A substantial violation (drugs, a deadly weapon, serious harm, sexual assault, a break-in, robbery, or over $1,000 of damage, on or within 300 feet of the premises) gets a 3-day notice with no cure (§ 47-8-33(I); Form 4-901A). NOHM’s notice is the first-breach notice; ask a lawyer for the others.
  • A posted notice must also be mailed first class or handed over (§ 47-8-13(D)). NOHM adds 3 days when you mail it.

Read the law: gaar.com, nmlegis.gov, supremecourt.nmcourts.gov

30-day notice to terminate a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month residency at least 30 days before the rental date named in it.

When
You want a month-to-month resident out, no fault needed.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-37(B); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-13(D)
Good to know
  • A week-to-week residency needs 7 days’ written notice before the termination date instead (§ 47-8-37(A)).
  • A fixed-term lease ends on its end date; the statute requires no notice for it. Check your lease.
  • New Mexico requires no separate demand for possession: if the tenant stays after the date, you may file a petition for restitution (§ 47-8-37(C)).
  • You can’t end a tenancy, or threaten to, in retaliation within 6 months after the tenant’s protected acts (§ 47-8-39).
  • NOHM adds 3 days when you mail it.

Read the law: gaar.com, firstdistrict.nmcourts.gov

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (Forms 4-904, 4-905, 4-909, 4-913 NMRA)

The court’s own petition for restitution and the papers after it, each with where to get it, the values from your lease to copy onto it, and the steps and deadlines.

When
The notice ran out (or the term ended) and the resident hasn’t left.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-42; N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-43; N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-46(A); N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-47(A)
Good to know
  • New Mexico’s Supreme Court has its own landlord-tenant forms. Clerks expect them, so NOHM fills in this worksheet for you to copy from, not a petition of its own. The worksheet isn’t filed.
  • Some courts use the Eviction Prevention and Diversion Program’s forms (4-904B petition, 4-905B summons) in nonpayment cases, and the 4-904B petition asks for a sworn affirmation and the court’s Resource Information Sheet. NOHM couldn’t confirm the program still runs in every district in 2026: ask the clerk which petition to use.
  • A company owner: in magistrate and metropolitan court, a closely held corporation or LLC may appear through an officer, member or manager it authorizes on Form 4-403 NMRA, sworn before a notary; in district court, it generally needs a lawyer. NOHM read the form, not the court rules themselves (Rules 2-107 and 3-107), so check with the clerk.
  • Never lock the resident out, shut off utilities or remove their things without a court order: the penalty is twice the monthly rent, and more (§ 47-8-36, § 47-8-48(C)).
  • Bring the rental agreement, every notice with proof of how and when you served it, and the rent ledger.

Read the law: gaar.com, selfrepresentation.nmcourts.gov, firstdistrict.nmcourts.gov, supremecourt.nmcourts.gov

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • New Mexico doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It’s good practice: a signed record of the home’s condition settles deposit disputes.
  • Give the resident a written rental agreement before the residency begins (§ 47-8-20(G)), and a copy of any rules when they sign it, or the rules can’t be enforced (§ 47-8-23(F)).
  • Disclose in writing, at or before the start, who manages the home and who receives notices and legal papers for the owner (§ 47-8-19(A)): NOHM has that disclosure.
  • Listings must show the base rent and every fee (§ 47-8-19.1); a screening fee is capped at $50, with the applicant’s written agreement and a receipt (§ 47-8-19.2).
  • Homes built before 1978: give the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Read the law: gaar.com, nmlegis.gov

Disclosure of owner and manager

Tells the resident, in writing, who manages the home and who receives notices and legal papers for the owner.

When
At or before the start of the residency, and again when either changes.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-19(A)
Good to know
  • Required in writing at or before the residency begins (§ 47-8-19(A)), and kept current (§ 47-8-19(B)).
  • Without it, the resident is relieved of the duty to give you the notices the Act otherwise requires (§ 47-8-19(D)).
  • A property manager may sign for the owner.

Read the law: gaar.com

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-24(A)
Good to know
  • Give 24 hours’ written notice of your intent to enter, its purpose, and its date and estimated time frame, unless you and the resident agreed otherwise (§ 47-8-24(A)(1)).
  • No notice is needed to make repairs the resident asked for within 7 days of the request, or when you’re with a public inspector or a utility or cable representative (§ 47-8-24(A)(2)); or in an emergency (§ 47-8-24(B)).
  • If the resident offers another reasonable time, try to accommodate it (§ 47-8-24(A)(3)). Don’t use entry to harass (§ 47-8-24(C)).
  • A resident who refuses lawful access can be ordered to allow it, or the rental agreement ended (§ 47-8-24(E)).

Read the law: gaar.com

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-15(F)
Good to know
  • Month-to-month: at least 30 days before the rental date the new rent starts (§ 47-8-15(F)). A fixed term: at least 30 days before the term ends. A residency shorter than a month: one rental period ahead.
  • Raising a fee in the rental agreement (not the rent) needs 60 days’ written notice (§ 47-8-19.4).
  • New Mexico has no rent cap. You can’t raise the rent to retaliate (§ 47-8-39(A)); an increase made uniformly is allowed (§ 47-8-39(C)).

Read the law: gaar.com, nmlegis.gov

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 30 days after the agreement ends or the resident leaves, whichever is later.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-18(C)
Good to know
  • Within 30 days after the rental agreement ends or the resident leaves, whichever is later, give an itemized list of the deductions and the balance. Mailing it, and any payment, to the resident’s last known address counts (§ 47-8-18(C)).
  • Nothing may be kept for normal wear and tear; uncleanliness isn’t normal wear and tear (§ 47-8-3(K)).
  • Miss the 30 days and you lose the right to keep any of it, to counterclaim for it, and to sue for damage to the home, and you owe the resident’s court costs and attorney’s fees (§ 47-8-18(D)). Keeping it in bad faith adds a $250 penalty (§ 47-8-18(E)).
  • Interest: on a one-year agreement with a deposit over one month’s rent, pay the resident interest yearly at the passbook rate (§ 47-8-18(A)(1)). For a shorter agreement the deposit can’t exceed one month’s rent (§ 47-8-18(A)(2)).

Read the law: gaar.com

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
N.M. Stat. Ann. § 47-8-34.1(A)
Good to know
  • This notice is for property left after the resident abandoned the unit: absent more than 7 days in a row without telling you, after the rent is delinquent (§ 47-8-3(A)).
  • Store the property at least 30 days, and give this notice in person or by first-class mail to the last known address. If it comes back, or that address is the vacated unit, also send one to any other address the resident gave you: work, family or emergency contact (§ 47-8-34.1(A)(3)).
  • The notice must give a telephone number and an address where the resident can reach you to get the property back (§ 47-8-34.1(A)(2)).
  • A resident who surrendered the unit: store their property at least 14 days, with reasonable access; no notice is required (§ 47-8-34.1(B)). After a writ of restitution is executed: no duty to store it past 3 days (§ 47-8-34.1(C)).
  • Property worth $100 or less may be disposed of any way. Over $100: sell it, or keep it at fair market value, and mail any surplus with an itemized statement within 15 days (§ 47-8-34.1(D)–(E)). You may ask for reasonable storage and moving costs before releasing it, but can’t hold it for other debts (§ 47-8-34.1(G)–(H)).

Read the law: gaar.com
