Oregon notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenAfter the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
  • The lawOr. Rev. Stat. § 90.425
  • StageMove-out

What to know.

  • Use it once the tenancy has ended and you reasonably believe the tenant left the property with no intent to claim it, after the tenant was absent 7 days following an unexecuted termination order, or after the sheriff restored possession to you (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.425(2)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class to the premises, any post-office box you know of, and any forwarding address (§ 90.425(3)).
  • The date to contact you must be at least 5 days after personal delivery or 8 days after mailing (§ 90.425(6)); NOHM requires 8.
  • Store the property safely, with reasonable care. After the tenant contacts you, make it available for 15 days by appointment (§ 90.425(7)–(8)).
  • Unclaimed, you may sell it, or dispose of it if it is worth $1,000 or less or less than the cost of storage and sale. From a sale’s proceeds deduct your costs and unpaid rent and remit the rest with an itemized accounting (§ 90.425(10), (13)).
  • Not for a manufactured dwelling, floating home or recreational vehicle: those have longer periods and lienholder notices.
  • Breaking these rules can cost twice actual damages (§ 90.425(15), (17)).
