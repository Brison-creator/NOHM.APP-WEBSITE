Oregon 30-day notice with stated cause (14 days to cure)

Says what the tenant did wrong and how to fix it: cure in 14 days, or the rental agreement ends in 30.

At a glance.

  • WhenThe tenant materially broke the lease or a tenant’s duty (not rent alone).
  • The lawOr. Rev. Stat. § 90.392(2)–(4); Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155; Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.160
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • In the description, say exactly what the tenant did or failed to do, AND at least one way they can fix it: the statute requires both (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.392(3)).
  • Causes: a material violation of the lease (including not paying a late charge or a utility or service charge), a material violation of the tenant’s duties under Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.325, or failure to pay rent (§ 90.392(2)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class (not certified: Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.100(18)) and NOHM adds the 3 days the law adds. Mail-and-attach works only if your written lease allows it for notices from the tenant to you too; mail-and-email only with the statutory email addendum signed after move-in (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155(1)(c)–(d), (5)). Neither adds days.
  • The same violation again within 6 months of this notice: a 10-day notice with no right to cure (§ 90.392(5)). Week to week: 7 days, 4 to cure (§ 90.392(6)). NOHM’s notice is the first one; ask a lawyer for the others.
  • Serious acts (threats or injury, reckless endangerment, intentional damage, an act “outrageous in the extreme”) allow a 24-hour notice (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.396). Ask a lawyer.
  • This is a “tenant cause” notice, so you may use it after the first year of a tenancy (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(3)(c)(A)).
  • If the tenancy ends and the tenant stays, file the eviction: no separate demand for possession is needed (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(11)).
