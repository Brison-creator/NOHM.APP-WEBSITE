Notices before court.

13-day notice of nonpayment of rent

Gives the tenant 13 days to pay the rent owed, or the rental agreement ends. Goes with the court’s rental-assistance notice.

When
Rent is unpaid on the 5th day of the rental period (counting the due date).
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.394(2)(b), (3); Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.395(2); Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155; Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.160
Good to know
  • Serve it no sooner than the 5th day of the rental period, counting the day rent is due: rent due on the 1st, serve on the 5th or later (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.394(2)(b)). Oregon also has a 10-day notice from the 8th day; NOHM uses the 13-day notice.
  • Attach the Oregon Judicial Department’s rental-assistance notice (FED-Notice.pdf, all its languages) to every copy. Without it the court must dismiss the eviction (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.395(3)).
  • The notice must give the amount and a date and time to pay: NOHM gives 11:59 p.m. on the last day, when Oregon’s day ends (Or. Rev. Stat. §§ 90.160(1), 90.394(3)). Weekends and holidays aren’t skipped.
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class (not certified: Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.100(18)) and NOHM adds the 3 days the law adds. Mail-and-attach works only if your written lease allows it for notices from the tenant to you too; mail-and-email only with the statutory email addendum signed after move-in (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155(1)(c)–(d), (5)). Neither adds days.
  • A payment the tenant mails within the period is on time, unless you served this in person, by mail-and-attach or by mail-and-email AND both your lease and this notice name a place to pay (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.394(4)).
  • You must accept a check or another commercially reasonable payment and may not insist on electronic payment; refusing an allowed payment bars this notice (Or. Laws 2026, ch. 23, § 3).
  • Taking partial rent after this notice waives it unless you and the tenant signed a non-waiver agreement (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.417(4)).
  • A week-to-week tenancy uses a 72-hour notice instead (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.394(1)); ask a lawyer.
  • If the rent isn’t paid, file the eviction: Oregon needs no separate demand for possession, so NOHM has none.

30-day notice with stated cause (14 days to cure)

Says what the tenant did wrong and how to fix it: cure in 14 days, or the rental agreement ends in 30.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease or a tenant’s duty (not rent alone).
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.392(2)–(4); Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155; Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.160
Good to know
  • In the description, say exactly what the tenant did or failed to do, AND at least one way they can fix it: the statute requires both (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.392(3)).
  • Causes: a material violation of the lease (including not paying a late charge or a utility or service charge), a material violation of the tenant’s duties under Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.325, or failure to pay rent (§ 90.392(2)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class (not certified: Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.100(18)) and NOHM adds the 3 days the law adds. Mail-and-attach works only if your written lease allows it for notices from the tenant to you too; mail-and-email only with the statutory email addendum signed after move-in (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155(1)(c)–(d), (5)). Neither adds days.
  • The same violation again within 6 months of this notice: a 10-day notice with no right to cure (§ 90.392(5)). Week to week: 7 days, 4 to cure (§ 90.392(6)). NOHM’s notice is the first one; ask a lawyer for the others.
  • Serious acts (threats or injury, reckless endangerment, intentional damage, an act “outrageous in the extreme”) allow a 24-hour notice (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.396). Ask a lawyer.
  • This is a “tenant cause” notice, so you may use it after the first year of a tenancy (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(3)(c)(A)).
  • If the tenancy ends and the tenant stays, file the eviction: no separate demand for possession is needed (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(11)).

Notice ending a month-to-month (30 or 90 days)

Ends a month-to-month: 30 days in the first year, no reason needed; after it, 90 days for a qualifying landlord reason.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out and the law allows it.
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(3), (5), (6); Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155; Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.160
Good to know
  • First year of occupancy (any tenant has lived there a year or less): 30 days, no reason needed (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(3)(b)). NOHM uses 30 days in the first year of the lease and 90 after it.
  • After the first year you may end a month-to-month tenancy only for tenant cause (use the nonpayment or violation notice) or for a qualifying landlord reason, with 90 days’ notice that states the reason and its supporting facts: you will demolish it or convert it to non-residential use; you will repair or renovate and it is, or will be, unsafe or unfit to live in; you or an immediate family member will live there and you own no comparable unit in the building that is free; or you accepted an offer from a buyer who will live there, with written evidence of the offer attached (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(5)–(6)). Write the reason and facts in the notice’s Reason box.
  • With that 90-day notice, pay the tenant one month’s rent when you deliver it, unless you own 4 or fewer units (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(6)).
  • Breaking these rules costs three months’ rent plus actual damages, and is a defense to the eviction (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(9)).
  • Shorter periods that NOHM doesn’t use: 60 days for an accepted owner-occupant purchase offer plus an extra month’s rent (§ 90.427(5)(b)); 60 days without cause in an owner-occupied building of 2 units or fewer (§ 90.427(8)). A week-to-week tenancy needs 10 days (§ 90.427(2)).
  • A fixed-term lease can’t be ended early without cause. One that ends in the first year: 30 days before its end date. One that ends after the first year becomes month to month unless one of the § 90.427(4) exceptions applies.
  • The tenancy ends on the date given, whatever the rental period (§ 90.427(10)). If you end a tenancy in its first year, the next tenant’s rent is capped (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.323(4)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class (not certified: Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.100(18)) and NOHM adds the 3 days the law adds. Mail-and-attach works only if your written lease allows it for notices from the tenant to you too; mail-and-email only with the statutory email addendum signed after move-in (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.155(1)(c)–(d), (5)). Neither adds days.
  • Portland and some other cities add relocation payments and longer notice; NOHM hasn’t confirmed them. Check your city.
  • If the tenant stays after the date, file the eviction: Oregon needs no separate demand for possession (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.427(11)).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (OJD Residential Eviction Complaint and Summons)

What to copy onto the Oregon Judicial Department’s eviction forms, where to get them, and each step and deadline after you file.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. §§ 105.110–105.168; Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.395
Good to know
  • Oregon’s courts use statewide eviction forms. Clerks expect them, so NOHM doesn’t write the complaint: copy these values onto the OJD forms (or use the court’s iForms at courts.oregon.gov/iforms).
  • Attach a copy of the notice you relied on to the complaint (Or. Rev. Stat. § 105.124(3)).
  • An agent or employee can appear for you, without a lawyer (§ 105.130(4)).

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
Good to know
  • Oregon doesn’t require a move-in checklist. It is good practice: walk through with the tenant, both sign it, and keep a copy for the deposit accounting.
  • In writing at or before move-in: who manages the premises, and an owner or the owner’s agent for service of process and notices (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.305(1)). Keep it current.
  • Before the lease is signed, for a building of 4 or fewer units: any notice of default or trustee’s sale, pending foreclosure, forfeiture or tax-lien foreclosure (§ 90.310).
  • In the rental agreement: that the unit is in a 100-year flood plain, if it is (§ 90.228); the smoking policy (§ 90.220(4)); the deposit and any fees (§§ 90.300(3), 90.302).
  • At move-in: carbon monoxide alarm testing instructions (§§ 90.316, 90.317), a receipt for the deposit (§ 90.300(2)), and a copy of the signed rental agreement (§ 90.220(3)).
  • Built before 1978: give the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 24 hours before you enter.
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.322(1)(f)
Good to know
  • Give at least 24 hours’ actual notice and enter only at reasonable times, unless you and the tenant agree otherwise for a specific entry (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.322(1)(f)). If the tenant says no after getting it, you may not enter.
  • In an emergency you may enter without notice; if the tenant wasn’t there, give actual notice within 24 hours after, with the date and time, the emergency and who entered (§ 90.322(1)(b)).
  • A tenant’s written repair request lets you enter for that repair for 7 days (§ 90.322(1)(c)).
  • An unlawful entry can cost at least one month’s rent (§ 90.322(8)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 90 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.323(2)–(3)
Good to know
  • Oregon caps rent increases: for 2027 the maximum is 10% (it was 9.5% for 2026). The Department of Administrative Services publishes each year’s by September 30 (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.324(1)(b)).
  • No increase in the first year of a tenancy, none more than once in 12 months, and at least 90 days’ written notice (§ 90.323(2)). Week to week: 7 days (§ 90.323(1)).
  • The notice states the amount of the increase, the new rent and the date it takes effect (§ 90.323(3)). NOHM’s paper does.
  • A building whose first certificate of occupancy is under 15 years old, and some regulated affordable housing, are exempt from the cap; the notice must then state the facts of the exemption (§ 90.323(5)). NOHM’s form doesn’t; ask a lawyer.
  • Breaking these rules costs three months’ rent plus actual damages (§ 90.323(6)). A fixed-term lease’s rent can’t be changed unless it allows it.

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 31 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.300(12)–(14)
Good to know
  • Give the written accounting, and the balance, within 31 days after the tenancy ends and the tenant gives back possession (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.300(12)–(13)).
  • Deliver it in person or by first-class mail; by email only under a signed email-notice addendum (§ 90.300(14)).
  • Deduct only what is reasonably necessary for the tenant’s defaults (including unpaid rent) and for damage they caused beyond ordinary wear and tear; labor at a reasonable hourly rate. Carpet cleaning only if it was machine-cleaned, cleaned before move-in, and your written lease allows the deduction (§ 90.300(7)).
  • A last month’s rent deposit is a security deposit; apply it to the last month after any termination notice other than a nonpayment one (§ 90.300(9)).
  • Withholding without a written accounting, or in bad faith, costs twice the amount withheld (§ 90.300(16)).
  • Oregon doesn’t cap deposits or require interest.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.425
Good to know
  • Use it once the tenancy has ended and you reasonably believe the tenant left the property with no intent to claim it, after the tenant was absent 7 days following an unexecuted termination order, or after the sheriff restored possession to you (Or. Rev. Stat. § 90.425(2)).
  • Hand it to the tenant, or mail it first class to the premises, any post-office box you know of, and any forwarding address (§ 90.425(3)).
  • The date to contact you must be at least 5 days after personal delivery or 8 days after mailing (§ 90.425(6)); NOHM requires 8.
  • Store the property safely, with reasonable care. After the tenant contacts you, make it available for 15 days by appointment (§ 90.425(7)–(8)).
  • Unclaimed, you may sell it, or dispose of it if it is worth $1,000 or less or less than the cost of storage and sale. From a sale’s proceeds deduct your costs and unpaid rent and remit the rest with an itemized accounting (§ 90.425(10), (13)).
  • Not for a manufactured dwelling, floating home or recreational vehicle: those have longer periods and lienholder notices.
  • Breaking these rules can cost twice actual damages (§ 90.425(15), (17)).
