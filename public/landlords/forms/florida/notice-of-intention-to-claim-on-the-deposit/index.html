Florida notice of intention to claim on the deposit

The statute’s notice of what you’ll deduct from the deposit, and why, giving the tenant 15 days to object.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenWithin 30 days after the rental agreement ends, if you’ll keep any of the deposit.
  • The lawFla. Stat. § 83.49(3)(a)
  • StageMove-out

What to know.

  • Within 30 days after the rental agreement ends, send this by certified mail to the tenant’s last known mailing address, or by e-mail under § 83.505. Miss it and you lose the right to claim against the deposit (Fla. Stat. § 83.49(3)(a)).
  • If you won’t claim anything, don’t send this: return the whole deposit, with any interest owed, within 15 days.
  • The tenant has 15 days after receiving it to object in writing. If they don’t, deduct the claim and send the balance within 30 days of the date of this notice (§ 83.49(3)(b)).
  • Add interest only if the deposit was in an interest-bearing account or under a surety bond (§ 83.49(1)).
  • A tenant who left without 7 days’ written notice, by certified mail or in person, with a forwarding address, relieves you of the notice requirement, but doesn’t forfeit the deposit (§ 83.49(5)).
  • The statute’s form is printed word for word; your address for objections is the one you give as yours.
