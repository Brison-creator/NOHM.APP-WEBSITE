Florida notice of right to reclaim abandoned property

The statute’s notice to a former tenant of the property left behind, where to claim it, and by when.

What to know.

  • After the tenancy ends and the tenant has left, by eviction or otherwise, send this to the former tenant (and a similar notice to anyone you believe owns any of the property) personally or by first-class mail to the last known address and any other address you know (Fla. Stat. § 715.104).
  • The date to claim it must be at least 10 days after you hand it over, or at least 15 days after you mail it.
  • Store the property with reasonable care, on the premises or in a safe place (§ 715.107). If it’s worth $500 or more and isn’t claimed, it’s sold at a public sale after advertising once a week for two weeks; the balance goes to the county (§ 715.109). Under $500 you may keep, sell or destroy it.
  • If your lease carries the legend of Fla. Stat. § 83.67(5), you need not follow this procedure after the tenant surrenders or abandons the unit, or after the last tenant dies.
  • When the sheriff executes a writ of possession, you may remove property found on the premises to or near the property line instead (§ 83.62(2)).
