Notices before court.

14-day notice of nonpayment (pay or quit)

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid and that the lease ends if it isn’t paid within 14 days.

When
Rent is unpaid when due (Virginia sets no grace period; with no written lease, rent is late after the 5th).
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1245(F); Va. Code § 55.1-1200; Va. Code § 55.1-1202; Va. Code § 55.1-1250(A)
Good to know
  • The notice is 14 days since July 1, 2026 (it was 5). Serve it any time after rent is unpaid when due; the statute sets no grace period. With no written lease, rent is due on the 1st and late after the 5th (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(C)(4)).
  • Serve it by hand, by regular mail to the tenant’s last known residence, electronically if the lease provides for it, or by the sheriff, and keep the certificate of service: the Act’s definition of notice requires one. NOHM adds 3 days when it’s mailed; the Act adds none.
  • The notice includes the acceptance-with-reservation language of Va. Code § 55.1-1250(A), so you can take a partial payment and still evict. If you file, give the court a copy of this notice to serve with the summons.
  • The tenant can still redeem by paying everything owed at or before the first court date, and after judgment up to 48 hours before the eviction (Va. Code § 55.1-1250). A landlord of 4 or fewer units can limit that to once per lease period by telling the tenant in writing.
  • For a tenant with a Housing Choice Voucher or other tenant-based rental assistance, the notice works only if the statewide legal aid number and website are on its first page, in type as large as the rest (Va. Code § 55.1-1202(D)). NOHM prints them in the statements; check they fall on page 1.
  • If a check bounced or a funds transfer was rejected, the notice must demand payment by cash, cashier’s check, certified check or a completed electronic funds transfer (Va. Code § 55.1-1245(F)). Say so in the notice.
  • To claim attorney’s fees or civil recovery later, the notice of Va. Code § 55.1-1202 may be included in this notice.
  • The 14th day can fall on a weekend or holiday; Virginia’s time rule (Va. Code § 1-210) doesn’t clearly move it. Give a later date if you want to be safe.
  • From July 1, 2027, the notice must include a statement of charges and payments over the tenancy or the past 12 months (Va. Code § 55.1-1202(E)), and a landlord of more than 4 units must offer a payment plan when the rent owed is one month’s rent plus late charges or less (Va. Code § 55.1-1245(G)). This notice doesn’t have them yet: don’t use it on or after that date without them.
  • The court won’t order possession unless a copy of a proper termination notice is in evidence (Va. Code § 8.01-126(D)). Keep this one and its certificate of service.

21/30-day notice of material noncompliance

Tells the tenant what breaks the lease: remedy it in 21 days, or the lease ends in 30.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease, or a health and safety duty.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1245(A); Va. Code § 55.1-1245(B); Va. Code § 55.1-1202
Good to know
  • Specify the acts and omissions that are the breach: the statute requires it.
  • You can’t file a lease-violation case until you’ve given the tenant the Statement of Tenant Rights and Responsibilities (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(H)).
  • This is the notice for a breach the tenant can remedy. For one that can’t be remedied, or a repeat of a like breach after a prior notice, the notice is 30 days with no chance to remedy, and must say so (and, for a repeat, refer to the prior breach) (Va. Code § 55.1-1245(C), (E)). A criminal or willful act that threatens health or safety lets you terminate immediately (Va. Code § 55.1-1245(C)). Ask a lawyer for those.
  • A lease doesn’t terminate solely because of an act of family abuse against the tenant (Va. Code § 55.1-1245(D)).
  • A breach that repair, replacement or cleaning can fix: you may instead give written notice that you’ll enter and do the work, and bill the tenant, the bill due as rent on the next due date (Va. Code § 55.1-1248).

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy, served at least 30 days before the next rent due date.

When
You want to end a month-to-month tenancy.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1253(A); Va. Code § 55.1-1204(F)
Good to know
  • The rental agreement can set a different notice period (Va. Code § 55.1-1253(A)). A week-to-week tenancy takes 7 days before the next rent due date.
  • An owner of multifamily premises who doesn’t renew 20 or more month-to-month tenancies, or half of them, within 30 days must give each tenant 60 days’ written notice (Va. Code § 55.1-1253(B)).
  • A landlord of more than 4 units must give written notice of nonrenewal at least 60 days before a fixed-term lease ends (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(K)). A lease that simply ends still needs a written notice: the court won’t order possession without a termination notice in evidence (Va. Code § 8.01-126(D)).
  • Virginia has no separate demand for possession: if the tenant stays after the date, file the Summons for Unlawful Detainer (Va. Code § 55.1-1253(C)). The lease may set holdover damages of up to 150% of the daily rent.
  • Virginia has no just-cause rule, but a notice in retaliation is barred (Va. Code § 55.1-1258).

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (DC-421, DC-413, DC-418, DC-422, DC-469)

Everything to copy onto the court’s Summons for Unlawful Detainer and the forms after it, with the steps and deadlines.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid, remedied or left.
The law
Va. Code § 8.01-126; Va. Code § 8.01-128; Va. Code § 8.01-129; Va. Code § 8.01-470; Va. Code § 8.01-471
Good to know
  • Virginia’s general district courts use the Supreme Court of Virginia’s forms, so NOHM doesn’t write the summons: copy these values onto DC-421 and file it. The clerk or magistrate has the forms too.
  • Ask for “all amounts due as of the hearing date” on DC-421 to have rent that comes due before the hearing included (Va. Code § 8.01-126(F)(1)).
  • For a lease violation, the tenant must have had the Statement of Tenant Rights and Responsibilities before you file (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(H)).

Move-in.

Move-in inspection report

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Within 5 days after the tenant moves in; they have 5 days after they get it to object in writing.
The law
Va. Code §§ 55.1-1214, 55.1-1215
Good to know
  • Give the tenant this report within 5 days after they move in (Va. Code § 55.1-1214). It is deemed correct unless they object in writing within 5 days after receiving it. Your written policy may let the tenant prepare it, or both of you together, and both sign it.
  • Fill in the mold row: the report must disclose whether there is visible evidence of mold. If there is, the tenant may end the lease, or you remediate it within 5 business days and give a new report (Va. Code § 55.1-1215).
  • Also at the start of a tenancy: offer a written lease and give the Statement of Tenant Rights and Responsibilities with it, both of you signing the Department of Housing and Community Development’s acknowledgment form, and give a signed copy of both within 10 business days (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(B), (H)).
  • The lease must have the fee disclosure statement on its first page (Va. Code § 55.1-1204.1), and you must give the names and addresses of the manager and of an owner or someone authorized to act for the owner (Va. Code § 55.1-1216(A)).
  • Give the renter’s insurance notice before signing if the lease doesn’t require renter’s insurance (NOHM’s form, Va. Code § 55.1-1206(D)), and a certificate that all smoke alarms are present, inspected and working (Va. Code § 55.1-1220(A)(8)).
  • Where they apply: the military air installation (Va. Code § 55.1-1217), defective drywall (§ 55.1-1218) and methamphetamine (§ 55.1-1219) disclosures, and the federal lead-based paint disclosure for homes built before 1978.

Renter’s insurance and flood notice

The written notice that your insurance doesn’t cover the tenant’s belongings, and that renter’s insurance doesn’t cover floods.

When
Before the lease is signed, when the lease doesn’t require renter’s insurance.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1206(D)
Good to know
  • Required before the tenant signs a lease that doesn’t require renter’s insurance (Va. Code § 55.1-1206(D)). Keep the tenant’s signed copy.
  • If you provide damage or renter’s insurance yourself, the tenant must also get notice of their right to their own policy and a summary of yours before signing (Va. Code § 55.1-1206(A)–(B)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter; at least 72 hours before routine maintenance the tenant didn’t ask for.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1229(A)
Good to know
  • Virginia asks for notice of your intent to enter and entry at reasonable times, except in an emergency or where notice is impractical (Va. Code § 55.1-1229(A)(4)). The statute sets no hours except for routine maintenance the tenant didn’t ask for: at least 72 hours, done within 14 days, and the notice must give the last date it may be done.
  • You may enter to inspect, make necessary or agreed repairs, decorations, alterations or improvements, supply services, or show the unit to purchasers, mortgagees, tenants, workmen or contractors (Va. Code § 55.1-1229(A)(1)).
  • Applying an insecticide or pesticide in the unit takes at least 48 hours’ written notice (Va. Code § 55.1-1223). A temporary relocation for a nonemergency repair takes 30 days’ written notice (Va. Code § 55.1-1229(B)).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1253(D)
Good to know
  • For a month-to-month tenancy (or a tenant holding over), a new rent takes effect only on the next rent due date coming 30 days after written notice (Va. Code § 55.1-1253(D)).
  • During a lease, rent changes only if both of you agree in writing (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(I)).
  • A landlord of more than 4 units must give written notice of a rent increase for a renewal term at least 60 days before the lease ends (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(K)); from July 1, 2027, 90 days, with a deadline at least 30 days out for the tenant to say whether they’ll renew.
  • Virginia has no rent cap, and localities can’t adopt their own landlord-tenant rules (Va. Code § 55.1-1201(E)). A retaliatory increase is barred (Va. Code § 55.1-1258).

Move-out.

Notice of the right to be at the move-out inspection

Tells the tenant they may be present when you inspect the home for the deposit.

When
When you ask the tenant to vacate, or within 5 days after the tenant tells you they’re leaving.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1226(G)
Good to know
  • Give it when you ask the tenant to vacate, or within 5 days after you get the tenant’s notice that they intend to vacate (Va. Code § 55.1-1226(G)).
  • If the tenant asks to be present, inspect within 72 hours after they deliver possession. Afterward, give them the written security deposit disposition statement with its itemized list of damages (NOHM’s deposit form).

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 45 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1226(A)
Good to know
  • Itemize the deposit and every deduction in a written notice, with any refund, within 45 days after the tenancy ends or the tenant vacates, whichever is later (Va. Code § 55.1-1226).
  • If damages exceed the deposit and need a third-party contractor, tell the tenant in writing within the 45 days; you then have 15 more days to itemize the damages and the cost of repair (Va. Code § 55.1-1226(E)).
  • With several tenants, one check payable to all of them goes to the forwarding address one of them gave. With no forwarding address, hold it; after a year it may go to the State Treasurer as unclaimed property (Va. Code § 55.1-1226(B)).
  • You may hold back enough for the final water, sewer or utility bill only if the tenant was told so in writing at least 15 days before; refund the rest within 10 days after the bill is paid (Va. Code § 55.1-1226(C)).
  • The deposit can’t be more than two months’ rent (Va. Code § 55.1-1226(A)). No interest is owed. Keep records of deductions for 2 years (§ 55.1-1226(F)).
  • Willfully failing to comply means returning the deposit with actual damages and attorney’s fees, unless the tenant owes rent (Va. Code § 55.1-1226(E)).

7-day notice of suspected abandonment

Asks a tenant who seems to have left to say in writing, within 7 days, that they intend to stay.

When
You can’t tell whether the tenant has abandoned the home.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1249; Va. Code § 55.1-1254
Good to know
  • Use it when you can’t determine whether the tenant has abandoned the premises (Va. Code § 55.1-1249). If they don’t answer in writing within 7 days, the premises are presumed abandoned and the rental agreement is deemed to end that day.
  • It includes the statement Va. Code § 55.1-1254 asks for, so property left may be disposed of within 24 hours after the 7 days end.
  • While the tenant is absent more than 7 days, you may enter at times reasonably necessary to protect your property (Va. Code § 55.1-1249).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Va. Code § 55.1-1254
Good to know
  • Use it after the tenancy has ended and possession is back with you. You may dispose of the property within 24 hours after 10 days from the date of this notice (Va. Code § 55.1-1254).
  • The same statement may instead go in the termination notice (disposal within 24 hours after termination) or in the 7-day abandonment notice (NOHM’s form).
  • Sale proceeds go to the tenant’s account, against what they owe and the costs; the rest is treated as a security deposit (Va. Code § 55.1-1254).
  • After a writ of eviction is executed, this section doesn’t apply: Va. Code § 55.1-1255 does. For a sole tenant who died, give the emergency contact at least 10 days’ written notice instead (Va. Code § 55.1-1256).
