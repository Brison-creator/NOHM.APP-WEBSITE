Virginia 30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy, served at least 30 days before the next rent due date.

Updated November 7, 2026

Sign in on the web

The facts.

  • WhenYou want to end a month-to-month tenancy.
  • The lawVa. Code § 55.1-1253(A); Va. Code § 55.1-1204(F)
  • StageNotices before court

What to know.

  • The rental agreement can set a different notice period (Va. Code § 55.1-1253(A)). A week-to-week tenancy takes 7 days before the next rent due date.
  • An owner of multifamily premises who doesn’t renew 20 or more month-to-month tenancies, or half of them, within 30 days must give each tenant 60 days’ written notice (Va. Code § 55.1-1253(B)).
  • A landlord of more than 4 units must give written notice of nonrenewal at least 60 days before a fixed-term lease ends (Va. Code § 55.1-1204(K)). A lease that simply ends still needs a written notice: the court won’t order possession without a termination notice in evidence (Va. Code § 8.01-126(D)).
  • Virginia has no separate demand for possession: if the tenant stays after the date, file the Summons for Unlawful Detainer (Va. Code § 55.1-1253(C)). The lease may set holdover damages of up to 150% of the daily rent.
  • Virginia has no just-cause rule, but a notice in retaliation is barred (Va. Code § 55.1-1258).
