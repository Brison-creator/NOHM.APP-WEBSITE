Notices before court.

5-day notice to pay rent

Tells the tenant the rent is unpaid and that the lease will end by a court filing if it isn’t paid within 5 days after they receive it.

When
Rent is unpaid when due (Arizona has no grace period).
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1368(B), (G); Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1313(B); Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 1-243(A)
Good to know
  • Arizona has no grace period by statute: you may give this once rent is unpaid when due.
  • You don’t have to accept a partial payment. If you do, you keep the right to proceed only if the tenant agrees in a contemporaneous writing to its terms, with the date the balance is due; otherwise accepting rent knowing of the default waives your right to terminate for it (A.R.S. § 33-1371). Housing assistance payments aren’t partial payments.
  • Hand it to the tenant, or send it by registered or certified mail; mailed, NOHM counts the 5 days the law allows for it to arrive (A.R.S. § 33-1313(B)). Posting it on the door or mailing it first class is not a way the law allows.
  • A last day that falls on a Sunday or a legal holiday moves to the next day (A.R.S. § 1-243(A)); NOHM also moves it off a Saturday, which is only later.
  • The court dismisses an eviction if the notice doesn’t meet the statute or wasn’t properly served (Rules of Procedure for Eviction Actions, Rule 13(a)(2), read from an unofficial copy).

10-day notice to remedy a lease violation

Says what the tenant did wrong; if it isn’t remedied within 10 days after they receive it, the lease ends.

When
The tenant materially broke the lease.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1368(A), (G); Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1313(B); Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 1-243(A)
Good to know
  • Describe exactly what the tenant did or didn’t do: the law requires the notice to specify it.
  • A noncompliance with the tenant’s duties (A.R.S. § 33-1341) that materially affects health and safety has a 5-day notice instead. A breach that is both material and irreparable (a weapon discharged, a serious crime, threats, or serious damage, among others) allows a notice of immediate termination. NOHM doesn’t draw those; ask a lawyer.
  • Some breaches can’t be cured: material falsification of the application (occupants, pets, income, Social Security number, employment, criminal or eviction history), and a second noncompliance of the same or a similar nature during the lease, which allows a special detainer action 10 days after a written notice saying so (A.R.S. § 33-1368(A)).
  • The tenant is responsible for guests’ lease violations they could reasonably foresee (A.R.S. § 33-1368(F)).
  • A last day that falls on a Sunday or a legal holiday moves to the next day (A.R.S. § 1-243(A)); NOHM also moves it off a Saturday, which is only later.

30-day notice to end a month-to-month

Ends a month-to-month tenancy at the end of a rental period at least 30 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out; Arizona needs no reason.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1375(B); Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1313(B)
Good to know
  • A week-to-week tenancy needs 10 days’ written notice instead (A.R.S. § 33-1375(A)).
  • Arizona has no just-cause rule, but you can’t end a tenancy to retaliate against a tenant (A.R.S. § 33-1381).
  • If the tenant stays after the date, you may file a special detainer action; a willful holdover not in good faith can cost the tenant up to two months’ rent or twice your actual damages, whichever is greater (A.R.S. § 33-1375(C)).

Demand for possession

A written demand to hand the home back, the step before filing to evict.

When
The lease ended, or a notice ended it, and the tenant is still there.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-1173(A)(1); Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1375(C)
Good to know
  • A tenant who stays after the lease term ends, or after receiving a written demand for possession, is in forcible detainer (A.R.S. § 12-1173(A)(1)). The demand isn’t strictly required after a month-to-month notice, but it’s the safe step when a fixed term simply runs out.
  • Arizona sets no waiting period for it; NOHM fills in 5 days. If you mail it, allow 5 days for it to arrive (A.R.S. § 33-1313(B)).

Court papers.

Eviction complaint (special detainer), verified Court

The eviction filing: the complaint, sworn before a notary, with the facts Arizona’s statute and eviction rules ask for.

When
The notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t left.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1377; Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 12-1175(A)–(B), (D); Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1368
Good to know
  • Arizona lets you write your own complaint: no court may require its form, and one that meets the statute is enough (A.R.S. § 12-1175(D)). The courts’ generic complaint (AOCLJEA2F) is accepted too.
  • Sign the verification in front of a notary: the complaint must be under oath (A.R.S. § 12-1175(A)). Attach the notice and your proof of delivering it as Exhibit A.
  • File it in the justice court for the precinct where the rental is, or in superior court. Justice courts hear claims up to $10,000 (the courts’ self-service page).
  • The court issues the summons the day you file, setting the trial 3 to 6 days later; it must be served at least 2 days before (A.R.S. § 33-1377(B)). Serve the courts’ Residential Eviction Information Sheet (AOCLJEA3F) with the summons and complaint.
  • If personal service is attempted and fails, posting the summons on the main entrance within a day of issuance and mailing it certified the same day counts as received 3 days after mailing (A.R.S. § 33-1377(B)).
  • No writ of restitution until 5 calendar days after judgment (A.R.S. § 12-1178(C)). Either side may appeal within 5 days (§ 12-1179). No bond is needed to file.
  • After the judgment is paid in full, file a satisfaction of judgment in the justice court within 40 days (A.R.S. § 22-247(A), from September 12, 2026).
  • Who may appear: the owner, an LLC’s member or manager, or an Arizona attorney; a property manager can’t represent the owner (Rules of Procedure for Eviction Actions, Rule 11(b)(1), and a Maricopa County Justice Courts handout, neither read from an official copy).
  • The heading “YOU ARE BEING SUED TO BE EVICTED”, the subsidized-housing statement and, for unpaid rent, serving the lease’s rent terms and six months of the ledger come from the Rules of Procedure for Eviction Actions, Rule 5, read from an unofficial copy (amended September 12, 2026).

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1321(C)
Good to know
  • Required: on move-in, give the tenant a signed copy of the lease, a move-in form for listing existing damage, and written notice that they may be present at the move-out inspection (A.R.S. § 33-1321(C)). This form does the last two.
  • Before the tenancy starts, give the tenant in writing who manages the home and who receives notices for the owner, and tell them the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act is on the Department of Housing’s website (A.R.S. § 33-1322(A)–(B)): NOHM’s disclosure form does it.
  • State in writing the purpose of any nonrefundable fee or deposit; one not called nonrefundable is refundable (A.R.S. § 33-1321(B)).
  • Give bedbug educational materials to new tenants, except in a single-family home (A.R.S. § 33-1319). With a pool, give the Department of Health Services’ pool safety notice (A.R.S. § 36-1681(E)).
  • If foreclosure has begun, the lease must say so in the statute’s words (A.R.S. § 33-1331). Utility billing by submeter or ratio must be disclosed (A.R.S. § 33-1314.01). The property must be registered with the county assessor (A.R.S. § 33-1902). For a home built before 1978, give the federal lead-based paint disclosure.

Disclosure of manager and owner

Tells the tenant in writing who manages the home and who receives notices for the owner, and where to read Arizona’s landlord and tenant law.

When
At or before the start of the tenancy, and again whenever it changes.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1322(A)–(C)
Good to know
  • Required at or before the start of the tenancy; keep it current, and give it again when the tenant asks (A.R.S. § 33-1322(A)–(C)).

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
At least 2 days before you enter.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1343(D)
Good to know
  • Give at least 2 days’ notice and enter only at reasonable times (A.R.S. § 33-1343(D)).
  • A tenant’s request for service or maintenance is permission to enter for it, and waives a separate notice (§ 33-1343(B)). In an emergency you may enter without consent (§ 33-1343(C)).
  • Abusing access can cost you actual damages of at least one month’s rent (A.R.S. § 33-1376).

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
Good to know
  • No Arizona statute sets the notice for a rent increase. NOHM asks for 30 days and starts the new rent on a due date: a month-to-month can only be changed by ending it on 30 days’ notice to a rental date (A.R.S. § 33-1375(B)), so that is the safe minimum.
  • During a fixed-term lease you can change the rent only if the lease allows it.
  • Arizona has no rent cap, and cities and towns can’t control rents (A.R.S. § 33-1329(A)).
  • Adding submetered or ratio utility billing mid-tenancy needs at least 90 days’ notice (A.R.S. § 33-1314.01(G)). No retaliatory increases (A.R.S. § 33-1381).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 14 business days after the tenancy ends, you get the home back, and the tenant asks for it.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1321(D)
Good to know
  • Within 14 days, not counting Saturdays, Sundays or legal holidays, after the tenancy ends, the tenant gives back possession, and the tenant asks for it, give the itemized list of deductions and the amount due (A.R.S. § 33-1321(D)).
  • Mail it first class to the tenant’s last known place of residence unless they arranged otherwise in writing. If the tenant doesn’t dispute it within 60 days after mailing, the list is final.
  • Wrongfully keeping deposit money can cost twice the amount withheld (A.R.S. § 33-1321(E)).
  • The deposit can’t be more than one and one-half months’ rent. A fee or deposit not stated in writing as nonrefundable is refundable (A.R.S. § 33-1321(A)–(B)). No interest is required.

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
The law
Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 33-1370(D)–(F)
Good to know
  • Use it after you retake an abandoned unit, or after a writ of restitution, when the tenant’s things remain (A.R.S. §§ 33-1370(D), 33-1368(E)). Send it by certified mail AND post it (§ 33-1370(A)).
  • Before retaking an abandoned unit, send the tenant a notice of abandonment by certified mail and post it on the door for 5 days (A.R.S. § 33-1370(A)–(B)). NOHM doesn’t draw that notice. If the tenant returned the keys and left things behind, you may remove them at once (§ 33-1370(I)).
  • Hold the property 14 calendar days after you retake possession; then sell it or donate it to a qualified charity, and keep records for 12 months (A.R.S. § 33-1370(F)–(G)). A tenant who gives written notice of intent to reclaim has 5 days to do so (§ 33-1370(H)).
  • Perishables, plants and contaminated items may be disposed of; animals go to a shelter (A.R.S. § 33-1370(E)).
