Notices before court.

3-day notice to pay rent or vacate

Demands the rent due: pay it in 3 days, or hand back the home.

When
Rent is past due (Idaho has no grace period), within a year of the due date.
The law
Idaho Code § 6-303(2); Idaho Code § 6-304; Idaho Code § 6-324; Idaho Code § 73-109
Good to know
  • State the rent now due only, with no other fees or charges such as damage (Idaho courts’ instructions). The notice may demand payment or possession, not both.
  • Serve it any time within one year after the rent was due (Idaho Code § 6-303(2)). Serve any subtenant living there too.
  • Fees are recoverable only because the notice says attorney fees shall be awarded to the prevailing party (Idaho Code § 6-324); NOHM’s says so.
  • Where a copy is mailed, NOHM gives 3 more days: whether the court rule’s 3 days for mail reaches this notice isn’t settled.
  • The Idaho courts’ form for this notice is CAO UD 1; whoever serves it can use CAO UD 2, the Affidavit of Service, which you file with the complaint.
  • At trial you must show it was served at least 4 days before you filed (Idaho courts’ instructions).

3-day notice to perform or quit

Tells the tenant to do what the lease requires in 3 days, or hand back the home.

When
The tenant broke a lease term other than paying rent.
The law
Idaho Code § 6-303(3); Idaho Code § 6-304; Idaho Code § 73-109
Good to know
  • Describe the condition or covenant broken and what the tenant must do.
  • A breach that can’t afterward be performed needs no notice to perform (Idaho Code § 6-303(3)). Assigning, subletting or committing waste against the lease ends it, and needs only a 3-day notice to quit (§ 6-303(4)). Drug activity on the premises is its own ground (§ 6-303(5)). Ask a lawyer for those.
  • A lease-violation case isn’t on the expedited track: the tenant has 21 days to answer the complaint (I.R.C.P. 12(a)), and the Idaho courts’ eviction forms don’t cover it.
  • The prevailing party is entitled to attorney fees (Idaho Code § 6-324).

One-month notice to end a tenancy at will

Ends a month-to-month or other tenancy at will on a date at least one month and 30 days out.

When
You want a month-to-month tenant out, or won’t renew a lease, no fault needed.
The law
Idaho Code § 55-208(1); Idaho Code § 55-304(2); Idaho Code § 6-304
Good to know
  • Idaho requires at least one month (Idaho Code § 55-208(1)) and, for a residential lease, at least 30 days (§ 55-304(2)). A month can be 28 days, or 31, so NOHM counts 31 days, which meets both every month. Any day: it needn’t end a rental period.
  • A fixed-term lease you won’t renew needs the same 30 days’ written notice before it ends (§ 55-304(2)). After the term ends, no further demand is needed before you file (§ 6-303(1)).
  • This notice is the only notice before court: Idaho needs no separate demand for possession. After the date passes, and not before, you may go to court (§ 55-209).
  • The statute says to serve it “in the manner prescribed by the code of civil procedure”; NOHM reads that as the § 6-304 methods.

Court papers.

Eviction filing worksheet (CAO UD 1-1, 1-2, 2-1, 8-1, 10-1)

Idaho’s courts use the Court Assistance Office’s statewide eviction forms: this lists them, fills in what to write on the complaint from the lease, and sets out the steps and deadlines.

When
The 3-day notice ran out and the tenant hasn’t paid or left.
The law
Idaho Code § 6-310; Idaho Code § 6-311A; Idaho Code § 6-316; Idaho Code § 6-318
Good to know
  • The Court Assistance Office forms are for eviction for unpaid rent (the expedited track). They can’t be used for another lease violation or a holdover: that complaint must be written to the facts and verified (Idaho Code § 6-318), and the tenant has 21 days to answer. Use a lawyer for it.
  • The owner must be the plaintiff and sign the complaint. A property manager or other agent can’t represent the owner unless they’re an Idaho lawyer (CAO instructions). A company (an LLC or corporation) generally must appear through a lawyer (from a case summary; not confirmed in the case itself).
  • Asking for damages as well as possession takes the case off the expedited schedule (Idaho Code § 6-311E).
  • The filing fee shown by the Court Assistance Office is $166; issuing the writ is $2.
  • Bring the lease, the notice and its affidavit of service, and the rent ledger.

Move-in.

Move-in condition checklist

The written record of the home’s condition when the tenant moves in, signed by both of you.

When
Give it at move-in, and keep the signed copy.
The law
Idaho Code § 6-320(a)(6)
Good to know
  • Idaho doesn’t require a move-in checklist; the Attorney General’s manual includes a sample, and a signed record helps with the deposit. Verifying the smoke detectors at the start is required (Idaho Code § 6-320(a)(6)), and this form records it.
  • List every fee in the written rental agreement: a fee not in it needs 30 days’ written notice (Idaho Code § 55-305(2)).
  • Write the deposit’s contingencies into the lease: only they can be deducted (Idaho Code § 6-321(1)).
  • Built before 1978: the federal lead-based paint disclosure and pamphlet.

During the lease.

Notice of entry

Tells the tenant when you’ll come in, and why.

When
Before you enter for an inspection, a repair or a showing.
Good to know
  • No Idaho statute sets notice before entry; your lease should. NOHM suggests at least 24 hours.
  • If the lease is silent, the Attorney General advises telling the tenant why you need to enter and agreeing on a reasonable time.
  • In an emergency involving life or property you may need to go in right away; tell the tenant as soon as you can.

The notice of entry in full · Read the law: ag.idaho.gov

Notice of rent change

Changes a month-to-month tenant’s rent from a rent due date.

When
At least 30 days before the new rent starts.
The law
Idaho Code § 55-304(2)
Good to know
  • A residential landlord must give written notice of a rent increase at least 30 days before it takes effect (Idaho Code § 55-304(2)).
  • A new or changed fee not in the written rental agreement also needs 30 days’ written notice (Idaho Code § 55-305(2)).
  • Idaho has no rent cap, and cities and counties can’t regulate rent, fees or deposits (Idaho Code § 55-306).

Move-out.

Security deposit: itemized deductions

The written list of what you kept from the deposit, and why, with the refund.

When
Within 21 days after the tenancy ends and you get the home back.
The law
Idaho Code § 6-321
Good to know
  • Refund the deposit within 21 days after the tenant surrenders the home, or the time the lease fixes, never more than 30 days (Idaho Code § 6-321(2)).
  • Any refund less than the full deposit needs this signed, itemized statement, with a detailed list of what was spent from it.
  • Deduct only for the contingencies the deposit arrangement names, never normal wear and tear (§ 6-321(1)).
  • A third-party manager must keep deposits in a separate account at a federally insured institution (§ 6-321(4)).
  • A tenant can sue for a deposit not returned as the law requires, after 3 days’ written notice; damages can be trebled, and the prevailing party gets attorney fees (Idaho Code §§ 6-320, 6-317, 6-324).

Notice of property left behind

Lists what the tenant left, and how long you’ll hold it before you dispose of it.

When
After the tenancy ended and the tenant left things behind.
Good to know
  • No Idaho statute covers property left after a tenant moves out on their own; this letter isn’t a statutory notice. NOHM fills in 14 days to hold it.
  • For property of value, the Attorney General advises filing an eviction complaint and asking the court for an order allowing its removal, and permission to sell it to cover costs or back rent. Idaho gives landlords no lien on it unless the lease does.
